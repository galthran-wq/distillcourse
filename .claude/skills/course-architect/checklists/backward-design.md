# Backward-design checklist (course-architect self-check)

Answer each with yes/no before reporting. Any "no" means fix first.

1. Were outcomes written (with Bloom levels) BEFORE any lesson was designed?
2. Does every module claim ≥1 course outcome, and is every outcome claimed?
3. Is the total 10–15 lessons? (More = cut scope, don't squeeze.)
4. Are the first two lessons the easiest in the course, each with a felt win
   reachable inside 10 minutes?
5. Does every lesson goal contain exactly ONE objective, starting with a
   Bloom verb?
6. Is every opening a precise declarative statement of the lesson's problem
   (no questions, no teasers), and every title a technical noun phrase (C6)?
7. Do prerequisites only point backwards (validator: prereq-cycle)?
8. Does every core concept from the first half get reinforced (`reinforces`)
   at least once in the second half?
9. Does every demo spec describe a concept that is dynamic/multi-parameter —
   would a static figure do the job instead (D1)?
10. Could a busy engineer look at the module list alone and tell exactly what
    they will be able to do after finishing? (If not, retitle.)
11. Did `npm run validate <slug>` pass with zero errors AND zero warnings?

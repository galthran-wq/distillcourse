## Explanation plan

1. **The problem.** Ryle removed the ghost and left a question: what *is* pain, if 'in pain' is a disposition — what is the base (the glass's molecular structure for brittleness)? Sub-problem: the three answers and why each was offered. Obvious attempt (behaviourism, Ayer 1936; Quine's 'too easy'): pain is the behaviour. Why it fails: a disposition is not its manifestation (Ryle's own point), and the private-language lesson's criteria are not constitution.
2. **The identity theory (1956–59).** Pain is a brain process — C-fibre firing — as lightning is electrical discharge and heat is molecular motion: a *contingent* identity, discovered by science, between a mental type and a physical type. Smart's 'topic-neutral' translation (standard): 'I am in pain' means 'something is going on in me like what goes on when…' — neutral between mental and physical, so that science can fill in. The because: it supplies Ryle's missing base, keeps the sensation (unlike behaviourism), and models itself on the identifications the Kripke module read as discoveries.
3. **Functionalism (1967).** Multiple realisability: an octopus, a Martian or a silicon system might be in pain without C-fibres; so pain is not a brain *type* but a *role* — the state caused by injury that causes avoidance, belief that one is hurt, and so on; the brain realises the role as hardware runs software; the same programme on different machines. Aristotle's 'if the eye were an animal, sight would be its soul; the soul of an axe is cutting' (Magee–Putnam). The because: identity theory tied the mind to one realiser; the role analysis keeps what was right (an inner state with a base) and drops the tie.
4. **The modal argument (Kripke 1970).** Recall the heat model (`<Ref>`): 'heat' fixed by sensation $S$; the identity 'heat = molecular motion' necessary; the feeling of contingency explained by 'something else could have caused $S$' — an *appearance* distinct from the thing. Now 'pain = C-fibre firing': both terms rigid ('pain' picks out pain by an essential property — its felt quality; 'C-fibre firing' by its physical essence); so if identical, necessarily identical. The Cartesian intuition — a world with the firing and no pain, or pain without the firing — would need explaining away as in the heat case; but there is no appearance of pain distinct from pain: 'to be in the epistemic situation of having a pain is to have a pain'. So the intuition cannot be explained away, and the identity theorist owes an account that the heat model cannot supply. The God-creating-the-world argument: having made molecular motion, God had nothing more to do to make heat; having made C-fibre firing, God had still to make the creatures *feel* it as pain — extra work means a distinct fact. The because: the Kripke module's machinery (rigidity; the illusion of contingency) applied to the one case where the illusion has no appearance to hide in.
5. **What functionalism answers and what it does not.** Multiple realisability already defeats *type* identity, so the modal argument's target may seem gone; but Kripke's second point hits the role analysis: if pain were the occupant of a causal role, being a pain would be *contingent* — the state might have played another role — and 'that is absurd' (pp. 146–147). Functionalism then needs either analytic functionalism (Lewis: 'pain' *means* the role, so the occupant is pain contingently and the role-term is non-rigid) or a posteriori physicalism (the identity is necessary and the intuition is a cognitive illusion of a different kind — phenomenal concepts). Named, not developed. The two next objections, one sentence each: Nagel's bat (what it is like; the subjective fact a functional description omits) and Searle's Chinese room (a system that realises the role and does not understand — the beetle objection from the Turing lesson, `<Ref>`).
6. **Where the tradition left it.** 'The mind–body problem is wide open and extremely confusing' (n. 77); Kripke disclaims dualism; the two live replies named; what survived — the modal argument as the test any physicalism must pass, functionalism as the working picture of cognitive science, and the Cartesian intuition as data nobody has explained away.

## EXERCISE PLAN

1. (warm-up) Classify each as behaviourism, identity theory or functionalism, with the sentence that decides: 'pain is whatever state plays the pain role'; 'pain is C-fibre firing'; 'to be in pain is to be disposed to wince and say "ouch"'.
2. (derivation — the modal argument in full) Write Kripke's argument as premises: rigidity of both terms; necessity of identity; the Cartesian intuition; the heat model's requirement of an appearance; the absence of one for pain; conclusion. Identify the premise an a posteriori physicalist must deny and the one an analytic functionalist must deny.
3. (boundary probe) Suppose 'pain' were fixed by a description ('the state that usually causes wincing') and so non-rigid. Show that the modal argument then fails, and say what this costs (being a pain becomes contingent — Kripke's 'absurd') and who pays it (Lewis).
4. (refute) "Multiple realisability refutes the identity theory, so Kripke's argument is unnecessary." Break it: show that multiple realisability defeats type identity but not token identity (this pain = this firing), and that the modal argument is stated for tokens too.
5. (claim–evidence — the God argument) Reconstruct the God-creating-the-world argument and find the premise ('having made the firing, God had still to make it felt') that a physicalist would call question-begging; say what independent support Kripke offers (the missing appearance) and whether it is independent.
6. (cross-lesson) Ayer 1936 called the mind–body 'gulf' a fictitious problem (constructions only); Wittgenstein §412 called it giddiness from a sleight of hand; Kripke calls it wide open. State in one sentence each what makes the problem disappear for the first two and reappear for the third — the status of the Cartesian intuition as data.
7. (FORWARD → parfit-personal-identity) Functionalism makes a mental state a role that different matter can realise. Say what this implies for a person copied, state by state, onto new matter — whether anything is lost — and what question about *identity* remains after every role is preserved.

## Explanation plan

1. **The problem.** The verification criterion (and the first dogma) need each statement to carry its own confirming experiences. But what does an experiment test? The needle's movement follows from the hypothesis *plus* the theory of the galvanometer *plus* 'nothing else is interfering'; when the needle stays still, any conjunct may be blamed. Ayer saw this in 1936 ('logically our freedom is unlimited') and drew no consequence. Sub-problem: if no statement is tested alone, what is 'the method of verification of a statement'?
2. **Obvious attempt: reductionism, term by term, then statement by statement.** Locke and Hume: every idea reducible to sense impressions (term-by-term); Frege's context principle moved the unit to the statement; Carnap's Aufbau (1928) is the serious attempt — point-instants as quadruples of real numbers, qualities assigned so as to make 'the laziest world compatible with our experience'. Why it fails: the connective 'is at' — quality $q$ is at point-instant $(x, y, z, t)$ — is never defined in sense-datum terms; the assignment is a *rule for adjusting* the whole, not a translation of a statement. Carnap himself abandoned the attempt. The because: the rule of assignment makes sense only for the system, so the system is the unit.
3. **Duhem generalised.** Statements face the tribunal 'not individually but only as a corporate body'; the unit of empirical significance is the whole of science. Then the first dogma reappears: if a statement had its own confirming experiences, its truth could be split into a factual component (those experiences) and a linguistic one (the rest); holism denies the split — 'the two dogmas are at root identical'.
4. **The web.** Total science as a fabric impinging on experience along the edges; a field of force with experience as boundary conditions; periphery (brick houses, centaurs) and centre (physics, logic); a recalcitrant experience forces *some* re-evaluation, and the re-evaluation can land anywhere: 'any statement can be held true come what may'; 'no statement is immune to revision' — the proposed revision of excluded middle for quantum mechanics is of a kind with Kepler superseding Ptolemy. For this reader: logic at the centre is not exempt, only maximally costly to revise. The because: holism gives no node a special relation to experience, so protection is a matter of position, not kind.
5. **Posits and ontology.** Physical objects are posits — 'cultural posits', epistemologically on a par with Homer's gods, differing in degree of efficacy, not in kind; classes are admitted because physics needs them (indispensability, Magee); properties and propositions rejected for want of an identity criterion (equilateral/equiangular). 'To be is to be the value of a bound variable': the ontology of a theory is read off its regimented quantifiers (Magee; IEP) — Carnap's external questions become internal to total science, and 'ontology is continuous with science' (the closing paragraph). The irrational-numbers analogy: experience is to physical objects as rationals to irrationals.
6. **Naturalism.** No first philosophy; philosophy is continuous with science at its abstract end (Magee); epistemology becomes a chapter of psychology (stated from the 1969 paper via IEP, flagged); semantic ascent explains why philosophers talk about language without being linguistic philosophers. Quine's pragmatism against Carnap's: framework choice is pragmatic *everywhere*, not only at the boundary.
7. **Why it mattered / what refuted or limited it.** Mattered: the fact/meaning boundary gone, naturalism the default of the next half-century, analysis-as-synonymous-rephrasing undermined (IEP). Limited: (a) holism says some revision is forced and not which — the next lesson's test; the 'maxim of minimum mutilation' is added by hand; (b) Grünbaum and others: Duhem's thesis is weaker than Quine's (not every auxiliary can rescue every hypothesis) — stated, not argued, no source on disk; (c) Putnam: the stereotype is a non-analytic, non-revisable-at-will obligation — holism is right about theories and silent about competence; (d) Davidson's 'third dogma' (scheme and content) in one sentence as the next turn of the screw, named only. Survived: the web as the picture everyone uses; naturalism; the Peircean 'what difference would it make' as the criterion's residue.

## Pitfalls / subtleties to flag

- The Aufbau is one section; do not reconstruct it. 'Point-instant' and 'quality' are the only terms needed, defined at use.
- 'Any statement can be held true' is often misread as 'anything goes'; the lesson states the cost structure (centre vs periphery) in the same paragraph and the next lesson tests it.
- 'Ontological commitment' and 'naturalised epistemology' come from texts not on disk; state them in one paragraph each, cite the Magee conversation in References, flag the texts as to-be-staged.
- Behaviourism about meaning (Magee) is *not* the private-language argument; one sentence that both distrust inner meanings, different conclusions, and a forward `<Ref>` to the Wittgenstein module.
- Quine's 'free will' and 'why-questions' remarks are seductive detail: cut or starred.

## EXERCISE PLAN

1. (warm-up) For 'the sample is acidic' tested with litmus, list the statements the observation 'the paper turned red' depends on, and state which two Quine's holism and Ayer's 1936 paragraph both say could be revised instead of the hypothesis.
2. (derivation) Show that if confirmation attaches to statements individually then each statement's truth splits into a factual and a linguistic component, and conversely that holism makes the split undefinable statement by statement — i.e. that the two dogmas are one.
3. (boundary probe) Take a statement at the exact centre: '$p \vee \neg p$'. State what revising it would cost in the web, what quantum logic proposed to revise instead (distributivity, not excluded middle — a correction to Quine's own example the reader can check), and whether 'held true come what may' is a description of practice or a logical possibility.
4. (refute) "Holism makes science arbitrary: any theory can be saved." Break it: quote the cost structure (periphery/centre; minimum mutilation), and construct the case where saving a hypothesis requires revising so much that the web no longer predicts the next observation — the practical limit the next lesson examines.
5. (claim–evidence) Quine admits classes and rejects properties. Write the identity criterion that classes have and properties lack (equilateral/equiangular), and then ask whether 'creature with a heart' and 'creature with a kidney' name one class or two; say what this does to the previous lesson's meaning/extension distinction.
6. (FORWARD → neptune-vulcan-web-of-belief) A planet is not where the law says. Using the three-revision figure, list the three places the revision could land (initial conditions; an unseen body; the law) and say what holism predicts about which will be chosen. The solution says: nothing — and the next lesson shows the same anomaly resolved both ways, with a `<Ref>`.
7. (research-shaped) 'To be is to be the value of a bound variable': regiment 'there are prime numbers between 10 and 20' and 'the average woman has 1.9 children' and read off the commitment of each; then say what Quine would say to Carnap's claim that the first is internal and the second a construction — one paragraph, both voices.

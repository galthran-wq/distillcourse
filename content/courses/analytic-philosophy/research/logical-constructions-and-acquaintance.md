## Explanation plan

1. **The problem.** The average British woman has 1.9 children — true, and no one adds her to the census. Why not? Because the sentence is shorthand: $\frac{\sum \text{children}}{\text{women}} = 1.9$. Sub-problem: how far does this go? 'The English State declared war' — is the State an entity over and above the people and offices? 'The table is brown' — is the table something besides what is seen and touched? Obvious attempt: take grammar at its word; every noun names a thing. Why it fails: the previous lesson showed grammar misleads for 'the King of France'; nothing stops it misleading for 'the table'.
2. **The definition.** $e$ is a logical construction out of $b, c, d$ iff every sentence mentioning $e$ can be rewritten as sentences about $b, c, d$ that do not mention $e$ (Ayer's form). The because: this is the theory of descriptions generalised — 'the average woman' is an incomplete symbol like 'the King of France', and the maxim follows: wherever possible substitute constructions for inferred entities. Not 'the average woman is fictitious like Hamlet'; the claim is about definability, not about what exists.
3. **Classes as the first construction — and the paradox answered.** Class talk (C1)–(C4) reduced to concept talk (Beaney); the theory of types in one section: a propositional function cannot take itself as argument, so there is no class of all classes not members of themselves to ask about. Contrast with Frege's ad hoc exclusion: Russell's restriction has a rationale (vicious circle), and classes become logical fictions — the paradox is answered by the construction, not by a patch.
4. **The epistemology that drives it: acquaintance.** Every proposition we understand is composed of constituents we are acquainted with (sense-data, universals); matter and other minds are known only by description; so a sentence about the table must be rewritten in terms of what is given. Ayer's sketch: sense-contents grouped by direct and indirect resemblance and continuity — symmetric, transitive relations partition them, so 'material things' are equivalence classes — the reader will see the structure at once (one figure, no more).
5. **The atomist picture (1918).** Atomic facts; atomic propositions as their pictures; every proposition a truth-function of atomic ones; the world described by all true atomic propositions plus 'that is all'. Why this and not something looser: the construction programme needs a bottom (the things one is acquainted with) and a glue (truth-functional composition), and atomism is the name for exactly those two commitments. Ramsey's 'paradigm of philosophy' as the verdict on the method.
6. **What refuted it.** Same-level vs new-level analysis (Stebbing): the theory of descriptions is same-level (logical), the reduction to sense-data is new-level (metaphysical), and the second does not follow from the first. Black's economists: 'every economist is fallible' is not a list of facts about Keynes and Stamp, because the general proposition says something about anyone who *might* be an economist — reductive analysis fails for generality (the 'and that is all' clause is itself a general fact). What survived: contextual definition, the maxim, and the question the Tractatus asks next — what the atoms and the glue must be for a proposition to picture a fact at all.

## Pitfalls / subtleties to flag

- 'Logical fiction' does not mean 'does not exist': Russell's claim is about the form of sentences; say this once and test it in an exercise.
- The theory of types is one section, with its rationale, and no ramified/simple distinction, no axiom of reducibility — the Tractatus's dismissal (3.333) gets one forward clause.
- 'Sense-data' is a term the reader has not met: define at first use (what is immediately given in perception, a colour patch) and do not defend the doctrine; Austin's attack on it (Sense and Sensibilia) is one forward clause in austin-speech-acts.
- The atomism lectures are not on disk: every claim about them is routed through IEP/Beaney until staged; the References list the lectures with the honest pointer.
- Keep the Aufbau for the Vienna module; here one sentence that Carnap's 1928 book is this programme carried out.

## EXERCISE PLAN

1. (warm-up) Rewrite 'the median household earns £30k' and 'the United Kingdom signed the treaty' as sentences about households and about people and offices; identify the construction in each.
2. (refute) "To call the table a logical construction is to say there is no table." Break it: state Ayer's definition, show it is a claim about sentences, and give the test that separates 'construction' from 'fiction' (the Hamlet contrast).
3. (derivation) Take Ayer's resemblance relation and show that if it is symmetric and transitive the sense-contents partition into disjoint groups; then exhibit why 'indirect resemblance' (a chain of small differences) threatens transitivity, and what the construction of 'the same material thing' needs to add.
4. (boundary probe) The class of all classes not members of themselves: show in two lines why, with types, the question cannot be posed; then state what Frege's exclusion did instead and why Russell called it ad hoc.
5. (claim–evidence) 'Every economist is fallible' vs the list 'Keynes is fallible, Stamp is fallible, …': construct the situation in which the list is true and the general sentence false, and say what clause the atomist must add ('and that is all') and why that clause is not itself atomic.
6. (FORWARD → tractatus-picture-theory) Atomism needs atoms it never exhibits. Write down what an atomic proposition would have to look like on the acquaintance principle ('this red patch here now…') and try to state one; the solution says the Tractatus will argue that simples must exist without giving an example, with a `<Ref>`.
7. (research-shaped) Russell's centre of mass of the solar system 'at the first instant of the twentieth century': compute from the lesson's account what sentences a physicist would use to fix that point and show that each is a description over masses and positions, none an acquaintance — then say what a physicist is acquainted with when reading an instrument.

## APPLICATION spec (L16)

- **Artefact:** the sciences' own statements of the three kinds. (i) Gold: IUPAC's definition of an element by atomic number $Z = 79$; natural gold is monoisotopic ($^{197}$Au) but synthetic isotopes exist and are gold; Au$^+$ and Au$^{3+}$ ions are gold in compounds; 'fool's gold' is FeS$_2$. (ii) Water: pure water is H$_2$O molecules in dynamic equilibrium with H$_3$O$^+$ and OH$^-$ ($K_w = 10^{-14}$ at 25 °C, about one molecule in $5.5 \times 10^8$ ionised); natural water is a mixture of isotopologues — H$_2^{16}$O, H$_2^{18}$O (~0.2%), HD$^{16}$O (~0.03%), D$_2$O (~0.0156% of hydrogen is deuterium); heavy water is toxic to mammals at high fractions; 'water' as a substance term covers ice, steam, sea water in ordinary use. (iii) Species: the biological species concept (interbreeding populations), the phylogenetic (smallest diagnosable lineage), the morphological; ring species (the Ensatina salamanders; the greenish warbler) where $A$ interbreeds with $B$, $B$ with $C$, and $A$ not with $C$ — non-transitive; species as individuals/lineages (Ghiselin, Hull) rather than kinds with a microstructural essence. (All standard; verify against the chemistry and the species-concept literature — research/sources.yaml — before writing; numbers framed in prose.)
- **Module results it uses:** rigid kind terms and the sample-fixed reference; the necessary a posteriori ('gold is 79'); Twin Earth's same$_L$ and its interest-relative 'important'; the division of linguistic labour (IUPAC and the taxonomists as the experts); the stereotype (gold is yellow; tigers are striped).
- **Where the artefact departs from the idealisation:** (1) gold is the one case where the theory's 'essence' is exactly the science's criterion of identity — and it is so because chemistry *defined* element identity by $Z$ (a Carnapian explication inside the framework, not a discovery of a Kripkean essence; the discovery was that $Z$, not atomic weight, orders the table — Moseley 1913); (2) 'water is H$_2$O' is true of the molecule and false of the substance as a mixture, so 'same liquid' must tolerate isotopic and ionic variation, which the theory already granted by making 'important' interest-relative — the escape clause is doing the work; (3) jade is the predicted failure, and the theory's counterfactual (had XYZ been plentiful, 'water' would be like 'jade') is confirmed in the sense that nothing in the semantics decides whether a kind is one or two — the science does; (4) species have no microstructural essence and no single concept; the family-resemblance account outperforms the essence account exactly here, and 'tigers are a species' is a claim about a lineage, not a hidden structure.

## Explanation plan

1. **The problem.** 'Water is H2O' is the textbook necessary a posteriori; but tap water is a solution, pure water self-ionises, heavy water is D2O and poisonous; 'jade' is two minerals; biologists do not agree what a species is. Sub-problem: when the theory says 'the essence science discovers', what does the science actually hand over — and is it an essence? Obvious attempt: the examples are idealised and the theory survives idealisation, as physics survives frictionless planes. The lesson tests that by running each example to the science and seeing which parts of the theory the idealisation was carrying.
2. **Gold.** Chemistry: an element is defined by $Z$; all isotopes of $Z = 79$ are gold; Au ions in compounds are gold. The theory's claim — 'gold is the element with atomic number 79', necessary a posteriori, fool's gold excluded, blue gold included — matches exactly. The division of labour: IUPAC is the expert; the stereotype (yellow, though pure gold is near-white — Putnam) is false and harmless. Where the match comes from: chemistry *chose* $Z$ as the identity criterion for elements after Moseley (1913) showed that $Z$ and not atomic weight orders the periodic table; before that, 'the same element' was fixed by chemical behaviour and weight, and isotopes (1913, Soddy) were a problem *for* the element concept — the essence was settled by an explication (`<Ref>` Carnap) inside a theory. The because: the theory's cleanest case is clean because the science made a definitional decision the theory reads as a discovery; the discovery was which quantity to define by.
3. **Water.** 'H2O' names a molecule; 'water' names a substance. Pure water: H$_2$O in equilibrium with H$_3$O$^+$ and OH$^-$ — a fraction of a billionth ionised at any instant; so 'water is H2O' is true of the dominant species and false of the sample taken as a set of molecules. Natural water: isotopologues at the fractions above (framed: about one hydrogen in six thousand is deuterium); D$_2$O freezes at 3.8 °C and is toxic in bulk — is heavy water water? Chemistry says: the same compound, different isotopologue; ordinary use says: a special kind of water. The theory's answer is already in Putnam: same$_L$ holds 'in important respects', and 'important' is interest-relative; so the theory tolerates the mixture by making the tolerance a parameter it does not set. The because: 'water is H2O' survives as a claim about *molecular identity* and the substance term 'water' is fixed by a sample that is a mixture — the theory is right about the molecule and silent about the substance.
4. **Jade.** Jadeite (a sodium aluminium silicate) and nephrite (a calcium magnesium iron silicate): one word, two microstructures, indistinguishable by look and feel; the Chinese never distinguished them; the theory predicted exactly this ('had XYZ been plentiful…'). What the case shows: whether a kind term picks out one hidden structure or two is not settled by the semantics; the chain and the sample fix a *use*, and the science finds how many structures lie under it. The because: the theory's counterfactual is confirmed, and confirmation here means the theory does not decide the case.
5. **Species.** 'Tiger' as a natural-kind term: the theory's essence is 'the internal structure that makes it a tiger' (Kripke: 'tigers are a species'). Biology: at least three species concepts that disagree at the margins; ring species, where interbreeding is not transitive ($A$–$B$, $B$–$C$, not $A$–$C$), so 'same species' is not an equivalence relation under the biological concept; species as lineages (segments of the tree of life) — individuals with a beginning and an end, not kinds with members sharing a microstructure (Ghiselin, Hull). The games matrix from the family-resemblance lesson, re-run on tigers: no feature common to all (Ziff's three-legged tiger was right) and no hidden structure either — a lineage. The because: the essence account fits elements, strains for compounds, and fails for species, where family resemblance *and* history do the work.
6. **Verdict.** Which module results the check used; where the artefact departs (the four points of the spec); and the synthesis: 'hidden structure' is a theory's decision about what to count as the same — chemistry decided by $Z$ for elements and by molecular formula for compounds and tolerates mixtures by convention; biology decided by lineage and tolerates non-transitivity. The Kripke–Putnam theory is right that the deciding is done by the science and not by the speaker (externalism survives intact); it is wrong, or silent, where it suggests that what the science hands over is always an essence. One forward sentence: the closing module's identity theory ('pain is C-fibre firing') is another such identification, and the same question — what counts as the same state? — is where multiple realisability bites.

## EXERCISE PLAN

1. (warm-up) For $^{197}$Au, $^{195}$Au, Au$^{3+}$ in AuCl$_3$, and FeS$_2$: state which are gold on the atomic-number criterion and which the stereotype (yellow metal) would misclassify.
2. (research-shaped) From the isotopic abundances (deuterium about 0.0156% of hydrogen; $^{18}$O about 0.2% of oxygen) compute the fraction of molecules in natural water that are exactly H$_2^{16}$O, and state in one sentence what 'water is H2O' is true of and what it is not.
3. (boundary probe — heavy water) D$_2$O: same compound, different isotopologue, freezes at 3.8 °C, toxic in bulk. Say whether it bears same$_L$ to the lake sample for a chemist, for a brewer and for a mammal, and what Putnam's 'important' has to be for each; then say whether any of the three is the *semantics'* answer.
4. (refute) "Jade refutes the Kripke–Putnam theory: one word, two essences." Break it with Putnam's own prediction (p. 160) and say what jade actually shows — that the number of structures under a word is found by the science, which is the externalist thesis, not its refutation.
5. (derivation — non-transitivity) Define the biological species concept as an interbreeding relation and show that a ring species makes 'same species' fail transitivity; state what this does to 'tigers are a species' read as 'share a microstructural essence', and what 'lineage' puts in its place.
6. (claim–evidence — Moseley) Before 1913 elements were ordered by atomic weight and isotopes were an anomaly; after, by $Z$. Argue in a paragraph whether 'gold is the element with atomic number 79' records a discovery about gold or a decision about 'element'; use the Carnap lesson's internal/external distinction and say what Kripke's 'necessary a posteriori' needs it to be.
7. (FORWARD → functionalism-and-its-objections) 'Heat is molecular motion' and 'pain is C-fibre firing' are both theoretical identifications. Using this lesson's verdict, say what question must be settled before either is an *identity* (what counts as the same state), and what the biology case suggests about states that can be realised in more than one structure. The solution says the closing module calls this multiple realisability, with a `<Ref>`.

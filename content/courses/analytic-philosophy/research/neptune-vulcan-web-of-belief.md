# Research brief: neptune-vulcan-web-of-belief (The web of belief tested: Neptune, Vulcan and the perihelion of Mercury)

status: planned
module: quine
type: core — APPLICATION lesson (L16) closing the Quine module
goal: Analyze two anomalies in Newtonian astronomy — Uranus's orbit in 1846 and Mercury's perihelion from 1859 to 1915 — as choices of which statement in a body of theory to revise, and state what holism does and does not predict about them.

## APPLICATION spec (L16)

- **Artefact:** the astronomical record. (i) 1846: Uranus's observed positions deviate from the Newtonian prediction by up to ~2′ of arc; Le Verrier (and independently Adams) keeps the inverse-square law and the known masses and posits an unseen planet, computes its position; Galle finds Neptune on 23 September 1846 within about 1° of the predicted place. (ii) 1859: Le Verrier reports an unexplained advance of Mercury's perihelion of ~38″ per century (later refined to ~43″); he posits an intra-Mercurial planet, Vulcan, or a belt of asteroids; decades of claimed sightings, none confirmed; alternatives tried — solar oblateness, a modified exponent in the law ($r^{-2.0000001574}$, Hall 1894), zodiacal matter; (iii) 1915: general relativity predicts 43″ per century from the field equations with no free parameter; the Newtonian law is replaced at the centre. (Numbers standard; verify against Roseveare 1982 before writing — research/sources.yaml.)
- **Module results it uses:** the corporate body (the prediction depends on law + masses + initial conditions + 'no other bodies'); the web's periphery/centre gradient; posits paying their way; 'any statement can be held true' (Vulcan was held true for sixty years); minimum mutilation.
- **Where the artefact departs from the idealisation:** holism, as stated, predicts only that *some* statement gives; the record shows the community applying criteria holism does not contain — a posit must be independently observable (Neptune was; Vulcan never was), a law is replaced only by a rival law with its own successes (1915 came with light-bending and the redshift), and the modified-exponent fix was rejected as ad hoc though it saved the phenomena. 'Conservatism' and 'simplicity' are named by Quine and not ranked; the scientists ranked them.

## Explanation plan

1. **The problem.** Uranus in 1846 and Mercury in 1859: the same observation sentence false ('the planet is at the predicted place'), the same body of theory, the same first move (posit a planet), opposite outcomes. Sub-problem: does the theory of evidence of the previous lesson say which move was right, or when to stop? Obvious attempt: holism says any statement can be saved, so both moves were legitimate and the difference is luck. Why that is not enough: the community did not treat Vulcan's sixty years as luck running out; it treated the posit as having failed a *test* — and holism must either contain that test or be silent about it.
2. **1846 in the web.** The prediction for Uranus depends on: the inverse-square law (centre); the masses of the known planets; Uranus's initial conditions; 'no unknown body of significant mass' (periphery). The residual (frame it: a couple of arc-minutes, about the apparent width of Jupiter — well outside observational error). Le Verrier revises the peripheral conjunct: a new posit, and the posit *predicts* — a position. Galle finds Neptune within about a degree, the width of two full moons, on the first night. Holism's picture is exactly right: the periphery absorbed the anomaly. What holism did not supply: the demand that the posit predict something independently checkable — which is what made the night of 23 September decisive.
3. **1859 in the web.** Mercury's perihelion advances by ~38″/century more than the planets account for (frame it: a hundredth of a degree per century — small, but the measurements were good to a few seconds). Le Verrier makes the same move: Vulcan. Sightings claimed, none repeatable; the posit held 'come what may' for decades — Quine's sentence literally true. Alternatives at the same level: solar oblateness (measurable, found insufficient); zodiacal matter; Hall's exponent $2.0000001574$ (saves the phenomenon exactly, rejected as ad hoc — why? because it predicted nothing else and spoiled the lunar theory). The web permitted all of these; the community rejected them by criteria the web does not state.
4. **1915.** General relativity yields 43″/century from the field equations with no adjustable parameter, alongside two other predictions (light-bending 1919; gravitational redshift). The centre gives way — Quine's 'Einstein over Newton' — but only because a rival centre arrived with independent successes; nobody revised the law *in order to* save Mercury. The because: a central revision is accepted when it is not a revision but a replacement, and the replacement's credit comes from elsewhere.
5. **What holism predicts and what it does not.** Predicts: that a revision is always available (true both times; the record shows four candidates for Mercury alone); that logic and law are revisable in principle (1915). Does not predict: which revision (silent both times); the criteria the scientists used — (a) a posit pays with an independent observation; (b) an ad hoc fix that predicts nothing new is rejected even when it saves the phenomenon exactly; (c) a law is replaced only by a law. Quine names conservatism and simplicity and does not rank them; Ayer in 1936 named the same two and said the choice is 'a matter of convenience'; the record shows a ranking.
6. **Verdict.** Which module results the reading used; where the artefact departs (the three criteria); what the departure shows — that 'minimum mutilation' is a maxim the web cannot state about itself, and that the episode is better described as *holism plus a realist's demand that posits exist* (Putnam's and Kripke's next move: Neptune was there, Vulcan was not, and no amount of coherence changes which).

## EXERCISE PLAN

1. (warm-up) Write the conjunction the 1846 prediction depended on as four statements, and mark which Le Verrier revised and which he held fixed.
2. (refute) "Holism was vindicated by Mercury: the law was revised, as Quine said it could be." Break it: show that the law was *replaced*, not revised to fit, and that the replacement's credit came from two other predictions; state what holism would have to add to explain why Hall's exponent — which fitted exactly — was rejected.
3. (boundary probe) Suppose Vulcan had been found in 1860. Describe the web after that; then say whether general relativity's 43″ would then have been an anomaly *for relativity*, and what that shows about 'the' revision an anomaly forces.
4. (derivation — the ad hoc criterion) Define 'ad hoc' as 'saves the anomaly and predicts nothing else'. Show that under holism alone an ad hoc fix is as good as any other, and then write the extra premise (a posit must pay with an independent observation) that ranks Neptune above Vulcan and Hall. Say where in Quine's text the nearest sentence occurs ('minimum mutilation').
5. (claim–evidence) Ayer in 1936 wrote that a hypothesis never abandoned 'is a definition'. Apply this to Vulcan: at what point, by Ayer's criterion, had 'there is an intra-Mercurial planet' become a definition rather than a hypothesis, and what observation would have been needed to make it one again?
6. (FORWARD → kripke-against-descriptivism) 'Neptune' was introduced as 'the planet causing the perturbations of Uranus' before anyone saw it; 'Vulcan' as 'the planet causing the advance of Mercury's perihelion'. Say what each name referred to in 1846 and 1859 respectively, and whether 'Neptune causes the perturbations of Uranus' was known a priori by Le Verrier. The solution says Kripke calls this a reference fixed by description and a contingent a priori truth, with a `<Ref>`.
7. (research-shaped) Reproduce the sign and rough size of the Hall correction: show that an exponent $2 + \delta$ advances the perihelion by about $180°\,\delta$ per orbit (a standard result the reader can derive from the precession of a nearly Keplerian orbit, or take as given), and compute $\delta$ for 43″/century given Mercury's 88-day period; compare with Hall's $1.574 \times 10^{-7}$. State in one sentence why this exact fit was not evidence.

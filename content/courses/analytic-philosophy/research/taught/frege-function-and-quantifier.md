# Taught: frege-function-and-quantifier

## Problem
What must a sentence be taken apart into for inferences with nested 'every'/'some' (every boy loves some girl ⇒ some girl is loved by a boy) to become mechanical? Answer: a function applied to arguments (any place may be left open), with quantifiers as second-level functions applied to the first-level concept that results; scope = order of application. Consequence: existence is a second-level concept.

## Explained
- Syllogistic's one sentence shape (quantity word + subject term + copula = the joining 'is' + predicate term); four forms (every/no/some/some…not); complete inside that shape (every two-premise argument with a shared term sorted valid/invalid; no 256/24 count given); cannot look inside a predicate — opening paragraphs
- Two readings of 'every boy loves some girl', (A) ∀∃ and (B) ∃∀; (B) implies (A), not conversely; the opening's conclusion 'some girl is loved by a boy' is a third sentence (C) ∃∃, differing from (B) in 'a boy' vs 'every boy'; (C) true in both models — "The sentence with no subject" (Example + two-readings figure)
- What 'could not state' means (claim scoped to the syllogistic / the logic in general use in 1878, not to every notation of the day; 1860s–70s logics of relatives not mentioned): the syllogistic writes a sentence for each reading only with the second quantifier sealed in an opaque term — table (A) every B is P / (B) some G is Q / (C) some G is R; so neither the (A)/(B) difference (which quantifier inside which) nor the inferences between them can be written (no shared term; a syllogistic inference runs only through a shared term); (A)→(C) attempt stalls at 'some girl-lover is a boy' (subalternation + conversion), 'girl' a fragment of a term; John loves Mary → something loves Mary / John loves something are two unrelated moves there — ### Forcing the sentence into subject and predicate

# Taught: ontological-argument-under-the-new-logic

## Problem
Which step of the ontological argument fails, in the original and in every parody, for one reason? Answer: 'existence is a perfection' either cannot be written (∃ is second-level, a mark is first-level) or, written with the closed conjunct ∃yD(y) or the degenerate E(x) = ∃y(y = x), changes nothing; one interpretation with all marks empty makes every definition true and ∃xD(x) false (perfect being, island, devil at once). The argument validly proves only ∀x(D(x) → E(x)). Versions built on NECESSARY existence (Anselm's reply / Proslogion III; the 1974 modal version; Gödel/Scott) pass the level check; their defect moves into premises/axioms (S5 premise ≡ conclusion; Gödel's axioms inconsistent; Scott's entail modal collapse).

## Explained
- Anselm, Proslogion II (c. 1078, monk at Bec in Normandy, later archbishop of Canterbury): Quote (Deane) + 5-move reductio with G = 'that than which nothing greater can be conceived' — "The argument, as its two classical authors gave it" / ### Anselm
- Descartes, Meditations V (1641, Paris): Quote §8 (mountain/valley, triangle) + 3 lines — ### Descartes
- Shared skeleton (concept fixed by description; existence counted among respects of perfection; conclusion: something answers) as FOUR moves (definition, understood, premise, conclusion: Anselm's supposition folded into the premise, contradiction stated as conclusion; Descartes = same minus 'understood') — 5-frame sequence `moves` + Task — ### What the two share
- Gaunilo (monk of Marmoutier near Tours), lost island Quote §6; scariest devil (modern); KeyFact: a parody shows failure, not location — "The parodies: the same form proves too much"
- Denying the premise = counter-assertion — "Two answers that do not locate the fault"
- Kant 1781: 'real predicate' (adds to the description of a thing) defined; hundred thalers Quote (German AA III 401 + Meiklejohn 'dollars'; thaler = silver coin of German states); figure thalers.svg; what it leaves open: existence only 'positing'; 'synthetic' defined (true/false by how the world is, not by what terms contain); defender's reply via Descartes 'conceive no other being… to whose essence existence [necessarily] pertains' — ### Kant's hundred thalers
- *mark* (Merkmal; Austin: 'characteristic') = first-level concept a defined concept is built from; essence = list of marks; property OF a concept vs mark; Quote Grundlagen §53 ('Because existence is a property of concepts the ontological argument … breaks down'); right-angled vs right-angled equilateral triangle — ### Marks and properties of a concept
- Example: three attempts to make existence a mark: (1) ∃ in a mark's place — unwritable; (2) D⁺ = D ∧ ∃yD(y), closed conjunct, ∃xD⁺ ↔ ∃xD ('thalers as a theorem'); (3) D^E = D ∧ E, D^E ↔ D, yields only ∀x(D^E(x) → E(x)); figure marks.svg; why the list is complete (∃ with no concept = not a formula; applied to a concept = closed sentence; open in x and saying only that x exists = E); in-body Exercise: same on the island (L(x) = Ix ∧ Xx) — ### Writing 'existence is a perfection'
- Theorem (no definition from non-logical marks proves its concept instantiated) motivated in two steps: (1) conservativity (Ref frege-logicism): definition makes ∃xD(x) ≡ ∃x(M1x ∧ … ∧ Mkx), a D-free sentence, so the definition proves it only if logic alone does; (2) validity = truth under all reinterpretations (Ref frege-function-and-quantifier), and a reinterpretation may empty a non-logical predicate; proof by the empty-marks interpretation on domain {a,b,c,d}; figure countermodel.svg (key_image); failing step = ∀x(D^E(x) → E(x)) to ∃xD(x), a fact about marks taken for a fact about the concept; KeyFact: marks can settle emptiness, never (non-logical) instantiation; 1884 §53 caveat 'going too far' + the logical mark x = x (∃x(x = x) a first-order theorem); no in-body preview of the exercise — ### One interpretation refutes every version
- Anselm's 'greater': G re-read as concept Gx ('x is something than which nothing greater can be conceived'); 'exists in the understanding alone' = concept grasped and nothing falls under it (¬∃xGx); 'greater' relates objects; move 4 read two ways, each once: object reading (existence as first-level mark = E, every object has it, no pair for the premise to rank, 'compares nothing with nothing'), then concept reading in one paragraph (move 4 = 'concept G instantiated ranks above G empty', grantable; move 1 ranks objects, move 5 needs an object greater than a G; none supplied, so no contradiction); both stop at move 4 — ### Anselm's 'greater' fails at the same line
- Reversal: Anselm's Reply III Quote ('cannot be conceived not to be'), Proslogion III; necessary existence ≠ existence; Descartes 'necessary and eternal existence' — "Where the diagnosis stops: necessary existence"
- □/◇ informally: possible world = complete way things might have been; ◇ = ¬□¬; modal first-level properties non-degenerate (7 prime in every world; you not reading in every world); each world has its own stock of objects, 'x exists at w' = E over w's domain, so 'exists in every world' is false of you (might not have existed), true of 7 if numbers are in every world: unlike E inside one domain it distinguishes — ### Necessity written as an operator
- Modal version (1974, author NOT named in body): X maximally excellent, M maximally great; 5 steps; step 4 ◇□p → □p is automatic on the one-stock-of-worlds reading; stated because some modal logics let what is possible depend on the world you start from; S5 = every world possible from every other = the informal reading (relative possibility named in one sentence only, no 'accessibility' term); level check passes (points back: 'exists in every world' inside M is not the degenerate E); premise not a definition; parody ◇¬∃X yields impossibility in 3 numbered steps (◇¬ = ¬□; contrapositive of step 4: ¬◇□∃X; step 2 in every world, so no world with ∃M: ¬◇∃M); in S5 ◇∃M, □∃M, ∃M equivalent — ### The modal version
- Gödel (1970 notes), Dana Scott (1972 variant, differs chiefly in the definition of essence); 'positive' glossed (what older writers called a perfection: purely good to have, no lack or limitation) and formally second-level; G God-like (∀φ over first-level properties); *essence* flagged as a different, technical sense from 'list of marks': property capturing x's whole nature, from which every property x has follows in every world; NE = each essence instantiated in every world (first-level); axioms informally; 3-step sketch of □∃G in Scott's version, parallel to the modal version, with forward labels to the ★ table (A3+T1: ◇∃G; T2+A5+D3: G(x) → □∃yG(y) in every world; S5 step 4: □∃G), P(NE) named as the premise that puts necessity in the concept; figure modal-levels.svg (old premise points down a level, new one up); 2013 machine check (classical HOL with world parameter; Leo-II, Satallax, Isabelle/HOL, Coq); table: Scott consistent + T3, weaker than S5 suffices; Gödel's own inconsistent (his definition of essence lacks one clause; Leo-II 2013; unnoticed four decades); modal collapse p → □p (by hand 1987, machine 2016) = 'proves too much' in modern form; no KeyFact (summary lives in Conclusion) — ### The logician's version and the machine
- ★ Scott's axiom table A1–A5, D1–D3, T1–T3, C (glosses); Gödel's D2 lacks φ(x); Theorem modal collapse with proof (λy.p, T2, D2, T3) — "★ The axioms, and the collapse proved"

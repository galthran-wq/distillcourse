## Explanation plan

1. **The problem.** Two unverifiable sentences in 1936 — Bradley's Absolute and the mountains on the far side of the moon; one is a statement, one is hard to call one. Sub-problem: what separates them? Obvious attempt: the moon sentence is *true or false* and the Absolute sentence is not. But that is the verdict, not a test; what is wanted is a test that can be applied *before* knowing the truth-value. The Circle's proposal: a sentence is factually significant iff one knows what observations would lead one to accept or reject it (Ayer p. 16); the moon sentence passes because we know what a rocket would show, the Absolute fails because nothing would count.
2. **Where it came from and what it was for.** 6.53 as the programme; the 1929 dictum; the manifesto; the enemy — metaphysics and theology, and (Magee–Ayer) the German idealist tradition in its political form. The Kant contrast (Ayer pp. 14–15): the limit is logical (rules of significance), not psychological — Kant's 'limits of the understanding' invited Wittgenstein's objection that one would have to think both sides; a rule of language does not. Metaphysics from grammar: every subject names an entity (substance, Being); 'unicorns are fictitious' vs 'dogs are faithful'; Heidegger's 'Nothing' as a quantifier taken for a name — the Frege/Russell lessons' tool turned polemical, with Beaney's caution that the reading is uncharitable.
3. **Strong verifiability and why it was abandoned.** Conclusive verifiability makes 'arsenic is poisonous' and 'Caesar crossed the Rubicon' meaningless (universal statements never conclusively verified; the past unobservable); Ayer mocks 'an important type of nonsense'. Schlick's patch (laws as rules for forming singular statements) and Ayer's own 1936 past-as-prediction ('if I dig I'll find relics'), later called 'desperately implausible' (Magee). Falsifiability fails symmetrically: an observation refutes only given background conditions.
4. **The weak criterion and its collapse.** Ayer's 1936 form: $S$ is significant iff some observation statement $O$ is deducible from $S$ together with auxiliary premises without being deducible from the premises alone. Then the 1946 admission: let $S$ be anything, take 'if $S$ then $O$' as the auxiliary; $S \wedge (S \to O) \vdash O$ while $S \to O \nvdash O$. So 'the Absolute is lazy' is significant. Write it as a three-line derivation for this reader; show the falsifiability version fails by the same trick with $\neg O$. The direct/indirect repair (pp. 181–182), and why it still fails (Church's 1949 counterexample is NOT in the dossier — state only that repairs kept failing and give Hempel 1950's verdict: 'useless to continue to search for an adequate criterion of testability in terms of deductive relationships to observation sentences'). Term-based and holistic alternatives (Hempel 1951: theories as units) point to Quine.
5. **The self-refutation charge and the reply.** 'The verification principle is neither analytic nor verifiable, so by its own standard meaningless.' Ayer: it is a definition (of 'literal meaning'), proposed, not discovered. Carnap/Uebel: it is stated in $L_{n+1}$ about $L_n$ — the charge is a category mistake. What this costs: a definition can be refused, and 'unlikely any metaphysician will yield' (Ayer p. 185); the principle persuades only those who already accept it.
6. **Why it mattered / what survived.** Mattered: for twenty years it set what philosophy was allowed to say; it made philosophy 'second-order', talk about talk (Magee–Ayer). Survived: the pragmatic maxim — 'if you want your reasoning responsible to evidence, avoid statements experience cannot touch' (Uebel §3.6); the habit of asking 'what would count'; the demarcation question reposed as Popper's; and Ayer's own balance sheet — 'nearly all of it was false', but 'there must be some backing for the currency'. The deflationary anti-metaphysics (Carnap's frameworks) is the next-but-one lesson.

## Pitfalls / subtleties to flag

- Uebel's entry is partisan for the left wing (Carnap, Neurath, Frank); say once that the Circle was plural and that the lesson's 'the criterion' is Ayer's statement of it, which the left wing had moved past by 1932 (Uebel §3.7).
- 'Verifiable in principle' vs 'in practice' must be stated before the moon example.
- The or-introduction counterexample is the lesson's one derivation; do it in full and do not hide it in a footnote. Do not claim Church's 1949 result without the paper on disk.
- 'Metaphysics' here means transcendent metaphysics (the Absolute, Being); Carnap's later deflationary sense (frameworks) is a different target and arrives two lessons on.
- Register for Heidegger: Beaney's 'uncharitable' is the course's verdict; no mockery.
- OCR: 'AVER', 'docs' — check every quote against print.

## EXERCISE PLAN

1. (warm-up) Classify under the weak criterion, with the observation that would be relevant: 'there is life on Europa'; 'every even number above 2 is the sum of two primes'; 'the Absolute is beyond change'; 'this painting is by Goya'.
2. (derivation — the trick run on falsifiability) Show that with $S$ arbitrary and auxiliary '$S \to \neg O$', $S$ is conclusively falsifiable by $O$; state why the repair 'auxiliaries must themselves be verifiable' does not help if '$S \to O$' is counted verifiable whenever $O$ is.
3. (boundary probe) Let $S$ be an observation statement itself. Show that the 1936 criterion counts it significant trivially, and then ask what it says about a *basic* statement that reports only a single experience: is it verifiable, and by what (the 1946 'nothing venture, nothing win' point)?
4. (refute) "The verification principle is meaningless by its own standard, so logical empiricism refutes itself." Break it: give Ayer's 'it is a definition' and the metalanguage reply, and then state what the replies cost (the principle becomes a proposal one may decline).
5. (claim–evidence, cross-lesson) Beaney calls Carnap's reading of Heidegger uncharitable. Using the quantifier analysis from the Frege lesson, state exactly what the diagnosis assumes about 'Nothing' and construct a reading of 'the Nothing nothings' on which 'Nothing' is not a failed quantifier; say whether the criterion then still excludes the sentence.
6. (FORWARD → holism-and-naturalism) The 1936 criterion needs 'auxiliary premises'. Take any real experiment (a litmus test for acidity) and list the auxiliaries the observation depends on; then ask which single statement the observation verifies. The solution says Quine will answer 'none alone', with a `<Ref>`.
7. (research-shaped) From the exodus list, compute how many of the Circle's named members were still in Vienna in 1937, and state in a sentence what this does to the common picture of 'the Vienna Circle' as a school with a doctrine (Uebel's plurality point) — and which single sentence of Ayer's 1946 Introduction concedes that the doctrine itself had already dispersed.

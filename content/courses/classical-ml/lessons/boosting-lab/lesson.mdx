The module's derivations become a running boosting engine: your own stump
fitter, your own residual loop, your own shrinkage schedule — validated round
by round against the reference implementation and a held-out error target.

Everything the lab builds was derived in this module: the sorted threshold
scan from <Ref lesson="decision-trees" />, run at depth 1; the function-space
gradient loop and its dials ν, J, M from <Ref lesson="gradient-boosting" />;
the reweighting round and the ±1 conventions from <Ref lesson="adaboost" />;
and the second-order leaf value $-G/(H+\lambda)$ from
<Ref lesson="xgboost" />. What the lab contributes is those results as
verified code that composes: the stump feeds the least-squares loop, the loop
feeds staged predictions, staged predictions feed test-error-versus-rounds
curves that reproduce Friedman's shrinkage finding on the California housing
data — measured, not asserted — and the same loop with a new gradient becomes
a spam classifier. The AdaBoost round you computed by hand on ten points runs
here as code, checked against the lesson's own table, and the optional Newton
checkpoint grafts XGBoost's leaf into your booster as the three-line change
the derivation promised it was.

The lab runs as a Jupyter notebook — open it in Colab with the button below,
or in any Jupyter you like. Pair the notebook with the code from this page;
each checkpoint submits your function's outputs for verification and the
checklist below tracks what stands. Six of the nine checkpoints are required
— the stump, both boosting loops, staged predictions, the shrinkage curves,
and the AdaBoost round; the Newton leaf, the open modeling task and the
written answer are optional — partial completion is a normal way to finish a
lab. Expect four to five hours of real work: the stump scan and both
boosting loops are your numpy end to end, the deviance gradient is refereed
by finite differences rather than printed, and the final task is open — any
configuration of your booster against a held-out probability-error bar on
the spam corpus, with the honest-evaluation discipline of
<Ref lesson="validation-lab" /> deciding when to stop. If a check goes red,
the verdict often names the exact bug — read it before rereading your code.

The staged predictions you build here return in module 9:
<Ref lesson="margin-bounds" /> draws AdaBoost's margin distributions from
exactly this machinery, and <Ref lesson="bounds-vs-practice" /> reads the
test curve that keeps falling after training error hits zero off the same
staged sums.

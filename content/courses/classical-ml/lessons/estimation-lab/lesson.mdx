The module proved a floor exists; this lab measures against it. You write the
score and the information, drive Newton's method with them, then run enough
replicates to watch the maximum-likelihood estimator settle onto the bound —
and one family where it beats the bound because a hypothesis you can name has
failed.

The Cramér–Rao inequality of <Ref lesson="fisher-information" /> is a claim
about variance across repeated samples, which no single dataset can confirm
or refute — so the lab generates the repetitions. You write the estimation
mathematics: the log-likelihood of an exponential-family sample in the
natural parameter of <Ref lesson="glm" />, the score refereed by finite
differences, the two informations (expected, from the moment factory;
observed, from the sample's own curvature), the Newton solver
<Ref lesson="gradient-descent" /> supplied, driven by both, and a replicate
harness that puts realized variance next to the $1/(nI)$ floor and scores the
standardized estimate against $\mathcal{N}(0,1)$ with a KS distance rather
than by eye. The harness takes a sampler and an estimator, not a family, which
is what lets the last synthetic section run it unchanged on the uniform
distribution on $[0, \theta]$ — where an unbiased estimator beats the
formula's floor by a factor that grows with $n$, and the written checkpoint
asks which hypothesis failed.

Two measurements pay for the whole apparatus. Observed and expected
information coincide *exactly* at the estimate for these families, and differ
by a tenth of themselves at $n = 20$ when evaluated at the truth: the
Efron–Hinkley distinction, in numbers. And the report card's ratio below one
is a legitimate trade rather than a bug, which is why it prints the bias
beside it.

The closing task turns the instrument on the portfolio of
<Ref lesson="insurance-claims" />: 8,000 policies of the French motor
third-party-liability data, a Poisson GLM with an exposure offset whose
**fit ships complete** — coding the IRLS loop <Ref lesson="glm" /> derived is
what <Ref lesson="logreg-lab" /> is for, and this lab's subject is the
information, not the ascent. You price the coefficients' standard errors from
the observed information and meet the fine print in production: the
information matrix's inverse, not its diagonal, because two columns
correlate at 0.97; and a
coefficient informed by 144 policies whose Wald interval holds under 90% of a
2,000-replicate bootstrap, because eight claims is not a large sample however
many rows the file has.

Every derivation from this module becomes running code: you derive the
gradient and the Hessian yourself, build the classifier and its scorer piece
by verified piece, then point them at real diagnostic data and beat a
server-scored AUC threshold. The gradient is refereed by finite differences,
not by a printed formula, and the data is real: the Wisconsin breast-cancer
diagnostic set (Wolberg, Street & Mangasarian, UCI, 1993), with the holdout
labels kept on the course server.

The loss, the gradient, the IRLS derivation and the separability pathology
were all earned in <Ref lesson="logistic-regression" /> earlier in this
module; step sizes and the cost of bad conditioning come from
<Ref lesson="gradient-descent" /> back in the estimation module. What the
lab contributes is
the module's classifier as verified code — including the pathology run live:
a checkpoint where your own trainer's weights grow without bound on
synthetic two-cluster data, separable by construction, until your own
penalty caps them — plus your first scorer,
a rank-based ROC-AUC.

The lab runs as a Jupyter notebook — open it in Colab with the button below,
or in any Jupyter you like. Pair the notebook with the code from this page;
each checkpoint submits your function's outputs for verification and the
checklist below tracks what stands. Seven of the ten checkpoints are
required; Newton's method, the open modeling task and the written answer are
optional — partial completion is a normal way to finish a lab.

Expect four to five hours of real work: the derivations are asked for, not
printed. Two checkpoints go deepest — Newton/IRLS with a hand-derived
Hessian, which converges in single-digit steps where your own gradient
descent on the same deliberately collinear features needs thousands, and
ROC-AUC computed as a rank statistic, which never builds the ROC curve at
all. The open task is where every piece you verified runs together on real
data. If a check goes red, the verdict often names the exact bug —
read it before rereading your code.

The AUC you implement here — and then try to beat — is defined, dissected
and traded off against its alternatives in
<Ref lesson="classification-metrics" />; the classifier–scorer pair you
leave with is exactly what <Ref lesson="validation-lab" /> wraps in honest
evaluation protocols.

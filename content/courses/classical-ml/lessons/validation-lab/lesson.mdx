The harness you build here is the referee for every later lab: honest folds,
bootstrap error bars, nested hyperparameter selection — proven honest by
running the famous blunder through it and watching the report change from 3%
to 50%.

Everything the lab uses was earned in this module: the fold protocols, the
63.2% arithmetic, and the one-standard-error rule from
<Ref lesson="cross-validation-bootstrap" />; the law that every fitted step
runs inside the fold from <Ref lesson="feature-engineering" />; the degenerate
fold that stratification exists to prevent from
<Ref lesson="imbalance-leakage" />; and the acceptance test itself — a
full-data feature screen on pure noise — reproduced in
<Ref lesson="wrong-way-cv" />. What the lab contributes is the module's
discipline turned into reusable code: a fold assigner, a scorer whose
fit-callable signature quarantines every fitted step, bootstrap standard
errors, and the one-SE selection — warmed up on the Auto data's polynomial
and ridge questions, then aimed at the ESL noise problem three times over.
The full-data screen reports about 5%; tuning the honest screen's size and
reporting the winner still lands about eight points below the truth; the
fully nested harness — selection in an inner loop, measurement in an outer
loop that never took part in the choice — reports 50%.

The lab runs as a Jupyter notebook — open it in Colab with the button above,
or in any Jupyter you like. Pair the notebook with the code from this page;
each checkpoint submits your function's outputs for verification and the
checklist above tracks what stands. The seven exact checkpoints are the
required set; the open task and the written answer are optional — partial
completion is a normal way to finish a lab. Expect about four hours of real
work: every fold, resample, and selection rule is your own numpy, and the
checkers verify the exact protocol each docstring states — determinism
included. If a check goes red, the verdict often names the exact bug — read
it before rereading your code.

Later labs assume this shape: the boosting and Gaussian-process labs evaluate
against held-out data through exactly this harness pattern, and the AUC
scorer you built in <Ref lesson="logreg-lab" /> drops into its `err` slot
unchanged.

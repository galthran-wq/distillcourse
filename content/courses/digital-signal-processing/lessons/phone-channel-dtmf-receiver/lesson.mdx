A DTMF receiver must turn finite Fourier evidence into a key-or-reject decision while keeping spectral features, temporal qualification, latency, and cost on declared coordinates.

<Ref lesson="fft-factorization-and-layout">FFT Factorization and Machine Layout</Ref> established that a full transform and selected sums are interchangeable only at the same declared coordinates, then handed their finite evidence to this application. <Ref lesson="sampling-aliasing-and-reconstruction">Sampling, Aliasing, and Reconstruction</Ref> fixed the hertz-to-radians/sample map and the rule that a digital detector cannot undo analog spectral overlap. The remaining task is to turn eight finite frequency measurements into an auditable stateful contract that emits a key only after every spectral and temporal gate passes; every failed or ambiguous gate emits `REJECT`.

The construction proceeds from the row-column code to feature coordinates, temporal state, the complete gate conjunction, and a held-out audit that preserves failed targets beside passed ones.

## A pair becomes a candidate only through two frequency groups

The inherited transforms return coefficients, but a DTMF decision begins with a stricter combinatorial condition than selecting two large values.

### Four inherited facts bound the detector

<Task>
Work closed-book. Answer exactly four retrieval prompts.

1. At $f_s=8$ kHz, map 770 Hz and 1336 Hz to radians/sample and state whether either nominal tone aliases across $\pi$.
2. Write the course's raw forward $N$-point DFT and state the conjugate-symmetry relation for a real record.
3. State the condition under which a physical-frequency sum $C_N(f)$ is the same coordinate as one ordinary DFT bin $X_N[k]$, then state what changes when 205 acquired samples are padded to length 256.
4. Distinguish analog aliasing from finite-record leakage by naming when each is created and one change that can affect it.

Proceed when prompt 3 and at least two of the other prompts are correct.
</Task>

<Aside title="Retrieval answers">
The two discrete frequencies are

$$
\omega_{770}=2\pi\frac{770}{8000}=0.1925\pi,
\qquad
\omega_{1336}=2\pi\frac{1336}{8000}=0.334\pi
$$

radians/sample. Both lie strictly below $\pi$, so neither nominal tone aliases at the declared sampling rate.

The raw forward transform is

$$
X_N[k]=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}x[n]e^{-j2\pi kn/N},
\qquad k=0,\ldots,N-1.
$$

For real $x[n]$, $X_N[(N-k)\bmod N]=X_N[k]^*$. A selected sum equals an ordinary bin under the same record and scale only when $f/f_s$ and $k/N$ are equal modulo one. Padding 205 samples with 51 zeros changes the sampled grid to $kf_s/256$ but adds no observation time and does not narrow the 205-sample record-window response.

Analog aliasing is created at acquisition when continuous-frequency replicas overlap; changing the analog anti-alias filter or sampling rate can prevent it. Leakage is created by finite observation and its window; changing the record or window changes leakage, while relabeling bins does not.
</Aside>

### One row and one column define a candidate

The DTMF code uses low-group frequencies 697, 770, 852, and 941 Hz and high-group frequencies 1209, 1336, 1477, and 1633 Hz. One member of each group defines a keypad intersection. For key `5`, the candidate pair is 770 Hz plus 1336 Hz. A lone 770 Hz component has no high-group member, while 770 Hz plus 852 Hz contains two low-group members; neither input has a valid keypad intersection.

<Figure src="./figures/dtmf-key-decision-grid.svg" alt="A four-by-four DTMF grid pairs low rows 697, 770, 852, and 941 hertz with high columns 1209, 1336, 1477, and 1633 hertz; row 770 and column 1336 intersect at key 5, while missing, duplicate, or failed gates lead to REJECT." caption="A key exists only at one qualified row-column intersection; every incomplete or conflicting pattern returns REJECT." />

The common rule “take the two largest bins” fails before any threshold is chosen. In the committed clean key-5 record, the two largest values over all 205 DFT bins occur at $k=34$ and $k=171$. They are the positive- and negative-frequency partners of the same real high-group tone, not one row plus one column.

Restricting the search to positive frequencies does not repair the rule. For a lone 770 Hz cosine in a 205-sample rectangular record, the two largest positive-bin magnitudes occur at $k=20$ and $k=19$. Both arise from the same off-grid low-group tone and its leakage. The replacement rule searches the two groups separately, requires one qualified member in each, applies the remaining gates, and retains `REJECT` as an ordinary output.

The group rule identifies which physical frequencies matter. An auditable receiver must next identify the mathematical coordinates at which those frequencies are measured.

## Feature coordinates must be named before thresholds or cost

A correct row-column rule still fails audit if an exact physical frequency is silently replaced by a nearby bin from a different transform length.

### Exact physical frequencies need their own coordinates

<Example title="key 5 on exact and N=205 coordinates">
Let $f_s=8000$ Hz and $N=205$. The record spans $205/8000=25.625$ ms and its DFT-bin spacing is

$$
\Delta f=\frac{f_s}{N}=39.024390\ \text{Hz}.
$$

For each nominal tone, compute $k=\operatorname{round}(fN/f_s)$ and $f_k=kf_s/N$:

| nominal $f$ | nearest $k$ | $f_k$ | $f_k-f$ | $100(f_k-f)/f$ |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 770 Hz | 20 | 780.487805 Hz | +10.487805 Hz | +1.362053% |
| 1336 Hz | 34 | 1326.829268 Hz | −9.170732 Hz | −0.686432% |

Both bin centers lie inside the named AT&T column of Q.24 Annex A's ±1.5% operate interval. That geometric inclusion does not make either bin an exact nominal coordinate and does not prove operation for every tone at the interval boundary.

The two evaluations use different kernels:

$$
C_{205}(770)=\sum_{n=0}^{204}x[n]e^{-j2\pi(770)n/8000},
\qquad
X_{205}[20]=\sum_{n=0}^{204}x[n]e^{-j2\pi(20)n/205}.
$$

Their exponent coordinates differ because $770/8000\ne20/205$. The same distinction holds between $C_{205}(1336)$ and $X_{205}[34]$.
</Example>

<Figure name="DtmfCoordinateComparison" alt="For key 5, exact correlations evaluate 770 and 1336 hertz; the nearest 205-point bins are 780.4878 and 1326.8293 hertz, while appending 51 zeros gives nearest 256-point bins at 781.25 and 1343.75 hertz." caption="Zero padding samples a different frequency grid; neither nearest-bin pair is the exact-nominal correlation pair." />

<Definition title="selected-frequency finite-record projection">
For an $N$-sample record acquired at $f_s$, the unscaled negative-sign projection at physical frequency $f$ is

$$
C_N(f)=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}x[n]e^{-j2\pi fn/f_s}.
$$

It equals $X_N[k]$ as a coordinate identity only when $f/f_s\equiv k/N\pmod 1$ under the same record and scale.
</Definition>

The canonical case is $C_{205}(770)$, which evaluates the exact nominal low component of key 5. A structurally different valid case appends 51 zeros and writes the selected padded-grid value as $C_{205}(kf_s/256)$; the zero samples need not appear in the sum. At the boundary $N=1$, the projection is $x[0]$ for every $f$. Calling $C_{205}(770)$ by the name $X_{205}[20]$ is a near-miss because it violates the exponent-coordinate clause. At 8 kHz, $f=20f_s/205=780.487805$ Hz is an exact $N=205$ bin coordinate.

### Coordinate identity precedes a fair cost ledger

Coordinate choice also controls a fair cost ledger. Eight exact $N=205$ direct projections use 1640 complex-product slots and 1632 complex additions. Eight real-input Goertzel evaluations at those same physical frequencies use 1656 real multiplications and 3288 real additions under the declared Lyons ledger. These heterogeneous units do not form one total.

For padded-grid coordinates, eight selected sums can omit the 51 known zeros and retain 1640 complex-product slots plus 1632 additions; a literal 256-iteration implementation records 2048 and 2040 instead. A full radix-2 256-point FFT records 1024 twiddle slots and 2048 complex additions. Runtime still depends on implementation, batching, memory traffic, and hardware.

<KeyFact>
Coefficient equality requires the same exponent coordinate; proximity, padding, or membership in a tolerance interval does not supply that identity.
</KeyFact>

Named feature coordinates describe one finite frame. The receiver's duration decision requires a longer temporal contract.

### The executable receiver carries state across five-millisecond blocks

The frozen executable study uses a different feature schedule from the lesson's $N=205$ cost comparison: its exact-nominal spectral record is 160 samples, or 20 ms, and its temporal state advances in 40-sample, 5 ms blocks. </KeyFact>

Duration is one gate among several. Emitting a key requires the entire set to agree at one decision time.

## A key is the conjunction of declared gates

Temporal qualification prevents short registrations, but it cannot repair a missing group, an out-of-profile frequency, failed dominance, or unacceptable twist.

### Standards boundaries and local gates serve different roles

The standards values and the local receiver thresholds serve different roles. ITU-T Q.23 specifies the sender frequencies and a ±1.8% sender tolerance. The named AT&T column of Q.24 Annex A supplies this lesson's laboratory receiver profile: operate-frequency offsets through ±1.5%, non-operation offsets from ±3.5%, accepted high-minus-low twist from −8 through +4 dB, operation duration from 40 ms, non-operation through 23 ms, pause of at least 40 ms, and interruption through 10 ms. It leaves the frequency and duration transition regions locally specified.

The frozen synthetic study selected its own implementation thresholds from 640 training trials before held-out scoring:

| local gate | frozen study value | role |
|:--|:--|:--|
| block energy | 0.028 | locate active 5 ms blocks |
| group energy | at least 0.035 | reject an absent or weak group |
| winner dominance | at least 3 dB | reject an insufficiently isolated group winner |
| measured twist | −10 through +5 dB | allow estimator error around the named profile's −8 through +4 dB operate interval |

These are training-selected design values, not numbers copied from Q.24. They must not be retuned on the held-out trial cards.

### Rectangular leakage remains an unresolved failure

The remaining visible failure is spectral rather than statistical. For a length-205 rectangular complex record, a unit line at bin coordinate 20.5 contributes normalized magnitude

$$
\left|\frac{\sin(\pi d)}{205\sin(\pi d/205)}\right|=0.212225
$$

at a point $d=1.5$ bins away. That is −13.4641 dB, which is 26.5359 dB above a neighboring line of amplitude 0.01, or −40 dB. Zero padding samples this same finite-record response more densely and cannot remove the masking.

<KeyFact>
The frozen study has two passes and two failures: clean and 10 dB ±1.5% accuracy pass, while all-negative rejection and maximum accepted hard-set latency fail; no pooled score can erase either failure.
</KeyFact>

The receiver contract is now auditable: spectral coordinates, named-profile gates, temporal state, latency origins, and arithmetic units are separated, and the held-out study preserves both passed and failed targets. **Windows, Leakage, and Resolution** next measures how the rectangular record's main lobe and sidelobes hide the weak neighboring line and how a changed window trades that leakage against resolution and calibration.

Proposal C changes the kernel without adding observation time. A different window may lower sidelobes near the weak line, but it also changes main-lobe width, coherent gain, scalloping, and noise bandwidth. Comparing raw peak heights would confound attenuation by the window with detection performance, so the values must be calibrated to the same amplitude convention.

A reproducible comparison freezes the sampling rate, physical tone frequencies, amplitudes, relative-phase cases, noise rule, estimator coordinates, and amplitude calibration. It varies padding length, acquired duration, and window family separately. For each run it reports grid spacing, observation time, main-lobe width, peak sidelobe level, coherent gain, scalloping loss, equivalent noise bandwidth, and whether the weak line remains stable under relative-phase changes. Those measurements separate display density from physical evidence and make the window choice a declared tradeoff. <Ref lesson="windows-leakage-and-resolution">Windows, Leakage, and Resolution</Ref> derives the window metrics needed to execute that comparison.

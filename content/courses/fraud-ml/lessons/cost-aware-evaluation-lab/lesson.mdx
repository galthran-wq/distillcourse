The lab builds the scorer every later lab is judged by: forward-in-time folds with a delay block, precision at the investigators' budget, savings in money and probabilities corrected for the undersampling the model was trained on — then uses it to settle which depth of tree to ship.
None of it is code yet, and until it is, no comparison between two detectors
can be trusted.

Almost every piece has been stated already. Average precision as a step sum, with a block of tied scores opened as
one cut, and card precision at k with one call per card and found cards
blocked on later days, come from <Ref lesson="alert-budget-metrics" />. The
training, delay and test blocks, the known-card rule (a card is known on test
day $d$ once it has a fraud on a day up to $d - \delta - 1$) and the validation block
moved back by the delay plus one block come from
<Ref lesson="delayed-labels-prequential" />; the lab adds one bound to the
known-card rule, counting frauds only from the fold's first training day so
that every fold is cleaned with the same lookback. The keep rate $\beta$ and the map
from an undersampled model's scores back to probabilities come from
<Ref lesson="undersampling-calibration" />. Cost, savings against the cheaper
trivial policy and the per-transaction decision $C_a \le \mathrm{Amt} \cdot p$ come from
<Ref lesson="example-dependent-cost" />. The depth question the lab ends on is
the delayed-labels lesson's, where ROC-AUC and average precision disagreed
about the fully grown tree; <Ref lesson="ulb-handbook-benchmark" /> showed
the same split across five models.

You write seven functions, one per checkpoint: `average_precision`,
`savings`, `prequential_split`, `card_precision_top_k`,
`undersampling_correction`, `bmr_decide_and_price` and
`prequential_selection`. The last one composes the others: given any model
behind a fit-and-score callable, it builds the validation folds with your
splitter, scores them with your metrics and reports each metric's choice.
The lab then points it at a decision-tree depth grid on a simulated card
stream of about 620,000 payments, and at three policies priced in euros on
the test week: the cost rule on raw undersampled scores, the same rule on
corrected probabilities, and a fixed 100 calls a day.

The lab runs as a Jupyter notebook — open it in Colab with the button above,
or in any Jupyter. Pair the notebook with the code from this page; each
checkpoint submits your function's outputs for verification, and the
checklist above tracks what stands. The seven exact checkpoints are the
required set. The written answer (why ROC-AUC forgives the fully grown tree
and average precision does not) and the open task (beat a ROC-AUC bar over
the card-days of a hold-out week from a second simulator run, choosing your
model with your own folds) are optional. Expect about four hours. When a
check goes red, the verdict often names the bug — a trapezoid where a step
belongs, the known-card list frozen at the end of training, a class ratio
used as $\beta$ — so read it before rereading your code.

With an honest referee in hand, the question becomes which features computed
from a card's past carry signal — the subject of
<Ref lesson="transaction-aggregates" />.

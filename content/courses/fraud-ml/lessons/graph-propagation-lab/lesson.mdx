From an edge list to an exposure score and a graph network: union-find for the rings, power iteration for the walk, a normalised adjacency for the layer, all refereed on a 20-node toy before the Bitcoin graph.

The graph module computed its numbers with a few lines of scipy in the
text: the toy's exposure ranking D, F, E, G, I, its table of degrees,
triangles and walk counts, and on the Elliptic graph a random forest ahead
of every graph network. Each of those numbers depends on a line that can
be wrong without raising an error: which index of the transition matrix
is the node the walker leaves, whether the restart vector sums to 1,
whether the self-loop is added before the degree is taken, which nodes the
loss is averaged over.

Every definition comes from an earlier lesson. The edge weight
$e^{-\gamma h}$, its horizon and the payment promoted to a node of the
tripartite graph come from <Ref lesson="fraud-as-a-graph" />. The walk
counts from powers of the adjacency, the triangles as $(A^3)_{vv}/2$ and
the toy's feature table come from
<Ref lesson="neighbourhood-and-centrality" />; union-find from
<Ref lesson="communities-and-components" />. The column-normalised
matrix $Q = WS^{-1}$, the walk with restart
$\xi = \alpha Q\xi + (1-\alpha)e$, its power iteration, the direction of
the walk on a transfer graph and the one-step score of a payment the graph
has not seen come from <Ref lesson="guilt-by-association" />. The
normalised adjacency $\tilde D^{-1/2}(A+I)\tilde D^{-1/2}$, the two-layer
network, its skip term and the 0.3/0.7 weighted loss come from
<Ref lesson="graph-neural-networks" />. The Elliptic data, its split into
steps 1–34 for training and 35–49 for scoring, and the forest that beat
the graph networks come from <Ref lesson="elliptic-illicit-nodes" />.

You write the tools in numpy and `scipy.sparse`, one checkpoint each: the
weight and transition matrices of an edge list, in either direction of the
money; connected components by union-find; the walk with restart; exposure
on a card graph with decayed weights, with the score of a new payment;
five egonet columns from powers of the adjacency; the normalised adjacency,
the forward pass and the weighted loss of a two-layer network with a skip
term; its gradient, derived by you and refereed in the notebook by finite
differences; and the training loop, Adam on early time steps, scoring later
ones. Every checkpoint is first checked locally against the toy's printed
values. Then the tools run on the Elliptic graph: your union-find finds 49
components, one per time step, which is why a walk from the known illicit
nodes can never reach the steps being scored, and your network is compared
with two forests on held-aside training steps.

The lab runs as a Jupyter notebook — open it in Colab with the button
above, or in any Jupyter. It needs no GPU. Its Elliptic part downloads the
three original data files, about 150 MB, on first run (the licence allows
sharing the files only unchanged, so the lab ships no cut-down copy) and
trains for a few minutes. Pair the notebook with the code from this page;
each checkpoint submits your function's outputs for verification, and the
checklist above tracks what stands. The eight exact checkpoints are the
required set. The written answer (why a forest on hand-built neighbour
aggregates beats the network that learns its aggregation) and the open
task are optional. The open task gives up the 72 aggregate columns and
asks whether columns you compute from the graph can take their place:
rank the 16,670 labelled nodes of steps 35–49 from the 93 local columns,
the graph and the labels of steps 1–34, through a fixed classifier, a
logistic regression that reaches a ROC-AUC of 0.858 on the local columns
alone, to above 0.915. The classifier is fixed because a free choice of
model would decide the grade instead of the graph: boosting on the local
columns alone reaches about 0.93, and the graph columns add about half a
point to it. Expect about five hours. When a check goes
red, the verdict often names the bug — rows normalised instead of columns,
the raw seed weights used as the restart, the degree taken before the
self-loop, the gradient sent through a ReLU that was off, the unknown
nodes counted as licit — so read it before rereading your code.

The Elliptic graph is a set of snapshots, and at step 43 every model of
the comparison fails at once, because the fraud changed when a dark
market closed. <Ref lesson="sequence-models-for-transactions" /> starts on
time as a feature in its own right: the order of an entity's events,
which a snapshot graph or a fixed window of aggregates loses.

The lab builds the feature pipeline: windowed aggregates over each entity's past, conditional sums, a terminal risk shifted by the label delay and a circular time feature, joined onto every scoring row as of its own timestamp — and a shipped reference pipeline hides one leak for the learner to find.
A training table that breaks a point-in-time rule has the same columns as
one that keeps it, plausible numbers in every cell, and a better offline
score. Only the code that built it says which one it is.

Every rule has been stated already. A card's windows hold its payments
strictly before the row and less than $w$ earlier, conditioned or not on
the payment's type and country, as in <Ref lesson="transaction-aggregates"
id="the-rule" />. The usual hour is a circular mean with a von Mises arc
around it, fitted only once the previous seven days hold seven payments,
from <Ref lesson="behavioural-profiles" id="how-much-history-the-arc-needs" />.
A terminal's fraud rate reads only labels that have arrived, so its window
ends $\delta$ days before the row, and a stored value reaches a row by an
as-of join, from <Ref lesson="point-in-time-features" />. Which columns of
a log are knowable at scoring time, and which merely look so, is the
reading <Ref lesson="paysim-feature-matrix" /> did on a public file.

You write six functions, one per checkpoint: `calendar_flags`,
`customer_windows` (around a window helper that the next checkpoint reuses
on a finer key), `conditional_aggregates`, `terminal_risk_delayed`,
`periodic_time_flag` and `point_in_time_join`. Each is checked against the
course's worked cases first (the seven-payment card, the two-sums terminal,
the late-night card) and then against inputs built to catch the usual bugs:
a payment inside its own window, same-second payments counted as earlier,
an undelayed label window, a mean of hours taken as numbers, an arc that
does not cross midnight, a join that reads the latest record. A shipped
nightly job then asks your terminal function for every terminal's delayed
rate at 00:30, and a shipped builder assembles your functions into an
18-column matrix, one row per payment, on a simulated card log of about
118,000 payments over 60 days, with a label delay of seven days. The
generator is the course's card simulator with three additions, so that
every feature has something to find: payment types and countries, web
merchants, and a personal hour for each card.

Then a second pipeline, shipped complete and written the ordinary way,
produces a matrix with the same columns that a frozen logistic regression
scores better on, 0.92 against 0.83 in ROC-AUC on the last week. One of
its columns reads the future. The written answer names it, gives the first
row where it differs from yours, and says why no offline number could have
shown it; the same cell shows what happens to that column on a week whose
labels have not arrived.

The lab runs as a Jupyter notebook — open it in Colab with the button
below, or in any Jupyter. Pair the notebook with the code from this page;
each checkpoint submits your function's outputs for verification, and the
checklist below tracks what stands. The six exact checkpoints are the
required set. The written answer and the open task are optional: the open
task keeps the model frozen and grades your matrix, plus any columns you
add, on the unlabelled week of a second simulator run, where the 18 columns
alone fall short of the bar. Expect about four and a half hours. When a
check goes red, the verdict often names the bug, so read it before
rereading your code.

What exists at the end is a leak-free matrix built by your own functions,
and the habit of diffing two pipelines row by row until the first row
where they disagree. The point-in-time lookup returns in
<Ref lesson="pipeline-capstone-lab" />, where the detector reads its
features as of each day of a simulated stream.

With an honest matrix scored by honest metrics, what remains of supervised
detection is choosing the model that reads it, starting from the one a
regulator can audit line by line in <Ref lesson="scorecards-woe" />.

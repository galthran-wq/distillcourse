# The AI Act and fraud detection: the high-risk line and what sits on each side — research brief

Lesson: `eu-ai-act-fraud` (module: eu-regulation, core, planned). NEW.
Goal: Classify a financial-crime model under the AI Act — the Annex III credit-scoring entry and its fraud carve-out, the Article 6(3) derogation and its profiling override — and map the high-risk obligations onto the pipeline.

## Key points
1. The classification first, as a decision tree the reader can run: is the system an AI system (Art 3(1); rules-only systems excluded)? which Annex III heading, if any? for 5(b): is it used "for the purpose of detecting financial fraud" (the carve-out — purpose-based, natural persons, 5(b) only; a system that also scores credit is not carved out by also detecting fraud)? if under a heading: does it profile natural persons (Art 6(3) last subparagraph → high-risk regardless)? else do the four 6(3) conditions apply (document, register under 49(2), stay re-evaluable under Art 80)? Rec 42's suspicious-transaction analytics on undertakings and Rec 59's FIU exclusion as the AML-side readings; Rec 58's "provided for by Union law" as the narrow-reading hook (the PSD2 RTS and AMLR ARE such law).
2. The honest mapping for the course's detectors: a card-fraud transaction scorer (natural persons, profiling by behavioural features, purpose fraud detection) — carved out of 5(b) by purpose, but the profiling clause lives inside Art 6(3), which only matters if a heading applies; the lesson's verdict: not high-risk under Annex III as written, subject to Commission guidelines (Art 6(5), due 2 Feb 2026) and to Art 7 amendments; a model that both scores credit and detects fraud → high-risk; an AML customer-risk profiler used to refuse onboarding → not Annex III (no heading), but AMLR Art 76(5) and GDPR Art 22 still bind; a KYC face match → 1(a)'s verification exclusion.
3. What high-risk would require, as pipeline artefacts (the lesson builds the table so the reader can price the line): Art 9 risk register with pre-registered metrics and thresholds (9(8)); Art 10 data sheet with bias examination incl. feedback loops (10(2)(f) — module 8's loop named in statute) and special-category data for bias testing (10(5)); Art 11/Annex IV documentation (what the system optimises for; labelling procedures — chargebacks, dispositions; subgroup metrics); Art 12 logs; Art 13 instructions with declared metrics and per-group performance; Art 14 oversight with automation bias; Art 15 robustness incl. the adversarial list and continuous-learning feedback loops; Art 72 post-market plan; Art 73 incidents; Art 49 registration; Art 43 internal-control conformity (no notified body for points 2–8); Art 86 explanation; Art 27 FRIA for 5(b)/(c) deployers (banks named in Rec 96).
4. Financial institutions' deeming rules: Art 17(4) (QMS via CRD governance except risk management, post-market monitoring, incident reporting), 18(3), 19(2), 26(5)–(6), 72(4); the prudential supervisor as market-surveillance authority (Art 74(6)–(7); Rec 158) with full data access (74(12)–(13)) — the same supervisor that reads the AML logs.
5. Retraining under the Act: Art 43(4) and Rec 128 — pre-determined changes assessed at conformity are not substantial modifications; a continuously retrained detector needs its retraining policy declared (the capstone's cadence as a documented pre-determined change); Art 111(2) legacy status lost on significant design change.
6. Enforcement and voluntary adoption: Art 80 re-evaluation and fines for misclassification; Art 99's ladder (and "losses avoided" as a factor); Art 95 codes for non-high-risk systems — the lesson's recommendation stated dryly: build the Arts 9–15 artefacts regardless, because AMLR Art 76(5), PSD2 RTS Art 3/19 and GDPR Art 22 already require most of them.
7. ★ Starred: GPAI chapters named as peripheral; sandboxes (Art 59 excludes financial fraud); the Digital Omnibus caveat; Annex I's product route.

## Set pieces & gems
- Annex III 5(b) verbatim — one clause.
- Rec 42's example: "assess the likelihood of financial fraud by undertakings on the basis of suspicious transactions".
- Art 6(3)'s last subparagraph: profiling → always high-risk.
- Art 10(2)(f): feedback loops in statute.
- Annex IV 2(b): "what the system is designed to optimise for".
- Art 17(4)'s three exceptions to the financial-institution deeming.
- Art 86's "based mainly".
- Art 15(5)'s list: poisoning, evasion, confidentiality.
- 35M/7% — and "losses avoided".

## Pitfalls
- Dates are original OJ; the omnibus may have moved them — flag at write time.
- Never state that fraud detection is "exempt from the AI Act": it is carved out of ONE Annex III entry; Arts 4 (literacy), 50 (transparency, with its crime-detection exemption), 95 (codes) and the prohibitions still apply.
- No legal advice register: the lesson classifies by the text and names what the guidelines (pending) will settle.

## EXERCISE PLAN
1. Warm-up: classify four systems by the tree (card scorer; credit + fraud scorer; AML risk profiler used for onboarding; KYC face match) citing the clause for each leaf.
2. Boundary probe: a rules-only transaction-monitoring engine (no inference from data) — is it an AI system under Art 3(1) and Rec 12? what changes when one rule's threshold is fitted from data; which obligations (AMLR Art 18(3)(f)) apply either way.
3. Claim to refute: "Fraud detection is exempt from the AI Act." Refute: carved out of 5(b) only; Arts 4, 50, 95 and the Art 5 prohibitions apply; a profiling system under any other heading is high-risk; name the three clauses that survive the carve-out.
4. Derivation the lesson skipped: show that Art 6(3)(c) ("detects decision-making patterns or deviations … not meant to replace or influence human assessment without proper review") describes an alert-triage model exactly when the referral band of score-to-action covers every decision — and fails the moment any decision is automated; state the condition as a sentence about the decision zones.
5. Research-shaped: draft the Annex IV 2(b) and 2(g) entries for the course's tuned GBM on the simulator: what it optimises for (the loss and the selection metric), the classification choices (threshold policy), and the metrics including per-group accuracy on a synthetic group attribute — with the numbers from the module-2 harness.
6. FORWARD (E7): write the Art 86 explanation and the GDPR Art 15(1)(h) "meaningful information about the logic involved" for the same declined transaction and compare their scope ("role of the system and main elements" vs "logic, significance, consequences") — the reader meets the GDPR duty before the lesson → lands on `gdpr-financial-crime-data`.
7. Mixed review (E3): place Art 15(5)'s four attack classes against adversarial-classification's game (which is evasion) and alert-feedback-selection-bias's loop (which Art 10(2)(f) names); state which attack the course's canon does not model (poisoning).

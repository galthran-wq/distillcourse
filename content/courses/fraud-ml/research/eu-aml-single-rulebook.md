# The EU AML single rulebook: AMLR, AMLD6 and AMLA as constraints on the model — research brief

Lesson: `eu-aml-single-rulebook` (module: eu-regulation, core, planned). NEW. The first legal lesson: read as a constraint on the pipeline, quoted short (recitals as intent, articles as obligation), never summarised as policy.
Goal: Identify in the AML package every obligation that binds a transaction-monitoring model — monitoring scope, suspicion criteria, non-outsourceable decisions, automated-decision safeguards, records, supervisory access — and map each to a pipeline component.

## Key points (each mapped to a pipeline component)
1. The package in one paragraph: AMLR = directly applicable obligations on obliged entities (what the model must do); AMLD6 = the national supervisory and FIU layer (who reads the outputs and on what clock); AMLA = the EU supervisor from 2025/2028 (who may audit the algorithm); dates: AMLR from 10 July 2027, guidelines/RTS 2026–2027.
2. Scope of monitoring → the scoring layer: Art 26 and Rec 72 (all products, risk-based intensity); Art 34(2)'s four triggers (complex, unusually large, unusual pattern, no apparent purpose) read as an anomaly specification; Art 34(4)(f) "selecting patterns of transactions that need further examination" = the rule/scenario/model tuning the course has done; Art 25's expected-activity profile = module 3's behavioural profile with a legal name; Art 33(5) = the feedback from monitoring to the customer's risk tier.
3. Suspicion and the report → the queue and the FIU loop: Art 69(1)–(2) (regardless of amount; the six bases as a feature taxonomy; attempted transactions); Art 71's refrain-then-3-working-days; Art 73 tipping-off (what the customer-facing explanation may not say — Art 76(5)(c)'s carve-out for Art 69 reports); AMLD6 Art 24's 10/5-working-day suspensions; Art 22(2)/28 annual feedback (the label loop of module 8 has a statutory clock); Art 19(3) FIU triage (statutory prioritisation language); STR quality measured by the FIU and used by supervisors (Art 23; AMLA Art 48).
4. Decisions that stay in-house → the decision layer: Art 18(3)(c)–(f) — risk profile, onboarding/transaction decision, STR filing, approval of detection criteria cannot be outsourced; a bought model is not outsourcing (Rec 47) but liability stays (Art 18(2)); Art 10(1)'s pre-launch risk assessment of a new technology (a new model is one).
5. Automated decisions → human intervention and explanation: Art 76(5)'s three conditions (Chapter III data only; meaningful human intervention for enter/refuse/maintain/execute/CDD-extent decisions; explanation and challenge except for reports); Rec 150–152 (profiling permitted; no decisions solely on irrelevant special-category data — the anti-bias clause); Rec 53/75/98 and AMLD6 Rec 114/Art 64(3) (de-risking: refusals recorded with grounds, financial exclusion policed) — the false-positive-harm lesson's legal form; Art 75(4)(g) (AI-generated information shared only with human oversight).
6. Records → the ledger: Art 77(1)(b) the assessment record kept whether or not an STR was filed (so alert dispositions, including false-positive closures, are records), unredacted, five years; Art 78 (answer enquiries on five years); the STR template and transaction-record format (ITS by 2026) — the schema the capstone's ledger must be able to emit.
7. Supervision → auditability: AMLD6 Art 37(7) (unannounced access to software, databases, IT tools; interviews of outsourcee staff); AMLA Art 18(1)(d) ("decision-making processes, including those developed by algorithms"); AMLA Art 8(2)/12(5) (risk benchmarks the supervisor computes — the institution's own feature-level risk indicators); fines (AMLD6 Art 55: 10% of turnover; AMLA Annex I multipliers) as the cost column of the governance cost matrix.
8. ★ Starred: information-sharing partnerships (Art 75) and AMLworld's cross-bank finding; the FIU's data inventory (Art 21(1)) as a feature-source catalogue; AMLA's selection arithmetic (40 groups, tie-break by third-country ratio).

## Set pieces & gems
- Art 18(3)(f): the detection criteria are not outsourceable — a vendor model's thresholds are the entity's decision.
- Art 34(2)'s four triggers as an anomaly spec; Art 69(2)'s six bases as a feature taxonomy.
- Art 76(5)'s three conditions, verbatim; footnote 45's "2024/xxx".
- Art 77(1)(b): the record of the alert you did NOT report.
- AMLA Art 18(1)(d): "developed by algorithms".
- Rec 145: "a suspicious transaction is not an indication of criminal activity".
- The fine arithmetic: 10% of turnover; ×3 intentional.
- Rec 52: "not an unduly permissive option".

## Pitfalls
- Quote short and cite by article; never paraphrase an obligation into advice; recitals are intent, not obligations — label each.
- Dates: original OJ text; AMLA guidelines/RTS pending — separate what the text fixes from what is to be specified.
- No US/UK comparison (out of scope per the brief).
- The lesson is 40 minutes: the mapping table is the deliverable; the catalogue of articles lives in the brief, not the prose (L12).

## EXERCISE PLAN
1. Warm-up: given a vendor contract that includes "the vendor sets and tunes the alert thresholds", list the Art 18(3) items it breaches and the clause that fixes it.
2. Boundary probe: a model that only prioritises alerts (never refuses, never exits) — which of Art 76(5)'s conditions still apply, and which decision types trigger the human-intervention requirement; then a model that auto-declines transactions.
3. Claim to refute: "Since AMLR Rec 150 permits automated decision-making and profiling, a fully automated onboarding refusal is lawful under the AML rules." Refute with Art 76(5)(b) (meaningful human intervention for refusals) and Art 21(3) (grounds recorded); name the difference between permitted processing and a permitted decision.
4. Derivation the lesson skipped: from AMLD6 Art 55 and AMLA Art 22 with Annex I, compute the maximum pecuniary sanction for an institution with EUR 400m turnover for a repeated (×1.1²), six-month (×1.5), systemic (×2.2) monitoring failure with quick self-reporting (×0.4) — and show where the cap binds.
5. Research-shaped: map Art 69(2)'s six suspicion bases to the course's feature families (modules 3, 6, 7) in a table and identify the one basis (links between transactions) that only the graph module can produce; cite the article for each row.
6. FORWARD (E7): compute, for a simulator week, which alerts the capstone's ledger must retain under Art 77(1)(b) (all assessed, reported or not) and what fields the ITS template will require (score, bases, action, disposition) — the reader builds the record schema → lands on `label-maturation`'s alert ledger (and the capstone's alert-ledger checkpoint).
7. Mixed review (E3): the FIU's annual feedback (Art 28) is a label source with a one-year clock; place it in the feedback/delayed-sample split of alert-feedback-selection-bias and state what the retraining cadence can do with a label that old.

# Guilt by association: relational classifiers and random walks with restart — research brief

Lesson: `guilt-by-association` (module: graphs, type: core, status: planned). NEW — full re-synthesis, no draft exists, so no REVISION block.
Goal: Derive the fraud-personalised PageRank as a random walk with restart on the transaction graph, implement it by power iteration with time-decayed weights, and explain why plain PageRank is useless for fraud
Opening (verbatim, the lesson must start with it): A cardholder has never been flagged, but three of the shops they paid at were used by cards later confirmed stolen. How much suspicion should flow from the confirmed frauds across the graph to this cardholder, how far, and how fast should it fade with time — computed for a million nodes before the next payment arrives?

## EXPLANATION PLAN
### The problem as the newcomer meets it
The reader has one-hop features and components. Neither answers the opening's question: the cardholder shares no label and no identifier with anyone flagged; the suspicion lies two hops away (cardholder → merchant → stolen card's transaction) and should be smaller than for a direct link, smaller still for older links, and summed over all such paths. What the newcomer lacks: a rule for one hop with weights (not yet: relational neighbour classifier), a rule that iterates it (not yet: collective inference), the ranking of the whole web that sums over all path lengths with decay (not yet: PageRank, personalised PageRank, random walk with restart, exposure score), the algorithm that computes it on a million nodes nightly (power iteration) and the device that scores a payment that has just arrived and is not yet in the graph (the cold-start update).

### Chain of sub-problems
1. **One hop, weighted.** — Obvious attempt: fraction of fraudulent neighbours. Answer with weights: P(c|n) = (1/Z) Σ_j w(n,n_j)·[class_j = c] over the neighbours — 2 fraud and 3 non-fraud neighbours with unit weights give 2/5, 3/5. Why it is not enough: unlabelled neighbours contribute nothing; so use the neighbours' *probabilities* instead of labels — the probabilistic relational neighbour, worked 2.25/5 = 0.45. Because: unlabelled nodes carry the information two hops away, and probabilities let it pass through them.
2. **Iterate until consistent: collective inference.** — Obvious attempt: compute the probabilistic rule once. Why it fails: the neighbours' probabilities depend on their neighbours'. Answer: iterate — iterative classification (hard labels, relational logistic regression), relaxation labelling (soft labels), Gibbs sampling (sample labels, burn in, count), loopy belief propagation (messages; belief = normalised product). Exact inference is NP-hard; these are linear in edges and come without convergence guarantees. Because: fraud on linked nodes is non-i.i.d.; one paragraph each, starred, as the family the walk belongs to.
3. **Sum over all paths with decay: the random walk.** — Obvious attempt: count 2-hop and 3-hop paths to fraud with A² and A³ and add them. Why it fails: path counts explode with length and favour high-degree nodes. Answer: a random surfer — from a node, follow a random out-edge with probability α, teleport with probability 1−α; the stationary distribution r = αQr + (1−α)e with Q column-normalised and e uniform is PageRank, α = 0.85; it sums over all path lengths with weight α^k. Why plain PageRank is useless for fraud: it tracks degree — the busiest merchant ranks first whatever its labels. Answer: put the teleport mass on the known-fraud nodes, e = 1/|fraud| on them and 0 elsewhere — the walk restarts only at fraud, and r becomes an exposure score: the share of time a fraud-restarting surfer spends at each node; on the 20-node toy it ranks D, F, E and puts legitimate G above fraudulent I, for a reason the graph shows (G sits between two fraud nodes). Because: "restart placement, not degree, decides the ranking" — the demo's discovery.
4. **Compute it nightly on a million nodes.** — Obvious attempt: solve (I − αQ)r = (1−α)e by inversion. Why it fails: a dense inverse of a 10⁶ × 10⁶ matrix. Answer: power iteration — repeat r ← αQr + (1−α)e from r = e until the change is below a tolerance; each step is one sparse matrix–vector product; convergence is geometric at rate α. Because: Q is sparse (one non-zero per edge) and the fixed point is unique for α < 1.
5. **Put labels on edges and age on weights.** — The card graph's labels are on payments, so run the walk on the tripartite Q_tri with the restart vector on confirmed-fraud transaction nodes weighted exp(−γh) by the time since the label; edge weights decayed by day / week / month give three graphs and nine exposure features (cardholder, merchant, transaction × long, medium, short term); scores are recomputed at midnight and used the next day. Because: a transaction node has exactly one cardholder and one merchant neighbour, so the walk alternates entity ↔ transaction and the exposure of a cardholder is the decayed mass of fraud reachable through its merchants.
6. **A payment that is not in the graph yet.** — The midnight scores do not include a payment arriving at 10:14 between a cardholder and a merchant that have never met. Answer: the local update — TXN score = CCH score/(Σw + 1) + MC score/(Σw + 1) with the new edge given weight 1: the payment absorbs a share of each endpoint's exposure. Because: six seconds do not allow a global recomputation; the local rule is one step of the walk from the two endpoints.
7. **How do we know it works.** — The ablation: nine network features alone AUC 0.920; RFM + demographics 0.953; all 0.986 with correlation ≤ 0.1 between the sets — about five points of AUC from the graph; 87.4% recall at 1% false positives. Signs: the long-term merchant score negative (safe merchants), the long-term cardholder and transaction scores positive. Gotcha!'s effects: *anticipating* (high exposure with no direct fraud link yet) and *forgiving* (low exposure despite an old link, because of decay); top-100 precision 24% → 44%. Because: the exposure feature is only ever one column of the module-4 table — the lesson ends by putting it there.

### Pitfalls and terms to define
- "Column-normalised" and the direction of Q: define which index is "from"; a row-normalised Q with the same formula gives a different fixed point.
- α is the follow probability (0.85), 1−α the restart; APATE's α is the same symbol as PageRank's damping, and Sec. 3.2.3 misprints γ as α — the lesson keeps α for the walk and γ for decay.
- "Exposure" vs "probability": the walk's output is a ranking, not a calibrated probability — say so when it becomes a feature.
- Restart vector must be normalised; a seed set of one node vs ten changes the scale, not the ranking.
- On a directed account graph the walk must go *against* the money (backward along in-edges from confirmed bad accounts) to find who fed them — the Anti-TrustRank direction; on the undirected card graph the question does not arise. State this where the AML case appears.
- Do not present belief propagation as if it converges.

## EXERCISE PLAN
1. [warm-up] Run three power iterations by hand on a 4-node graph with one fraud seed, α = 0.85, and report the ranking after each.
2. [boundary probe] α → 0 and α → 1: what does ξ become in each limit (the restart vector itself; the plain stationary distribution), and why is neither a fraud score; then the seed set empty — what does the iteration do?
3. [claim to refute] "A merchant with a high exposure score is a fraudulent merchant." Refute with the sign reading (long-term merchant score negative) and the magnitude argument (mass sums to 1, busy merchants collect it); name what the score is evidence of.
4. [derivation skipped] Show that the fixed point r = (1−α)(I − αQ)⁻¹e expands as (1−α)Σ_k α^k Q^k e, so the exposure is the decayed sum over all path lengths from the seeds, and derive the geometric convergence rate of power iteration from it.
5. [research-shaped] On the Elliptic subset, propagate backward from the illicit nodes of the first 34 time steps along in-edges (Anti-TrustRank direction) and report the AP of the resulting score on step 35's labels against degree as a baseline; state what would count as "the graph adds signal".
6. FORWARD → `graph-neural-networks`: write one step ξ ← αQξ + (1−α)z as ξ ← Q̂ ξ W + b for a suitable W, b, then ask what changes if W is learned from labels and a nonlinearity follows — the reader has written a GCN layer's shape before it is named.
7. [mixed review] Using `point-in-time-features`: the restart vector at day t may hold only frauds labelled by t − δ; show on a 10-day toy how a 7-day δ changes the exposure of a cardholder whose merchant was compromised 3 days ago, and which of the three horizons (day / week / month) the delay hollows out.

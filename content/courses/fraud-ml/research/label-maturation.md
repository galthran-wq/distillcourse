# Label maturation: chargebacks, reports and the training set as of a date — research brief

Lesson: `label-maturation` (module: system, type: core, status: planned). NEW — full re-synthesis, no draft exists, so no REVISION block.
Goal: Design the labelling pipeline of a deployed detector — which outcomes become labels, when each settles, how alert dispositions are recorded — and assemble a training set as of a date
Opening (verbatim, the lesson must start with it): A card fraud label can arrive from an investigator in days, from a customer dispute up to thirteen months later, or never; a laundering label arrives when a report is filed, and most laundering is never labelled. Which outcomes become training labels, when does each settle, how are alert dispositions recorded, and how do you assemble the training set as it was known on a given date?

## EXPLANATION PLAN
### The problem as the newcomer meets it
The reader has trained on tables where every row carried a label. In production the label column is being written over months by three different processes, and for most laundering rows it is never written. The newcomer does not have: a label source as a thing with a clock, "settled", a disposition, a chargeback/dispute window, an assessment record, a training set "as of" a date, label completeness as a property of a dataset (module 1 named it for public datasets; here it is the bank's own).

### Chain of sub-problems
1. **Where does a label come from?** — Obvious attempt: "the fraud column". Why it fails: there is no column; there are events. Answer: four sources with four clocks — an investigator's disposition on an alerted card (days), a customer's dispute (up to 13 months after the debit, with a refund due by the next business day), a filed report and the intelligence unit's reply (a year or never), and silence, which the system reads as genuine. Because: each source labels a different subset at a different time, and the law fixes two of the clocks.
2. **Is silence a label?** — Obvious attempt: unlabelled rows are unknown, drop them. Why it fails: that drops 99.7% of the stream. Answer: after the delay δ a row with no dispute and no disposition is taken as genuine; the error this makes is the undetected fraud sitting in the genuine class, unavoidable because a card is used several times before it is caught. Because: a detector needs negatives and the only honest negatives are "nobody complained in time".
3. **Which rows get the fast labels?** — Answer: the alerted cards' recent transactions (feedbacks F_t), chosen by the previous model — few, fresh, biased; everything else arrives as delayed samples D_{t−δ} — many, stale, unbiased. Because: the queue of module 9 decides who gets looked at; the label loop is the alert loop.
4. **What about laundering?** — Answer: the label is "a report was filed", produced by the bank itself; the unit replies to a fraction and the law's feedback is at least annual on report quality; most laundering is never labelled, and a synthetic world's complete transitive taint (every dollar downstream of illicit funds) shows how far from complete a real label set is. Because: there is no customer to complain; the only label source is the detector's own output, which makes the selection bias of module 8 total.
5. **What must be written down for every alert?** — Obvious attempt: store the confirmed frauds. Why it fails: the law requires the record of every assessment whether or not reported, and the false-positive closures are the negatives the feedback classifier trains on. Answer: an alert ledger — per alert: score, top attributions, action taken, the investigator's disposition, every timestamp — kept five years; the feedback label and the audit record are the same row. Because: the assessment record (AMLR Art 77) and the training set share a source.
6. **How is a training set assembled as of date t?** — Obvious attempt: take the table today. Why it fails: today's table holds labels that had not arrived at t and lacks the rows that were later re-labelled; a model trained on it is evaluated on a future it could not have seen. Answer: rows up to t; features from the store as of each row; labels from events settled by t (feedbacks up to t, disputes and silence up to t − δ); cards blocked before the row's day removed; the card id itself dropped. Because: the known-card removal and the delay block of module 2 are the training-set version of the same rule, and the card id leaks the block.
7. **Which labels do the law and the loop expect you to keep?** — Answer: the definitions of "fraud" the bank collects statistics under are a licensing requirement (PSD2 Art 5(1)(i)); the annual fraud statistics feed the reference rates of module 10; the assessment record is what the supervisor reads. Because: the label pipeline is not only training data; it is the regulator's record.

### Set pieces and worked examples to recreate
- The stacked day-by-day timeline at the end of day t: F_t … F_{t−6} small and balanced-ish, D_{t−7}, D_{t−8} huge and skewed (Dal Pozzolo 2018 Fig. 2; δ = 7, M = 2, Q = 9 in Fig. 4).
- The four clocks with their statutory numbers: next business day (refund), 13 months (dispute), 3 working days (refrain), 5 working days / 24 h (FIU request), annual (feedback), 5 years (record) (PSD2 Arts 71, 73; AMLR Arts 69, 71, 77; AMLD6 Art 28).
- The insurance claim funnel: suspicion check → worth investigating by claim size → court or settlement — each stage with its own error, small claims never labelled (Baesens Ch4 p123).
- Transitive taint: $100 illicit → $50 → $25, all labelled laundering, including co-mingled funds (Altman Sec 3.4); 19,461 of 100,604 laundering transactions in a canonical pattern (Table 8).
- "Several false negatives are unavoidable": an account is used for several frauds before being caught (Bolton & Hand Sec 8); the ~3 frauds per compromised card per day of the Worldline replay (Handbook Ch3 RWD).
- The card-id argument: remove CARD ID from features because real cards are blocked after their first detected fraud (Dal Pozzolo 2018 Sec VI-A).
- The reference pipeline's "time-travel to a date when labels are settled" and its simplification "we only go back to yesterday" (FraudFinder bqml/04).

### Pitfalls and terms to define
- "Settled": a label that will not change; define before "as of".
- "Disposition": the investigator's verdict on an alert; "chargeback/dispute": the customer's; keep them apart — one is fast and selected, the other slow and unselected.
- The 13-month window is PSD2 Art 71's limit for the payer, not the time most disputes take; say what is a bound and what is typical.
- "Silence = genuine" is a modelling decision with a known error; say the error's direction (missed frauds in the negatives) and that it is why 100% recall is unmeasurable.
- "Report" (the bank's filing) vs "label" (the training target): for AML the course uses the filing as the label and says why that is weak.
- Do not re-explain the feedback/delayed aggregation of module 8; recall it.

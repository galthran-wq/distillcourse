# Model risk: backtesting, stability indices and the life cycle — research brief

Lesson: `model-risk-monitoring` (module: governance, type: core, status: planned). NEW — full re-synthesis, no draft exists, so no REVISION block.
Goal: Compute population and performance stability tests for a deployed detector and design its monitoring, champion–challenger and retirement life cycle
Opening (verbatim, the lesson must start with it): A detector that scored well last quarter is in production now, and the fraudsters, the customers and the products have all changed since. Which numbers should be computed every week to tell whether its inputs have shifted, whether its ranking still works and whether its probabilities still mean what they say — and what happens to the model when a number turns red?

## EXPLANATION PLAN
### The problem as the newcomer meets it
Module 7 gave the reader detectors that watch the error stream — but the error stream arrives δ days late and only for the alerted payments, and it answers one question: has the error changed? A bank's model-risk function asks three, every week, with a traffic light each, and has to say in writing what happens at red. First, have the inputs moved — is the population the model now scores the population it was built on? That can be answered today, without a single label. Second, does the ranking still work — the metrics of module 2 recomputed on the latest labelled window against the development sample, with a statement of whether a drop is noise. Third, do the probabilities still mean what they say — a 5% band should contain 5% fraud, because the frontier of the previous lesson multiplies probabilities by amounts. And then the model is not a file: it was developed, tested, staged next to the old one, put into production, and will be retired — and each stage has a gate.
Terms the newcomer does not yet have: backtest (ex-ante vs ex-post), stability index (system / population / characteristic), expected vs observed bin shares, traffic lights, out-of-time sample, bootstrap backtest, calibration pool, binomial critical rate, Hosmer–Lemeshow, champion–challenger, changeover (direct, phased, parallel), retirement.

### Chain of sub-problems
1. **Has the population moved, with no labels yet.** Obvious attempt: compare the mean score this week to last week. Why it fails: a mean hides a redistribution (more mass at both ends, same mean), and it is one number with no scale for "how much is too much". Answer: bin the score (e.g. ten bands), take the development sample's shares as expected and this week's as observed, and compute SSI = Σ_i (obs_i − exp_i)·ln(obs_i/exp_i); it is the information value of module 4 between two periods (the lesson derives this identity: IV = Σ(Dist_good − Dist_bad)·ln(Dist_good/Dist_bad) with the two periods as the two "classes"); the lights < 0.10 / 0.10–0.25 / ≥ 0.25. Then per variable, because a stable score can hide two variables moving against each other. Worked: total 0.0605 green; 'Years client' at t+1 = 0.304 red. Because: a symmetric divergence over bins sees redistribution, and the IV thresholds carry over with their meaning.
2. **What the index cannot tell.** The claim to make: a red index on p(x) is virtual drift in the module-7 sense; it does not say the model is wrong, only that it is being asked about a population it has not seen; and a green index does not rule out real drift (p(y|x) can change with p(x) fixed). Because: the taxonomy of `concept-drift` decides what a red light means.
3. **Does the ranking still work.** Obvious attempt: recompute AUC / AP / CP@k on the latest labelled window and compare to the development number. Why it fails: the two numbers differ even with no change, by sampling; "5% lower" needs a scale. Answer: a performance-metric report per period with lights by absolute change (< 5% green, 5–10% yellow, > 10% red) as the cheap version; and a bootstrap as the honest one — pool the development and out-of-time samples, resample both at their original sizes with replacement, compute the metric difference, repeat 1,000+ times, green if not significant at 95%, yellow at 95% not 99%, red at 99%. Because: AUC and AP have no convenient closed-form distribution under dependence, and pooling makes the null (no change) the thing resampled.
4. **Which metrics.** For a ranking detector: AUC, accuracy ratio (2·AUC − 1), KS, top-decile lift — and, from module 2, AP and CP@k, which the textbook list lacks and the fraud setting needs; time to detection (module 1) as the operational one nobody computes. Because: the monitor must use the metric the budget is measured in, or it will stay green while the queue fills.
5. **Do the probabilities mean what they say.** Obvious attempt: Brier score (with the 1/n). Why it fails as a monitor: one number, no location. Answer: calibration pools — bin by predicted probability, compare the observed fraud rate θ_i/n_i to the predicted P_i; the binomial test with critical rate p* = P̂ + N⁻¹(α)·√(P̂(1−P̂)/n) (normal approximation when nP > 5 and n(1−P) > 5), four colours at 90/95/99%; Hosmer–Lemeshow across pools Σ(n_i P_i − θ_i)²/(n_i P_i(1−P_i)) as a chi-square. The caveat: the binomial assumes independent outcomes, and fraud outcomes are not (one compromised terminal, many cards; the graph of module 6), so the test over-rejects and is read as an early warning. Because: the frontier and the Bayes-minimum-risk rule spend probabilities as money; a miscalibrated band misprices every decision in it.
6. **How this relates to module 7's detectors.** Answer: the error-stream detectors (CUSUM, Page–Hinkley, DDM, ADWIN) are the per-item, online version of the performance backtest and need labels; the stability index is the per-period, label-free version of the input check; the two are complements with different clocks — the index sees a product launch the day it happens, the detector sees the effect δ days later. Because: the monitoring design is a choice of clocks, like the system of module 1.
7. **What happens at red.** Obvious attempt: retrain. Why it fails alone: a retrained model is a new model, and a new model needs the gates. Answer: the life cycle — development; testing (latency under the budget, missing and extreme inputs, dummy fraud cases); staging in parallel with the champion for a period (champion–challenger); production; retirement into a repository for benchmarking. Changeover: direct, phased (small claims first), parallel (weights shifting to the new score over months). Because: the gate at each stage is where a red light has somewhere to go; and in module 10 the law fixes some of these gates (yearly audit of the TRA model; a pre-determined retraining policy is not a substantial modification; a new technology is risk-assessed before launch).
8. **The documentation test.** Could a newly hired team continue the model from the documents alone? The design questions (data, features, metrics, thresholds, monitoring plan, retirement criteria). Because: the supervisor of module 10 reads the documents, not the code; and Annex IV of the AI Act is this test written as a list.

### Pitfalls and terms to define
- "Expected" in SSI means the development sample's share, not a model prediction; say it in the first sentence of the derivation.
- The SSI thresholds are conventions, not tests; the bootstrap and the binomial are tests. Keep the two kinds of light apart.
- The binomial test's independence assumption is false for fraud (one terminal, many cards); state it where the test is introduced, as the textbook does, and read the result as a warning.
- Brier score: include 1/n (the textbook omits it).
- Champion–challenger means parallel scoring on live traffic, not a validation split.
- Do not re-teach CUSUM/PH/DDM/ADWIN; position them (step 6) with a <Ref>.
- The FraudFinder thresholds (skew 0.5, drift 0.05) are named once as what the next lesson examines; no judgment here.

## EXERCISE PLAN

2. [boundary probe] Let one bin have expected share zero and observed share 0.01: show SSI is undefined, propose the floor practitioners use, and say which direction of the move the floor hides — the index's silent assumption.
3. [claim to refute] "A green stability index on every variable means the model is still valid." Refute with a real-drift example (p(y|x) changes, p(x) fixed — the simulator's scenario 2 terminals): the index stays green while CP@100 falls; name the module-7 object that would have seen it.
4. [derivation skipped] Show that SSI equals the information value with the two periods as the two classes, and that it is the symmetrised Kullback–Leibler divergence between the binned distributions; state why symmetry matters for a monitor.
5. [derivation skipped] Derive the binomial critical rate from the normal approximation and show how the independence assumption enters; compute how much a within-terminal correlation of ρ inflates the variance for a pool of n cards on m terminals.

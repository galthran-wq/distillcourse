# PSD2, strong customer authentication and the transaction-risk-analysis exemption — research brief

Lesson: `psd2-sca-tra` (module: eu-regulation, type: core, status: planned). NEW — full re-synthesis, no draft exists, so no REVISION block.
Goal: Derive the constraint the PSD2 RTS places on a fraud model — the value-weighted 90-day fraud rate per exemption band, its cessation rule and the liability shift — and compute a lawful exemption policy
Opening (verbatim, the lesson must start with it): An online card payment of EUR 240 can go through without the customer typing a second code — but only if the issuer's fraud model is good enough, and 'good enough' is written into law as a number. What is that number, how is it computed over which transactions, what happens when it is exceeded, and who pays for the fraud that slips through?

## EXPLANATION PLAN
### The problem as the newcomer meets it
The engineer's detector scores every card-not-present payment. The business wants to use the score for something new: letting low-risk payments skip the second-factor step that loses customers at checkout. They are told the law allows it under conditions. The questions: what the second factor is and when it is required; what "low risk" means to the legislator; what number the issuer must stay under, computed how, over what; what happens to the exemption when the number is exceeded; and who eats the loss when an exempted payment turns out to be fraud. Terms to define at first use: payment service provider, PSP (the issuer or the acquirer's bank — the regulated party); payer and payee; account-servicing PSP (one clause); remote electronic payment (Art 4(6): initiated via the internet or a device for distance communication); strong customer authentication, SCA (Art 4(30): two or more independent elements of knowledge, possession, inherence); RTS (regulatory technical standards: a delegated regulation the Commission adopts on the EBA's draft under Art 98); exemption threshold value, ETV; competent authority (the national payments supervisor); EBA (one clause).

### Chain of sub-problems
1. **When is the second factor required?** — Obvious attempt: for every card payment. Why it fails: Art 97(1): SCA when the payer "accesses its payment account online", "initiates an electronic payment transaction", or "carries out any action through a remote channel which may imply a risk of payment fraud"; Rec 95 leaves mail and telephone orders outside. Answer: electronic remote payments need SCA, and for those Art 97(2) adds dynamic linking: the code is bound to "a specific amount and a specific payee". Because: a stolen credential must not be reusable for another amount or payee. Map: MOTO and non-electronic channels are where fraud moves when SCA bites — a feature (channel) and a drift the model will see.
2. **Why is an exemption allowed at all?** — Obvious attempt: convenience. Why it fails: Rec 96 and Art 98(3): exemptions rest on "the level of risk", "the amount and/or recurrence", "the payment channel"; the RTS turns that into Art 18. Answer: the law trades friction for a demonstrated low fraud rate. Map: the issuer's model precision becomes a licence condition.
3. **What must the monitoring do before any exemption?** — Obvious attempt: a model is optional. Why it fails: RTS Art 2(1): "transaction monitoring mechanisms … based on the analysis of payment transactions taking into account elements which are typical of the payment service user in the circumstances of a normal use"; Art 2(2): at a minimum, compromised or stolen credential lists, the amount, known fraud scenarios, malware signs, device logs. Answer: per-user behavioural monitoring is mandatory for everyone, exemption or not. Map: module 3's profile deviations are a statutory minimum; the blocking rules of module 1 are Art 2(2)(a).
4. **What does "low risk" mean in Art 18?** — Obvious attempt: score below a threshold. Why it fails: Art 18(2) needs all three: (a) the PSP's fraud rate for that transaction type is "equivalent to or below the reference fraud rates" of the Annex; (b) the amount does not exceed the ETV; (c) real-time analysis found none of six flags — abnormal spending or behavioural pattern, unusual device/software access, malware, a known fraud scenario, abnormal payer location, high-risk payee location. Art 18(3): the PSP "shall take into account at a minimum" previous spending patterns, each user's history, payer and payee location where the PSP supplies the device, abnormal patterns — and "combine all those risk-based factors into a risk scoring for each individual transaction". Answer: a per-transaction score (the model), a band on the amount, and a licence-level rate — three layers, two of them not about the single payment. Because: a single score cannot prove that the issuer as a whole is below the reference. Map: the score is module 4's model; the six flags are hard rules in front of it (module 1's layers); the rate is a controller over the whole stream (module 9's monitoring).
5. **What is the number?** — Obvious attempt: fraud count over transaction count. Why it fails: Art 19(1): "the total value of unauthorised or fraudulent remote transactions, whether the funds have been recovered or not, divided by the total value of all remote transactions for the same type of transactions, whether authenticated with the application of strong customer authentication or executed under any exemption … on a rolling quarterly basis (90 days)". The Annex: cards 0.13% at EUR 100, 0.06% at EUR 250, 0.01% at EUR 500; credit transfers 0.015% / 0.01% / 0.005%. Answer: value-weighted, per transaction type, over 90 rolling days, with SCA-authenticated and exempted flows both in the denominator, recovered fraud still in the numerator. Because: value weighting stops a PSP from exempting many small payments to dilute a count; the shared denominator stops it from shifting payments between lanes; counting recovered funds stops recovery from masking detection failure. Carry the arithmetic: 90 days of EUR 300 m remote card volume with EUR 150 000 fraud by value is 0.05% — lawful at EUR 250, unlawful at EUR 500; two large frauds of EUR 20 000 each move it to 0.063% and trip the EUR 250 band while the count-weighted rate barely moves. Map: a value-weighted daily series with a 90-day window is the controller's input; the cost metric of module 2 (amount-weighted) is the right instinct.
6. **What happens when the rate is exceeded?** — Obvious attempt: fix it quietly. Why it fails: Art 20(1): report "immediately" to the competent authority with the measures intended; Art 20(2): "immediately cease" the Art 18 exemption for that transaction type "in the specific exemption threshold range where their monitored fraud rate exceeds for two consecutive quarters the reference"; Art 20(3): do not resume "until their calculated fraud rate equals to, or is below, the reference … for one quarter"; Art 20(4): notify with evidence before resuming. Answer: a controller with hysteresis — trip after two bad quarters, release after one good one — per type and per band. Because: the legislator wanted stability, not flapping. Map: exactly the controller the capstone builds; a precision drop of module 7's drift kind becomes a legal event with a quarter's lag.
7. **Who checks the number?** — Obvious attempt: the PSP reports it. Why it fails: Art 19(2)–(3): the calculation is assessed by the audit of Art 3(2); "the methodology and any model … shall be adequately documented and made fully available to competent authorities and to EBA"; Art 3(2): audited at least yearly, by an external auditor in the first year and at least every three years; Art 21: quarterly recording of fraud value, total value, rate, average transaction value, exemption counts, broken down by SCA vs each exemption and remote vs non-remote. Answer: the model is an audited object with a quarterly ledger. Map: module 9's performance backtest becomes an audit artefact; the monitoring breakdown is a report the pipeline must emit.
8. **Who pays?** — Obvious attempt: the customer who lost the card. Why it fails: Art 73(1): refund "immediately, and in any event no later than by the end of the following business day", except with "reasonable grounds for suspecting fraud" communicated in writing to the authority; Art 74(1): the payer bears at most EUR 50, and nothing if the loss was not detectable or caused by the PSP's side; Art 74(2): if the payer's PSP "does not require strong customer authentication, the payer shall not bear any financial losses unless the payer has acted fraudulently"; a payee or its PSP that does not accept SCA refunds the payer's PSP; Art 72(2): recorded use of the instrument is not by itself proof of authorisation. Answer: an exempted payment's fraud lands on the issuer, or on a payee that refused SCA; the issuer's precision is a liability choice. Because: the party that chose to skip the factor bears the consequence. Map: the savings metric of module 2 priced with the issuer bearing every exempted false negative; the audit trail of module 9 is what Art 72 demands as proof.

### Set pieces and worked examples to recreate
- The Annex as a step function: allowed fraud rate vs amount band, two series (cards 0.13 / 0.06 / 0.01%; credit transfers 0.015 / 0.01 / 0.005%), with the factor 13 between the EUR 100 and EUR 500 card bands (RTS Annex; directiveeu-rts.txt line 1345).
- The rate worked on a 90-day window: EUR 300 m volume, EUR 150 000 fraud = 0.050%; add two EUR 20 000 frauds → 0.063%; add 1,000 EUR 20 frauds instead → 0.057% — value weighting moves with the large ones (RTS Art 19(1); line 742).
- The denominator trick: a PSP that exempts only EUR 50 payments and forces SCA on the rest cannot lower its rate, because both lanes are in the denominator (Art 19(1) "whether authenticated … or executed under any exemption").
- The controller on eight quarters of a made-up rate series: Q1 0.05, Q2 0.07, Q3 0.08 (trip at the end of Q3: two consecutive above 0.06), Q4 0.05 (one quarter below: may resume after notifying with evidence), Q5 … (RTS Art 20(2)–(4); lines 766–778).
- Art 18(3)'s last sentence, "combine all those risk-based factors into a risk scoring for each individual transaction", set beside the module-4 model: the regulator wrote the model into the rules (line 713 ff).
- The six real-time flags as hard rules in front of the model, with the module-1 layer diagram (Art 18(2)(c)(i)–(vi); lines 699–711).
- The liability inversion in one table: SCA applied / exempted × fraud / genuine → who bears what (Arts 73–74; directiveeu-s3-psd2-title4-7.txt lines 435–480).
- Other numbers to quote: EUR 30 / EUR 100 / five transactions (Art 16), EUR 50 / 150 / five (Art 11), 90 days of account access (Art 10), five failed attempts and 5 minutes (Art 4), 13 months to notify (PSD2 Art 71), application 14 September 2019 (Art 38).

### Pitfalls and terms to define
- The TRA exemption is Art 18 of the RTS, not Art 16 (Art 16 is the EUR 30 low-value remote exemption); fraud-rate calculation is Art 19, cessation Art 20, monitoring Art 21.
- The Annex is the one in Delegated Regulation 2018/389 as adopted; the EBA was to review the reference rates by 14 March 2021 (Art 37) — the human checks the consolidated RTS and the PSD3/PSR proposals at write time; nothing from those is in the dossier.
- "Fraud rate" in Art 19 is by value, per transaction type, rolling 90 days — never say "per quarter" as if calendar quarters; Art 20 then speaks of "two consecutive quarters" — the lesson must reconcile the rolling window with the quarterly test explicitly (the rate is computed on a rolling basis and assessed per quarter).
- The RTS data-protection recitals cite Directive 95/46/EC; the applicable text is the GDPR (two lessons on).
- PSD2 recitals explain; only Title IV articles and the RTS bind. Table 4 of the dossier's APATE summary is unrelated — do not import "1% false-positive rate" into this lesson as a legal number.
- No EBA opinion or Q&A is in the dossier; the lesson may only say EBA guidance exists on the Art 18 flags.
- Keep card-present contactless (Art 11) distinct from remote (Art 16, Art 18); the lesson is about remote electronic payments.

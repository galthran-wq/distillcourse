# Sequence models over an entity's transactions — research brief

Lesson: `sequence-models-for-transactions` (module: sequences-drift, core, planned). NEW.
Goal: Construct fixed-length transaction sequences per cardholder and evaluate 1-D convolutions, LSTMs and attention against hand-built aggregates on card-present and e-commerce data.
Prerequisite: neural-tabular-detectors (MLP, Adam, early stopping, embeddings); behavioural-profiles (tdelta, the cumulative line).

## Key points
1. The flaw the sequence model fixes: aggregates are hand-chosen selection + aggregation; a sequence model learns both from the ordered history — if the history has consistent structure.
2. Building the sequence: landmark = customer; seq_len from the ~4-frauds-per-compromised-account fact; the padding row and the groupby-shift construction (worked on customer 0's table); why the fixed-length cut matters for batching; account-level sampling because rows of one card are dependent (Jurgovsky Sec. 4.1); the delayed-label gap in the test split (recall module 2).
3. Three architectures on the same (seq_len, features) input: 1-D causal convolution with the shape arithmetic; LSTM's final hidden state (the recurrence and the gradient problem in two paragraphs, LSTM cells by reference); attention as a learned weighted sum with the context from the last transaction — the weights demo (uniform vs one-hot on the last transaction).
4. The result on the simulator: CNN/LSTM/attention ≈ FFNN ≈ XGB on all three metrics — "only competitive because the aggregates already carry the signal"; architecture matters even at equal inputs (CNN < LSTM on AP); the compute (7.5 h for a CNN grid).
5. The result on real data (Jurgovsky): F2F BASE 0.138 → 0.200 with the LSTM; ECOM 0.179 vs 0.180; AGG lifts both models far more (ECOM 0.18 → 0.40) and closes the LSTM gap; the physical-constraints hypothesis; the Jaccard finding (25% TP overlap) — models find different frauds, so combine; the handbook's real-data table agrees on AP (sequence > trees) and CP@100 (tie).
6. HMM and counting features as the lighter alternative: eight HMM likelihood features (+15%); t-STIDE on discretised amount sequences; where these sit (features into a tree model — module 4's winner).
7. Regularisation under drift (Sec. 6.1): the model that fits today is wrong in days — dropout as time-stability; a hand-off to concept-drift.
8. ★ Starred: BPTT and the eigenvalue condition; the pipeline-through-skorch trick; the seq2seq autoencoder named as the handbook's unfinished stub.

## EXERCISE PLAN
1. Warm-up: compute the Conv1d output shapes for seq_len 10, 20 features, 64 filters of length 3 then 32 of length 2, with and without causal padding.
2. Derivation the lesson skipped: write the groupby-shift construction for seq_len = 5 and prove it yields, for each transaction, the indices of its 4 predecessors within the same customer (or the padding index) — then count the padding rows for a customer with n < 5 transactions.
3. Boundary probe: seq_len = 1 — the LSTM/CNN become an MLP on the current transaction (BASE's FFNN); show the attention weights are trivially [1]; what the F2F gain of 0.138 → 0.200 then measures (history alone).
4. Claim to refute: "The LSTM's gain on card-present data means card-present fraud is sequential and e-commerce fraud is not." Refute with the AGG rows: with aggregates the F2F gap closes (0.241 vs 0.246) — the LSTM was learning what the aggregates encode; name what differs between channels (consistency of the history, not the fraud).
5. Research-shaped: on the simulator, build sequences (seq_len 5) and train a small LSTM vs the FFNN on BASE features; report AP on the test week with the harness and whether the gap exceeds the fold std.
6. FORWARD (E7): compute per-test-day AP for the FFNN over 20 simulator days and plot it with the daily fraud count — the reader sees AP co-moving with the number of frauds and asks whether the model or the day changed → lands on `concept-drift`'s day-distinguishability measure.
7. Mixed review (E3): at recall 0.2 on a simulator week, compute the Jaccard overlap of true-positive sets between the tuned GBM (gbm-model-selection) and the LSTM; then compute CP@100 of their score average (W1A fusion from one-class-and-reconstruction).

# arms-race-replay — ledger

NOTE (data): every number in this lesson is the writer's own seeded runs (five seeds) on the course's rewrite of the simulator (the cost-aware-evaluation-lab generator, 183 days), not a published figure. Protocol A: scenario 1's amount>220 rule switched off, an adaptive fraudster inserted (5 payments/day on cards whose holder pays that day; his payment's card windows = holder's + itself; terminal/time copied from one of the holder's payments that day), depth-2 or depth-6 sklearn tree, K = 30, δ = 7, fraudster pays from day 0 (band €260–300, unanswered until day 45), detector from day 45 (first window days 8–37 skips the first week; tree on day t trains on days t−37…t−8), blocked cards reissued after 28 days. Protocol B: standard simulator (scenarios 1–3 on) + planted night method from day 100 (5 cards/day leak, 1 payment 1–4 a.m., €20–60, 14 days); balanced RF of 50 trees (hand-built: all frauds + equal genuine draw, max_features sqrt); loop days 25–182, scored 55–182 (640 seed-days; day 55 = feedback window full two weeks + first blocks reissued). Real-stream numbers (0.12, CP@100 all-labels 0.30 vs delayed 0.25, 7.7% vs 12.5%) are Ref'd to alert-feedback-selection-bias, not re-run. The juxtaposed-month run was NOT run: it is an exercise (design + prediction). Study code lived in the writer's scratchpad, not in the repo; the final QA pass reran Protocol A (seed 0–4, weekly refit, probing and stay) with leaf-level diagnostics and reproduced arms_results.json exactly.

**Problem.** Replay the adversarial repeated game and the feedback/delayed comparison on the public simulator: how fast does each side move, and what does the simulator fail to reproduce? Answer: a one-knob (amount) fraudster who drops under the split when called and probes €20 up when clean alternates with period two against ANY split — the frozen-tree control shows it as clearly (so the alternation is his rule, not the refit's forgetting); the refit follows ~3–4 weeks late (label delay + 30-day window), daily vs weekly refit changes his take by 1.7% for the same weekly fraudster (€707 daily refit vs €695 weekly; the Conclusion's 'under 2%') and by 3.5% when both sides move daily (€671); a depth-6 tree + amount ÷ card's 30-day mean cuts his take from €695 to €257/day (€195 of it the ratio's, controlled by depth-6 without the ratio at €452); the ratio moves with the amount, its denominator (the holder's past) does not. The five feedback designs tie in level; on rank sums (CD = 154) only the average is shown first, the order below it is not; its lead is under a card a day because separation pays only for what feedbacks teach that delayed samples do not, and all-labels ties delayed (scripted fraud never changes; fresh signal travels via a delayed label-built input), the remaining margin (average − pooled 0.003–0.008) is attributed BY ELIMINATION only, to the false alarms or to averaging two 50-tree forests vs one, untested (a 100-tree pooled model would separate them); a planted method shows the feedback model alone learning at about half the all-labels rate; the average was not locked out (cut ≈ 0.5 on the simulator).

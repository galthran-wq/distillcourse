# score-to-action — ledger

NOTE (data): every number is this lesson's own arithmetic on stated, assumed prices (C_a €5, c_S €0.50, q 0.1, c_D €50) and on the eight-group day of example-dependent-cost; nothing is measured. The 10,000 false alarms on a million accounts is the published 1%-of-a-million arithmetic, stated without source in the body. The investigator screen has landed as `figures/investigator-link-screen.png` (sourced insurance-claims link-analysis screenshot, fraudanalyti-s6-sas-link-analysis), standing in for the case screen; its caption states: one case opened, the claims around it and the people, addresses, cars and phones they share, fraud-flagged claims in red, a slider replaying the network up to the date shown.

**Opening development.** One approve/decline threshold uses two actions, gives 0.93 the same answer at €10 and €10,000, and, carried over from the call rule, charges a wrong decline C_a instead of a lost customer.

**Problem.** A calibrated p and an amount must become approve / step-up / decline / review, under a call capacity, as rules that can be checked: how? Answer: four expected costs, take the cheapest (zones in the p × Amt plane); a capacity of k calls = rank by saving Δ over the best automatic action = surcharge λ on the call; per-group false-positive rates beside the total; rule sets checked as expanded decision tables for gaps and conflicts, contracted/minimised to read them; a referred case is rescored and triaged by Δ' = min(p'·Amt, (1−p')c_D) − C_a (block at once only above p' 0.9, where the expected cost of a wrong block (1−p')c_D is below C_a; else ask first and block on a "no"; unreached cases take min(block, leave)), then investigated, recorded, its disposition the next label.

## Explained
- Step-up defined (second factor: phone code / app approval; genuine pass after seconds, some abandon; fraudster with card number only fails, phone takeover / talked-out code passes); four-action table (decided when, cost if genuine, cost if fraud, limit) — §Four actions, two clocks, four prices / ### What each action costs, and whom
- Review row keeps example-dependent-cost convention (C_a on both outcomes, caught fraud saves the amount), with its reason stated once here: the reviewed payment is already through, the call stops the card (block before the next payments), and the amount stands in for that further spending (Ref economics #what-a-miss-costs-the-amount-or-more); c_D ≠ C_a (refused customer); q and c_S are in no log the bank keeps (unchallenged fraudsters never tested, abandoned challenges never labelled) — same ###
- Reversal: fraud-detection-system said scoring before the answer gains nothing (only refusal there); step-up's cheap mistakes make a pre-answer score (stored profiles) useful; all four actions, review included, chosen before the answer on the pre-answer p; a payment sent to review is approved at the till and queued; the full model rescores it afterwards on recomputed history, which sets its place in the queue (see §The alert as a case) and is read first by the investigator but cannot change the action; the four costs compare mutually exclusive answers, and a case opened on top of a decline or step-up (block + call, §The alert as a case, exercise 8) is a further step outside that comparison — ### Why the second factor makes a score before the answer worth computing (no KeyFact)
- Law, one line each: PSD2 Art 79(1) refusal notified with reasons + correction procedure; Art 68(2)–(4) card block on objectively justified grounds, inform before/immediately after, unblock; Art 73(1) refund by end of next business day (why an approved fraud costs the amount); SCA default for online payments, model decides the skip → psd2-sca-tra — ### The legal limits
- Worked: p 0.93 at €400 → decline (approve 372, step-up 37.24, review 5, decline 3.50); at €10 → step-up (9.30 / 0.97 / 5 / 3.50); in-body exercise p 0.05: €300 → step-up (€1.98), €2,000 → review (step-up €10.48) — §The cheapest action for one payment / ### The 0.93 payment
- Formulas E[approve] = p·Amt, E[step-up] = (1−p)c_S + p q Amt, E[review] = C_a, E[decline] = (1−p)c_D; Definition four-action policy, decision zones; = Bayes minimum-risk with four actions; Task: approve vs review recovers C_a ≤ Amt·p — ### Four expected costs (figure envelope.svg at €300: crossings 0.0018, 0.15, 0.90)
- Decline beats review iff p > 1 − C_a/c_D = 0.9, whatever the amount (both stop the fraud, so neither cost contains the amount: c_D on a genuine customer vs C_a on every case); decline vs step-up depends on the amount (step-up leak p q Amt), so at €10 decline starts only at ≈0.98 — same ###
- Zones bars (zones.svg): €10 approve ≤0.053, step-up ≤0.98, decline; €300 approve ≤0.0018, step-up ≤0.15, review ≤0.9, decline; €2,000 step-up ≤0.023, review ≤0.9, decline (approve only below 0.0003); referral band = the review band (handed to a person who may overrule; on either side an automatic action is cheaper than a call; none at €10, stated in caption + one sentence); traffic light green/amber/red has no colour for the automatic step-up — ### Zones that move with the amount
- Plane (plane.svg, key image; log-odds p × log Amt; G1–G8 and the 0.93 points): approve|step-up boundary ≈ constant expected amount €0.55; review lower edge bends to ≈€55 at p 0.9 — ### The plane, and the table analysts read
- Banded 6×6 table (banded.svg; p bands <0.001/0.001–0.01/0.01–0.1/0.1–0.5/0.5–0.9/≥0.9 at reps 0.0005/0.003/0.03/0.25/0.7/0.95; amount bands <50/50–200/200–1,000/1,000–2,000/2,000–5,000/≥5,000 at reps 20/100/450/1,400/3,200/10,000); investigation frontier = boundary of review cells, a staircase; two-action case = constant p·Amt staircase (Ref economics #-which-alerts-first-expected-amount-not-probability, reminder: worth a call while expected amount exceeds the call's cost); banding error: single €1,000–5,000 band would put G5 in review; needs calibrated p (Ref example-dependent-cost #the-rule-reads-probabilities-as-numbers). KeyFact: frontier is not constant expected amount once step-up and decline exist — same ###
- Eight-group day under four actions: G1,G2 approve; G3–G6 step-up; G7,G8 review; cost €2,908.60 vs €5,356 (two-action), savings 80.7% vs 64.4%; calls 40 vs 140; 2,794 genuine challenged (~3 in 10 payments); G5 leaves review (step-up leak €3.60 < €5) — §A hundred calls a day / ### The step-up empties most of the review band
- k = 30 example: Δ(G7) = 16.30 − 5 = €11.30 (next-best step-up), Δ(G8) = 10 − 5 = €5 (next-best decline); by saving €250 vs by expected amount €313 → €63/day — ### When calls run short
- Δ_i = min(approve, step-up, decline) − C_a; Proposition "the review budget" (with proof, exchange/sum argument): review k largest positive Δ ⇔ policy with call priced C_a + λ, λ = k-th largest Δ — ### Capacity acts as a surcharge on the call
- FigureSequence surcharge-1..3 (λ = 0, 8, 25) + Task; decline edge p = 1 − (C_a + λ)/c_D: 0.9 → 0.74 → 0.4; understaffing → false declines; λ set from recent days in real time, a monitored number; staffing itself Ref economics — same ###
- €80 example: established card p 0.002 approve (0.16 vs 0.52), new card p 0.02 step-up (1.60 vs 0.65), 98% of challenged genuine; day with p×10 (capped 0.95): challenged genuine 28% → 57% (5,373/9,504; G5 to review), G7/G8 declined — §What a wrong decline costs / ### Friction goes where p is high
- Definition false-positive rate of action a in group g; false-positive harm = these rates per group/action beside the total; three remedies (better model, cheaper action, group c_D = business/legal decision); 1% of a million accounts = 10,000/day; decline zone FP count Σ(1−p_i) — ### Measuring the harm group by group
- De-risking defined (AMLD6 Recital 114: restricting categories of customers to avoid risk; less secure channels, financial exclusion, supervisors watch); decline zone on one group = payment-level analogue (argued). KeyFact — ### The law reads declines one customer at a time
- Rules R1–R4 (new device / amount > €500 / failed attempts > 3) + Task — §Rules written by people / ### Four rules, one conflict, one gap
- Definition decision table (conditions with partitioning states, actions, columns, expanded); verify.svg: col 4 conflict (R2 step-up vs R4 approve), cols 7–8 gap (known device, ≤ €500 = the most common payment) — ### Laying the rules out as a table
- Definition complete / consistent / gap / conflict; engine default and evaluation order as unwritten policy, fix = explicit "otherwise" and written priority; numeric states from the union of all cut points; columns = product of state counts (30 binary ≈ 10^9); conflicts by rule pairs, gaps need combinations; in-body exercise R5 (amount > €500 AND attempts > 3 → decline): col 1 redundant, col 5 new conflict — ### Completeness and consistency
- Expanded table read as a question tree in a fixed condition order; contracted = drop a question where every answer leads to the same subtree (dash, merge columns, bottom-up, repeatable) — order-dependent; minimised = the order with fewest columns (condition with a self-settling answer first); order-free pairwise merging reaches the same minimised table; eight card rules (watch-list merchant, amount ≤/> €1,000, card present) order watch→amount→card: 3+4 (card dropped), 5+7 and 6+8 (amount dropped) → 5 columns; card first → 1, 3, 5+7, 2+4+6+8 = 4 columns (FigureSequence contract-1..3); reveals card-not-present → always step-up, present + not watch-listed → approve — ### Contracting and minimising
- Banded policy = two-condition decision table, complete & consistent by construction; hand rules multiply conditions, conflicts appear there; priority model vs rule as explicit row (no KeyFact) — ### The model's table and the hand rules in one table
- Case order (### Block, call or leave; renamed from Mitigation before investigation): after the till the second score p' chooses leave / block / call; Δ'_i = min(p'·Amt, (1−p')c_D) − C_a = Δ with the step-up row dropped and decline read as a block; p' > 0.9 → block at once + notify (Art 68(3)); p'·Amt < C_a → leaves the queue; rest called in order of Δ' (G7 €25 vs G8 €5 if p' = p; ranking by p·Amt repeats the 30-calls mistake); a case the day's calls do not reach takes min(block, leave) — G8 blocked, €10 vs €200, as its payment was declined in the 30-calls example. Below 0.9 a case that will be called is not blocked first (review band mostly genuine; blocking first would cost C_a + (1−p)c_D > decline, band vanishes); contact first ("was it you?", text then call), block/freeze/hold on a "no", before investigation (Ref economics what-waiting-costs), fraudster last; routing = decision table on type/amount → desk, checked for gaps; figure case.svg (8 boxes, each ticked to the audit strip: second score, 1 triage (block if p' > 0.9; else call, or block/leave if unreached), 2 priority, 3 routing, 4 contact, 5 mitigation, 6 investigation, 7 disposition) — §The alert as a case

## Notation
- p calibrated probability, Amt amount, C_a call cost (from economics/example-dependent-cost)
- c_S cost of a step-up to a genuine customer; q share of frauds that pass the second factor; c_D cost of declining a genuine customer
- Δ_i saving of a call over payment i's cheapest automatic action; m_i that cheapest automatic cost (proof only); λ surcharge (shadow price of a call slot); k review capacity

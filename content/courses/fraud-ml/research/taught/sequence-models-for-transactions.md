# sequence-models-for-transactions — ledger

**Problem.** 24-hour aggregates and the gap cannot tell two cards apart whose payments differ only in order: can a model read a card's latest payments as a sequence, and on which payments does the order carry what aggregates do not? Answer: fixed-length landmark sequences with a padding row, read by a 1-D convolution, recurrence/LSTM or attention. An LSTM on raw fields gains on card-present (AUCPR 0.138 → 0.200), not on e-commerce (0.179 vs 0.180), a gain that measures history and order together because a raw-field forest has no history; judged on one scale (up to 0.046), that gain is modest (0.062, 0.094); with aggregates the gap is well inside it on both, evidence that much of the gain was history; the tying AGG pairs overlap little on card-present (Jaccard ≈ 0.27, ~43% shared) and more online (≈ 0.43, ~60% shared). HMM likelihood columns lift a forest most on raw fields, online too (+41%), but six of the eight read the gap or the terminal's stream and a raw-field forest has no history, so the online lift cannot be credited to order; the split was not measured. The simulator shows no gain because no script depends on order; the real-stream AP rise over the feed-forward net barely separates and does not show order is the cause.

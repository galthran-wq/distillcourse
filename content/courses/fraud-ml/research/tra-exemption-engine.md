# A PSD2-compliant transaction-risk-analysis exemption engine — research brief

Lesson: `tra-exemption-engine` (module: eu-regulation, type: core, status: planned). NEW — full re-synthesis, no draft exists, so no REVISION block. Module-final APPLICATION lesson (L16): a computational study on the handbook simulator.
Goal: Design and run a TRA exemption engine — a per-transaction risk score, the Annex exemption bands, the rolling 90-day value-weighted fraud rate and the cessation controller — on the simulator and state where it departs from the RTS
Opening (verbatim, the lesson must start with it): Build what the PSD2 rules describe in words: a per-transaction risk score, the three amount bands, a rolling 90-day fraud rate weighted by value, and a switch that turns the exemption off after two bad quarters — then run it on the simulator and see how much friction a given model precision buys and where the legal ceiling bites first.

## EXPLANATION PLAN
### The problem as the newcomer meets it
The previous lessons gave the law in words; the engineer has to make it run. A deployed module-4 model emits a probability per payment; the business wants to exempt low-risk remote card payments from SCA in the EUR 100, 250 and 500 bands. Nothing in the law says which score threshold to use — the law constrains an aggregate (the 90-day value-weighted fraud rate per band) that the threshold moves. The newcomer must build: the score (given), the band assignment, the per-transaction exemption decision, the rolling rate per band computed as Art 19 says, the Art 20 controller with its two-quarter trip and one-quarter release, and the Art 21 quarterly report — then run all of it over 183 simulated days and read off, for each threshold, how much volume is exempted and when the exemption is lost. Terms the newcomer has: all of module 10's lesson 3; new here only "exempted lane" vs "SCA lane", and "friction" as the checkout loss per SCA challenge (a stated assumption, not a law).

### Chain of sub-problems
1. **What is the engine's input?** — Obvious attempt: scores and labels. Why it fails: Art 19 needs amounts (value weighting), transaction type (cards here), the lane each payment went through (SCA vs exempted — both in the denominator), the fraud outcome "whether the funds have been recovered or not", and the date (90 rolling days, quarters). Answer: a row per payment with (date, amount, score, band, lane, fraud label, label-known date). Because: the rate is defined over all of these. Map: the module-11 feature log plus the decision log; the label-known date is module 2's delay — the engine's rate can only be computed on labels that have arrived, so the "current" rate is δ days stale.
2. **How does a score become a decision?** — Obvious attempt: exempt if score < τ. Why it fails: Art 18(2) adds the band (amount ≤ ETV) and the six flags; and Art 18(2)(a) makes the decision depend on the band's rate being at or under the reference right now. Answer: exempt iff lane open for the band ∧ amount ≤ ETV ∧ no flag ∧ score < τ_band. Because: three conditions, two of which are not about the payment. Map: a per-band state (open/closed) owned by the controller; the six flags as hard rules the simulator cannot supply (say so: scenario 2's compromised terminals stand in for "known fraud scenario").
3. **How is the rate computed as the law says?** — Obvious attempt: fraud count over count in the exempted lane. Why it fails: Art 19(1) — value, both lanes, rolling 90 days, recovered counted. Answer: for each day d and band b: rate_b(d) = Σ amount of fraudulent remote payments in band b over [d−89, d] ÷ Σ amount of all remote payments in band b over the same window — with "in band b" meaning amount ≤ ETV_b, so the bands nest (the EUR 500 rate includes all payments under EUR 500, exempted or not). Because: the denominator is "all remote transactions for the same type", not the exempted ones. The lesson must settle the band semantics explicitly (nested by ETV) and say that the RTS text ("in that exemption threshold range") can be read as nested or as disjoint ranges — choose nested, flag the choice as the engine's interpretation. Map: a rolling sum, as in module 3's windows; the delayed-label version lags by δ.
4. **How does the controller work?** — Obvious attempt: close the lane the day the rate exceeds the reference. Why it fails: Art 20(2): cease "where their monitored fraud rate exceeds for two consecutive quarters the reference"; Art 20(3): resume only after "one quarter" at or below; Art 20(1): report immediately on any exceedance with intended measures. Answer: a state machine per band — OPEN, EXCEEDING(1), CLOSED, RECOVERING — stepped at quarter ends on the quarter's rate (the rolling 90-day rate read at quarter end), with an immediate report event on first exceedance. Because: the law's hysteresis is two up, one down. Map: module 9's traffic lights with a legally fixed red; module 7's drift detector with a quarter's granularity.
5. **What does the engine measure?** — Obvious attempt: fraud caught. Why it fails: the business question is friction bought for a given precision. Answer: for each threshold τ and band: exempted volume (count and value), fraud value in the exempted lane (the issuer's liability under Art 74(2)), the 90-day rate series, the quarters OPEN, and the savings under module 2's cost with the issuer bearing exempted fraud and a stated checkout-abandonment cost per SCA challenge. Because: those are the numbers a product owner and a supervisor each want. Map: the Art 21 quarterly report is the output table.
6. **What does the study show?** — Expected shape on the simulator (to be computed, not asserted): the simulator's fraud rate by value is far above 0.13% (0.84% by count; scenario 1's frauds are the largest amounts, so by value higher still), so with τ = 1 (exempt everything under the ETV) every band is closed after two quarters; lowering τ removes high-score payments from the exempted lane but not from the denominator's fraud — Art 19's "both lanes" means the rate does not fall with τ at all unless the SCA lane actually stops fraud. The lesson must therefore model SCA as a filter: assume SCA stops a stated share (e.g. 90%) of the frauds routed through it (an assumption outside the dossier, declared as such), so that the rate becomes rate(τ) = [fraud value exempted + (1−s)·fraud value SCA'd] / total value. Then: the EUR 100 band opens at some τ, the EUR 250 band needs a lower τ, and the EUR 500 band at 0.01% may be unreachable on the simulator at any τ — the ceiling bites first at the top band. Because: the reference rates fall 13-fold while the volume above EUR 250 is small. Map: this is the lesson's headline, derived live.
7. **Where the engine departs from the RTS** — (i) the simulator's fraud is scripted card-present-style fraud, not remote-card fraud, and its rate is an order of magnitude above the Annex; (ii) the six real-time flags cannot be computed (no device, malware, location) — scenario 2 stands in for one; (iii) SCA's stopping power is an assumption; (iv) no yearly audit, no competent authority, no report; (v) the band semantics (nested) is a reading; (vi) the controller's hysteresis is the law's, not a tuning choice — the engine must not "optimise" it; (vii) labels are instant on the simulator; with module 2's delay the rate is known δ days late and the quarter-end decision uses incomplete labels (show the gap).

### Pitfalls and terms to define
- The RTS reads "exemption threshold range"; whether bands are nested (≤ ETV) or disjoint ((100, 250], etc.) is the engine's interpretation — state it, do not assert it as law.
- "Two consecutive quarters" vs "rolling quarterly basis (90 days)": the engine computes a rolling rate daily and tests it at quarter ends; say that the law does not fix the reading day.
- The simulator has no SCA, no device or location flags, instant labels, and a fraud rate an order of magnitude above the Annex; every number the study produces is about the mechanism, not about any real issuer.
- SCA's stopping share s is an assumption with no source in the dossier; vary it (0.5, 0.9, 1.0) and show the result's sensitivity.
- Do not "tune" the hysteresis or the reference rates; the exercise is to find τ under a fixed law.
- The TRA exemption is RTS Art 18 (not 16); rates Art 19; cessation Art 20.
- Labels on the simulator are complete; with module 2's known-card removal the exempted lane's fraud value changes — decide whether blocked cards' later frauds count (they do in life: the fraud happened) and say so.

## EXERCISE PLAN
1. [warm-up] Compute by hand the EUR 250 band's 90-day value-weighted rate from a given 10-row table with lanes and labels, then the same with one fraud "recovered".
2. [boundary probe] Set the reference to exactly the rate observed in two consecutive quarters. Does Art 20(2) ("exceeds") trip? Now make the second quarter 0.0001 percentage points higher. State what the engine must store to make "consecutive" well defined when quarters are read from a rolling window.
3. [derivation skipped] Prove that under Art 19(1) the rate is independent of τ when s = 0 (SCA stops nothing), and derive the rate as a function of τ for general s; find the τ at which the EUR 100 band opens on the simulator for s = 0.9.
4. [claim to refute] "Lowering τ to zero for the EUR 500 band keeps it open, because then nothing fraudulent is exempted." Refute from the denominator and the SCA lane's residual fraud, and say when the claim would hold (s = 1).
5. [research-shaped] Run the engine with module 2's 7-day label delay: at each quarter end, compare the controller's decision on known labels with the decision on settled labels, and count the quarters in which the two differ over a second simulator seed.
6. [mixed review, E3] Replace the fixed τ by the per-payment Bayes-minimum-risk rule from module 2 with $C_{FN}$ = amount and $C_{FP}$ = the checkout loss c: show whether the resulting exempted lane keeps the EUR 250 band open, and explain why a per-payment optimum and a lane-level legal constraint can disagree.
7. FORWARD → `pipeline-capstone-lab`: package the rate and the controller as a function of a daily (date, amount, lane, label) frame with a label-known date — the capstone's `fraud-rate-controller` checkpoint calls exactly this.

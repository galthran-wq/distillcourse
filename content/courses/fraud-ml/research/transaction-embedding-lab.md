# Lab: a contrastive transaction encoder judged against your own aggregates — research brief

Lesson: `transaction-embedding-lab` (module: learned-representations, type: lab, status: planned). NEW — no draft exists, so no REVISION block. This brief is what the lab-author skill is grounded in.
Goal: Build a contrastive encoder pipeline for real bank event sequences — slice sampling, the margin loss with hard negatives, point-in-time embeddings at every cut, windowed aggregates by prefix sums and a client-level bootstrap of the paired difference — and judge the embedding against your own aggregates with a confidence interval, at full and at scarce labels
Opening (verbatim, the lesson must start with it): The lab builds the encoder the module described, on real anonymised bank histories with product-purchase targets and no fraud label: slices and a margin loss trained on CPU in minutes, an embedding read for every client at every month's cut without a later event, aggregates by prefix sums as the opponent, and a bootstrap that says whether the two differ — at all the labels and at a tenth of them.

## EXPLANATION PLAN
### The problem as the newcomer meets it
Four lessons gave the reader the task, the loss, the tokeniser, the fusion and the checklist; none has been run by them. The lab makes the contrastive pipeline theirs and makes them the judge: the head-to-head is an open question answered with an interval, not a bar to clear. The learner must know, before the first checkpoint: the data has product-purchase labels and no fraud label (so it shows the technique on real behaviour, not that it catches fraud); its dates are jittered with the hour kept (so order within a day is noise); a row labelled for month `mon` may see only events before that month starts (the point-in-time trap the spike hit); and the encoder may be pretrained only on clients outside the test fold and events before the pretraining cut.

### Chain of sub-problems
1. **Slices from one history** — a seeded sampler returning k contiguous index ranges of length in [m, M]. Because: the task needs positives, and the module's assumption is that two windows of one life look alike.
2. **The loss on a batch** — unit-normed embeddings, the distance matrix by one matrix product, ½Y d² + ½(1−Y) max(0, ρ − d)². Because: collapse without the margin; the matrix because hard negatives need every distance.
3. **Hard negatives** — the h nearest other-client slices per row from the same matrix. Because: random negatives are mostly past the margin.
4. **The encoder** — per-field embeddings plus two numerics (log amount standardised, log gap) into a GRU; the last state normalised. Because: a GRU state is what the embedding-at-a-cut step reads.
5. **Embedding at every cut, no later events** — one GRU pass per client over the full history, reading the state just before each month's cut by `searchsorted` on event time; zero vector when nothing precedes the cut. Because: a row labelled for `mon` may only see events before `mon` starts; one pass serves all cuts because the state at index n−1 depends only on events before n.
6. **Aggregates as the opponent** — additive per-event columns (count, log amount, its square, one-hots of the top event types and top source types, log amount × type), prefix sums, three windows (30 days, 90 days, all) by two `searchsorted`s, plus days since the last event. Because: the module's fair opponent is the aggregation baseline, and prefix sums make windows at thousands of cuts cheap — module 3's difference-of-two-rolling-sums again.
7. **The paired bootstrap** — resample clients with replacement, recompute each target's ROC-AUC for both models on the resampled rows, average the difference over targets; 2.5 and 97.5 percentiles. Because: twelve monthly rows per client are correlated, so rows cannot be resampled; and the question is the difference, not either score.
8. **The head-to-head** — a boosted tree on aggregates, a logistic regression on the embedding, a rank average of the two; at all labels and at 10% and 3% of the training clients. Because: the module's claim is a conditional one, and the interval is what licenses or forbids each reading.
9. **The released next-event weights** — embed the same test clients at the same cuts with the public EventGPT checkpoint and rank-average it in. Because: the module's generative family should meet the learner's contrastive one on the same rows — and the checklist applies: the public weights were pretrained on 2021–2022 events including the test clients' months (leakage measured at 0.004–0.006 by the author), which the learner states.
10. **Optional: a fraud label at last** — the synthetic card set at ~0.1% fraud: strong windowed aggregates (module 3's) against a learner-made embedding through a fixed tree, reported as AP with the prevalence. Because: it is the module's one fraud-prevalence number, and the baseline's 13 features are unlisted, so the learner builds a stronger one.

- MBD's noise: say "dates jittered, hour kept, order within a day unreliable" in the data cover story and let the shuffled-order experiment be a notebook exercise, not a checkpoint.
- MBD has no fraud label: the cover story says what the four targets are and that the lab shows the technique on real behaviour; the only fraud-prevalence step is the optional TabFormer one.
- The pretraining cut (events before 2022-08-01, non-test clients only) is a contract the manifest states; the leak variant (pretraining on test clients) is declared wrong but cannot be graded by `exact` — it is a notebook assert on the client set.
- Three "δ"s again: the margin ρ, the hard-negative count h, the label-month cut; name variables `margin`, `n_hard`, `cut`.
- ROC-AUC per target averaged over four targets is the benchmark's metric; say why not AP here (targets at 2–10% positives, a ranking task, and the published numbers use it).

Checkpoints (ids as in course.yaml; the lab-author may split or deepen):
1. `slice-sampler` (exact, warm-up): `sample_slices(n_events, k, min_len, max_len, rng)` → list of (start, length) with length uniform on [min_len, min(max_len, n)] and start uniform; seeded; verified on fixed seeds (digest). Wrong variants: clipping an over-long draw to `max_len` instead of redrawing; start drawn up to `n` so the slice runs past the end.
2. `coles-loss` (exact, warm-up): `coles_loss(z, client_ids, margin)` on unit-norm rows: the positive term over same-client pairs (excluding the diagonal) and the negative term max(0, margin − d)² over cross-client pairs; verified on a hand-shaped 8 × 16 batch. Wrong variants: diagonal counted as a positive pair; the negative term with no clamp (a repulsion that never stops); squared distance from the dot product without the unit norm.
3. `hard-negatives` (exact, warm-up): `hard_negatives(dist, client_ids, n_hard)` → for each row the indices of the `n_hard` nearest other-client rows; the loss's negative term restricted to them. Wrong variants: nearest same-client rows included; farthest instead of nearest.
4. `slice-encoder` (exact): `SliceEncoder` — per-field `nn.Embedding` with the manifest's dimensions, two numerics, a GRU of hidden 128, packed sequences, last state normalised; verified by the output of a fixed-weight forward pass on a fixed batch (weights seeded by the manifest's seed, digest). Wrong variants: mean of states instead of the last; the unsorted pack with `enforce_sorted=True`.
5. `embed-at-cuts` (exact): `embed_at_cuts(encoder, seq, cuts)` → one row per cut from a single pass, the state at index `searchsorted(event_time, cut) − 1`, zeros when no event precedes; consumes 4; verified on a fixed-weight encoder and a 6-client fixture with 3 cuts each. Wrong variants: `searchsorted(side='right')` letting an event at the cut's exact timestamp in; the state after the cut's first event (the point-in-time trap the spike hit); re-encoding the prefix per cut (right, but the manifest's text says a single pass — a property assert on runtime, not a grade).
6. `window-aggregates` (exact): `window_aggregates(seq, cuts, windows, top_types, top_src)` → count, mean and variance of log amount, distinct destinations, type and source counts, type-weighted amounts over 30 / 90 / all days by prefix sums, plus days since the last event (9999 if none); verified on the module-3 seven-row table extended with types. Wrong variants: the window including the cut's own day; variance without the max(·, 0) floor; the prefix array off by one (cum[hi] − cum[lo] with hi inclusive).
7. `paired-client-bootstrap` (exact): `paired_client_bootstrap(y, score_a, score_b, client_ids, n_boot, rng)` → the 2.5 / 97.5 percentiles of the mean-over-targets difference in ROC-AUC, resampling clients; targets with zero positives in a draw skipped; verified on a seeded synthetic set. Wrong variants: resampling rows; percentiles of each model's AUC separately then differenced.
8. `pretrain-and-embed` (exact): the training loop — pretrain for a fixed number of steps on the non-test clients' events before the cut, then `embed_at_cuts` for every target row; consumes 1–5; verified by the embedding digest after a FIXED small number of steps (e.g. 20) on a fixed client subset with fixed seeds and threads (the full 5–10 minute run is the notebook's, and is not graded — its result is the open question).

## Key points
1. What travels and with whom: the funds fields (Art 4) and the crypto fields (Art 14) side by side; the sender verifies, the receiver checks completeness and admissibility; intermediaries carry and may act; the UTI for account-less transfers; the LEI where it exists.
2. The threshold logic for funds: intra-Union minimal set; EUR 1,000 single or linked (AMLR Art 2(1)(20)'s "linked" definition) switches on the full set and verification; cash and anonymous e-money override; outside the Union the batch-file rule — and the crypto rule: no threshold (Rec 30's four reasons), except the EUR 1,000 self-hosted ownership assessment.
3. Self-hosted addresses: definition; transfers to/from one with a CASP involved are in scope; above EUR 1,000 the CASP must assess control; AMLD Art 19a's mitigation; what "assess ownership" can mean technically (signed message, prior deposit history, DLT analytics — the KYT tools Art 37 asks the Commission to evaluate).
4. The receiving institution's checks as rules: admissible characters (a data-quality rule in statute); missing-field detection before or after crediting; the execute/reject/return/suspend decision as risk-based; the repeat-offender ladder (warnings → deadlines → reject/restrict/terminate → report) as counterparty risk scoring; missing information as an Art 69 suspicion factor.
5. Sanctions screening (Art 23): the fields the travel rule fixes are the fields screened against designation lists; the screening is matching, not learning (typology's point); AMLR Art 20's > 50% control rule and periodic rescreening.
6. Where the ML sits: the fields are the entity-resolution identifiers of module 6 (names, accounts, addresses — a cross-institution graph the travel rule makes legible); Rec 30's structuring is the motif of fraud-as-a-graph; mixers/privacy wallets as risk factors a graph feature can carry; Elliptic's "mixers ignored" as the label gap.
7. ★ Starred: the correlation to 2015/847; crypto-ATMs; NFTs out of scope unless classified under MiCA; the EBA guideline dates.

2. Boundary probe: two EUR 600 transfers an hour apart between the same parties — linked under AMLR Art 2(1)(20)? which set applies and why the crypto equivalent has no such question (Rec 30).
3. Claim to refute: "The travel rule requires crypto transfers to carry the originator's data on the blockchain, which is impossible for a public ledger, so it cannot be complied with." Refute with Art 14(4) (in advance of / simultaneously, securely — off-chain messaging) and Rec 50; name the clause.

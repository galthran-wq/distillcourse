Conditional entropy H(Y|X) is the uncertainty remaining in Y once X is
observed; the chain rule decomposes joint uncertainty into sequential
parts. This lesson extends entropy from one variable to a pair: the joint
distribution table and its marginals, the joint entropy $H(X,Y)$, the
conditional entropy $H(Y \mid X)$ computed two equivalent ways, the chain
rule $H(X,Y) = H(X) + H(Y \mid X)$, and the inequality
$H(Y \mid X) \le H(Y)$ with its equality case.

## The joint table: one probability per pair

One day of weather involves two coupled variables: the sky
$X \in \{\text{clear}, \text{overcast}\}$ and the ground
$Y \in \{\text{dry}, \text{wet}\}$. The pair $(X, Y)$ is a single random
object, and its distribution is a **table**: one probability for each pair
of values.

| $p(x,y)$ | $y = \text{dry}$ | $y = \text{wet}$ | $p(x)$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $x = \text{clear}$ | $\tfrac{1}{2}$ | $0$ | $\tfrac{1}{2}$ |
| $x = \text{overcast}$ | $\tfrac{1}{4}$ | $\tfrac{1}{4}$ | $\tfrac{1}{2}$ |
| $p(y)$ | $\tfrac{3}{4}$ | $\tfrac{1}{4}$ | $1$ |

The four cells sum to 1. Summing each row recovers the distribution of $X$
alone: $p(\text{clear}) = \tfrac{1}{2} + 0 = \tfrac{1}{2}$. Summing each
column recovers the distribution of $Y$:
$p(\text{dry}) = \tfrac{1}{2} + \tfrac{1}{4} = \tfrac{3}{4}$. These row
and column sums are the *marginals* — the distributions written in the
margins of the table.

The zero cell states that a clear sky with wet ground never occurs. It
contributes nothing to any entropy in this lesson, by the convention
$0 \log_2 0 = 0$ from <Ref lesson="entropy-as-average-surprise"/>.

<KeyFact>
The joint table is the complete description of the pair: the marginals and
every entropy in this lesson are computed from its cells alone.
</KeyFact>

## Joint entropy: plain entropy on the flattened table

The uncertainty of the whole day is the entropy of the pair. A pair-valued
variable is still a discrete variable; its outcomes are the four cells.
Reading the table as one flat distribution
$\{\tfrac{1}{2}, 0, \tfrac{1}{4}, \tfrac{1}{4}\}$ over pair-outcomes
reduces the problem to the three-step computation of
<Ref lesson="entropy-as-average-surprise"/>.

<Example title="joint entropy of the weather table">
Compute $H(X,Y)$ for the table above.

**Step 1: surprisal of each nonzero cell.**
$(\text{clear}, \text{dry})$: $-\log_2 \tfrac{1}{2} = 1$ bit;
$(\text{overcast}, \text{dry})$ and $(\text{overcast}, \text{wet})$:
$-\log_2 \tfrac{1}{4} = 2$ bits each. The zero cell is skipped.

**Step 2: weight each surprisal by its cell probability.**
$\tfrac{1}{2} \cdot 1 = 0.5$; $\;\tfrac{1}{4} \cdot 2 = 0.5$;
$\;\tfrac{1}{4} \cdot 2 = 0.5$.

**Step 3: sum.** $H(X,Y) = 0.5 + 0.5 + 0.5 = 1.5$ bits.

**Bounds check.** The value lies below $\log_2 3 \approx 1.585$ bits, the
maximum for a distribution supported on three cells.
</Example>

<Definition title="joint entropy">
The **joint entropy** of a pair of discrete random variables $(X, Y)$ with
joint probability mass function $p(x,y)$ is
$$
H(X,Y) = -\sum_{x,y} p(x,y) \log_2 p(x,y)
       = \mathbb{E}\bigl[-\log_2 p(X,Y)\bigr],
$$
measured in bits. Cells with $p(x,y) = 0$ contribute nothing.
</Definition>

No new machinery enters: joint entropy is the entropy formula applied to
pairs. It is **symmetric**, $H(X,Y) = H(Y,X)$, because transposing the
table permutes the same multiset of cell values. One observation to hold:
the marginal entropies are $H(X) = 1$ bit (a fair coin) and
$H(Y) = H(\tfrac{1}{4}) \approx 0.811$ bits, so
$H(X) + H(Y) \approx 1.811 > 1.5 = H(X,Y)$. The coupled pair is $0.311$
bits cheaper than its parts priced separately; the last two sections
account for the gap.

<KeyFact>
Joint entropy is not a new primitive: flatten the table into one
distribution over pairs and apply the entropy formula.
</KeyFact>

## Conditional entropy: the average leftover uncertainty

A forecaster who sees the sky needs a number for the uncertainty that
remains about the ground. That number must average over what the sky can
show.

### Each row, renormalized, is a conditional distribution

Dividing a row of the joint table by its row sum $p(x)$ gives the
*conditional distribution* $p(y \mid x)$. Here:

- Given clear, the row $(\tfrac{1}{2}, 0)$ renormalizes to $(1, 0)$:
  certainty. Leftover uncertainty $H(Y \mid X{=}\text{clear}) = 0$ bits.
- Given overcast, the row $(\tfrac{1}{4}, \tfrac{1}{4})$ renormalizes to
  $(\tfrac{1}{2}, \tfrac{1}{2})$: a fair coin.
  $H(Y \mid X{=}\text{overcast}) = 1$ bit.

Each is an ordinary entropy of a two-outcome distribution, the binary
entropy function of <Ref lesson="entropy-as-average-surprise"/>. Two
numbers result, one per value of $X$.

### One number: weight the rows by how often they occur

The single summary weights each row's entropy by the probability of that
row:
$$
H(Y \mid X) = \tfrac{1}{2} \cdot 0 + \tfrac{1}{2} \cdot 1 = 0.5
\text{ bits}.
$$
Half of all days the sky settles the question completely; the other half
it leaves a fair coin.

<Definition title="conditional entropy">
The **conditional entropy** of $Y$ given $X$ is
$$
H(Y \mid X) = \sum_{x} p(x)\, H(Y \mid X{=}x)
            = -\sum_{x,y} p(x,y) \log_2 p(y \mid x),
$$
measured in bits. Values $x$ with $p(x) = 0$ are skipped: $p(y \mid x)$ is
undefined there, and such rows carry weight zero.
</Definition>

### The two forms are the same sum

Expanding the first form shows the equality: substitute
$H(Y \mid X{=}x) = -\sum_y p(y \mid x) \log_2 p(y \mid x)$ and use
$p(x)\, p(y \mid x) = p(x,y)$ to merge the weights into the joint
probabilities. The second form needs no renormalization step, which makes
it the faster route on a given table.

<Example title="the joint-form computation">
Recompute $H(Y \mid X)$ for the weather table from
$-\sum_{x,y} p(x,y) \log_2 p(y \mid x)$, one nonzero cell at a time.

$(\text{clear}, \text{dry})$: $p(y \mid x) = 1$, term
$-\tfrac{1}{2} \log_2 1 = 0$.
$(\text{overcast}, \text{dry})$: $p(y \mid x) = \tfrac{1}{2}$, term
$-\tfrac{1}{4} \log_2 \tfrac{1}{2} = \tfrac{1}{4}$.
$(\text{overcast}, \text{wet})$: likewise $\tfrac{1}{4}$.

Sum: $0 + 0.25 + 0.25 = 0.5$ bits, matching the weighted-average form.
</Example>

<Remark title="an average, not a pointwise guarantee">
$H(Y \mid X)$ is one number, averaged over $x$; it is not
$H(Y \mid X{=}x)$ for any particular $x$. The distinction matters because
a single observation can raise uncertainty: here
$H(Y) \approx 0.811$ bits, yet seeing an overcast sky leaves
$H(Y \mid X{=}\text{overcast}) = 1$ bit — more uncertainty than before
looking. The inequality proved two sections below bounds only the
average.
</Remark>

<KeyFact>
$H(Y \mid X)$ is the $p(x)$-weighted average of the row entropies: one
number, $0.5$ bits here, summarizing every possible observation of $X$.
</KeyFact>

## The chain rule: one total, two splits

The three computed numbers satisfy an exact identity:
$$
H(X) + H(Y \mid X) = 1 + 0.5 = 1.5 = H(X,Y).
$$
Total uncertainty about the day equals uncertainty about the sky plus the
average uncertainty about the ground once the sky is known: **learn $X$,
then learn $Y$ given $X$**.

### The other order: learn Y first

The split works equally in the other order, with the columns playing the
rows' part. Given dry (probability $\tfrac{3}{4}$), the column
renormalizes to $p(x \mid \text{dry}) = (\tfrac{2}{3}, \tfrac{1}{3})$,
with entropy $\log_2 3 - \tfrac{2}{3} \approx 0.918$ bits; given wet, the
column is $(0, 1)$, entropy $0$. So
$$
H(X \mid Y) = \tfrac{3}{4} \cdot 0.918 + \tfrac{1}{4} \cdot 0
\approx 0.689 \text{ bits},
\qquad
H(Y) + H(X \mid Y) \approx 0.811 + 0.689 = 1.5.
$$

<Figure
  src="./figures/chain-bars.svg"
  alt="A 1.5-bit bar for H(X,Y) split two ways: as H(X) = 1 plus H(Y|X) = 0.5, and as H(Y) = 0.811 plus H(X|Y) = 0.689"
  caption="The chain rule: the same 1.5-bit total splits as H(X) + H(Y|X) or as H(Y) + H(X|Y)."
/>

<Theorem title="chain rule">
For discrete random variables $X$ and $Y$,
$$
H(X,Y) = H(X) + H(Y \mid X) = H(Y) + H(X \mid Y).
$$

<Proof>
For every cell with $p(x,y) > 0$, the product rule
$p(x,y) = p(x)\, p(y \mid x)$ gives, after taking $-\log_2$,
$$
-\log_2 p(x,y) = -\log_2 p(x) - \log_2 p(y \mid x).
$$
Average both sides with weights $p(x,y)$. The left side is $H(X,Y)$. The
first term on the right is
$\sum_{x,y} p(x,y) \bigl(-\log_2 p(x)\bigr)
= \sum_x p(x) \bigl(-\log_2 p(x)\bigr) = H(X)$, since
$\sum_y p(x,y) = p(x)$. The second term is the joint form of
$H(Y \mid X)$. Exchanging the roles of $X$ and $Y$ gives the second
equality. This is [Cover & Thomas](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/book/10.1002/047174882X),
Theorem 2.2.1.
</Proof>
</Theorem>

<Remark title="conditioning is asymmetric">
$H(Y \mid X) = 0.5$ while $H(X \mid Y) \approx 0.689$: the two
conditional entropies differ, and only the total $H(X,Y)$ is symmetric.
Subtracting the two forms of the chain rule fixes the difference exactly:
$H(X \mid Y) - H(Y \mid X) = H(X) - H(Y)$, here
$1 - 0.811 = 0.189$ bits.
</Remark>

### The area picture: uncertainties tile an interval

The chain rule makes the five quantities fit one additive diagram, the
bar form of [MacKay's](https://www.inference.org.uk/itprnn/book.pdf)
figure 8.1. $H(X)$ and $H(Y)$ overlap inside the total $H(X,Y)$; the
crescent of $H(X)$ outside the overlap is $H(X \mid Y)$, the crescent of
$H(Y)$ outside it is $H(Y \mid X)$.

<Figure
  src="./figures/area-bars.svg"
  alt="Aligned bars on a common 1.5-bit axis: H(X,Y) spans the whole interval, H(X) covers the left 1.0 bit, H(Y) the right 0.811 bits; their 0.311-bit overlap is shaded, with the crescents H(X|Y) = 0.689 and H(Y|X) = 0.5 outside it"
  caption="The additive diagram: H(X) and H(Y) tile H(X,Y) with a 0.311-bit overlap; the crescents are the conditional entropies."
/>

The overlap has width
$H(X) + H(Y) - H(X,Y) \approx 0.311$ bits: uncertainty counted in both
marginals but only once in the total. It is the same $0.311$-bit gap
observed after the joint-entropy definition, still unnamed here.

<KeyFact>
One total, two splits: $H(X,Y)$ decomposes as $H(X) + H(Y \mid X)$ and as
$H(Y) + H(X \mid Y)$, and the conditionals differ by exactly
$H(X) - H(Y)$.
</KeyFact>

## Conditioning reduces entropy on average

In the weather table, $H(Y \mid X) = 0.5 < 0.811 \approx H(Y)$: on
average, seeing the sky removed uncertainty about the ground. This is a
theorem, not a feature of the example.

<Theorem title="conditioning reduces entropy">
For discrete random variables $X$ and $Y$,
$$
H(Y \mid X) \le H(Y),
$$
with equality if and only if $X$ and $Y$ are independent.

<Proof>
Sketch; [Jensen's inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen%27s_inequality)
for the concave $\log_2$ is used without proof, as in the range theorem of
<Ref lesson="entropy-as-average-surprise"/>. Sum over cells with
$p(x,y) > 0$:
$$
H(Y \mid X) - H(Y)
= \sum_{x,y} p(x,y) \log_2 \frac{p(y)}{p(y \mid x)}
\le \log_2 \sum_{x,y} p(x,y)\, \frac{p(y)}{p(y \mid x)}
= \log_2 \sum_{x,y} p(x)\, p(y) \le \log_2 1 = 0,
$$
using $p(x,y) = p(x) p(y \mid x)$ in the middle step and, in the last,
that $\sum p(x) p(y)$ over a subset of pairs is at most 1. Strict
concavity makes the Jensen step an equality only when the ratio
$p(y)/p(y \mid x)$ is constant on the support; combined with the last
inequality being tight, the constant is 1, i.e.
$p(y \mid x) = p(y)$ wherever $p(x) > 0$: independence. This is
[Cover & Thomas](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/book/10.1002/047174882X),
Theorem 2.6.5.
</Proof>
</Theorem>

### The two endpoints: independence and determinism

**Independence** is the equality case. The product table with the same
marginals as the weather table has cells
$p(x,y) = p(x)\,p(y)$: rows $(\tfrac{3}{8}, \tfrac{1}{8})$ and
$(\tfrac{3}{8}, \tfrac{1}{8})$. Every row renormalizes to
$(\tfrac{3}{4}, \tfrac{1}{4})$, each row entropy is $0.811$ bits, and the
average is $H(Y \mid X) = H(Y)$. The chain rule then reads
$H(X,Y) = H(X) + H(Y)$: joint entropy is additive exactly for independent
variables, the distribution-level echo of the additivity of surprisal in
<Ref lesson="what-is-surprise"/>.

**Determinism** is the other endpoint. If $Y$ is a function of $X$, every
row of the table is certain, each row entropy is $0$, and
$H(Y \mid X) = 0$; the chain rule collapses to $H(X,Y) = H(X)$. The
reverse conditional need not vanish: in the weather table the wet column
already determines the sky, yet $H(X \mid Y) \approx 0.689 > 0$, and
Exercise 6 builds a deterministic $Y = f(X)$ with
$H(X \mid Y) = \tfrac{5}{3} \approx 1.667$ bits.

<KeyFact>
Observation never hurts on average: $H(Y \mid X) \le H(Y)$, with equality
exactly under independence. A particular observation can still raise
uncertainty; only the average cannot.
</KeyFact>

## The drop H(Y) − H(Y|X): what conditioning bought

Across the three tables of the sequence, the drop $H(Y) - H(Y \mid X)$ is
$0$, $0.311$, and $0.811$ bits: the uncertainty about rain that seeing
the sky removes. By the chain rule the drop equals
$H(X) + H(Y) - H(X,Y)$, the overlap width in the area diagram, so both
routes measure one quantity. <Ref lesson="mutual-information"/> defines
mutual information as exactly this drop and develops its properties.

<Exercise>
For the joint table $p(a,c) = \tfrac{1}{2}$, $p(a,d) = \tfrac{1}{4}$,
$p(b,c) = \tfrac{1}{8}$, $p(b,d) = \tfrac{1}{8}$ (rows $X \in \{a,b\}$,
columns $Y \in \{c,d\}$): compute the marginals, $H(X,Y)$, $H(X)$, and
$H(Y \mid X)$ by the weighted-average form, and verify the chain rule.
Use $\log_2 3 \approx 1.585$.

<Solution>
**Marginals.** Row sums: $p(a) = \tfrac{3}{4}$, $p(b) = \tfrac{1}{4}$.
Column sums: $p(c) = \tfrac{5}{8}$, $p(d) = \tfrac{3}{8}$.

**Joint entropy.** The flattened table is
$\{\tfrac{1}{2}, \tfrac{1}{4}, \tfrac{1}{8}, \tfrac{1}{8}\}$ — the
four-symbol source of <Ref lesson="entropy-as-average-surprise"/>, so
$H(X,Y) = \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot 1 + \tfrac{1}{4}\cdot 2 +
\tfrac{1}{8}\cdot 3 + \tfrac{1}{8}\cdot 3 = 1.75$ bits.

**Marginal entropy.** $H(X) = H(\tfrac{1}{4}) =
-\tfrac{3}{4}\log_2\tfrac{3}{4} - \tfrac{1}{4}\log_2\tfrac{1}{4}
= \tfrac{3}{4}(2 - 1.585) + \tfrac{1}{4}\cdot 2 \approx 0.811$ bits.

**Conditional entropy.** Row $a$ renormalizes to
$(\tfrac{2}{3}, \tfrac{1}{3})$:
$H(Y \mid X{=}a) = \log_2 3 - \tfrac{2}{3} \approx 0.918$ bits. Row $b$
renormalizes to $(\tfrac{1}{2}, \tfrac{1}{2})$:
$H(Y \mid X{=}b) = 1$ bit. Average:
$H(Y \mid X) = \tfrac{3}{4}\cdot 0.918 + \tfrac{1}{4}\cdot 1
\approx 0.689 + 0.25 = 0.939$ bits.

**Chain rule.** $H(X) + H(Y \mid X) \approx 0.811 + 0.939 = 1.75
= H(X,Y)$.
</Solution>
</Exercise>

<Exercise>
For the weather table of the lesson, compute $H(X \mid Y)$ directly from
the column conditionals, verify the second form of the chain rule,
$H(Y) + H(X \mid Y) = H(X,Y)$, and check the difference identity
$H(X \mid Y) - H(Y \mid X) = H(X) - H(Y)$.

<Solution>
**Columns.** Given dry ($p = \tfrac{3}{4}$):
$p(x \mid \text{dry}) = (\tfrac{2}{3}, \tfrac{1}{3})$, entropy

**Average.** $H(X \mid Y) = \tfrac{3}{4}\cdot 0.918 +
\tfrac{1}{4}\cdot 0 \approx 0.689$ bits.

**Chain rule.** $H(Y) + H(X \mid Y) \approx 0.811 + 0.689 = 1.5
= H(X,Y)$, matching $H(X) + H(Y \mid X) = 1 + 0.5$.

**Difference.** $H(X \mid Y) - H(Y \mid X) \approx 0.689 - 0.5 = 0.189$
and $H(X) - H(Y) \approx 1 - 0.811 = 0.189$: equal, as subtracting the
two chain-rule forms requires.
</Solution>
</Exercise>

<Exercise>
In the weather table, identify a value $x$ with
$H(Y \mid X{=}x) > H(Y)$, and state why this does not contradict the
theorem $H(Y \mid X) \le H(Y)$.

<Solution>
$x = \text{overcast}$: the conditional distribution is
$(\tfrac{1}{2}, \tfrac{1}{2})$, so $H(Y \mid X{=}\text{overcast}) = 1$
bit, exceeding $H(Y) \approx 0.811$ bits. Seeing an overcast sky moves
the rain distribution from $(\tfrac{3}{4}, \tfrac{1}{4})$ to a fair coin
— closer to uniform, hence more uncertain. No contradiction: the theorem
bounds the average over $x$, and the average
$\tfrac{1}{2}\cdot 0 + \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot 1 = 0.5$ bits stays below
$0.811$, because the overcast row's excess is outweighed by the clear
row's certainty.
</Solution>
</Exercise>

<Exercise>
Build the independent table with marginals
$p(\text{clear}) = \tfrac{1}{2}$ and $p(\text{dry}) = \tfrac{3}{4}$, then
verify $H(Y \mid X) = H(Y)$ and $H(X,Y) = H(X) + H(Y)$, the latter by
computing $H(X,Y)$ directly from the cells. Use
$\log_2 3 \approx 1.585$, so $\log_2 \tfrac{8}{3} \approx 1.415$.

<Solution>
**Cells.** $p(x,y) = p(x)p(y)$:
$p(\text{clear},\text{dry}) = p(\text{overcast},\text{dry}) =
\tfrac{1}{2}\cdot\tfrac{3}{4} = \tfrac{3}{8}$;
$p(\text{clear},\text{wet}) = p(\text{overcast},\text{wet}) =
\tfrac{1}{8}$.

**Conditional entropy.** Both rows renormalize to
$(\tfrac{3}{4}, \tfrac{1}{4})$, each with entropy
$H(\tfrac{1}{4}) \approx 0.811$ bits; the weighted average of two equal
values is that value, so $H(Y \mid X) = 0.811 = H(Y)$ bits.

**Joint entropy directly.**
$H(X,Y) = -2\cdot\tfrac{3}{8}\log_2\tfrac{3}{8}
- 2\cdot\tfrac{1}{8}\log_2\tfrac{1}{8}
\approx 2\cdot\tfrac{3}{8}\cdot 1.415 + 2\cdot\tfrac{1}{8}\cdot 3
= 1.061 + 0.75 = 1.811$ bits.

**Additivity.** $H(X) + H(Y) \approx 1 + 0.811 = 1.811 = H(X,Y)$: the
chain rule with $H(Y \mid X) = H(Y)$.
</Solution>
</Exercise>

<Exercise>
Roll a fair die $X$ and set $Y = 1$ if $X \ge 5$, else $Y = 0$. Compute

<Solution>
$H(X) = \log_2 6 \approx 2.585$ bits: a uniform six-outcome draw, per the

$Y$ is a function of $X$: every row of the joint table is certain, so
$H(Y \mid X) = 0$, and the chain rule gives
$H(X,Y) = H(X) + 0 \approx 2.585$ bits.

By the other order, $H(X \mid Y) = H(X,Y) - H(Y) \approx 2.585 - 0.918
= 1.667$ bits. Directly: given $Y = 1$ (probability $\tfrac{1}{3}$), $X$
is uniform on $\{5, 6\}$, entropy $1$ bit; given $Y = 0$, uniform on
$\{1,2,3,4\}$, entropy $2$ bits; average
$\tfrac{1}{3}\cdot 1 + \tfrac{2}{3}\cdot 2 = \tfrac{5}{3} \approx 1.667$
bits. Determinism is one-sided: $H(Y \mid X) = 0$ while
$H(X \mid Y) \approx 1.667 > 0$.
</Solution>
</Exercise>

<Exercise>
<Ref lesson="what-is-surprise"/> established the per-event decomposition
$I(A \cap B) = I(A) + I(B \mid A)$ for surprisal. Derive the chain rule
$H(X,Y) = H(X) + H(Y \mid X)$ from it by averaging.

<Solution>
Apply the decomposition to the events $A = \{X{=}x\}$ and
$B = \{Y{=}y\}$ for a cell with $p(x,y) > 0$:
$$
-\log_2 p(x,y) = -\log_2 p(x) - \log_2 p(y \mid x).
$$
Take the expectation of both sides under the joint distribution, i.e.
multiply each cell's equation by $p(x,y)$ and sum. The left side becomes
$H(X,Y)$ by the definition of joint entropy. The first right-hand term
becomes $\sum_{x,y} p(x,y)(-\log_2 p(x)) = \sum_x p(x)(-\log_2 p(x))
= H(X)$, since the inner sum over $y$ collapses to the marginal. The
second becomes $-\sum_{x,y} p(x,y)\log_2 p(y \mid x) = H(Y \mid X)$, the
joint form of the definition. Hence $H(X,Y) = H(X) + H(Y \mid X)$: the
chain rule is the surprisal decomposition, holding cell by cell, taken in
expectation.
</Solution>
</Exercise>

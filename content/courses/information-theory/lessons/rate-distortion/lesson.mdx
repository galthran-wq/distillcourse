Lossy compression trades bits for fidelity, and the rate-distortion function
R(D) = min I(X;X-hat) subject to expected distortion at most D is the fewest
bits per symbol that buy reconstruction within tolerance D. This lesson
prices two crude schemes on a fair-bit stream, defines distortion measures
and the rate-distortion function, pins down the function's endpoints and
shape, computes it exactly for a Bernoulli source under Hamming distortion,
states the Gaussian squared-error result, and closes where the theory runs
in practice today: neural codecs trained to minimize $R + \lambda D$.

## Two crude schemes: lossy coding priced on a fair-bit stream

A source emits fair, independent bits. The lossless price is settled:
$H(X) = 1$ bit per symbol, the floor of
<Ref lesson="source-coding-theorem-lite"/>, met here by copying the stream.
Now fix a budget of half a bit per symbol. Perfect reconstruction is
impossible below the floor; the open quantity is how much error the budget
forces.

Two schemes bracket the problem:

- **Send nothing** (rate 0). The decoder outputs all zeros. Each source bit
  disagrees with its reconstruction with probability $\tfrac12$: half the
  stream is wrong.
- **Send every other bit** (rate $\tfrac12$). Odd positions arrive exactly;
  even positions are guessed, each wrong with probability $\tfrac12$.
  Average error fraction:
  $\tfrac12 \cdot 0 + \tfrac12 \cdot \tfrac12 = \tfrac14$.

Rate one-half buys a 25% error fraction this way. The theory of this lesson
puts the true optimum at rate one-half near **11% errors**, and section 4
computes it; the schemes that approach it code long blocks jointly instead
of deciding bit by bit.

### Distortion: the error metric is part of the problem statement

Counting "wrong bits" silently chose an error metric. The general theory
takes that choice as an explicit input.

<Definition title="distortion measure">
A **distortion measure** on a source alphabet $\mathcal{X}$ and a
reconstruction alphabet $\hat{\mathcal{X}}$ is a function
$d : \mathcal{X} \times \hat{\mathcal{X}} \to [0, \infty)$. For blocks,
distortion is averaged per symbol:
$d(x^n, \hat x^n) = \tfrac1n \sum_{i=1}^n d(x_i, \hat x_i)$. The two
standard instances:

- **Hamming distortion** for discrete alphabets:
  $d(x, \hat x) = 1$ if $x \ne \hat x$, else $0$. Its average is the
  fraction of misreconstructed symbols.
- **Squared error** for real alphabets: $d(x, \hat x) = (x - \hat x)^2$.
  Its average is the mean squared error of the reconstruction.
</Definition>

Both crude schemes above are points in a plane: coordinates
$(D, R)$, distortion paid against rate spent. Send-nothing sits at
$(\tfrac12, 0)$, send-every-other-bit at $(\tfrac14, \tfrac12)$. The
subject of this lesson is the exact boundary between the $(D, R)$ pairs
some code achieves and the pairs none does.

<KeyFact>
Lossy compression is a two-number problem: every scheme is a point
$(D, R)$, and the rate-distortion function is the exact lower boundary of
the achievable region.
</KeyFact>

## The rate-distortion function: the least information compatible with the budget

### Reconstruction is a channel from the source

Whatever machinery sits between source and reconstruction, encoder, bit
stream, decoder, its end-to-end effect on single symbols is a conditional
distribution $p(\hat x \mid x)$: for each source symbol, the probabilities
of the reconstructions it may become. This conditional distribution is
called the *test channel*. The direction matters: $\hat X$ is **computed
from** $X$, so $X \to \hat X$ is a processing step, not a generative model
of the source, and the data-processing inequality of
<Ref lesson="data-processing-inequality"/> applies to everything
downstream of $X$.

The rate this channel needs is measured by the mutual information
$I(X; \hat X)$ of <Ref lesson="mutual-information"/>: the compressed file
is a bottleneck between $X$ and $\hat X$, and a file of $R$ bits per
symbol cannot carry more than $R$ bits of information about the source
into the reconstruction (the converse below makes this exact). Minimizing
rate therefore means choosing the test channel that satisfies the
distortion budget with the **least** mutual information.

<Definition title="rate-distortion function">
For a source $X \sim p(x)$ and a distortion measure $d$, the
**rate-distortion function** is
$$
R(D) \;=\; \min_{p(\hat x \mid x)\,:\; \mathbb{E}[d(X, \hat X)] \le D}\; I(X; \hat X),
$$
the minimum taken over all test channels whose expected distortion,
$\mathbb{E}[d] = \sum_{x, \hat x} p(x)\, p(\hat x \mid x)\, d(x, \hat x)$,
stays within the budget $D$.
</Definition>

### The theorem: the minimum is achievable and tight

The definition is a formula about single-symbol distributions. Shannon's
theorem, [1948](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~ctm/home/text/others/shannon/entropy/entropy.pdf)
(Part V, Theorem 21) with the discrete case completed in his 1959 paper,
gives it operational force.

<Theorem title="rate-distortion theorem">
For an i.i.d. source $X \sim p(x)$ and a bounded distortion measure $d$,
$R(D)$ is the boundary of achievability: for every $\varepsilon > 0$
there exist block codes of rate $R(D) + \varepsilon$ whose expected
distortion is at most $D + \varepsilon$ for all sufficiently large block
length, and no sequence of codes with rate below $R(D)$ meets the budget
$D$.

<Proof>
Sketch; the full argument is Cover & Thomas §10.4 (converse) and §10.5
(achievability).

**Converse.** A rate-$R$ code compresses $X^n$ into an index
$W \in \{1, \dots, 2^{nR}\}$ and reconstructs $\hat X^n$ from $W$ alone,
so $X^n \to W \to \hat X^n$ is a Markov chain. The data-processing
inequality (<Ref lesson="data-processing-inequality"/>) and the entropy
bound on $W$ give
$I(X^n; \hat X^n) \le I(X^n; W) \le H(W) \le nR$. For an i.i.d. source
the block information single-letterizes,
$I(X^n; \hat X^n) \ge \sum_{i=1}^n I(X_i; \hat X_i)$, each term is at
least $R(\mathbb{E}[d(X_i, \hat X_i)])$ by the definition of $R$ as a
minimum, and convexity of $R(D)$ (next section) pulls the average
through: $nR \ge n\,R\bigl(\mathbb{E}[d(X^n, \hat X^n)]\bigr)$. A code
meeting budget $D$ therefore spends at least $R(D)$.

**Achievability.** Fix the minimizing test channel and draw a codebook of
$2^{n(R(D) + \varepsilon)}$ reconstruction words i.i.d. from the induced
marginal $p(\hat x)$. For a typical source block, with high probability
at least one codeword is jointly typical with it, and joint typicality
forces per-symbol distortion near $\mathbb{E}[d] \le D$. Encoding
transmits the index of that codeword. The calculation that
$2^{nI(X;\hat X)}$ codewords suffice is the covering counterpart of the
random-coding argument sketched for
<Ref lesson="noisy-channel-coding"/>.
</Proof>
</Theorem>

The theorem is a statement about limits, not about any particular
quantizer: $R(D)$ is approached by coding long blocks jointly, and every
practical one-shot scheme sits strictly above the curve, as the
send-every-other-bit point did.

<KeyFact>
$R(D) = \min I(X; \hat X)$ subject to $\mathbb{E}[d] \le D$ is the exact
price of fidelity: achievable in the block-length limit, unbeatable by
any code.
</KeyFact>

### Capacity's mirror image: the same quantity, the opposite extreme

<Ref lesson="channel-capacity"/> defined
$C = \max_{p(x)} I(X;Y)$: the channel is fixed and the input distribution
is tuned to push the **most** information through. Rate-distortion flips
every role: the source $p(x)$ is fixed and the channel
$p(\hat x \mid x)$ is tuned to let the **least** information through
while staying within the distortion budget. Communication maximizes
mutual information; compression minimizes it. The two problems are dual,
and one algorithm, Blahut–Arimoto, alternates the same two updates to
compute either function numerically
([Blahut 1972](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.1972.1054855); treatment in
[Polyanskiy & Wu](https://people.lids.mit.edu/yp/homepage/data/itlectures_v5.pdf)).

## Endpoints and shape: from H(X) to zero, convex in between

Three structural facts hold for every source, before any specific curve
is computed.

<Theorem title="shape of R(D)">
For Hamming distortion (and any $d$ with $d(x, \hat x) = 0$ exactly when
$\hat x = x$, on a common alphabet):

1. $R(0) = H(X)$: the zero-tolerance corner is lossless source coding.
2. $R(D)$ is non-increasing in $D$, and $R(D) = 0$ exactly when
   $D \ge D_{\max} = \min_{\hat x} \mathbb{E}[d(X, \hat x)]$, the
   distortion of the best constant guess.
3. $R(D)$ is convex on $[0, D_{\max}]$.

<Proof>
**(1)** Budget $D = 0$ forces $P(X \ne \hat X) = 0$, so
$I(X; \hat X) = I(X; X) = H(X)$
(<Ref lesson="mutual-information"/>, self-information).

**(2)** A larger $D$ only enlarges the set of admissible test channels,
so the minimum cannot rise. If $D \ge D_{\max}$, the constant channel
$\hat X \equiv \arg\min_{\hat x} \mathbb{E}[d(X, \hat x)]$ meets the
budget with $I = 0$. If $D < D_{\max}$, zero rate is out of reach:
$I(X; \hat X) = 0$ makes $\hat X$ independent of $X$, and then
$\mathbb{E}[d] = \sum_{\hat x} p(\hat x)\, \mathbb{E}[d(X, \hat x)] \ge
D_{\max} > D$, violating the budget.

**(3)** Let channels $q_1, q_2$ attain $R(D_1), R(D_2)$. The blended
channel $q_\lambda = \lambda q_1 + (1 - \lambda) q_2$ has expected
distortion exactly $\lambda D_1 + (1-\lambda) D_2$, since
$\mathbb{E}[d]$ is linear in the channel, and mutual information is
convex in $p(\hat x \mid x)$ for fixed $p(x)$ (Cover & Thomas Theorem
2.7.4), so
$I_{q_\lambda} \le \lambda R(D_1) + (1 - \lambda) R(D_2)$. The minimum
at the blended budget is no larger. This is Lemma 10.4.1 of Cover &
Thomas; exercise 5 reaches the same conclusion operationally, by
time-sharing two codes.
</Proof>
</Theorem>

The curve therefore always runs from the lossless corner
$(0, H(X))$ down to $(D_{\max}, 0)$, decreasing and convex, exactly the
shape shaded in the first figure. The next two sections fill in the only
two cases with standard closed forms.

<KeyFact>
$R(D)$ interpolates between the two prices already known: the source
coding theorem at $D = 0$ and free majority-guessing at
$D = D_{\max}$, convexly in between.
</KeyFact>

## Bernoulli(p) under Hamming distortion: R(D) = H(p) − H(D)

### The closed form

For a biased-bit source the minimization has an exact solution, one of
the two classical closed forms
([Cover & Thomas](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/book/10.1002/047174882X)
§10.3.1).

<Theorem title="Bernoulli rate-distortion function">
For $X \sim \text{Bernoulli}(p)$ under Hamming distortion,
$$
R(D) =
\begin{cases}
H(p) - H(D), & 0 \le D \le \min(p,\, 1-p), \\[2pt]
0, & D \ge \min(p,\, 1-p),
\end{cases}
$$
where $H(\cdot)$ is the binary entropy function of
<Ref lesson="entropy-as-average-surprise"/>.

<Proof>
Take $p \le \tfrac12$ (relabel otherwise), so $\min(p, 1-p) = p$ and
$D \le p \le \tfrac12$.

**Converse: no admissible channel beats $H(p) - H(D)$.** Let
$p(\hat x \mid x)$ satisfy $P(X \ne \hat X) = \mathbb{E}[d] \le D$.
Write the error indicator $Z = X \oplus \hat X$ (XOR). Given $\hat X$,
knowing $Z$ is knowing $X$, so
$H(X \mid \hat X) = H(Z \mid \hat X) \le H(Z)$: conditioning cannot
raise entropy
(<Ref lesson="joint-and-conditional-entropy"/>). $Z$ is a binary
variable with $P(Z = 1) \le D \le \tfrac12$, and the binary entropy
function increases on $[0, \tfrac12]$, so $H(Z) \le H(D)$. Chaining:
$$
I(X; \hat X) = H(X) - H(X \mid \hat X)
\;\ge\; H(p) - H(Z) \;\ge\; H(p) - H(D).
$$

**Achievability: one channel meets the bound.** Build the joint
distribution backwards. Let $\hat X \sim \text{Bernoulli}(\pi)$ with
$\pi = \tfrac{p - D}{1 - 2D}$, and let $X = \hat X \oplus Z$ with
$Z \sim \text{Bernoulli}(D)$ independent of $\hat X$: the
reconstruction passes through a binary symmetric channel with crossover
$D$ to produce the source. The marginal checks out:
$P(X = 1) = \pi(1 - D) + (1 - \pi) D = D + \pi(1 - 2D) = p$. The
distortion is $P(X \ne \hat X) = P(Z = 1) = D$, on budget. The rate:
$$
I(X; \hat X) = H(X) - H(X \mid \hat X) = H(p) - H(D),
$$
since given $\hat X$ the only remaining uncertainty in $X$ is the
independent flip $Z$. For $D \le p \le \tfrac12$ the parameter $\pi$
lies in $[0, 1]$, so the construction is a valid distribution, and the
converse bound is attained.
</Proof>
</Theorem>

The formula reads as an information ledger: $H(p)$ bits describe the
source exactly; a tolerated error pattern of rate $D$ carries $H(D)$
bits; the encoder must pay only the **difference**. The sequence below
assembles the curve for $p = 0.3$.

The flat tail is exact, not approximate. Once $D \ge \min(p, 1-p)$, the

that much error makes compression free.

### Worked: the fair source, and the opener's gap closed

<Example title="fair bits at rate one-half">
For $p = \tfrac12$: $R(D) = 1 - H(D)$ on $[0, \tfrac12]$.

**Rate one-half.** Solve $1 - H(D) = 0.5$: $H(D) = 0.5$, so
$D \approx 0.110$, since
$H(0.11) = 0.11 \cdot 3.184 + 0.89 \cdot 0.168 \approx 0.500$. The
optimum at the crude scheme's rate misreconstructs **11%** of bits, not
25%.

**The crude scheme's distortion, priced.** At $D = 0.25$ the curve gives
$R = 1 - H(0.25) = 1 - 0.811 = 0.189$ bits. The error fraction the naive
scheme bought with $0.5$ bits is available for $0.189$: the naive scheme
overspends by a factor of $2.6$.

**Bounds check.** $R(0) = 1 - H(0) = 1 = H(X)$, the lossless rate; and
$R(0.5) = 1 - H(0.5) = 0$, the free all-zeros guess.
</Example>

<Example title="a biased source">
For $p = 0.25$: $H(0.25) = 0.811$ bits, so $R(D) = 0.811 - H(D)$ on
$[0, 0.25]$.

At $D = 0.1$:
$R = 0.811 - 0.469 = 0.342$ bits per symbol, a $58\%$ saving over the
lossless $0.811$ in exchange for errors on one symbol in ten.

The curve ends at $D_{\max} = \min(0.25, 0.75) = 0.25$: always guessing
$0$ errs exactly on the $25\%$ of symbols that are $1$, at zero rate.
$R(0.25) = 0.811 - H(0.25) = 0$, consistent.
</Example>

<KeyFact>
$R(D) = H(p) - H(D)$ up to $D = \min(p, 1-p)$, then exactly zero:
tolerated errors refund their own entropy, and once the budget covers the
minority symbol, the majority guess compresses for free.
</KeyFact>

## The Gaussian source under squared error: R(D) = ½ log₂(σ²/D)

The second classical closed form is continuous. It is stated here, not
derived: it is this course's one use of *differential entropy* beyond the
remark in <Ref lesson="max-entropy-principle"/>, and the derivation
(Cover & Thomas §10.3.2) runs the Bernoulli converse's pattern with
differential entropies in place of discrete ones.

<Theorem title="Gaussian rate-distortion function">
For $X \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$ under squared-error distortion,
$$
R(D) =
\begin{cases}
\tfrac12 \log_2 \dfrac{\sigma^2}{D}, & 0 < D \le \sigma^2, \\[4pt]
0, & D \ge \sigma^2.
\end{cases}
$$
</Theorem>

Two flags on the statement. First, discrete intuitions do not transfer
silently: differential entropy can be negative and changes under a change
of coordinates, but $R(D)$ is a **difference** of differential entropies,
and the coordinate-dependent terms cancel, so the rate is well-defined.
Second, the endpoints behave like the discrete case at one end only:
$D_{\max} = \sigma^2$ is the squared error of the best constant guess
$\hat X = \mathbb{E}[X] = 0$, but as $D \to 0$ the rate grows without
bound, because a continuous value needs infinitely many bits to
reconstruct exactly.

halving the root-mean-square error, a drop of $6.02$ dB. Audio engineers

$\sigma^2 = 1$: one bit per sample leaves $D = 0.25$, two bits leave
$0.0625$, eight bits leave $1.5 \times 10^{-5}$.

<Aside>
The closed form has a geometric reading, the covering picture of
Polyanskiy & Wu's lecture notes. A typical length-$n$ Gaussian block lies
near a sphere of radius $\sqrt{n \sigma^2}$; a reconstruction word covers,
within squared error $D$ per symbol, a ball of radius $\sqrt{nD}$. A
codebook meeting budget $D$ must cover the source sphere with such balls,
and volumes in $n$ dimensions scale as radius$^n$, so the codebook needs
on the order of
$\bigl(\sqrt{n\sigma^2} / \sqrt{nD}\bigr)^n = (\sigma^2 / D)^{n/2}$
words: exactly $2^{n \cdot \frac12 \log_2(\sigma^2 / D)}$, the theorem's
rate. Compression is sphere covering, where channel coding
(<Ref lesson="noisy-channel-coding"/>) was sphere packing.
</Aside>

<KeyFact>
$R(D) = \tfrac12 \log_2(\sigma^2 / D)$ for the Gaussian source: rate buys
error on a log scale, one bit per halving of RMS error, with no finite
rate reaching $D = 0$.
</KeyFact>

## The trade-off made trainable: neural codecs minimize R + λD

### The Lagrangian reading of the curve

Because $R(D)$ is convex and decreasing, its constrained definition has
an unconstrained twin: every point of the curve minimizes
$$
R + \lambda D
$$
for some multiplier $\lambda \ge 0$, namely the negative slope of the
curve at that point, and sweeping $\lambda$ from large to small walks the
solution from the low-distortion corner toward the zero-rate tail. This
is the same constraint-to-multiplier trade seen for
<Ref lesson="max-entropy-principle"/>: a hard budget becomes a price.

### The objective, implemented with networks

[Ballé, Laparra & Simoncelli (2017)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.01704)
train image codecs on exactly this objective. An encoder network maps the
image $x$ to a latent vector, which is **quantized** by rounding and
entropy-coded; a decoder network maps the quantized latent $\hat y$ back
to a reconstruction $\hat x$. The loss is
$$
\mathcal{L} \;=\;
\underbrace{\mathbb{E}\bigl[-\log_2 p(\hat y)\bigr]}_{\text{rate } R}
\;+\; \lambda\,
\underbrace{\mathbb{E}\bigl[d(x, \hat x)\bigr]}_{\text{distortion } D},
$$
where the rate term is the cross-entropy of the quantized latent under a
learned prior $p(\hat y)$, which by
<Ref lesson="compression-is-prediction"/> is the achievable size of the
entropy-coded bitstream. One trained model realizes one point of the
codec's rate-distortion curve; retraining across a grid of $\lambda$
values sweeps it, exactly as the multiplier sweeps the theoretical curve.

Rounding has zero gradient almost everywhere, so the objective as stated
cannot be trained directly. The paper's fix is a **uniform-noise
relaxation**: during training, rounding is replaced by adding noise drawn
uniformly from $(-\tfrac12, \tfrac12)$, which matches the marginal
statistics of quantization error, makes both terms differentiable, and
is dropped at test time in favor of true rounding plus arithmetic coding.

<Aside>
Ballé et al. §3.1 push the correspondence further: the uniform-noise
relaxation of $R + \lambda D$ coincides, term for term, with the negative
evidence lower bound of a variational autoencoder whose approximate
posterior is uniform, the rate term matching the KL term of the ELBO
(<Ref lesson="kl-divergence"/>) and the distortion term matching the
log-likelihood under a Gaussian observation model. Training a codec and
training a VAE at fixed $\lambda$ are the same optimization; generative
modeling and lossy compression meet at this objective.
</Aside>

One assumption has run silently through the whole lesson: someone chose
$d$. Hamming and squared error are declarations about which differences
matter, and a codec optimized against squared error will spend bits on
imperceptible pixel deviations while discarding structure a human
notices. When no hand-picked distortion measure is defensible, the
constraint itself can be replaced: keep not "low $d$" but "information
about a target variable", trading $I(X;T)$ against $I(T;Y)$. That
objective is the information bottleneck of
[Tishby, Pereira & Bialek](https://arxiv.org/abs/physics/0004057), and
<Ref lesson="information-bottleneck"/> develops it as rate-distortion
with the distortion measure derived rather than assumed.

<KeyFact>
$\min R + \lambda D$ is rate-distortion theory as a loss function:
neural codecs train it end to end, $\lambda$ picks the operating point,
and the one hand-picked ingredient left, the distortion measure, is what
the information bottleneck later removes.
</KeyFact>

## Exercises

<Exercise>
For the fair-bit source, compute $R(D)$ at $D = 0.02$, $D = 0.05$, and
$D = 0.25$, using
$H(0.02) \approx 0.141$ and $H(0.05) \approx 0.286$. State what fraction
of the lossless rate each tolerance saves, and where the savings grow
fastest.

<Solution>
$R(D) = 1 - H(D)$ for the fair source.

$R(0.02) = 1 - 0.141 = 0.859$ bits: a $14.1\%$ saving.
$R(0.05) = 1 - 0.286 = 0.714$ bits: a $28.6\%$ saving.
$R(0.25) = 1 - 0.811 = 0.189$ bits: an $81.1\%$ saving.

The saving at tolerance $D$ is exactly $H(D)$, and the binary entropy
function is steepest near $0$ (its slope $\log_2 \tfrac{1-D}{D}$ grows
without bound as $D \to 0$): the first percent of tolerated error buys
more rate than any later percent. Raising the tolerance from $2\%$ to
$5\%$ (hardly a visible change in fidelity) doubles the saving.
</Solution>
</Exercise>

<Exercise>
A sensor emits $X \sim \text{Bernoulli}(0.1)$. Compute $R(0)$,
$D_{\max}$, and $R(0.05)$, using $H(0.1) \approx 0.469$ and
$H(0.05) \approx 0.286$. Name the zero-rate scheme that attains
$D_{\max}$.

<Solution>
$R(0) = H(0.1) \approx 0.469$ bits: the lossless rate of the 90/10
source.

$D_{\max} = \min(0.1, 0.9) = 0.1$: the constant guess $\hat X \equiv 0$
(the majority symbol) errs exactly when $X = 1$, a $10\%$ error
fraction, at zero rate. Hence $R(D) = 0$ for $D \ge 0.1$.

$R(0.05) = H(0.1) - H(0.05) \approx 0.469 - 0.286 = 0.183$ bits.
Tolerating errors on one symbol in twenty cuts the rate to $39\%$ of
lossless; the whole curve lives below half a bit.
</Solution>
</Exercise>

<Exercise>
A Gaussian source has $\sigma^2 = 16$. Compute the rate for budgets
$D = 1$ and $D = 0.25$, the budget reachable at $8$ bits per sample, and
the number of additional bits needed to halve the RMS error from any
operating point.

<Solution>
$R(1) = \tfrac12 \log_2 16 = 2$ bits per sample.
$R(0.25) = \tfrac12 \log_2 64 = 3$ bits per sample.

At $8$ bits: $D = \sigma^2 \cdot 4^{-8} = 16 / 65536 \approx 2.4 \times
10^{-4}$.

Halving RMS error quarters the squared error, and
$R(D/4) - R(D) = \tfrac12 \log_2 4 = 1$: exactly **one extra bit**,
from any starting point, the 6 dB per bit rule. Consistency check
between the first two answers: $D$ fell by a factor $4$ and the rate
rose by one bit.
</Solution>
</Exercise>

<Exercise>
Verify the achievability construction for the fair source. Let
$\hat X \sim \text{Bernoulli}(\tfrac12)$, let
$Z \sim \text{Bernoulli}(D)$ independent of $\hat X$ with
$D \le \tfrac12$, and set $X = \hat X \oplus Z$. Show that $X$ is a fair
bit, that the expected Hamming distortion is $D$, and that
$I(X; \hat X) = 1 - H(D)$.

<Solution>
**Marginal.** $P(X = 1) = P(\hat X = 1)P(Z = 0) + P(\hat X = 0)P(Z = 1)
= \tfrac12(1 - D) + \tfrac12 D = \tfrac12$: fair, as required
($\pi = \tfrac{p - D}{1 - 2D} = \tfrac12$ at $p = \tfrac12$).

**Distortion.** $X \ne \hat X$ exactly when $Z = 1$:
$\mathbb{E}[d] = P(Z = 1) = D$, meeting the budget with equality.

**Rate.** $I(X; \hat X) = H(X) - H(X \mid \hat X)$. The marginal gives
$H(X) = 1$. Given $\hat X = \hat x$, the source is
$X = \hat x \oplus Z$ with $Z$ independent of $\hat X$, so the
conditional distribution of $X$ is a $D$-flip of a known value:
$H(X \mid \hat X = \hat x) = H(D)$ for both values, hence
$H(X \mid \hat X) = H(D)$ and $I(X; \hat X) = 1 - H(D) = R(D)$. The
channel achieves the curve, matching the converse bound.
</Solution>
</Exercise>

<Exercise>
Prove that the achievable region is convex by time-sharing, without any
information quantities: given a code achieving $(D_1, R_1)$ and a code
achieving $(D_2, R_2)$, construct, for each $\lambda \in [0, 1]$, a
scheme achieving $\bigl(\lambda D_1 + (1-\lambda) D_2,\; \lambda R_1 +
(1-\lambda) R_2\bigr)$. Conclude that $R(D)$, the region's lower
boundary, is convex.

<Solution>
Split the source stream into blocks and apply code 1 to a fraction
$\lambda$ of them and code 2 to the rest (a fixed, position-based split,
so the decoder knows which code to apply where; no signaling bits are
needed in the limit).

**Rate.** Bits are spent at $R_1$ on a $\lambda$ fraction of symbols and
$R_2$ on the rest: $\lambda R_1 + (1 - \lambda) R_2$ bits per symbol
overall.

**Distortion.** Per-symbol distortion averages the same way:
$\lambda D_1 + (1 - \lambda) D_2$.

Every convex combination of achievable points is therefore achievable.
A region closed under convex combinations has a convex lower boundary:
if $R(D)$ ever bulged above a chord, time-sharing the chord's endpoint
codes would achieve points below the function, contradicting the
converse. Hence $R\bigl(\lambda D_1 + (1-\lambda) D_2\bigr) \le
\lambda R(D_1) + (1 - \lambda) R(D_2)$.
</Solution>
</Exercise>

<Exercise>
The optimal fair-source test channel at $D = 0.25$ has the joint table
$P(X{=}0, \hat X{=}0) = P(X{=}1, \hat X{=}1) = \tfrac38$ and
$P(X{=}0, \hat X{=}1) = P(X{=}1, \hat X{=}0) = \tfrac18$. Using the
recipes of <Ref lesson="mutual-information"/> (marginals from row and
column sums, conditional entropy as an average of column entropies),
compute $\mathbb{E}[d]$ and $I(X; \hat X)$ from the table, and check the
result against the Bernoulli formula.

<Solution>
**Distortion.** The off-diagonal mass is
$\tfrac18 + \tfrac18 = \tfrac14$: $\mathbb{E}[d] = 0.25$, on budget.

**Marginals.** Row and column sums all equal $\tfrac12$:
$H(X) = H(\hat X) = 1$ bit.

**Conditional entropy.** Given $\hat X = 0$ (probability $\tfrac12$),
the column $(\tfrac38, \tfrac18)$ renormalizes to
$(\tfrac34, \tfrac14)$, entropy $H(0.25) \approx 0.811$ bits; the
$\hat X = 1$ column gives the mirror image, the same entropy. So
$H(X \mid \hat X) = 0.811$ bits.

**Mutual information.**
$I(X; \hat X) = H(X) - H(X \mid \hat X) = 1 - 0.811 = 0.189$ bits.

**Check.** $R(0.25) = 1 - H(0.25) = 0.189$ bits: the table attains the
minimum, so it is an optimal test channel. (It is the exercise-4
construction at $D = 0.25$, written as a joint table.)
</Solution>
</Exercise>

<Exercise>
Prove in general that $R(D) = 0$ for $D \ge D_{\max} =
\min_{\hat x} \mathbb{E}[d(X, \hat x)]$ and that $R(D) > 0$ for
$D < D_{\max}$, and evaluate $D_{\max}$ for (a) $X \sim
\text{Bernoulli}(p)$ under Hamming distortion and (b) $X \sim
\mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$ under squared error.

<Solution>
**Zero rate suffices for $D \ge D_{\max}$.** Let $\hat x^\ast$ attain
the minimum in $D_{\max}$. The constant channel
$\hat X \equiv \hat x^\ast$ has $I(X; \hat X) = 0$ (a constant is
independent of everything) and expected distortion $D_{\max} \le D$: an
admissible channel with zero information, so the minimum is $0$.

**Zero rate fails for $D < D_{\max}$.** $I(X; \hat X) = 0$ forces
independence (<Ref lesson="mutual-information"/>, nonnegativity's
equality case). Under independence,
$\mathbb{E}[d] = \sum_{\hat x} p(\hat x)\,\mathbb{E}[d(X, \hat x)]$, an
average of terms each at least $D_{\max}$, so
$\mathbb{E}[d] \ge D_{\max} > D$: no zero-information channel meets the
budget, hence $R(D) > 0$.

**(a)** The constant $\hat x$ errs with probability $p$ (choosing
$\hat x = 0$) or $1 - p$ (choosing $\hat x = 1$); the better choice is
the majority symbol: $D_{\max} = \min(p, 1-p)$.

**(b)** For a constant $c$,
$\mathbb{E}[(X - c)^2] = \sigma^2 + (\mu - c)^2$, minimized at
$c = \mu$: $D_{\max} = \sigma^2$, attained by always outputting the
mean.
</Solution>
</Exercise>

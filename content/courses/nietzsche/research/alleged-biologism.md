# Research brief: alleged-biologism (The Alleged-Biologism Objection to Naturalism)

status: planned
module: where-the-reading-breaks
type: core
goal: Formulate Heidegger's argument (Nietzsche Vol. III, Part One §6) that Nietzsche's biological vocabulary is not biology because no science can ground the concept of its own field, and evaluate what it would prove against methodological naturalism if sound.

## ARC (L10)

- **Inherits:** Heidegger's reading with its apparatus and evidence base; the naturalist hypothesis as stated in corpus-and-protocol (continuity with the sciences in method and results; the Doctrine of Types); will to power sorted into psychological / biological / cosmological (will-to-power-as-psychology); BGE 259's 'life is essentially exploitation'.
- **Contributes:** The one argument in the course that, if sound, makes the working hypothesis a category error: reconstructed from Vol. III §6 (the field-concept premise; 'we must learn to read'), with §§11–13's schematization (the blackboard) and Vol. IV §22's end-of-metaphysics as its context; Leiter's methodological naturalism restated at its strongest (continuity, not reduction; results-continuity as an empirical bet); Schacht's 'non-scientistic naturalism' and Leiter's NDPR reply as the same objection inside the analytic camp; the verdict on this argument alone: what it proves (no empirical proof of the cosmological doctrine — which the naturalist had already conceded) and what it does not (that the psychological doctrine is unscientific).
- **Hands off:** To naturalist-reading-verdict.

Spine step: "Where the reading breaks: Nehamas turns truth into character and a life into a text; Heidegger turns the psychology into metaphysics and argues that no science can ground its own field-concept; the audit states which theses each reading can hold as arguments and where the naturalist hypothesis stands."

## Key points the lesson must cover

1. **The naturalist hypothesis restated at its strongest.** Leiter's methodological naturalism (Ch. 1): Nietzsche's explanations are continuous with the sciences in method (causal, empirical, type-based) and in results (they draw on the physiology and psychology of his day — Lange's History of Materialism, the German materialists); it is not substantive naturalism (that only natural things exist) and not reductive materialism; results-continuity is an empirical bet, revisable. BGE 230's 'translate man back into nature' and BGE 23 as the textual charter. State it so that the objection meets it, not a strawman.
2. **Vol. III §6 reconstructed.** (P) Nietzsche's vocabulary — 'life', 'beast of prey', 'breeding', 'physiology' — sounds biological, and the 'biologistic' reading takes 'life is will to power' as a thesis of biology. (P) A science has a field only because it presupposes a concept of that field ('what is living?' for biology; 'what is nature?' for physics); no science establishes its own field-concept — it works within it. (P) 'Life is will to power' is a claim about what the living is as such — a field-concept claim. (C) It cannot be a biological thesis; it is metaphysical; reading it as biology is a category error. Corollary: the same for 'nature', 'force', 'power' — 'we must learn to read'. Then §16: Nietzsche's 'biological' interpretation of knowledge (knowing as a condition of life) is the same metaphysical thesis in epistemology. Reconstruct exactly; the reader has the premise/conclusion apparatus to see it is valid.
3. **The context that gives the argument its scope.** §§11–13: knowing as schematizing a chaos according to practical need (the blackboard: entering a room and seeing Greek letters on the board is already schematized 'chaos'); perspectives and horizons commanded, not found; the law of contradiction as command (§18). Vol. IV §22: the end of metaphysics — Nietzsche's animalitas as the completion of subjectivity, with the 'blond beast' as its 'password and countersign': the phrase read as a metaphysical symbol, not a racial one, and not a biological one either. State that Heidegger's reading of 'biology' is a reading of the whole naturalist register as metaphysics in disguise.
4. **What the argument proves.** Grant the field-concept premise (it is defensible: biology does not derive 'life' from its results). Then: (a) the cosmological doctrine ('the world is will to power') cannot be an empirical result — which the naturalist reading already conceded (BGE 36 begs the question; Clark); (b) the biological doctrine (BGE 13, 259: 'life is will to power') is not a result of biology but a claim about what biology presupposes. The naturalist must concede (b) too, or read BGE 13 as an empirical generalization about organisms (Kaufmann's inductive route — Clark Ch. 7 §4), which Twilight's 'Anti-Darwin' §14 makes hard (Nietzsche argues against Darwin on a priori-sounding grounds: 'the weak win because they are more numerous and cleverer').
5. **What the argument does not prove.** The psychological doctrine (Reginster's three conditions; BGE 19's command among drives) is not a field-concept claim; it is a claim about human motivation testable against cases (the priest; the philosopher; the last human being), continuous with psychology's methods. The Doctrine of Types is a claim about the causal role of constitution, not about 'what life is'. The Genealogy's mechanisms (I:10, II:16, III:15) use the psychological doctrine only (will-to-power-as-psychology's 'line'). So the objection, sound, removes from the naturalist reading exactly what it had already set aside, and leaves the critique of morality standing as naturalism. Reconstruct this as an argument, since it is the course's own contribution.
6. **The same objection inside the analytic camp.** Schacht (J&R Ch. 10): Nietzsche is a naturalist but not a scientistic one — normativity via historically emergent 'forms of life'; Leiter's reply: this mischaracterizes methodological naturalism as reductive materialism and substitutes a formulation so permissive that nearly anyone counts. State both; note that Schacht's move is Heidegger's field-concept point in analytic dress (a naturalism that does not defer to the sciences on 'life') and that Leiter's reply concedes nothing about the psychological doctrine. Verdict as fact: the objection is sound against the cosmological and biological readings, which the working hypothesis does not need; it is not an objection to the psychological reading, which it does; the audit records the cost (Nietzsche's own biological register is read as metaphor or overreach — BGE 259 stays unexplained by the naturalist).

## Pitfalls / subtleties to flag

- The argument must be reconstructed as valid before it is bounded; a lesson that dismisses it has failed the design intent (rival readings at their strongest).
- 'Methodological naturalism' must be Leiter's (continuity), not a strawman; the dossier's notes and Leiter's NDPR review are the verifiable sources.
- The 'blond beast as password' is Heidegger's phrase; it is quoted once, in the context of §22, with the refutations from Modules 2–3 referenced, not redone.
- The course's own contribution (§5) is stated as the course's; do not attribute it to Leiter or Clark.
- No general philosophy-of-science excursus; the field-concept premise is stated in Heidegger's terms and granted.

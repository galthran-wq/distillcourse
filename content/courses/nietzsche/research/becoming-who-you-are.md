# Research brief: becoming-who-you-are (Becoming who one is: style and self-creation)

status: planned
module: affirmation (7 of 9), lesson 36 of 45
type: core
goal: Explain 'become who you are' as the achievement of unity among one's drives and evaluate 'giving style to one's character' as an ideal

## ARC (L10)
- **Spine step (11, "Saying yes"):** "The reply to nihilism is affirmation: loving what is necessary, tested by the demon's offer to live this life again forever, a test whose force does not depend on the physics; a self that can pass it is achieved, not given, by giving style to one's drives, and Ecce Homo is Nietzsche putting his own life to the test"
- **Inherits:** from eternal-recurrence, the demand (a life wanted whole) and the forward exercise asking what self could answer the demon; from self-as-society-of-drives, the self as "a social structure of the drives and affects" whose unity is an achievement (BGE 12, 19); from sublimation, drives redirected rather than cut; from amor-fati, GS 276's "then I shall be one of those who make things beautiful". No dependence on creating-values or higher-type (module 8).
- **Contributes:** the paradox of "become who you are" dissolved on the drive picture; GS 290 as a recipe with a formal criterion (one taste); EH II 9's becoming without knowing (the organizing idea; consciousness a surface); the constraint of GS 335's "physics" and BGE 231's "granite"; the fork among three pictures of the self; the objection that one taste can be monstrous and that a self which must not know itself cannot be made on purpose, with the reply and the open sentence. Introduces becoming-who-one-is and giving-style.
- **Hands off:** to ecce-homo (next): the subtitle and EH II 9 are the theory, the book is the author applying it to his life. To higher-type and creating-values (module 8): wholeness in manifoldness and the authority of made values are left open here.

## STANCE (binding)
- **Favorable:** Nietzsche saw, before psychology had the words, that a person is many drives with no given centre, and drew the consequence nobody else drew: unity of character is a work, not a fact, done by taste rather than will. GS 290 states an ideal no moral code can state (coherence, not conformity); EH II 9 describes a task organizing a person from below years before it can be named. Both are shown as prescient about the unconscious.
- **Hard passages this lesson must quote whole:** BGE 231 whole (bge-zimmern.txt lines 4120-4135; German bge-german.txt line 5031), including its last sentence, which introduces "a few truths about 'woman as such'" and opens 232-239 on women; the lesson says the "granite" passage is Nietzsche's own preface to those sections, with their locator. GS 290 whole (pp.232-233), including "It is the weak characters without power over themselves that hate the constraint of style" and "Whoever is dissatisfied with himself is continually ready for revenge; and we others will be his victims". EH II 9 (pp.253-255) whole, including "neighbor love" and "living for others" as "a protective measure for preserving the hardest self-concern". D 548 (pp.220-221), including "The order of rank of greatness for all past mankind has not yet been determined."
- **What the words meant:** "style" (GS 290): the constraint of one taste over a whole, from art criticism, not manners; "second nature" (GS 290, D 455): habits added by discipline until as much one's own as the first; "physics" (GS 335): the non-metaphysical study of "everything that is lawful and necessary in the world", including in oneself (Kaufmann n.67); "granite" (BGE 231): the "unteachable" at the bottom of a person, the limit on what can be made; "weak characters" (GS 290): natures without power over their drives, a type judged, and the judgment is part of the ideal; "the hardest self-concern" (EH II 9): the self's preservation using even selflessness as a tool.
- **Objection answered from his side:** (i) style is not enough: one taste can be monstrous, and the formal criterion admires unity wherever found; (ii) a self that "must not have the faintest notion what one is" cannot follow GS 290's recipe. Reply, texts first: GS 290 ends on what unity is for, "that a human being should attain satisfaction with himself", because the dissatisfied are "continually ready for revenge"; a style is judged by the life it makes affirmable and by whether it ends ressentiment (amor-fati, EH I 6). On (ii), D 560 and D 552 are the two halves he kept together: one may dispose of one's drives "like a gardener", and "blow to the winds all presumptuous talk of 'willing' and 'creating'", the model is pregnancy; GS 290's survey is the artist's eye, the commanding is the organizing idea's. Gap sentence: the ideal rules out the weak and scattered more clearly than the strong and evil, and the texts do not reconcile the gardener with the surface that must stay clear.
- **Humanizing reading to test:** (a) the existentialist reading, self-creation by free choice: GS 335 continues "we must become physicists in order to be able to be creators", BGE 231 sets the granite, D 560's trellis is chosen but the plants are given. (b) The type-facts reading, self-creation as mere unfolding: GS 290's "a large mass of second nature has been added" and TI 49's "he created himself" say more. Both are the outer prongs of the fork; the middle is built from GS 290 and EH II 9.
- **Facts against the popular image:** none in this lesson's texts.

## The reconstruction
- **Claim and where.** GS 290: one thing is needful, to give style to one's character under a single taste; GS 335: we want to become those we are, by learning what is lawful and necessary; EH II 9 and subtitle: one becomes what one is without knowing it, through an organizing idea that commands from below.
- **Argument in plain steps.** (1) "Become who you are" looks empty or contradictory. (2) On the drive picture a person is many drives with no given centre; "who you are" is not yet determinate. (3) So the phrase names the unity those drives could come to have: a character under which every deed, including the weaknesses, counts as one person's. (4) Achieving it is the work: add second nature, remove, conceal, reinterpret, until one taste governs (GS 290). (5) The work is constrained by given materials ("physics", "granite", the gardener's plants), so it is neither invention from nothing nor the unfolding of a seed. (6) The commanding is not done by consciousness, a surface; an organizing idea ranks the capacities before it is known (EH II 9), which is why the imperative is a conscience's answer (GS 270), not a plan. (7) The criterion is formal, one taste; its purpose is satisfaction with oneself, the end of revenge, which ties it to amor fati.
- **Unstated premise.** That unity of character is worth having for itself (GS 290 asserts it as "needful"); that the past can be integrated by reinterpretation (Z II 20; EH III's rereading of the books, used in ecce-homo).
- **Strongest objection and Nietzsche's reply.** As in STANCE; the plain sentence on what is left open.
- **Connections.** self-as-society-of-drives; sublimation; amor-fati (GS 276; EH I 6); eternal-recurrence (BGE 70's recurring character); ecce-homo (next); higher-type and creating-values (module 8).

## Where the claim forks (named by content)
(a) Uncovering a hidden true self; (b) actualizing a fixed potential, acorn to oak, in naturalist form the type-facts picture (BGE 231); (c) creating a unity that did not exist, the self as what one's integrated life turns out to be, the literary-character picture. GS 335 and BGE 231 pull toward (b); GS 290's "second nature added" and TI 49's "he created himself" toward (c); SE 1's "an infinite height above thee" is the early form of (a) turned upward. The lesson argues (c) fits the drive-self best, shows what (b) contributes (one cannot become just anything), and rejects (a) as what "consciousness is a surface" forbids.

## Pitfalls
- The phrase is Pindar's (Pythian II 71 per Kaufmann n.69); first in Nietzsche's books at GS 270 (1882); SE (1874) has "sei du selbst", not Pindar; EH (1888) carries it as subtitle without naming Pindar. Not "forty years": 1882-1888 with an 1874 anticipation in other words.
- GS 335 is plural ("we want to become those we are"); EH II 9's exact words as given; GS 270 is a conscience's answer in a catechism, quoted with its neighbour on pity.
- "Self-creation" is not choice from nowhere (L13; documented in the existentialist reception): refute once with GS 335's continuation and BGE 231.
- Z IV 1 is Zarathustra to himself, an illustration. creating-values and higher-type come later: no dependence on the authority puzzle or on BGE 212.
- One literary analogy suffices; no scholars' names in the body.

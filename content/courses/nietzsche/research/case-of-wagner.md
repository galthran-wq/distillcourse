# Research brief: case-of-wagner (The Case of Wagner read whole: decadence diagnosed in an artist)

status: planned
module: the-problem (1 of 9), lesson 5 of 45
type: synthesis (module application, L16)
goal: Assess The Case of Wagner with the module's diagnosis, separating the art Nietzsche first hailed as tragedy reborn from the decadence he found in the same music sixteen years later

## ARC (L10)

- **Spine step (3, "The problem"):** "'God is dead' is a claim about belief, not about God: the Christian God became unbelievable, the values that rested on him lost their foundation, and their own demand for truthfulness turns them into nihilism; Wagner's art is the first case Nietzsche diagnosed with this"
- **Inherits:** from schopenhauer-and-the-birth-of-tragedy, the aesthetic justification, Apollo/Dionysus (1872 sense), the Foreword and GT 16, 19-22 (Wagner as tragedy reborn), Self-Crit 6-7; from nihilism, decadence (A 6; TI Socrates 11) and passive nihilism as the wish for rest; from death-of-god, the shadow (redemption without belief).
- **Contributes:** the module's results cashed out on one text read whole, the 1888 pamphlet, and on the one artist Nietzsche knew in the flesh. Introduces romantic-pessimism (GS 370); reinforces decadence, aesthetic-justification, apollonian-and-dionysian, nihilism.
- **Hands off:** to module 2: the diagnosis says Wagner's instincts work against themselves and his art needs exhausted listeners (CW 5, 7), which needs a psychology of drives (self-as-society-of-drives). The Epilogue's master-morality against Christian morality reaches to master-morality and slave-revolt-and-ressentiment; its "optics of values" to perspectivism; CW 7's "equal rights for all" to herd-morality-in-modern-dress.

## STANCE (binding)

- **Favorable:** the pamphlet as its author says it is: not revenge but the most reflexive diagnosis he wrote ("I am just as much a child of my age as Wagner—i.e., I am a decadent. The only difference is that I recognised the fact", Preface), by the man who calls Wagner "the great benefactor of my life" (EH II 6) and says a philosopher "is not free to pass him by" because "Wagner is modernity in concentrated form" (Preface). What he saw first: an artwork can be read as a symptom; "style of decadence" names a structural fact (the part overruns the whole, CW 7); the modern need for redemption is the shadow of God set to music.
- **Hard passages this lesson must quote whole:** CW 3 (early-wa.txt l.178-283): the redemption catalogue and its women ("adoring women are their ruin"; "Man is a coward in the face of all that is eternally feminine, and this the girls know"; love as "a refined form of parasitism"); the Dutchman's "eternal Jew" who "marries, that is to say, he concerns us no longer"; Goethe "found honest admirers only among Jewesses"; "Holiness—the only remaining higher value still seen by the mob or by woman". CW 5 (l.335-411): "Is Wagner a man at all? Is he not rather a disease?"; "his success with nerves, and therefore with women". CW 7 (l.516-607): "anarchy among the atoms, disaggregation of the will,—in moral terms: 'freedom of the individual,'—extended into a political theory 'equal rights for all'". CW 11: "The definition of a German: an obedient man with long legs." Epilogue (l.1189-1262): master-morality "the symbolic speech of ... ascending life, and of the Will to Power as a vital principle" against "the gospel of the lowly, the doctrine of the need of salvation"; Wagner "thoroughly feminini generis"; "Christianity adjusted for female Wagnerites". Postscript (l.1025): Wagner "flatters every nihilistic (Buddhistic) instinct".
- **What the words meant:** "decadent/décadence": the French critics' word for the condition in which instincts work against themselves and "what is harmful lures the exhausted" (CW 5); "hysteria", "neurosis" ("Wagner est une névrose"): 1880s clinical terms used as symptom-reading, not diagnosis; "woman" in CW 3: the audience the redemption plots are written for and the type who "saves" the artist by adoring him, carrying the argument that the need to be redeemed is the need of the exhausted; "the eternal Jew": the Dutchman's figure, the wanderer, turned into the figure of the artist; "equal rights for all" (CW 7): the political form of the atomised style; "master-morality" (Epilogue): BGE 260 and GM I a year early.
- **Objection answered from his side:** the diagnosis is ad hominem and a genetic fallacy about art, reading a work from the maker's unobservable health; and self-undermining, since the Epilogue says the decadent and the classical aesthetic are "both necessary", "different points of view which cannot be circumvented either with arguments or counter-arguments". Reply: GS 370 makes the "backward inference from the work to the maker" a method with a criterion, what the work needs of its listener (rest, redemption, intoxication, a saviour-god, versus the strength to afford "the terrible deed"); CW 5-7 give observable features (the redemption plot; effect before structure; the miniature that overruns the whole; "Above all, no thoughts!" CW 6); CW 8 gives the actor's criterion ("everything which has to strike people as true, must not be true"); and the Epilogue's charge is not decadence but falsity, "the instinctive duplicity which would fain regard this antithesis as no antithesis at all: just as Wagner did", the modern who "says yea and nay in one breath". The Preface's self-application answers the ad hominem. Gap sentence: the standard of ascending against declining life that the Epilogue rests on is asserted, not argued, and the physiological vocabulary is earned only in module 2.
- **Humanizing reading to test:** the break "caused not by Wagner's art but by Bayreuth" (Kaufmann Ch.1 pp.30-41), making it political and moral; Wagner as father-substitute, Cosima as Ariadne (pp.32-35). The texts: EH III HAH 3 (p.286), "not a break with Wagner: I noted a total aberration of my instincts of which particular blunders, whether Wagner or the professorship at Basel, were mere symptoms"; EH II 5 (p.248), what he never forgave was that Wagner "became reichsdeutsch"; CW Preface, the break as a recovery. Text first, Bayreuth as one reading. Also to test: that he was never a devoted Wagnerian of the music dramas (Kaufmann n.1 to EH II 6, after Love); CW 3 says "I was one of the most corrupted Wagnerites".
- **Facts against the popular image:** the pamphlet ends "inspired by gratitude" (Epilogue); "I have loved Wagner" (EH III CW 1, p.317); EH II 6 still seeks "a work that equals the dangerous fascination and the gruesome and sweet infinity of Tristan"; the Munich wreath "Salvation to the Saviour" altered to "Salvation from the Saviour" (Postscript).

## The reconstruction

- **Claim and where.** CW Preface, 5, 7, Epilogue: Wagner is "the artist of decadence", his music "sick", his art modernity concentrated; proof in the works: the redemption plot (3), the Ring's Schopenhauerian turn (4), the need for exhausted listeners (5), the style in which the part overruns the whole (7), the actor's primacy (8-11). Reflexively (Preface): the author is a decadent who recognised it.
- **Argument in plain steps.** (1) Decadence is instinct turned against itself: "what is harmful lures the exhausted" (CW 5; A 6). (2) A work can be read backward to the need that made it, by asking what it does for its audience (GS 370). (3) Every Wagner opera turns on someone saved (CW 3), and the Ring was rewritten from revolt into resignation (CW 4): the works answer a need for redemption. (4) Formally, effect before structure, "no thoughts", colour before melody, a whole "composed, reckoned up, artificial" (6-7): the style of decadence. (5) The man is an actor, so the art is "an art of lying" (8, 12). (6) Therefore Wagner is the case in which modernity's decadence can be studied whole, and the module's problem, a culture that lost God and kept the need for redemption, is visible in one artist: Parsifal is "Christianity adjusted for female Wagnerites".
- **Unstated premise.** That ascending and declining life is an objective standard for an age's virtues and an artist's forms (Epilogue), and that physiology can be read off a style. Asserted, never argued (also TI Skirmishes 33, A 6); module 2 supplies the psychology.
- **Strongest objection and Nietzsche's reply.** Genetic fallacy and self-undermining; replies and gap in STANCE.
- **Connections.** schopenhauer-and-the-birth-of-tragedy (GT 16, 19-22 against CW 4, 10; the two Dionysians); nihilism (decadence; the Buddhistic instinct; GS 370's anarchists); death-of-god (the shadow); self-as-society-of-drives; herd-morality-in-modern-dress (CW 7); master-morality (Epilogue); ascetic-ideals-in-artists-and-philosophers (GM III 2-5); perspectivism ("optics"); amor-fati (NcW Epilogue); nietzsche-and-politics (CW 11; "reichsdeutsch").

The sentence: the reader can now take a work he knows, ask what it needs of its audience, and say whether it was made from hunger or from overflow, in Nietzsche's words and by his criterion.

## Key points the lesson must cover

1. **The corpus as a dated list with genres.** Birth of Tragedy (1872), Untimely Meditations (1873–76), Human All Too Human (1878–80), Daybreak (1881), Gay Science (1882; Book V 1887), Zarathustra (1883–85), BGE (1886), GM (1887), Case of Wagner, Twilight, Antichrist, Ecce Homo (all 1888; the last two published posthumously). Three genres: sustained treatise (GM's three essays; Antichrist), aphorism collection (HH, D, GS, BGE, TI), dramatization (Z). The genre fixes what a section can be cited for: a Zarathustra speech is testimony from a character.
2. **The aphorism as unit.** GM III:1 worked: an outline of the whole treatise (artists, philosophers, women, the physiologically deformed, priests, saints) compressed into the closing formula — the section is the argument, the last sentence its conclusion. GS 381: 'quickly into them and quickly out again'. The consequence for reconstruction: a premise is usually the section's last sentence or its first; the middle is evidence.
3. **The five-step protocol.** (1) Locate the claim — one sentence, cited by siglum. (2) List the stated premises, each tied to a sentence. (3) Name the missing premise — the step validity needs that the text lacks; classify it (is–ought; a type-fact; the free-will presupposition; a historical assertion). (4) Check a second passage — same claim, other book — and say whether it supplies the missing premise. (5) State the strongest opposite reading and the sentence that tells against it. Run the protocol on madman-argument's result as the first demonstration (steps 4 and 5 were skipped there; do them now with GS 357 and the atheist reading).
4. **The published-works rule.** Clark's statement, its reasons, and the consequence for the course: WP numbers are cited only when a published passage backs them; Nachlass fragments corroborate dates and drafts. The siglum convention adopted for the whole course.
5. **The working hypothesis stated as testable.** Methodological naturalism: explanations continuous with the sciences in method (causal, empirical, type-based) and results. The Doctrine of Types: type-facts bound a life without fixing it (the tomato seed will not yield corn). Three predictions the course will check: GM II:2's sovereign individual must be a natural artefact, not an ideal; value creation must be explicable without a faculty of legislation; will to power and eternal recurrence must survive as psychology, not cosmology. Named now, tested in Modules 3, 6, 7.
6. **The corpus ends in January 1889.** State the endpoint as a fact of the corpus (the last letters, the collapse in Turin) in two sentences; the handwriting pair is the one visual. No further biography.

## Pitfalls / subtleties to flag

- Do not teach the protocol as a formalism; there is no notation. Premises are sentences in English with a German anchor.
- Leiter is toc-only: state the Doctrine of Types in Leiter's own published terms as far as the dossier verifies them (the tomato-plant analogy, 'type-facts', 'causal essentialism'), and do not invent page numbers.
- The 'twelve books' count depends on how the Untimely Meditations and the two-volume Human All Too Human are counted; say 'roughly a dozen' in prose and give the exact list in the figure.
- Ecce Homo is an authorized manuscript, not a published work; the eKGWB category matters when the course later cites EH on Zarathustra.
- The handwriting pair is evidence about a hand, not about a philosophy; the caption says only that the corpus ends where the second letter was written.
- The Doctrine of Types must be quoted from Knobe & Leiter 2007 (on disk), not paraphrased from reviews of the 2015 book; the 2015 chapter numbers stay as pointers.

## EXERCISE PLAN (floor: ≤1 warm-up; ≥2 of boundary / refute / derivation / research; one FORWARD)

1. (warm-up, chronology) Order BGE, GM, GS Book V, Z IV and Antichrist by composition date and state the genre of each; then say which of the five may be cited as an argument and which only as testimony from a character.
2. (protocol run) Apply all five steps to BGE 68 ('I did that, says my memory…'); the solution gives the two premises, names the missing one (that a drive can edit a memory without the person's consent), and checks it against GS 354.
3. (refute) "Because Nietzsche wrote in aphorisms, he did not argue; reconstruction imposes a structure he rejected." Break it with GM III:1 and GM Preface §8's own instruction (the treatises are commentaries on aphorisms, and reading is 'rumination').
4. (boundary probe) Take the published-works rule to its limit: Ecce Homo was prepared for print but published posthumously, edited by the Archiv. Decide, with reasons, which rung it occupies, and what that does to any claim resting on EH alone (e.g. the Sils-Maria dating of eternal recurrence).
5. (research-shaped) Using nietzschesource.org, resolve GM-I-13, JGB-19 and NF-1881,11[141] to their live pages, and record for each the category (published / Nachlass), the date, and the first sentence — the reader's first verification of a course citation.
6. (derivation) The Doctrine of Types is stated as 'type-facts bound without fixing'. Write out what the tomato-plant analogy commits Nietzsche to (a range, a ceiling, no unique outcome) and what it does not (determinism of every act), and find one sentence in BGE 231 ('deep down… granite of spiritual fate') that the analogy paraphrases.
7. (FORWARD → will-to-power-the-book) WP 1067 ('this world is the will to power — and nothing besides') is the most-quoted sentence of the compiled book. Apply step 4 of the protocol: find the published passage closest to it (BGE 36) and note its grammatical mood; the solution says the difference between 'is' and 'suppose' is the next lesson's subject.

# Research brief: creating-values (Creating values: can a naturalist call anything good?)

status: planned
module: higher-human-being (8 of 9), lesson 41 of 45
type: core
goal: Evaluate the main answers to how Nietzsche can create and rank values after denying that anything has value in itself

## ARC (L10)
- **Spine step (12, "The higher human being and the order of rank"):** "Affirmation needs a type able to bear it: the overman against the comfortable last man, the higher type who is as manifold as he is whole, the order of rank in which every elevation of man has needed an aristocratic society and the many have existed for the few, and the philosopher who creates values knowing they are made; 'What Is Noble?' is Nietzsche's own last statement of the type"
- **Inherits:** from order-of-rank, a ladder of "differences of worth" and the philosophers' task of "the determination of the order of rank among values" (GM Essay I Note); from perspectivism, "only a perspective knowing" and objectivity as more eyes (GM III 12); from value-of-untruth, honest illusion (GS 107) and "the falsity of a judgment is not yet an objection" (BGE 4); from nihilism, the revaluation; from overman-and-last-man, the child's "sacred Yes" (Z I 1); from master-morality, the nobles who "seized the right to create values" (GM I 2) and the noble as "value-creating" (BGE 260).
- **Contributes:** the course's hardest question met directly: the authority puzzle (if morality's authority falls with its objectivity, why not Nietzsche's ideal?) and the four answers the texts support, named by content. Introduces value-creation, value-anti-realism. Reaches the plain sentence on what the texts leave undecided.
- **Hands off:** to what-is-noble: BGE 260-261's "value-creating" and 287's "reverence for itself" as the module's text puts them; to the-system-assembled: this lesson's gap is the weakest link in the reply to nihilism, and the closing synthesis weighs it.

## STANCE (binding)
- **Favorable:** Nietzsche is the first philosopher to say in plain words that value is bestowed, not found ("nature is always value-less ... it was we who gave and bestowed it", GS 301), and to draw the consequence that the loss of God's values is not the loss of value but the exposure of our "best power", which "we fail to recognize" (GS 301). The thought that a people's table of goods is "the voice of their will to power" (Z I 15) and that "the real philosophers are commanders and legislators" (BGE 211) anticipates a century of debate about whether moral facts exist and, if not, what moral language does. The lesson shows why that is the honest place to stand after the death of God, and how much Nietzsche can keep from it.
- **Hard passages this lesson must quote whole:** BGE 211's legislator sentence (Zimmern line 3450; course translation in bge.json notes): "the real philosophers, however, are commanders and legislators: they say 'thus it shall be!', they first determine the Whither and Wherefore of man ... Their 'knowing' is creating, their creating is a legislation, their will to truth is, will to power" (verify eKGWB JGB-211); A 2 (p.570): "What is good? Everything that heightens the feeling of power in man, the will to power, power itself. What is bad? Everything that is born of weakness"; TI "Improvers" 1 (p.501): "there are altogether no moral facts"; BGE 43 (line 1361): "'My judgment is my judgment': another has not easily a right to it"; "a common good is a contradiction in terms"; EH IV 4 (p.330): the good "crucify him who writes new values on new tablets"; Z II 12 (p.228): "Whoever must be a creator in good and evil, he must first be an annihilator and break values." None of these lesson texts touches race, women or slavery; the hard passages here are hard in the argument, not in the vocabulary, and are read whole for that reason.
- **What the words meant:** "Value" (Werth): what a valuer esteems, a weight placed on a thing (Z I 15: "Only man placed values in things to preserve himself ... Therefore he calls himself man, which means: the esteemer"); "value in itself" is what Nietzsche denies of nature. "Create" (schaffen): to bestow a value a thing did not have, by naming and esteeming (GS 58: "it is enough to create new names and estimations and probabilities in order to create in the long run new things"); never making from nothing, always re-weighting what is there ("the weights of all things must be determined anew", GS 269). "Legislator" (Gesetzgeber, BGE 211; notebook 38[13]): the philosopher who sets the "Whither and Wherefore" rather than fixing existing valuations into systems (the "philosophical labourers", Kant and Hegel). "Will to power" as measure (A 2): the standard by which Nietzsche ranks, stated as a definition and, in the notebooks, as the "quantum of power that you are" (11[?], Bittner p.208, unpublished); the lesson states that this is a definition he gives, not a derivation.
- **Objection answered from his side:** the authority puzzle itself: if there are no moral facts, Nietzsche's own rankings have no more standing than the morality he attacks, and "my judgment is my judgment" concedes it. Reply from the texts: (1) the critique of morality's presuppositions (free will, a transparent self, one nature for all) stands on factual grounds that do not depend on the puzzle (TI "Four Great Errors"; BGE 21; GS 335); (2) Nietzsche never claimed the authority he denies to morality: "my judgment is my judgment", "a common good is a contradiction in terms" (BGE 43), the book "for all and none"; his values bind "those whose ears are related to ours" (GS 381); (3) A 2 and TI "Anti-Nature" 5 ("life itself values through us when we posit values") give a measure that is not arbitrary: a valuation is a symptom of ascending or descending life, and that can be read off; (4) GM III 12 gives a sense of objectivity without a view from nowhere: the valuation that has let more affects speak is more complete. The plain gap sentence: Nietzsche's texts decide that values are made and that they bind the kind of life that makes them, and they do not decide whether the maker's ranking can claim anything against a rival maker who shares none of his drives; the four answers below are the readers' attempts to fill a space the texts leave open.
- **Humanizing reading to test:** two readings soften the texts in opposite directions. The realist reading (power as the objective measure of value, built on A 2 and the notebook "quantum of power") gives Nietzsche a foundation he never argued for in a book; its cost is the step from "everyone wills power" to "power is good", which has the form of an inference Nietzsche mocks elsewhere ("Man does not strive for pleasure; only the Englishman does", TI Maxims 12). The fictionalist reading (values as make-believe we knowingly keep, built on GS 107's "good will to appearance" and the child's "game") turns the legislator into a player; its cost is Z II 12's annihilator and BGE 211's "thus it shall be!", which are not the tone of make-believe, and the fact that revaluation re-weights the same values rather than inventing fictions. What the text says: GS 301 "we gave and bestowed it", Z I 15 "To esteem is to create", BGE 211 "their will to truth is will to power", A 2's definition, GM III 12's many eyes. The lesson states each reading by what it says and tests it against these five.
- **Facts against the popular image:** Nietzsche is not a relativist for whom any value is as good as any other: A 2 ranks, TI Skirmishes 33 ranks by ascending and descending life, GM III 12 ranks by completeness; and he is not a nihilist who abolishes value: GS 301's complaint is that we "underestimate ourselves" as creators, and GM III 25 denies that science "creates values" at all.

## The reconstruction
- **Claim and where.** GS 301: nothing has value in itself; value was bestowed by us. Z I 15: "To esteem is to create." BGE 211: the philosopher legislates values. TI "Improvers" 1: no moral facts. A 2: what is good is what heightens power.
- **The authority puzzle in plain steps.** (1) Nietzsche denies that moral values have the objective standing their believers claim (TI "Improvers" 1; BGE 108). (2) He criticises those values from a standard of his own (ascending life, the higher type: TI Skirmishes 33; A 2). (3) If his standard has no objective standing either, his critique seems to have no more authority than the morality it attacks. (4) Yet he writes as if his rankings were correct ("thus it shall be!"). The question is how (2) and (4) can be held with (1).
- **Four answers, named by content, each with its cost.** (a) Power is the measure: will to power is the essence of life (BGE 13, 36 conditional; A 2), so what heightens power is good. Cost: from "life wills power" to "power is good" is the inference from the desired to the desirable, and the universal premise leans on notebooks (38[12], 14[121]) rather than books. (b) Honest illusion: values are fictions knowingly kept, as in art and the child's game (GS 107, 382; Z I 1). Cost: a fiction gives no steer about which fiction to adopt, and revaluation re-weights the same values rather than inventing new fictions. (c) Values that fit a valuer's drives: a value is real as a relation between a kind of life and the world, as "nutritious" is real for a kind of body (GS 301's "world that concerns man"; TI "Anti-Nature" 5; BGE 43); new values are new sensibilities. Cost: no value binds everyone; Nietzsche's rankings bind those who share the life they express, which he may accept ("my judgment is my judgment"). (d) Perspectival objectivity: a valuation is better the more affects it has let speak (GM III 12 extended from knowing to valuing; BGE 211's "many eyes and consciences"). Cost: it is unclear that value perspectives combine as perceptual ones do, and GM III 12 speaks of concepts, not values.
- **Unstated premise.** In (1): that value's authority depends on objective standing, the premise Nietzsche shares with the believer when he says "no moral facts" counts against morality. (c) and (d) deny the premise; (a) accepts it and supplies a fact; (b) accepts it and lives without.
- **Strongest objection and Nietzsche's reply.** As in the STANCE block; the reply is that his project needs no more than (c) or (d): he addresses "all and none", denies that one morality must bind all (BGE 228, 43), and his critique's factual half stands alone. What he cannot have is authority over everyone, which he denied to morality. The gap sentence as above.
- **Verdict the lesson reaches.** Values as made, and binding on those whose life they express; the child of the three metamorphoses as the figure of a valuing that knows itself (illustration); the plain statement of what the texts leave undecided.
- **Connections.** perspectivism (GM III 12); value-of-untruth (GS 107); will-to-power-as-psychology (A 2's definition); master-morality (GM I 2, BGE 260-261); order-of-rank (ranking values); becoming-who-you-are (GS 335: "who give themselves laws, who create themselves"); what-is-noble (287's faith); the-system-assembled (the weakest link).

## Where the claim forks (named by content)
- **Power as the measure** (A 2; BGE 13, 36; notebooks 38[12], 14[121] unpublished): realism about one value. **Honest illusion** (GS 107, 382; Z I 1): fictionalism. **Values that fit a valuer's drives** (GS 301; TI "Anti-Nature" 5; BGE 43; GS 381): subjective or relational realism. **Perspectival objectivity** (GM III 12; BGE 211, 207): objectivity as completeness of affects. The lesson states each by content, gives its best text and its cost, and crowns none; the course's judgment is that Nietzsche's project needs (c) or (d) and that the texts do not choose between them.

## Pitfalls
- Readable for a non-specialist: no "metaethics", "cognitivism", "error theory", "fictionalism" as labels; state each view in a sentence of plain words and let the Secondary carry the names.

- Perspectival objectivity's text is GM III 12 (and BGE 211's "many eyes and consciences"); BGE 219 is about moral judging as the revenge of the shallow and does not belong here.
- GM I 16 (p.52) describes a higher nature as a battleground of two value systems; it is not an argument that revaluation must be internal, and the brief's inference is marked as such.
- Z I 15 in Kaufmann's exact wording (p.171); the contents list's "Thousand and One Coals" is a print slip.

## EXERCISE PLAN
Floor check: claim to refute (ex. 2), derivation (ex. 3), boundary probe (ex. 4); research-shaped (ex. 5).
1. Retrieval (E3 from perspectivism and value-of-untruth): state GM III 12's "more eyes" and GS 107's "good will to appearance" in one sentence each, and say which of the four answers each underwrites.
2. Claim to refute: "Since Nietzsche thinks values are created, he must think any value anyone creates is as good as any other." Break it with A 2, TI Skirmishes 33 and GM III 12, naming the step at which the claim confuses "made" with "equal".
3. Derivation: show that "life wills power, therefore power is good" has the same form as "everyone desires pleasure, therefore pleasure is good", the inference TI Maxims 12 mocks, and state the extra premise both need; then say whether BGE 36's conditional supplies it.
4. Boundary probe: a person whose drives value cruelty for its own sake. On the answer that values fit a valuer's drives, is cruelty a value for him? What, if anything, in A 2, TI Skirmishes 33 or GM III 12 lets Nietzsche rank his value below Goethe's, and what does the case show about that answer's limit?
5. Research-shaped (sourcing drill): BGE 211 (1886) and its draft 38[13] (June-July 1885, unpublished) say the same thing in two registers; apply metadata, prediction and records/argues/omits to each, and state what the published text drops from the draft (Plato's self-persuasion; the founders' "thou shalt") and what that omission can be used to establish.
6. FORWARD (reaches what-is-noble): BGE 287 says what is noble is "not the works, but the BELIEF ... some fundamental certainty which a noble soul has about itself" and ends "THE NOBLE SOUL HAS REVERENCE FOR ITSELF". Say how a faith that "is not to be sought, is not to be found" sits with this lesson's answers, which all make value something achieved by valuing.

# Research brief: debt-and-punishment (Debt, promise and punishment)

status: planned
module: guilt-and-the-priest (4 of 9), lesson 17 of 45
type: core
goal: Explain Nietzsche's derivation of guilt from the creditor-debtor relation and of the promising animal from memory burned in by pain

## ARC (L10)

- **Spine step (7, "Guilt and the priest"):** "Guilt began as debt: a promising animal learned memory through pain, cruelty was the festival pleasure that higher culture refined rather than abolished, cruelty with no outlet turned inward as bad conscience, the debt to God made it unpayable, and the ascetic priest gave suffering a meaning by making the sufferer its cause"
- **Inherits:** from genealogy-as-method, origin versus purpose (GM II 12) and the documented method (Pref 7); from doer-behind-the-deed, that blame presupposes a doer who could have done otherwise (II 4); from will-to-power-as-psychology, the pleasure in exercising power (step 6).
- **Contributes:** the first two stages of the history of guilt: the promising animal bred by a memory of pain under the morality of custom, and the creditor-debtor relation in which pain repays a debt because inflicting it gives pleasure, so Schuld is debt before it is guilt; plus justice, mercy, punishment's meanings, and that punishment does not produce bad conscience. Introduces promising-animal, morality-of-custom, creditor-debtor.
- **Hands off:** to cruelty-and-culture, the premise used here and examined there: making suffer was "the great festival pleasure of earlier men" (GM II 6-7, pp.65-69). One forward sentence only.

## STANCE (binding)

- **Favorable:** Nietzsche saw first that the vocabulary of guilt is a legal-economic one with the ledger still visible (Schuld/Schulden; "debts" and "trespasses" in the Lord's Prayer), and that punishment is older than belief in free will (II 4, p.63). The derivation explains why guilt feels like owing, punishment like payment, confession like settling an account.
- **Hard passages this lesson must quote whole:** GM II 3 (p.61), the catalogue of punishments (genealogy--text.txt 1834-1891); GM II 5 (p.64), the Twelve Tables, "a warrant for and title to cruelty", "a right of the masters"; GM II 11 (pp.75-76, line 2388), law as "exceptional conditions", a universal legal order "hostile to life", the hundred steps; notebook 10[50], autumn 1887, Bittner pp.183-184, unpublished (latenotebooks-full.txt 9140-9170), whole, including "castrate him". Texts in Set pieces.
- **What the words meant:** "Cruelty" (Grausamkeit): pleasure in making suffer as a feeling of power (II 5-6; D 18; HH 103), a drive, not a pathology. "Master" (Herr, II 5): the creditor's legal position over another's body. "Morality of custom" (Sittlichkeit der Sitte, D 9): obedience to tradition because it commands. "Criminal race" (Verbrecher-Rasse, 10[50]): a criminal type held hereditary; the note's frame is war on an enemy of the social order, so it passes from "do not punish" to "castrate" without a break; both follow from reading crime as rebellion rather than guilt, and the lesson shows that they do.
- **Objection answered from his side:** The history is speculative; kinship, gift and status are as old as exchange. Reply: II 4-5 give three documents, linguistic (Schuld/Schulden), legal (the debtor's body), ritual (punishment as festival); the argument downstream needs only that the moral concept was shaped by the economic one; II 4 argues independently that punishment predates blame. Gap sentence: the priority of exchange over gift and kinship is asserted, not shown.
- **Humanizing reading to test:** Kaufmann reads the sovereign individual (II 2) as Nietzsche's ideal; the text calls him the "ripest fruit" of the morality of mores, "autonomous and supramoral", and drops him (fork below). Mercy (II 10) read as humane progress; the text: "the privilege of the most powerful", and II 11 calls a universal legal order hostile to life.
- **Facts against the popular image:** II 11 is aimed at Eugen Dühring, the antisemitic "apostle of revenge" whom III 14 (p.124) names with "the anti-Semites".

## The reconstruction

- **Claim and where.** GM II 4 (p.62): "the major moral concept Schuld has its origin in the very material concept Schulden", and punishment as repayment "developed quite independently of any presupposition concerning freedom or non-freedom of the will".
- **Argument in plain steps.** (1) Forgetting is an active, healthy power (II 1). (2) Social life needs promises, so a memory of the will was bred and man made calculable: the morality of custom (II 2; D 9). (3) The method was pain (II 3). (4) The oldest relation between persons is creditor and debtor; valuing and exchanging are the oldest thinking (II 8). (5) A debtor who cannot pay pledges body, wife, freedom, afterlife (II 5). (6) The creditor may take pain in place of payment because making suffer is a pleasure, a feeling of power; so suffering balances a debt (II 5-6). (7) So "you owe" is economic before it is moral; punishment begins as repayment, not as an answer to a guilty will (II 4). (8) The community is creditor too; the criminal is a defaulter; the stronger the community, the milder the punishment, until mercy (II 9-10). (9) Justice is imposed by the strong against ressentiment (II 11); punishment's meanings are many and late (II 13); punishment makes hard and prudent, not guilty (II 14-15).
- **Unstated premise.** Step 6: universal pleasure in cruelty as feeling of power (next lesson). Step 4: exchange prior to every other relation.
- **Strongest objection and Nietzsche's reply.** As in STANCE; gap sentence on exchange versus gift and kinship.
- **Connections.** cruelty-and-culture (step 6); bad-conscience (II 14); guilt-before-god (II 19; GS 250); doer-behind-the-deed (II 4; TI "Errors" 7); order-of-rank (II 11, law as exception); what-is-noble (II 10; BGE 260).

## Where the claim forks (named by content)

The sovereign individual (II 2): Nietzsche's ideal of the autonomous agent with a "long will", or the self-portrait of the agent the morality of mores produced, called "supramoral" and left behind. Placement supports the second, warmth the first. One paragraph, no names in the body.

## Pitfalls

- II 13, not II 12, lists punishment's meanings. GM II 4-8 are not in the Portable; Vintage pages.
- D 9 is custom, not debt. 10[50] is unpublished: lead with D 202-203, bring the note whole as scaffolding.
- Do not resolve the sovereign-individual fork by fiat; no clinical vocabulary in II 14. The Lord's Prayer parallel establishes the double sense of Schuld, nothing more.

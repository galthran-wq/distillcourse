# Research brief: doer-behind-the-deed (No doer behind the deed: free will and responsibility)

## ARC (L10)

- **Spine step (4, "The drive-self"):** "Before asking where values came from, Nietzsche asks who values: not a unified soul that sees itself clearly, but a society of drives with a thin conscious surface made for talking to the herd, and grammar's invented doer whom morality holds responsible." This lesson carries the last clause.
- **Inherits:** from self-as-society-of-drives: the plural self and BGE 19's command-and-obedience; from consciousness-and-language: consciousness as the herd's sign-system and the FORWARD exercise on "the lightning flashes".
- **Contributes:** the critique of free will and responsibility in three arguments (grammar, causa sui, the willing complex) and what survives (strong and weak will, character). Introduces `doer-deed-fiction`, `causa-sui`. It does NOT introduce the four great errors or the innocence of becoming (lesson 11's); TI "Errors" 3 and 7 appear only as pointers.
- **Hands off:** to will-to-power-as-psychology (next): if no doer moves the drives, what does; GS 360's "dammed-up energy" is the doorstep. Later: slave-revolt-and-ressentiment (GM I 13), danger-of-dangers, guilt-before-god, four-great-errors.

## STANCE (binding)

- **Favorable:** Nietzsche saw that retributive responsibility requires a metaphysics nobody can supply (being one's own cause), located the illusion in grammar rather than only in theology, and refused the determinist's mirror image too (BGE 21). The lesson builds the argument at that strength and shows it leaves a psychology of strong and weak wills standing.
- **Hard passages this lesson must quote whole:** GM I 13 (pp.44-46; lambs and birds of prey, strength that must express itself as strength, the weak's need for the subject); BGE 21 (bge-zimmern.txt line 725ff; both diagnoses, "the fatalism of the weak-willed", "socialist sympathy for criminals", "la religion de la souffrance humaine"); GM II 4 (pp.62-63; "he could have acted differently" as a late, subtle judgment); D 13 (p.13; "jailers and hangmen"); D 202-203 (pp.120-122; "every 'guilty person' is a sick person").
- **What the words meant:** "Doer" (Thäter) and "deed" (That) are grammatical as much as moral terms; "subject" is what grammar posits behind a verb. "Strong and weak will" (BGE 21) are degrees of coordination among drives, not of freedom. "Fatalism" has two senses, TI "Errors" 8's "piece of fatum" (lesson 11) and EH I 6's "Russian fatalism" (p.230). "Birds of prey" and "lambs" are types of valuation, not peoples.
- **Objection answered from his side:** the compatibilist's: responsibility needs only that I act from my own character and reasons; deliberation is real. Reply, texts first: what deliberation observes is "a comparison of the pictured consequences" while the fight that decides is "quite invisible" (D 129, pp.79-80); the conscious purpose is "a match versus a ton of powder", a direction but not the cause of acting (GS 360, pp.315-316); intention is "a sign and symptom" (BGE 32); remorse arises "only because man deems himself free" (HH 39). The gap, in one sentence: Nietzsche argues against the moralist's "could have done otherwise" and retributive blame, never against a compatibilist responsibility that asks only for action from one's own character; BGE 21's "strong will" is such a notion, left undeveloped.
- **Humanizing reading to test:** the reading that keeps "freedom" as autonomy or self-mastery (GS 347, Kaufmann n.27; Kaufmann Ch. 7). The texts support a non-moral strong will (BGE 21, GS 347) and deny responsibility for being "thus-and-thus" (GM I 13; notebook 11, Bittner pp.231-232, unpublished); the lesson keeps both and says the strong will is not a free will. "Barometers drop before storms but do not cause them" is a scholar's analogy (Leiter), not Nietzsche's.
- **Facts against the popular image:** Nietzsche is not a hard determinist: BGE 21 calls "unfree will" mythology; the postcard on discovering Spinoza (July 1881, Portable p.92) lists denial of free will among their agreements.

## The reconstruction

- **Claim and where.** GM I 13: no being behind doing. BGE 21: free will "in the superlative metaphysical sense" requires causa sui; "unfree will" is the same mistake run backwards. BGE 19: the will is "a unity only as a word". For blame: the moralist's blame loses its premise; strong and weak will, character and rank among persons remain.
- **Argument 1, grammar.** (1) Language requires a subject for every verb (BGE 17, 20). (2) So every event is a thing doing something: the lightning flashes (GM I 13). (3) Applied to a person this splits him into a doer and his deeds. (4) Nothing but grammar supports the split; the person is the drives in action (GM I 13; BGE 19). (5) So the free subject is a fiction, "the deed is everything".
- **Argument 2, causa sui.** (1) Ultimate responsibility for an act requires responsibility for the character it came from. (2) That requires having made one's character. (3) That requires a prior self, and so on. (4) Nothing causes itself (BGE 21). (5) So ultimate responsibility is impossible; and "unfree will" is also a myth.
- **Argument 3, the willing complex (BGE 19).** Sensations away-from and toward, a commanding thought, an affect of superiority; we identify with the commander and take credit for what the obeying drives do.
- **Unstated premise.** Argument 1 needs "nothing but grammar supports the split"; D 129 and GS 360 are the attempt to supply it. Argument 2 needs "ultimate" responsibility and does not touch the compatibilist's.
- **Strongest objection and Nietzsche's reply.** As in STANCE.
- **Connections.** will-to-power-as-psychology (GS 360; BGE 19's affect of command); slave-revolt-and-ressentiment (GM I 13); danger-of-dangers; debt-and-punishment, guilt-before-god (GM II 4; TI "Errors" 7); four-great-errors; reason-in-philosophy; becoming-who-you-are.

## Where the claim forks (named by content)

Whether the conscious will does causal work: an accompaniment, the sense of deciding a story told alongside (TI "Errors" 3; GS 360; notebook 14 p.266), or real and effective as a drive-order commanding rather than a free subject choosing (BGE 19; BGE 21's "strong will"). Blame falls under either; BGE sits on the second side and TI (lesson 11) moves toward the first.

## EXERCISE PLAN

Floor check: claim to refute (ex. 2), derivation (ex. 3), boundary probe (ex. 5).

1. Retrieval (E3 from consciousness-and-language): why a creature whose consciousness speaks a subject-predicate language is prone to the doer fiction (GS 354, BGE 17, 20), in two sentences.
2. Claim to refute: "Nietzsche says we are puppets of our drives, so nobody can do anything." Use BGE 21 to show which half of the free/unfree pair he also rejects and what replaces it.
3. Derivation: run the causa sui argument on praise instead of blame; does it remove the right to admire a great composer; what survives.
4. Counter-argument stem: "The lamb says the bird of prey is evil because it could have refrained." What the judgment presupposes (GM I 13) and does for the lamb; two facts it fails to account for; what would have to be true for it to survive.
5. Boundary probe: a choice after an hour of weighing reasons; which argument has the hardest time with it, and what each side of the fork says the hour was (D 129, GS 360).
6. FORWARD (E7, reaches will-to-power-as-psychology): GS 360's "quantum of dammed-up energy" waiting for a direction: if no doer moves the drives, what is the energy and what does it want. Solution: will-to-power-as-psychology names it (`<Ref>`; BGE 13).

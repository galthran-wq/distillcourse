# Research brief: drive-psychology-and-experiment (Nietzsche's psychology and the experimental record)

status: planned
module: the-human-being (2 of 9), lesson 12 of 45
type: extra (off-path)
goal: Assess Nietzsche's claims about drives, self-knowledge and fixed types against modern findings on heritability, confabulation and attitudes

## ARC (L10)

- **Spine step (5, "Will to power"):** "One principle runs through the drives: each wants to grow and overcome resistance, not merely to survive or to stop wanting; that will to power can be spiritualized rather than extirpated, and the Four Great Errors show the whole psychology turned on morality and religion." This lesson hangs off the step as a starred detour: the module's psychology held against measurements made a century later. It is not in the spine's lesson list and no core lesson may require it.
- **Inherits:** from doer-behind-the-deed and sublimation (prerequisites), and through them the module: the drive-self, GS 354, D 129's invisible conflict, the doer fiction, will to power, the three fates of a drive. From four-great-errors (read, not required): Cornaro, referred to by `<Ref>` and not re-taught.
- **Contributes:** Nietzsche's empirical claims stated as testable propositions and set against a real body of evidence, as a record of his prescience. Introduces `type-facts`, `confabulation`. Off-path: nothing later depends on it; it enriches danger-of-dangers and genealogy-as-method for readers who take it.
- **Hands off:** to genealogy-as-method (next core lesson): if values are expressions of types, a history of values is a history of which types made them.

## STANCE (binding)

- **Favorable:** the lesson's purpose is prescience. Nietzsche claimed, before any experiment existed, that stated reasons are often not causes, that conscious attitudes predict conduct weakly, that character is set more by constitution than by upbringing, and that we are "experiments" who should "want to be" them (D 453); twin and adoption studies, the attitude-behaviour literature and confabulation research later measured claims of exactly this shape. The lesson shows which claims the record bears out and in what form, and names the one place he underrated the situation.
- **Hard passages this lesson must quote whole:** BGE 231 (bge-zimmern.txt line 4121; German line 5031; "some granite of spiritual fatum", "I am this", to its last sentence, which announces "a few truths about 'woman as such'", the opening of 232-239, read whole in herd-morality-in-modern-dress); GM III 16 (p.129; psychological pain "more probably" a matter of the belly); EH II 2 (p.240; "the spirit itself is after all merely an aspect of this metabolism"); GM Preface 2 (p.16; values "grow out of us with the necessity with which a tree bears fruit"); TI Skirmishes 33 (pp.533-534; egoism measured by ascending or descending life; pointer to module 5).
- **What the words meant:** "type" (Typus) and "constitution" are individual physiological kinds in BGE 231, GM Preface 2, EH II 2; "heredity" there is a person's inheritance; the race and breeding vocabulary belongs to other texts and is read whole where they are (master-morality, order-of-rank, the-nazi-appropriation). "Granite" in BGE 231 covers "certain decisions and answers", a floor, not every trait. "Experiment" (Versuch) is a life conducted as a trial (GS 319, 324; D 453, 501; Z I 22 §2). "Sign-language of the affects" (BGE 187) is what a system of morals is: a code in which its makers' affects show.
- **Objection answered from his side:** the findings do not test his theory: his "drives" are not the traits psychologists measure; heritability is a population statistic; situations explain a real share of conduct, which fixed types cannot. Reply, texts first: Nietzsche allows plasticity. D 560 (p.225) says one can dispose of one's drives like a gardener and calls unchangeable character a philosophers' prejudice; D 326 (p.161) says the feeling of power varies with circumstance; D 462, 534 (pp.193, 211) prescribe slow change in small doses; GS 290 gives style to what one has. So the type-claim is a floor (BGE 231's granite), and heritability estimates fit a floor. The gap, in one sentence: Nietzsche gives no method for telling a type-fact from a habit, and he underrated how far situations bend conduct; the lesson shows the numbers that say so.
- **Humanizing reading to test:** here the softening runs the other way: a scholarly reconstruction (the "Doctrine of Types", Leiter) turns scattered texts into a thesis the experiments can test, and Nietzsche becomes a proto-scientist. The texts (BGE 231, GM Preface 2, EH II 2, TI "Errors" 1-2) carry the thesis; the name and systematic form are a reader's. Second, the old brief's BGE 187 as "the same drive takes different cultural forms": BGE 187 says moral systems are "a sign-language of the affects" and lists what they aim at (justify, soothe, crucify, revenge, conceal, glorify, forget); the one-drive-many-forms claim is D 38 (p.26) and HH 107.
- **Facts against the popular image:** the "irrationalist" wrote "long live physics!" (GS 335, p.266) and asked for histories of avarice, cruelty and nourishment as a research programme (GS 7, pp.81-82); D 453 and GS 319 ask for the experimental attitude toward one's own life.

## The reconstruction

- **Claim and where.** Nietzsche's empirical claims, each tied to its text: (a) stated reasons are often not the causes of action (D 119, 129; GS 44; TI "Errors" 3-4); (b) conscious attitudes predict conduct weakly (GS 354; D 115-116); (c) traits are substantially constitutional and stable (BGE 231; GM Preface 2; EH II 2); (d) upbringing shapes the form, not the substance, of a type (D 38; HH 107; BGE 187); (e) a life can be conducted as an experiment on these claims (GS 319, 324; D 453, 501).
- **Argument in plain steps.** (1) State each claim as a proposition that could be false. (2) Name the evidence that would bear on it: for (a)-(b), sincere reasons that did not cause the act, and stated attitudes diverging from conduct; for (c)-(d), the resemblance of relatives raised apart and of unrelated children raised together. (3) Report what the record shows, each number framed: for (a), confabulation after choice and the split-brain narratives; for (b), LaPiere's couple served at all but one of about 250 establishments whose proprietors then, by letter, overwhelmingly said they would refuse them, and attitude-behaviour correlations leaving most variance unexplained; for (c)-(d), about half the variation among people in many traits heritable, and children adopted into the same home hardly more alike in personality than strangers. (4) Report what cuts against him: situational effects are real; heritability changes with environment and says nothing about one person's fixity; the measured traits are not his drives. (5) Verdict: the negative psychology (opacity, confabulation, weak conscious control) is well supported; the type-claim is supported as a floor, in the form his own D 560 allows; will to power and the drives as such are not what the experiments measure.
- **Unstated premise.** That his "drives" and the psychologists' traits are the same kind of thing; and that a population statistic bears on individual constitution, which needs the premise that the individual is typical.
- **Strongest objection and Nietzsche's reply.** As in STANCE.
- **Connections.** consciousness-and-language, doer-behind-the-deed (the claims tested); four-great-errors (Cornaro); self-as-society-of-drives, will-to-power-as-psychology (what is not tested); danger-of-dangers (BGE 228 against measured variety of types); genealogy-as-method (GS 7); becoming-who-you-are (GS 335).

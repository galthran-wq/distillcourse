# Research brief: guilt-reconstructed (Leiter's Three-Stage Reconstruction of Guilt, Tested)

status: planned
module: second-treatise
type: core (module application, L16)
goal: Test Leiter's three-stage reconstruction of the Second Treatise — conscience, bad conscience, guilt moralized — against the sections it claims.

## ARC (L10)

- **Inherits:** The Second Treatise's three inputs reconstructed separately (promise/custom; debt/creditor; internalization) and the reader's own ordering of them; the sovereign-individual dispute left open; the moral step located and unexplained.
- **Contributes:** Leiter's reconstruction stated as an explicit argument with section citations, then checked stage by stage: which sections it fits, which it must deflate (II:2's sovereign individual; II:18–19's 'pregnancy'), which it leaves unexplained (II:23–25's prophecy). The Leiter/Anderson dispute adjudicated on II:2's sentences. The verdict for the naturalist reading on this treatise: it holds, at the price of one paragraph.
- **Hands off:** To the Third Treatise (ascetic-ideal-across-types): the Second Treatise's own closing (II:21–25) says the moralized guilt is what the ascetic ideal will manage; and to the final audit, which reuses this lesson's scorecard format.

Spine step: "Second Treatise: guilt is debt moralized — a promising animal bred by the morality of custom, the creditor–debtor relation, cruelty turned inward when society caged it, escalating to a debt to God — and the sovereign individual is the naturalist reading's first strain."

**APPLICATION spec (L16).** Artefact: a published scholarly result — Leiter's three-stage reconstruction of GM II (Nietzsche on Morality, Ch. 7) — reproduced as an explicit argument and checked against the primary text section by section. Module results used: II:1–3 (memory of the will, morality of custom), II:4–8 and 19–22 (debt, escalation), II:16–18 (internalization), plus I:13 (the doer) as the moralizing premise. Departure from the idealisation: the reconstruction is clean; the text is not — II:2's sovereign individual carries achievement vocabulary the deflation must explain away, II:18–19's 'pregnancy' gives bad conscience a positive product the three stages do not use, and II:23–25's prophecy is not a stage. The lesson records each departure as a cell in a scorecard, the format the course's final audit reuses.

## Key points the lesson must cover

1. **The reconstruction stated.** Stage 1 — conscience: a bred memory of the will produced by the mnemonics of pain and the morality of custom (II:1–3). Stage 2 — bad conscience: cruelty turned inward once a pacified, enclosed society blocked its outward discharge (II:16–18). Stage 3 — guilt: bad conscience moralized when the free-will presupposition ('I could have done otherwise') is grafted on and the debt is owed to God (II:19–22, with I:13). Christianity as the maximization of indebtedness through the creator myth (II:21). Write it as numbered premises with a section beside each — the format of the whole course.
2. **Stage-by-stage check.** For each stage: the sections it cites, what they supply, what they say that the stage does not use. Stage 1 fits II:1 and II:3; II:2 supplies the process and the fruit — the fruit is not used. Stage 2 fits II:16–17; II:18's 'artist's cruelty' and the positive products are not used. Stage 3 fits II:19–22 given I:13; II:14–15's negative result (punishment does not produce guilt) is used implicitly as the reason guilt needs another route — make it explicit.
3. **The sovereign individual adjudicated.** Leiter: a natural artefact; 'free', 'autonomous', 'sovereign' as persuasive definitions relabeling a bred regularity; the passage continuous with I:13. Anderson: an ideal of self-legislation; the vocabulary of right, privilege and measure is not relabeling but ranking; a self that emerges from drives is numerically distinct from them and can be sovereign. Adjudicate on the text: list II:2's sentences, mark each as artefact-vocabulary or achievement-vocabulary, and state what each reading must say about the other's sentences (Leiter: honorifics; Anderson: a process can produce an achiever). Verdict as fact: the passage underdetermines; the deflation is the cheaper reading given I:13, at the cost of calling one paragraph rhetoric; Leiter's reply to Anderson (question-begging against drive-reduction) and Anderson's counter noted.
4. **The 'pregnancy' remainder.** II:18–19 make bad conscience the origin of ideals and beauty; the three stages use it only as guilt's material. State what the naturalist reading loses if it drops the positive product (the revaluation of suffering in Module 6 needs it) and that nothing in the three stages prevents keeping it — a remainder, not a refutation.
5. **The prophecy.** II:23–25 is not a stage: 'the Antichrist and antinihilist' who will 'redeem us from the great nausea' — the treatise's own statement that the moralized guilt is a problem the ascetic ideal will administer (III) and a counter-ideal must overcome (Modules 6). The reconstruction ends where the text points forward.
6. **The scorecard.** Rows: II:1–3, II:2 (the fruit), II:4–8, II:9–15, II:16–17, II:18–19, II:19–22, II:23–25. Columns: 'fits stage n', 'deflated', 'unused', 'not a stage'. Filled in the lesson; the final audit reuses the format for the whole course.

## Pitfalls / subtleties to flag

- Leiter is toc-only: the three stages are attested across the dossier (Reginster's review, secondary summaries, Leiter's own restatements) but the lesson must not quote Leiter's prose; it states the reconstruction 'as Leiter's, Ch. 7' and the human verifies.
- Adjudicating is not refuting: the verdict is 'underdetermined; deflation cheaper given I:13' — stated as fact with the cost named. No 'Leiter is right'.
- The scorecard's cells must cite Nietzsche's sections (verifiable on disk), never the secondary pages.
- Do not preview the ascetic priest beyond II:23–25's own words.
- The application genre: this is 'a paper's result reproduced and checked'; say so in one sentence at the top so the format contract is visible.

## EXERCISE PLAN (floor: ≤1 warm-up; ≥2 of boundary / refute / derivation / research; one FORWARD)

1. (warm-up) State the three stages with one section each and the one premise from another treatise the third stage needs.
2. (refute) "Leiter's reconstruction ignores half the Second Treatise (II:9–15 on the community, justice and what punishment does not do)." Break it: show that II:14–15's negative result is a premise the reconstruction needs (guilt cannot come from punishment, so it must come from internalization), and state where II:9–11 fit (the community as creditor — stage 3's route, not a fourth stage).
3. (derivation) Reconstruct Anderson's reading of II:2 as an explicit argument (three premises: achievement vocabulary; the emergent self; sovereignty as ranking), then write Leiter's reply as an objection to one premise, and identify which premise the text of II:2 actually decides — if any.
4. (boundary probe) Suppose I:13 were false — there is a doer behind the deed. Which of the three stages survive unchanged, and what happens to stage 3's 'moralization' (does it become correct rather than an error)? State what this shows about how much of the Second Treatise's critical force rests on the First.
5. (research-shaped) Fill the scorecard yourself for II:16–22 from the Samuel text before opening the lesson's version; then compare and record each disagreement with the section that decides it.
6. (translation comparison) 'Vermoralisierung' (II:21) and 'zurückgeschoben in das schlechte Gewissen': compare Samuel with the German (and a verified modern rendering); state whether 'moralization' or 'moralizing' preserves the claim that a pre-existing state (bad conscience) was reinterpreted, and why stage 3 needs that reading.
7. (FORWARD → ascetic-ideal-across-types) II:24 says the counter-ideal must come from 'a different kind of spirit… strengthened by wars and victories'. Write what the Second Treatise's moralized guilt is still lacking as an explanation of morality's victory (who administers the guilt, and to what end); the solution says the Third Treatise's ascetic ideal is the administration and its first lesson is next.
8. (derivation) Janaway's grid has four cells and Leiter's story three stages. Derive which cell each stage occupies, find the cell no stage names (simple cruelty to self, uninterpreted), and state from II:16 whether Nietzsche describes that state or skips to guilt — the solution says the grid predicts a pre-moral self-cruelty that II:16's 'bad conscience in its beginnings' matches.

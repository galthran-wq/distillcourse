# Research brief: heidegger-one-thought (Heidegger: Will to Power and Eternal Recurrence as One Metaphysical Thought)

status: planned
module: where-the-reading-breaks
type: core
goal: Formulate Heidegger's reading — will to power as what beings are, eternal recurrence as how they are, one thought named five ways, Nietzsche as the last metaphysician — and evaluate its dependence on the Nachlass against the published-works rule.

## ARC (L10)

- **Inherits:** The compilation's editorial status (will-to-power-the-book); BGE 36 sorted (the cosmological reading question-begging); the fable read Clark's way; the recurrence's cosmological reading and its Nachlass base; Nehamas's reading as the first rival; 'On Self-Overcoming' read as testimony.
- **Contributes:** The second rival at its strongest: Heidegger's apparatus (guiding vs. grounding question), the unity thesis (will to power = essentia, eternal recurrence = existentia; 'to stamp Becoming with the character of Being'; five rubrics as one thought), the fable reread as 'twisting free', the gateway Moment and the 'smallest gap' (Heidegger's own rejection of the dwarf's circle), Nietzsche as the last metaphysician of subjectivity ('will to will'). The evidence base examined: Vol. I §2's citation convention (WM numbers; the 1906 compilation as text), Krell's audit — and what survives if only published passages count. Verdict as fact: the reading holds text the naturalist deflates (the unity of the two thoughts; the metaphysical register of BGE 36 and Z) at the price of the published-works rule.
- **Hands off:** To alleged-biologism (Heidegger's direct argument against naturalism); to the audit.

Spine step: "Where the reading breaks: Nehamas turns truth into character and a life into a text; Heidegger turns the psychology into metaphysics and argues that no science can ground its own field-concept; the audit states which theses each reading can hold as arguments and where the naturalist hypothesis stands."

## Key points the lesson must cover

1. **The apparatus, in Heidegger's terms and one paragraph each.** The guiding question of metaphysics: 'what is a being?' (ti to on); the grounding question metaphysics never poses: 'what is Being itself?'; a 'fundamental metaphysical position' as an answer to the guiding question; Nietzsche read as giving the last such answer (Vol. I §11; Vol. II §25). The reader needs these three terms and no more of Heidegger's vocabulary; define each at first use.
2. **The unity thesis reconstructed.** Will to power answers 'what beings are' (their constitution, essentia); eternal recurrence answers 'how beings are as a whole' (their way to be, existentia — 'that-being'); the two are one projection seen from two sides (Vol. II §26; Vol. III Part Two's six steps — reproduce the six as a numbered list from the text); WM 617's 'to stamp Becoming with the character of Being — that is the supreme will to power' as the single sentence in which the two meet (Nachlass); Parmenides ('being is') and Heraclitus ('being becomes') fused — 'the end of metaphysics' closing the ring the Greeks opened. The five rubrics (Vol. IV §1): nihilism, revaluation, will to power, eternal recurrence, overman as one thought named five ways — 'the most essential thought (recurrence) is thought first'. Reconstruct the unity argument as premises with the WM/GM/Z citations Heidegger gives, marking each as Nachlass or published.
3. **Heidegger's own refusals — the reading at its strongest.** The 'proof' of recurrence is not natural science but 'the unfolding of a projection' (Vol. II §§14–16); the dwarf's 'time is a circle' is the cheap reading — the Moment is where the lanes collide for one who decides (Vol. II §§6, 24 — the 'smallest gap' between 'everything is indifferent' and 'nothing is indifferent'); the animals' ditty is 'a song for barrel organs' (Vol. II §8); will to power is not a psychological drive or Schopenhauer's striving but self-command (Vol. I §7); Baeumler's and Jaspers's dismissals of recurrence refuted (Vol. I §4). State these because the caricature ('Heidegger reads Nietzsche as a cosmologist') is not his reading; his reading is metaphysical, and he rejects the cosmological cliché as firmly as Clark does.
4. **The fable reread (Vol. I §24).** Stage six as 'twisting free' (Herausdrehung) of Platonism, not escape: the new hierarchy of sensuous over non-sensuous (§25) is still a ranking inside the guiding question; dated to 1888 — Nietzsche's last move. Against real-world-became-a-fable's Clarkian reading (the pair abolished, ordinary truth kept): Heidegger says the pair is abolished and the ranking remains — metaphysics completed, not left. State both readings' claim about stage six and the sentence each cites ('with the true world we have also abolished the apparent one'; 'a new interpretation of sensuousness').
5. **Nietzsche as the last metaphysician of subjectivity (Vol. IV §§14–22).** Protagoras → Descartes → Nietzsche on the same four axes (how man is himself; the projection of Being; the essence of truth; the measure); the cogito attacked by a stricter subjectivity (the body); Hegel's rationalitas and Nietzsche's animalitas exhausting one possibility-space; 'will to power is in truth the will-to-will' (Vol. IV Part Two); the release into 'machination' — technology — as the destination. One section; the reader has Descartes.
6. **The evidence base.** Vol. I §2: Heidegger declares the 1906/1911 compilation his text and gives the citation convention (WM n); the plans (§3) and Krell's ~140-plan catalogue; Krell's audit of the GOA against Colli–Montinari; the bracketed 1961 addition (Vol. III §3 p. 21) in which will to power becomes 'Being's abandonment of beings' — the reading revising itself; the 'twisting free' dated by a published text (Twilight) but the unity thesis by WM 617. Run the published-works rule: what survives (BGE 36 as an interpretation; Z's three stagings; Twilight's fable; GS 341) and what does not (WM 617; the finite-force proof; WM 1067's indicative). Verdict as fact: the unity thesis is stated in a Nachlass sentence and dramatized in Zarathustra; the published works neither assert it nor deny it; the reading holds the metaphysical register of BGE 36 and Z that the naturalist deflates ('suppose'; 'testimony'), and pays the evidence rule.
7. **Krell's built-in countercritique.** Fink's Dionysos; 'the decadence of redemption' (the Antichrist's redeemer-type turned on Heidegger's 'redemption from revenge'); Deleuze and Klossowski against metaphysical closure — one paragraph: the canonical reading carries its own rebuttals, which the audit may use.

## Pitfalls / subtleties to flag

- Heidegger's vocabulary must be limited to three defined terms; the lesson is about his reading of Nietzsche, not about Being and Time.
- The caricature (cosmologist) is refuted in the lesson; do not reintroduce it in the verdict.
- The Internet Archive marks the Krell translation public domain; the underlying edition may be in copyright — quote sparingly and verify before long quotations.
- The four volumes are 1936–46 lectures; the political context (Baeumler as a Nazi-aligned editor; Heidegger's own position) is one sentence, dry, in the evidence section, and no more.
- Do not run the biologism argument here (next lesson).

## EXERCISE PLAN (floor: ≤1 warm-up; ≥2 of boundary / refute / derivation / research; one FORWARD)

1. (warm-up) State the unity thesis in two lines (what each doctrine answers) and name the one sentence Heidegger cites for their meeting, with its evidential status.
2. (refute) "Heidegger reads eternal recurrence as a cosmological theory of a repeating universe." Break it with Vol. II §§14–16 (the proof as projection) and §§6, 24 (the dwarf's circle rejected); state what Heidegger's reading is instead (a metaphysical answer to how beings are) and why that is harder for the naturalist to dismiss than the cosmological cliché.
3. (derivation) Reconstruct the six-step unity argument as premises and mark each as Nachlass or published; then derive what remains of the conclusion if the Nachlass premises are removed (the two doctrines as compatible, not one), and state which reading of BGE 36 and GS 341 that residue supports.
4. (boundary probe) Suppose the published-works rule were relaxed to admit Nachlass fragments that are dated and repeated (WM 617's thought recurs in several 1887–88 notebooks). Would Heidegger's unity thesis then be grounded? State what 'grounded' requires (an author-authorized location) and whether repetition in drafts supplies it — and what Krell's audit of the GOA adds.
5. (research-shaped) Compare Heidegger's reading of Twilight's stage six (Vol. I §24, 'twisting free') with Clark's (Ch. 4, the pair abolished) as two readings of one published sentence: reconstruct each as an argument, find the sentence in 'Reason in Philosophy' §6 or the fable's stage six that each must explain, and report whether the published text decides between them.
6. (translation comparison) 'Wille zur Macht' as Krell's 'will to power' and Heidegger's 'Wille zum Willen' as 'will to will'; 'Herausdrehung' as 'twisting free'; state what each rendering commits the reading to (a faculty? a self-relation?) and whether Norman's BGE 19 ('under-wills') can be read in Heidegger's sense.
7. (FORWARD → alleged-biologism) Heidegger's reading has so far been exegetical (what the texts say) and evidential (which texts count). Write the one argument of his that is neither — a claim about what any science can and cannot do — and find it in Vol. III Part One §6's title; the solution says the next lesson reconstructs the 'alleged biologism' argument as the direct challenge to methodological naturalism.

# Research brief: herd-morality-in-modern-dress (Herd morality in modern dress: equality, democracy, socialism, emancipation)

status: planned
module: what-morality-does (5 of 9), lesson 25 of 45
type: core
goal: Explain Nietzsche's claim that modern egalitarian ideals are Christian morality surviving the death of the God that founded it, and evaluate his contempt for them

## ARC (L10)

- **Spine step (8, "What morality does to us"):** "What this morality does to us: pity multiplies suffering and spends the strong, the whole system rests on false beliefs about the self and harms the highest human beings, and it survives the death of God in the secular dress of equality, democracy, socialism and emancipation, which Twilight's Skirmishes diagnose as the modern form of decline"
- **Inherits:** from death-of-god, the shadows of God (GS 108) and the claim that "the whole of our European morality" was built on the faith (GS 343); from slave-revolt-and-ressentiment, the inversion and BGE 46's French Revolution as "the last great slave insurrection"; from consciousness-and-language, the herd instinct (GS 116); from danger-of-dangers, the charge sheet and BGE 202's "I am morality itself".
- **Contributes:** the prediction and its forms: after God, Europe keeps the Christian valuation as equal rights, democracy, socialism, the religion of pity and the emancipation of woman, and the herd's morality claims to be morality itself. Introduces herd-morality. The emancipation-of-woman passages are presented whole and read for the argument they make.
- **Hands off:** to skirmishes-of-an-untimely-man, where TI 33-41 run this diagnosis over egoism, freedom, democracy and the labour question and name the standard (ascending or descending life); to nietzsche-and-politics and the-nazi-appropriation, where GS 377 and BGE 251 are read for the politics question; to real-world-became-a-fable, since values asserted as "inherent" after God belong to the true world that is about to go the same way.

## STANCE (binding)

- **Favorable:** Nietzsche predicted in 1886-87 what the twentieth century did: Europe kept Christian morality after losing the Christian God, and he named the forms — equal rights, democracy, socialism, "the religion of pity", the abolition of suffering, the emancipation of woman — and the mechanism, "English consistency" (TI Skirmishes 5). The lesson shows the claim as a genealogical discovery (the equality of souls before God becomes equal rights; love of neighbour becomes solidarity; the kingdom becomes progress) and shows why it is compelling from inside: a value whose only ground was a dead God is held up by nothing, and BGE 202's herd that says "I am morality itself, and nothing besides is morality!" has forgotten that it is one morality among possible others.
- **Hard passages this lesson must quote whole:** BGE 199-203 (`bge-zimmern.txt` lines 2901-2963; German `bge-german.txt`), 202 in full: "Morality in Europe today is herd-animal morality" ("Heerdenthier-Moral"), "the democratic movement is the heir of the Christian movement", the anarchists and socialists "at one in their faith in the morality of mutual pity" ("Religion des Mitleidens"); 203: "the degeneracy and dwarfing of man to an absolutely gregarious animal". BGE 231-239 (lines 4121-4346; German lines 5026-5301), the whole stretch: 231's "some truths about 'woman as she is' ... they are merely — MY truths" ("meine Wahrheiten"); 232's "Woman wishes to be independent ... one of the worst developments of the general UGLIFYING of Europe", "her great art is falsehood"; 238's "he must conceive of her as a possession, as confinable property ... upon the immense rationality of Asia"; 239's "WOMAN RETROGRADES", "the 'emancipation of woman' ... proves to be a remarkable symptom of the increased weakening and deadening of the most womanly instincts", "as though slavery were a counter-argument, and not rather a condition of every higher culture", "her first and last function, that of bearing robust children". EH III 5 (pp.266-268, `eccehomo-full.txt`): "Perhaps I am the first psychologist of the eternally feminine"; "Woman is indescribably more evil than man; also cleverer"; "The fight for equal rights is actually a symptom of a disease"; "Love — in its means, war; at bottom, the deadly hatred of the sexes"; "how one cures a woman — 'redeems' her? One gives her a child"; "'Emancipation of women!' — that is the instinctive hatred of the abortive woman, who is incapable of giving birth, against the woman who is turned out well"; "the preaching of chastity ... the real sin against the holy spirit of life". Z I 18 (p.179, `zarathustra-full.txt`): "You are going to women? Do not forget the whip!" GS 377 (pp.338-340, `gayscience-s2-bk4-bk5-appendix.txt`), whole: "we are no humanitarians", "Humanity! Has there ever been a more hideous old woman among all old women (unless it were 'Truth')?", the "new slavery" that "every strengthening and elevation of the type 'man'" involves. GS 361 and 363 (pp.316-319): the actor, "Jews" and "women"; A 43 (p.619): "the poison of the doctrine of 'equal rights for all'"; A 57 (p.646): "The inequality of rights is the first condition for the existence of any rights"; TI Skirmishes 38 (p.542): the well-being "dreamed of by shopkeepers, Christians, cows, females, Englishmen, and other democrats"; TI Skirmishes 48 (p.553): equality as "poisonous poison". BGE 251 (line 4711) belongs to nietzsche-and-politics; one sentence with a <Ref>.
- **What the words meant:** "herd" (Heerde) and "herd instinct" are GS 116's "morality is herd instinct in the individual" and BGE 199's instinct of obedience bred over millennia; "herd-animal morality" is one type of morality that denies there are others (BGE 202). "Levellers" (Nivellirer, BGE 44) are the false free spirits who want "the universal green-meadow happiness of the herd". "Modern ideas" (BGE 44, 253; EH II 5) is Nietzsche's standing phrase for the democratic-utilitarian package. "The democratic movement" is "a form of the decay of political organization" and "of man" (BGE 203). "Woman as she is"/"woman as such" (das Weib an sich, BGE 231) is the object of the "truths" he labels "MY truths" — his own framing, which the lesson takes as the key to the whole stretch: these are, by his own account, the "granite of spiritual fate" of one thinker, stated as such (231). "Abortive" (missrathen) and "turned out well" (wohlgerathen, EH III 5) are the physiological type-words module 2 met, here applied to women. "Emancipation" (Emancipation des Weibes) is read by him as a symptom of the same movement as equal rights: a defeminising, "an almost masculine stupidity" (BGE 239). "The eternal war between the sexes" (EH III 5) and "the profoundest antagonism ... an eternally hostile tension" (BGE 238) state his premise about nature. "The whip" (Z I 18) is spoken by a little old woman to Zarathustra as her "little truth"; Kaufmann (pp.120, 263) reads Z III 15, where life and Zarathustra dance, as the image's reversal — his reading. "Humanitarians" (GS 377) and "English consistency" (TI Skirmishes 5) name the keepers of the morality without the God. "New slavery" (GS 377) is BGE 257's "slavery in some sense", read in order-of-rank.
- **Objection answered from his side:** the strongest philosophical objection has two halves. (a) The genealogy of equality through Christianity is widely granted by historians, but equality has other grounds (Stoic, Kantian, utilitarian), and the ressentiment motive is asserted, not shown; moreover the prediction of fragility can be tested, and the values have lasted a century without the God. Reply from the texts: TI Skirmishes 5 — "when one gives up the Christian faith, one pulls the right to Christian morality out from under one's feet"; GS 343 and 108 — values outlive their ground as God's shadow, and that is precisely his complaint, not a refutation of it; BGE 202-203 predict not collapse but degeneration under the values; the question is whether the values are held up by anything, not whether they are held. Gap sentence: whether the type has been "dwarfed" by equality is not decidable from his texts, and the lesson states the prediction as open. (b) On woman: the argument of BGE 238-239 rests on a premise about "nature" ("her NATURE, which is more 'natural' than that of man") that is asserted, and 239's "WOMAN RETROGRADES" was a prediction about influence that the record since 1886 bears on. Reply from the texts: BGE 231 frames the whole stretch as "MY truths", the "unteachable" in one thinker — Nietzsche himself marks the claims as the fatum of a type, which is exactly module 2's doctrine of types applied to its author; 239 distinguishes "rights and claims" from "influence", so the record of rights does not by itself answer him; EH III 5's "the fight for equal rights is actually a symptom of a disease" is the same diagnosis as BGE 202's, applied to women, and stands or falls with it. Gap sentence: the premise about the sexes' nature is stated, never argued, and 231's "MY truths" concedes as much.
- **Humanizing reading to test:** Kaufmann's notes to GS 377 (nn.147-148) that the section must be read in its anti-nationalist context and is quotable out of context to the contrary; his n.93 that GS 361 and 363 are on a "lower level"; his judgment that GS 60-75 on women is "strikingly inferior"; his reading of Z III 15 as the whip reversed (pp.120, 263); and the reading that the passages on woman are a lapse separable from the philosophy. What the text says: GS 377 attacks nationalism and race hatred and in the same breath the religion of pity, equal rights, the free society, and affirms a "new slavery"; BGE 239 derives the emancipation argument from BGE 202's levelling and from 257's slavery; EH III 5 applies the type-vocabulary to women as a case of the same diagnosis. The passages are of a piece with the argument, and the lesson reads them as such.
- **Facts against the popular image:** in the lesson's texts: GS 377 — "we are not nearly 'German' enough ... to advocate nationalism and race hatred"; BGE 241-243 on "good Europeans"; TI Skirmishes 39 (p.543) on the "German Reich" as a hybrid of decline; BGE 251's demand to expel the "anti-Semitic bawlers" (with its other edge; <Ref> nietzsche-and-politics). Reported because the texts say so.

## The reconstruction

- **Claim and where.** BGE 202: "the democratic movement is the heir of the Christian movement"; morality in Europe today is herd-animal morality claiming to be morality itself. TI Skirmishes 5: giving up the faith removes the right to the morality. GS 377: the homeless reject the whole package — nationalism and race hatred, the religion of pity, progress, equal rights, the free society — and affirm "new slavery". BGE 239 and EH III 5: the emancipation of woman is a symptom of the same movement.
- **Argument in plain steps.** (1) Christian morality is herd morality that had a ground: the equality of souls before God, love of neighbour, the kingdom to come (BGE 199-202; GS 116). (2) The ground has gone (death-of-god); the values remain as God's shadow (GS 108, 343). (3) Held without their ground, they take secular forms: equal souls become equal rights; love of neighbour becomes solidarity, socialism, "the religion of pity"; the kingdom becomes progress and the abolition of suffering (BGE 202; D 132; TI Skirmishes 34-35). (4) The herd, which bred obedience for millennia (BGE 199), does not notice it holds one morality among possible others and says "I am morality itself" (BGE 202; D 164). (5) The same levelling reaches the relation of the sexes: where the industrial spirit has triumphed over the military and aristocratic, woman "strives for the economic and legal independence of a clerk", unlearns fear of man, and "retrogrades" — emancipation as defeminising, "a symptom of the increased weakening and deadening of the most womanly instincts" (BGE 239; EH III 5: "the fight for equal rights is actually a symptom of a disease"). (6) The upshot is decline of the type: "the dwarfing of man to an absolutely gregarious animal" (BGE 203), the last man (Z Prologue 5), and the hope is placed in new philosophers who command (BGE 203) and in a "new slavery" (GS 377).
- **Unstated premise.** That a value's only possible ground was the one it historically had (step 2-3), so that once the God is gone nothing else could hold it up; and in step 5, the premise about the nature of the sexes, which BGE 231 itself marks as "MY truths".
- **Strongest objection and Nietzsche's reply.** As in STANCE: other grounds for equality and the fragility test (reply: TI Skirmishes 5; GS 108/343; the gap sentence on the "dwarfing" prediction); the argument from nature about woman (reply: 231's framing; 239's rights/influence distinction; the gap sentence on the unargued premise).
- **Connections.** death-of-god (the shadow); slave-revolt-and-ressentiment (BGE 46, 195; GM I 16's French Revolution; the tarantulas); against-pity (BGE 202's religion of pity; GS 377); danger-of-dangers (BGE 202's "I am morality itself"); skirmishes-of-an-untimely-man (TI 34-41 read whole); order-of-rank (GS 377's "new slavery"; BGE 239's "slavery ... a condition of every higher culture"; A 57); overman-and-last-man (Z Prologue 5); nietzsche-and-politics (GS 377, BGE 251); the-nazi-appropriation (what in these texts could be used without forgery); drive-psychology-and-experiment (BGE 231's "granite of spiritual fate" is the lesson's own key text on type-facts, turned on its author).

## Pitfalls

No sentence of moral distancing.
- The BGE reader did not read 232-239; the lesson writer reads the Zimmern and German text directly (lines given above) before quoting.
- Z I 18's whip is spoken by the little old woman; name the speaker; Kaufmann's "reversal" in III 15 is his reading.
- "Herd" is a type of valuation and a bred instinct; the lesson defines it at first use and does not let it become an insult.
- The old brief's Universal Declaration of Human Rights application is gone with the lesson's change of type; a modern document may appear only as an exercise, never as the lesson's artifact.

4. Boundary probe: a right that protects the exceptional individual against the herd (freedom of thought and expression). Does Nietzsche's lens classify it as herd morality? Use BGE 44 (the real free spirits) and GS 377 to decide, and say what the case shows about the lens's reach.
5. Research-shaped: read BGE 239 as a prediction ("WOMAN RETROGRADES", influence declining as rights increase) and state what kind of evidence since 1886 bears on it, which of its two terms (rights, influence) the evidence can reach, and what Nietzsche's own distinction between them lets him reply.
6. FORWARD (reaches skirmishes-of-an-untimely-man): BGE 203 and GS 377 judge the democratic movement a decline. Ask by what measure. The solution notes that <Ref> skirmishes-of-an-untimely-man finds the measure named in TI Skirmishes 33: whether a person or culture "represents the ascending or the descending line of life".

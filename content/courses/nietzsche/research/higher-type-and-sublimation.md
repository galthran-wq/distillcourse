# Research brief: higher-type-and-sublimation (The Higher Type: Pathos of Distance and Sublimation)

status: planned
module: affirmation
type: core
goal: Formulate Nietzsche's positive ideal from BGE 257–262, Twilight's 'Morality as Anti-Nature' and GS 290 — the higher type, spiritualization of the passions, giving style to one's character — and state the normative component of the critique of morality it grounds.

## ARC (L10)

- **Inherits:** MPS's normative component named but not grounded (genealogy-as-method: 'altruism, equality, pity harm higher men'); the pathos of distance (master-and-slave-morality); will to power as overcoming resistance; bad conscience's positive product ('artist's cruelty'); affirmation and its test; the reader's forward question — what type answers the demon.
- **Contributes:** The higher type reconstructed from the published works: BGE 257–259 (aristocratic society, the sipo matador, life as appropriation — how much of the political premise the ideal needs); Twilight 'Morality as Anti-Nature' (spiritualization vs. castration of the passions; 'the value of life cannot be estimated' — the is–ought bridge's status); GS 290 (giving style to one's character); BGE 212, A §§3–4 (the higher type as 'lucky accident'); the two analytic reconstructions (Kaufmann's sublimation; Janaway's greatness conditions — strength, multiplicity, unity in conflict). The normative component grounded: MPS harms this type — with Reginster's objection (one ideal unevenly realized, not many flourishings; Leiter 'democratizes').
- **Hands off:** To value-creation-and-normativity (the ideal named; now how a naturalist can call it good); to overman-staged (the ideal dramatized).

Spine step: "The positive doctrine: nihilism splits into disorientation and despair, affirmation is tested by eternal recurrence, the higher type is defined by pathos of distance and sublimation — and value creation is where a naturalist must say how values can be made rather than found; the overman and the last human being are its dramatization."

## Key points the lesson must cover

1. **The need.** The normative charge — MPS harms the highest human beings — has been used since genealogy-as-method without an account of what a highest human being is; Leiter's Ch. 4 names the traits (solitude, the pursuit of a unifying project, self-reverence, affirmation, an instinct for what benefits one's own health); the reader's forward question from the demon was which type could say 'you are a god'. State the need as the lesson's problem.
2. **BGE 257–259: the aristocratic premise and how much is needed.** §257: every enhancement of the type 'man' has been the work of an aristocratic society; the pathos of distance between castes yields the 'other, more mysterious pathos' — the distance within the soul, 'the self-overcoming of man'. §258: the corrupt aristocracy that throws away its privileges; the healthy one that takes itself as the meaning and justification of the society — the sipo matador (the vine that climbs the oak and flowers above it). §259: 'life itself is essentially appropriation, injury, overpowering… exploitation'; 'exploitation' as 'a consequence of the essence of what is living' — a primary organic function, will to power. Reconstruct the argument from §257 to the ideal: (P) the enhancement of the type requires a felt hierarchy; (P) the felt hierarchy between castes is the origin of the hierarchy within the soul; (C) the ideal (self-overcoming) needs the pathos of distance. Then separate what the ideal uses (the inner pathos — a soul that ranks its own drives) from what §257 asserts historically (an aristocratic society as the precondition) and §259 asserts biologically (exploitation as essence): the ideal's argument needs the first; the second and third are asserted, in the register the course has graded since Module 2. State it plainly; do not soften §259.
3. **Twilight, 'Morality as Anti-Nature': spiritualization.** §1: the Church's practice against the passions is 'castration' — 'the extirpation of the passions'; 'we others… love the passions'; §1–2 spiritualization: the passion made into a spirit ('the spiritualization of sensuality is called love'; 'the spiritualization of enmity' — having enemies as a condition of one's own strength); §4 anti-natural morality condemns the instincts of life; §5 'the value of life cannot be estimated' — a living person is a party to the dispute, not a judge; a judgment on life is a symptom. Reconstruct: (P) the passions are the material of every achievement; (P) extirpation removes the material; spiritualization transforms it; (C) the ideal's method is transformation — Kaufmann's 'sublimation': the great soul as the passionate man who masters his passions. §5's principle is also where the is–ought bridge is disowned: if life cannot be evaluated, 'good = heightens power' (A §2) is a symptom-report, not a verdict — flag for value-creation.
4. **GS 290: giving style.** 'One thing is needful: to "give style" to one's character'; surveying strengths and weaknesses, fitting them into an artistic plan until each appears as art and reason; the weak who hate constraint. Reconstruct: the ideal at the scale of one life — the soul's rank-order (BGE 12, 19) as a made order. SEP §3.2.4 and §4: self-fashioning; Nehamas's authorship reading (forward) vs. Millgram's disunified agent. GS 335: 'we want to become who we are — the new, the unique, the incomparable, those who give themselves laws' — the forward doorstep to value-creation.
5. **BGE 212, A §§3–4: the type's rarity.** The philosopher as 'the bad conscience of his time'; the higher type 'has appeared often — as a lucky accident, never willed'; not a breeding programme ('not what should replace mankind'); BGE 62's 'the sovereign religions' as having preserved 'too much of what ought to perish' — the harm charge in its strongest published form. State the strongest form and the weakest, and that the lesson keeps both on the table.
6. **The two analytic reconstructions and the objection.** Kaufmann: sublimation — power as the single explanatory principle; greatness as mastery of passion. Janaway: greatness as strength, multiplicity, and conflict-held-in-unity of drives; morality as both symptom of and danger to healthy drive-states; eternal recurrence as the test of that state. Leiter: MPS harms the higher type — the type-relative reading (each type has its flourishing). Reginster's objection (NDPR): Nietzsche has one ideal unevenly realized, not many flourishings — elitism, not pluralism; Leiter 'democratizes'. State the fault line as open; what is settled is that the normative charge has a subject.

## Pitfalls / subtleties to flag

- §259 is the passage the political reading rests on; the lesson quotes it whole, grades it (asserted; biological; the ideal's argument needs the inner pathos, not this), and does not editorialize.
- 'Sublimation' is Kaufmann's word (Nietzsche's is Vergeistigung; 'Sublimirung' occurs in HH 1); say so.
- Kaufmann and J&R are on disk in full — quote; Leiter Ch. 4 is toc-only, so the normative component of MPS is stated in the terms Reginster's review and Leiter's restatements verify, and the harm claim is taken from Hurka's and Robertson's quotations of Leiter.
- The elitism/pluralism fault line is an open scholarly dispute; the lesson states both readings' texts and does not decide.
- No breeding-programme reading is licensed by A §§3–4; the overman lesson refutes it — one sentence here.

## EXERCISE PLAN (floor: ≤1 warm-up; ≥2 of boundary / refute / derivation / research; one FORWARD)

1. (warm-up) List the three published sources of the ideal (BGE 257, TI 'Anti-Nature', GS 290) with the one sentence from each that states its contribution.
2. (refute) "BGE 259 shows Nietzsche's ideal is domination of others." Break it by separating what the ideal's argument uses (§257's inner pathos; GS 290's self-ordering; TI's spiritualization — all about the soul's own drives) from what §259 asserts about life; then state what §259 does say and why refuting the misreading does not remove it from the record.
3. (derivation) Derive from Reginster's three conditions and TI 'Anti-Nature' §1 why extirpation of a passion lowers power and spiritualization raises it: which condition each operation affects (the end; the resistance; the overcoming), and state what the derivation assumes about passions as resistances.
4. (boundary probe) Consider a type with maximal strength and multiplicity of drives and no unity (Janaway's third condition absent). On BGE 19 and GS 290, can it will or give style at all? State what unity contributes and whether Millgram's 'disunified agent' reading (SEP §4) could count it as Nietzsche's ideal.
5. (research-shaped, sourcing) Read BGE 62 and A §4 as two statements of the harm charge; run records / argues / omits on each (what type is named; what harm; what is omitted — the mechanism by which MPS harms a type, which GM III:14 supplies); report which is the strongest published form and what evidence it offers.
6. (missing premise) TI §5 says the value of life cannot be estimated; A §2 says 'what is good? all that heightens the feeling of power'. Write the premise that would reconcile them (A §2 as a type's symptom-report, not a judgment on life) and state what that does to the normative charge (it becomes 'MPS harms this type', not 'MPS is bad') — and which reading (Leiter's) that supports.
7. (FORWARD → value-creation-and-normativity) GS 335 says the ideal is 'those who give themselves laws'. Write what 'giving oneself a law' must mean if there is no doer behind the deed (I:13) and no value in being (the fable) — a drive-order that ranks itself — and state the problem this raises for calling any such order 'good'; the solution says the next lesson is that problem under the name value creation.

# Research brief: jesus-and-paul (The Antichrist on Jesus and Paul read whole)

status: planned

goal: Assess The Antichrist 24-47 as a test of the slave-revolt mechanism, finding where it fits, where Nietzsche himself exempts Jesus, and where the text goes past the module

## ARC (L10)

- **Spine step (6, "Good and evil"):** "Values have a history: the nobles called themselves good and the rest bad; the oppressed, unable to strike back, invented evil and called their weakness good, and Christianity is that revolt's victory, which The Antichrist traces from Jesus to Paul"
- **Inherits:** from slave-revolt-and-ressentiment, the mechanism (blocked revenge, inversion, imaginary revenge) with I 10's marks of the reactive, and the expressive reading with the priest as knowing agent; from master-morality, the asymmetry and the priestly aristocracy; from genealogy-as-method, reinterpretation as the way a practice acquires a new meaning (GM II 12; GS 353); from the reader's background, the Gospels, the cross, Paul's letters, the Last Judgment.
- **Contributes:** the module's mechanism run over twenty-four consecutive sections of a text Nietzsche finished, with the twist that makes the application honest: he finds no ressentiment in Jesus and lays the revolt at the disciples' and Paul's door. Introduces redeemer-type.
- **Hands off:** to module 4: Paul's gospel turns on guilt, sacrifice and a debt paid on the cross (A 41-43; GM II 21), and to understand that Nietzsche goes further back, to the origin of guilt in debt (debt-and-punishment). Forward also to herd-morality-in-modern-dress: A 43's "equal rights for all" as Christian dynamite.

## STANCE (binding)

- **Favorable:** The Antichrist is Nietzsche's psychology of religion at full stretch: he separates a founder's practice from the doctrine built on his death, reconstructs the founder as a type from the "psychology of the evangel" rather than from the creeds, and shows the doctrine as the work of the very passion his Genealogy had described. The lesson shows how much of this was new in 1888 — a reading of the Gospels as the record of a revolt that the revolt's heirs wrote — and why the exemption of Jesus is the strongest part, not a softening: a mechanism that fits everything would explain nothing.
- **Hard passages this lesson must quote whole:** A 24-26 (`portable-late.txt`, pp.590-597): the Jewish priests' "falsification" of Israel's history, "the priest lives on sins", "the instinct of ressentiment, which had here become genius" (24, p.593); A 44 (pp.620-622): the Gospels as "the art of holy lying", "The Christian is merely a Jew of 'more liberal' persuasion" (p.622), and the "Jew" stereotypes of the section; A 45 (pp.624-625): Paul "the greatest of all apostles of vengeance", 1 Corinthians 1:20ff read as "chandala morality"; A 46 (p.626): "one does well to put on gloves when reading the New Testament ... We would no more choose the 'first Christians' to associate with than Polish Jews — not that one even required any objection to them: they both do not smell good"; and Pilate's "What is truth?" as the one figure in the New Testament "who commands respect". Beside them, quoted whole as well: A 55 (pp.640-641): "An anti-Semite certainly is not any more decent because he lies as a matter of principle." GM I 16 (pp.52-54, `genealogy--text.txt`): "three Jews, as is known, and one Jewess". A 29 (pp.600-601): "To make a hero of Jesus! ... even an entirely different word would still be more nearly fitting here — the word idiot", with Kaufmann's note that the sister cut the last three words.
- **What the words meant:** "idiot" (A 29) is the Dostoevskian word, Prince Myshkin's type — "a mixture of the sublime, the sick, and the childlike" (A 31), a being without resistance; Kaufmann's note (p.601) records that Elisabeth Förster-Nietzsche removed it. "Decadent" and "decadence" carry A 6's definition: an individual or species is corrupt "when it loses its instincts, when it chooses, when it prefers, what is disadvantageous for it"; Jesus is a decadent type (incapable of enmity, "instinctive hatred of reality", A 29-30), which is a diagnosis, not a charge of ressentiment. "Chandala" (A 45; TI Improvers 3-4) is Manu's outcast, used by Nietzsche as the type of the lowest caste's hatred. "Jew" in A 24-27 and 44 names the priestly instinct and its valuation, which Christianity continued ("the consequence of Jewish instinct, not its opposite", A 24); the Polish-Jews aside of A 46 is a slur of smell and uncleanliness that the sentence applies first to the first Christians; both are quoted as written, glossed by what they do in the argument (uncleanliness is A 46's charge against the New Testament as a whole), and set beside A 55 and GM III 26, which attack the anti-Semites on principle. "Dysangelist" (A 42) is Nietzsche's coinage for Paul, bearer of ill tidings. "Evangel" (A 33) is the practice, "a new way of life, not a new faith".
- **Objection answered from his side:** two, philosophical. (a) The historical: Nietzsche's Jesus is a reconstruction against the sources, selecting what fits (as Renan and Strauss selected), and no method tells which sayings belong to the type and which to the disciples. Reply from the texts: A 28 and 31 concede the corruption of the sources (the tradition, in Nietzsche's account, bears the marks of the disciples' hatred and propaganda) and claim only to read the type through the whole psychology of the evangel; the test the lesson can run is internal — the sayings of non-resistance, of the kingdom "in you", of no distinction between God and man cohere as one practice (A 29, 33-34), while judgment, resurrection, sacrifice cohere as the mechanism's work (A 40-42). Gap sentence: the division of the Gospel into type and accretion is made by the psychology it is meant to confirm, and the lesson says so plainly. (b) The believer's: the Last Judgment is justice, not revenge, and Jesus himself taught it (Matthew 25). Reply from the texts: GM I 14-15 and A 40 do not deny the doctrine is in the Gospels; they ask what wanting it reveals (Tertullian's "How shall I admire, how laugh, how rejoice"; Aquinas's bliss "more delightful"); A 45's reading of 1 Corinthians 1:20ff ("God hath chosen the weak things of the world ... to confound the things which are mighty") shows the inversion of I 7 in Paul's own words. Where a believer answers that a just judge may take no pleasure, Nietzsche's reply rests on his psychology (the passages where the pleasure shows), not on the text, and the lesson states that too.
- **Humanizing reading to test:** that The Antichrist "is not anti-Semitic" and its target is only Christian anti-Semitism (Kaufmann, Nietzsche Ch.10 pp.298-301; the Portable's editor's preface to A, pp.565-568, on Förster and Stöcker), and that "Dionysus versus the Crucified" contrasts with the Gospel Jesus's resentment, not with the historic Jesus (Kaufmann, EH Intro 2). What the text says: A 55 and GM III 26 attack anti-Semites; A 44 and 46 use the stereotypes quoted above; D 68 and A 42-47 read Paul as the inventor; A 29-35 exempt Jesus. The lesson gives all four and lets the reader weigh.
- **Facts against the popular image:** in the lesson's texts: A 55 (anti-Semites lie "as a matter of principle"); BGE 52 (the Old Testament praised, the binding of the two Testaments "the greatest audacity and sin against the spirit"); GM III 22 (pp.143-145) "I do not like the New Testament", the Old Testament's "incomparable naivete of the strong heart"; the Portable's editor's preface (pp.565-568) and GS 135-141 with Kaufmann Intro 6 (pp.21-24) read as a tu quoque. "Nietzsche hated Jesus" is a real misreading (L13): A 29-35, 39 and Z I 21 are the refutation.

## The reconstruction

- **Claim and where.** A 39: "in truth, there was only one Christian, and he died on the cross"; A 40-43: the disciples and Paul built the slave revolt's victory on his death; A 24-26: its root is the priestly valuation GM I 7 named; A 33: what Jesus himself practised was "a new way of life, not a new faith".
- **Argument in plain steps.** (1) If Christianity is the slave revolt's victory, its texts should show I 10's marks: an enemy named first, weakness renamed as merit, revenge deferred and enjoyed. (2) The priestly layer (A 24-26) shows them: history rewritten as reward and punishment, "the priest lives on sins", ressentiment "become genius". (3) The founder does not: non-resistance from instinct, no enemy, no judgment, the kingdom as a state of heart, death on the cross as the practice carried through (A 29-35). (4) His death left the disciples with a question — who killed him? — and the mechanism supplies the answer: ruling Judaism as the enemy, the Master as judge, the Second Coming, the Last Judgment (A 40). (5) Paul completes it: resurrection and personal immortality turn the practice into a faith, give the priest his handle, and enjoy revenge in advance — D 68's "perfect revenge" (A 41-43). (6) The church fathers write the revenge down (Tertullian, GM I 15; the Apocalypse, GM I 16). Conclusion: the mechanism fits the doctrine and its history; it does not fit the founder, and Nietzsche says so himself.
- **Unstated premise.** That a type can be read off from a corrupted record by psychology alone (A 28, 31); and that the disciples' question "who killed him?" had to be answered by revenge rather than by grief — the step the expressive reading of ressentiment supplies and the texts do not argue.
- **Strongest objection and Nietzsche's reply.** As in STANCE: the historical objection (reply: internal coherence of the two layers; gap sentence on circularity) and the believer's objection (reply: Tertullian, Aquinas, 1 Corinthians 1:20ff; the reply rests on psychology where the believer denies the pleasure).
- **Connections.** slave-revolt-and-ressentiment (the mechanism tested); genealogy-as-method (reinterpretation, GS 353); debt-and-punishment and guilt-before-god (A 41's sacrifice doctrine, GM II 21); against-pity (A 7); herd-morality-in-modern-dress (A 43 "equal rights for all" as dynamite); will-to-truth (A 47 and 50 "faith makes blessed: consequently it lies"); the-nazi-appropriation (A 44, 46 and 55 as the texts the question of antisemitism must be settled on); ecce-homo (EH IV 9).

## APPLICATION spec (L16)

- **The text read whole:** The Antichrist 24-47, Kaufmann's translation, The Portable Nietzsche pp.590-627, `portable-late.txt` (sections begin at the centred numerals; running heads give printed pages). Beside it, GM I 15 (pp.48-52) for Tertullian and D 68 (pp.39-42) for the 1881 root.
- **Section map:** A 24-27 → slave-revolt-and-ressentiment (I 7: the priestly people; I 10's "ressentiment becomes creative" as A 24's "become genius") and genealogy-as-method (II 12: a history reinterpreted by whoever masters it; "the priest lives on sins" as reinterpretation of misfortune). A 28-35 → master-morality's asymmetry fails to apply (no enemy, no "evil" named first) and doer-behind-the-deed (A 33: a practice, not a belief about doers); the redeemer-type introduced. A 36-41 → slave-revolt-and-ressentiment step by step (I 14's workshop: revenge renamed judgment; I 15's deferred enjoyment). A 42-43 → the same mechanism in Paul, with D 68 as the earlier, fuller psychology; A 43's "equal rights" forward to herd-morality-in-modern-dress. A 44-46 → GM I 8 (the Gospel as "seduction", "bait"); the stereotype passages read as written beside A 55. A 47 → forward to will-to-truth and real-world-became-a-fable (the beyond invented from hatred of reality).
- **Where the text goes further / departs from the module's idealisation:** (a) the founder is exempted: the module's mechanism does not cover Christianity's origin, only its doctrine; (b) the vocabulary of decadence and physiology (A 29-31: "idiot", "morbid", "a hedonist on a thoroughly morbid basis") goes beyond GM I's psychology and anticipates module 5; (c) A 24-27's history of Israel is a reconstruction the module did not make and the sources contest; (d) the stereotype passages (A 44, 46) stand beside A 55 and the module gives no rule for reconciling them — the lesson quotes both and reads them as written; (e) A 43's "equal rights for all" and A 45's "chandala" reach past the module into herd morality and the order of rank.
- **Reading order and the one sentence:** read A 24-27, then 28-35, then 36-43, then 44-47, keeping a two-column ledger (practice / doctrine) as you go; then GM I 15 and D 68. The reader can now take any passage of the New Testament and say whether it shows the marks of ressentiment (an enemy named first, weakness renamed, revenge deferred) or the marks of the redeemer type (no resistance, no enemy, the kingdom as a state of heart), and can say which of the two Nietzsche's mechanism explains.

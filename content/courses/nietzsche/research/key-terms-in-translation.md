## Key points the lesson must cover

1. **schlecht / böse / gut.** Two contrasts in German (gut/schlecht; gut/böse), one word 'good' in English and a choice between 'bad' and 'evil'. Show the asymmetry argument (master-and-slave-morality) with the terms swapped: if a translator renders schlecht as 'evil' anywhere in I:4–11, the minimal pair collapses into one pair. Check Samuel's consistency across I:4, I:10, I:11; note where he slips.
2. **Schuld.** One word for debt and guilt (II:4); 'the moral concept Schuld descends from the material concept Schulden'. English must choose, and every choice makes the premise look either trivial (if 'guilt' throughout) or like wordplay (if 'debt' throughout). The lesson shows the premise's actual form: an etymological identity that the argument uses as evidence, not as proof (genealogy-as-method's grading applies).
3. **Mitleid.** 'Pity' (Hollingdale, Samuel) vs. 'compassion' (Del Caro, some Norman) — Reginster's point that Nietzsche's objection is to the state's structure (self-dissolution) and not merely to its condescension; 'pity' imports the condescension, 'compassion' hides the Schopenhauer target. GM Preface §5's 'Mitleids-Moral'.
4. **Wille zur Macht.** 'Will to power' is settled, but 'Macht' spans power/might/force and 'Wille' is not a faculty (BGE 19); Heidegger's 'will to will'. The point for the course: the English phrase invites the reading 'desire for domination' that BGE 19 and Reginster's analysis both deny.
5. **Übermensch.** 'Superman' (Common, Shaw's shadow), 'overman' (Kaufmann, Del Caro), 'overhuman' (Parkes), 'beyond-man'. The prefix über- as 'over/across' (Z Prologue's rope and bridge) vs. 'super-' as 'above' — which the Prologue's imagery supports; GM I:16's Napoleon 'Unmensch und Übermensch' as the one argued-book use.
6. **Rausch and the coined compounds.** TI 'Expeditions' §8's Rausch: 'intoxication' (Hollingdale) vs. 'rapture' (Krell) vs. 'frenzy'; Del Caro's practice of footnoting Versucher/Versuch (tempter/attempt/experiment — three senses in one word at the head of 'On the Vision and the Riddle') and Herrschsucht ('lust to rule' with the pathology of -sucht) rather than domesticating.
7. **The method stated.** For each term: the German, the candidate renderings, the argument-premise the term sits in, the reading each rendering makes easy or impossible. A translation is a reading; the check is to run the reconstruction under each.

## EXERCISE PLAN (floor: ≤1 warm-up; ≥2 of boundary / refute / derivation / research; one FORWARD)

1. (warm-up) For each of the six terms, write the German, one standard English rendering, and the course lesson whose argument the term sits in.
2. (research-shaped) Run Samuel's GM I:4, I:10, I:11 through the schlecht/böse check: list every occurrence of 'bad' and 'evil' with the German behind it, and report whether the 1913 translation keeps the two contrasts apart without exception.
3. (refute) "'Will to power' means the desire to dominate others; the translation is exact and so is the reading." Break it with BGE 19's anatomy of willing and BGE 13's 'discharge of strength'; state what 'Macht' must mean for BGE 259's 'exploitation' sentence to be the same doctrine.
4. (derivation) II:4's premise is an etymological identity. Derive what it would prove if 'Schuld' had two unrelated senses (nothing), and what it does prove given one root (a historical conjecture with a datable evidence base — the law codes of II:5); then state which translation choice hides the conjecture's status.
5. (boundary probe) Take a term with no translation problem at all (e.g. 'Ressentiment', kept in French by Nietzsche himself). Explain why keeping the foreign word is itself a translation decision, what it signals (rumination, impotence, a French moralist register), and whether the asymmetry argument would change if 'resentment' were used.
6. (translation comparison) Compare Hollingdale's 'intoxication' and Krell's 'rapture' for Rausch in TI 'Expeditions' §8 against the German; state which reading of art as 'physiology' each makes easy, and which Heidegger's Vol. I §14 needs.
7. (FORWARD → overman-staged) Read Z Prologue §§3–4 in Del Caro and list every image built on 'over/across' (rope, bridge, going-under, going-over). Decide whether 'superman' could carry them; the solution says the choice of 'overman' is the reading that overman-staged defends against the breeding-programme misreading.

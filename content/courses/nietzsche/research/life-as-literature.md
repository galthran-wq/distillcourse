# Research brief: life-as-literature (Nehamas: Perspectivism, Style, and the Self as Literary Character)

status: planned
module: where-the-reading-breaks
type: core
goal: Formulate Nehamas's aestheticist reading — the world as text, the self as character, eternal recurrence as a work's integrity, 'an opinion is only as good as its author' — and evaluate its answer to perspectivism's self-refutation against Clark's.

## ARC (L10)

- **Inherits:** The self-refutation worry, named and deferred in perspectivism and will-to-truth; GS 290 and 'giving style'; the recurrence test with the literary reading previewed; the normativity problem left underdetermined; the forward question — what Nietzsche's philosophy is if its defence is not truth but self-creation.
- **Contributes:** The first rival reading at its strongest: Nehamas's aestheticism reconstructed chapter by chapter (the world as text, things as the sum of their effects, untruth as a condition of life, recurrence as literary integrity, becoming what one is, Nietzsche as Plato to his own Socrates), with its answer to self-refutation ('an opinion is only as good as its author' — views individuated by character); the cost (the Genealogy's claims lose their standing as claims); Clark's counter (1990 Ch. 5; the 2018 retrospective): anti-foundationalism needs no aestheticism, and aestheticism does not secure the view against self-refutation either. Verdict as fact: what Nehamas holds that the naturalist cannot (the reflexive answer; the self's unity) and what he pays (truth).
- **Hands off:** To heidegger-one-thought (the second rival); to the audit.

Spine step: "Where the reading breaks: Nehamas turns truth into character and a life into a text; Heidegger turns the psychology into metaphysics and argues that no science can ground its own field-concept; the audit states which theses each reading can hold as arguments and where the naturalist hypothesis stands."

## Key points the lesson must cover

1. **The self-refutation problem, precisely.** (P) Perspectivism claims that every view is perspectival and none is a view from nowhere. (P) That claim is itself a view. (C) Either it is perspectival — and then why prefer it — or it is not, and it refutes itself. Nietzsche's own hand at the problem: BGE 22's 'supposing this too is only interpretation… so much the better'; GS 374's 'we cannot see around our corner'. State what the naturalist reading claims for its own results (that they are true — Clark's ordinary truth) and why that makes the problem the naturalist's to answer.
2. **Nehamas's project.** The unity thesis: Nietzsche's disparate views cohere once one sees that he models the world on a literary text, persons on characters, and knowledge on interpretation — the two organizing concepts, perspectivism and aestheticism. Ch. 2 'The Most Multifarious Art of Style' (EH III 4): style as method — many styles so as not to be read as a system. Ch. 3 'Untruth as a Condition of Life' (BGE 4): the early-and-late question Clark answered developmentally, answered here by aestheticism. Ch. 4 'A Thing Is the Sum of Its Effects': no essence apart from the totality of a thing's relations — as a character is nothing beyond what the text attributes to it (BGE 21's cause and effect as fictions; GM I:13's doer) — the metaphysical leg. Reconstruct each as a claim with its primary text.
3. **The answer to self-refutation.** 'An opinion is only as good as its author', not 'every opinion is as good as any other': views are individuated and evaluated by the character of whoever holds them; Nietzsche's philosophy is defended not as objectively true doctrine but as the coherent self-creation of a particular kind of person — he 'fashions a literary character out of himself and a literary work out of his life', playing Plato to his own Socrates (ch. 8). Reconstruct: (P) there is no view from nowhere; (P) what remains as a standard is the character a view expresses and the life it makes possible; (C) perspectivism is not self-refuting because it does not claim the standing it denies to others — it claims the standing it grants: a view as good as its author. The reader's judgment: this is a genuine answer and it changes what 'Nietzsche's claims' are.
4. **Recurrence and the self (chs. 6–7).** 'This Life — Your Eternal Life': recurrence not as cosmology but as the demand a well-made work makes — nothing subtractable without the whole unravelling; to will one's life again is to will it as a whole in which every detail is essential (GS 341 as the integrity test). 'How One Becomes What One Is' (EH's subtitle, from Pindar): a unified self is an achievement modelled on literary character; 'giving style' (GS 290); most people lack it. Compare with eternal-recurrence's practical reading (Clark's marriage analogy: whole-relation, but not integrity as a work) and with higher-type-and-sublimation (the unity of drives — Janaway's third condition — now as authorship). Millgram's counter (SEP §4: the disunified agent) named.
5. **The cost.** If the Genealogy's claims (guilt descends from debt; the priest redirects ressentiment) are defended only as a character's coherent self-expression, they are no longer claims about what happened; the naturalist reading needs them to be. Reginster's split applied: aestheticism answers disorientation (values as authored) and leaves the historical theses' truth-value where? State the cost as Nehamas's own choice (life as literature, not as history), and the reviewer's verdict in the dossier ('banal, vague, inconsistent' as a moral view; 'wonderful' as an interpretation) as the distinction between evaluating a reading and evaluating the philosophy it recovers.
6. **Clark's counter.** 1990 Ch. 5 §5: anti-foundationalism (no self-justifying beliefs; all justification contextual) secures perspectivism without aestheticism; the reflexive charge is answered by noting that perspectivism does not claim to be a view from nowhere — it claims to be true in the ordinary sense, from within a perspective, revisable; nothing about 'authors' is needed. 2018 retrospective: Clark nearly abandoned her book in 1985 fearing Nehamas had said it all; decades later she holds that his account does not secure Nietzsche's perspectivism against self-refutation either — because 'as good as its author' either smuggles a standard (what makes an author good — truth-related?) or does not adjudicate. Verdict as fact: Nehamas gives the reflexive problem an answer the naturalist cannot give (the naturalist's answer is Clark's, and it is weaker on that one point), at the cost of the historical theses' standing; Clark's objection shows the answer is not free.

## Pitfalls / subtleties to flag

- Nehamas is on disk with page numbers: quote the (P) argument (pp. 66–68), GS 290 (p. 185) and the closing sentence (p. 234) directly. The old notes file's error about Ch. 4 is corrected above (Ch. 4 is the Genealogy chapter; Ch. 3 is the sum-of-effects chapter).
- Do not caricature aestheticism as 'anything goes'; Nehamas's own gloss ('only as good as its author') is the refutation of that.
- Poellner's 'aestheticist ethics' is a different thesis (value grounded in affective experience); one paragraph to keep them apart.
- The reviewer's 'banal' verdict is quoted as a datum about reception, not endorsed.
- The lesson decides nothing about the naturalist hypothesis; it records what Nehamas holds that the naturalist cannot and what he pays.

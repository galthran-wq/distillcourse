# Research brief: naturalist-reading-verdict (The Naturalist Reading Audited)

status: planned
module: where-the-reading-breaks
type: synthesis (module application, L16)
goal: Judge thesis by thesis which of Nietzsche's central claims the naturalist reading states as sound arguments, which it must deflate, and where Nehamas's and Heidegger's readings hold text the naturalist cannot — using Janaway and Robertson's five questions and Leiter's own retraction as the audit.

## ARC (L10)

- **Inherits:** Everything: the ten key results, the three application scorecards (guilt-reconstructed, the Antichrist, the biologism 'line'), the three rivals (Clark & Dudrick inside the analytic camp; Nehamas; Heidegger), the working hypothesis with its three predicted test sites named in corpus-and-protocol (sovereign individual; value creation; will to power / recurrence).
- **Contributes:** The audit: ten theses × three readings, each cell a section citation and a verdict (fits / deflates / cannot accommodate); the naturalist's wins (GM I–III as mechanism plus evidence; the doer; the priest; the fable), its strains (the sovereign individual; value creation; the drive rank-order; BGE 259), the rivals' holdings (Nehamas: the reflexive answer, GS 290; Heidegger: the unity of the two thoughts, BGE 36's and Z's register) and their prices (truth; the published-works rule); Leiter's 2015 retraction as the reading correcting itself; Janaway and Robertson's five questions as the open ledger with the course's answer to each stated as fact.
- **Hands off:** Nothing — the course ends; the closing states what is established and what remains open, as fact.

Spine step: "Where the reading breaks: Nehamas turns truth into character and a life into a text; Heidegger turns the psychology into metaphysics and argues that no science can ground its own field-concept; the audit states which theses each reading can hold as arguments and where the naturalist hypothesis stands."

**APPLICATION spec (L16).** Artefact: two published results checked against the course's own reconstructions — the framing of Janaway and Robertson's Nietzsche, Naturalism, and Normativity (Ch. 1's five organizing questions: the scope of the critique; the positive ideal; the metaethics; the moral psychology; the relation of the revaluative project to naturalism) and Leiter's 2015 Postscript (the retraction on epiphenomenalism; the revised treatment of objectivity of value and autonomy). Module results used: all three rival readings as reconstructed; the scorecard format from guilt-reconstructed. Departure from the idealisation: the working hypothesis predicted three strains and the audit finds four (the fourth: BGE 259's biological register, which the biologism objection leaves the naturalist unable to explain rather than deflate); the volume's five questions are answered by the course as facts about the texts where the volume leaves them as open debates — the lesson says which of its answers is a verdict and which is a report of underdetermination. Both secondary sources are toc-only: every cell cites Nietzsche's sections, verifiable on disk, never the secondary pages.

## Key points the lesson must cover

1. **The hypothesis as stated and its predictions.** Repeat corpus-and-protocol's statement in one paragraph with a Ref: methodological naturalism + Doctrine of Types; three predicted strains. The audit's question: for each of the course's ten theses, does the naturalist reading render it as mechanism-plus-evidence (fits), as rhetoric relabeled (deflates), or is there a sentence it cannot accommodate?
2. **The ten theses (from `key_results`), each with its cell for the three readings.** (1) The death of God as collapse — naturalist: fits (GS 125; III:27's mechanism); Nehamas: fits as a change in the author's world; Heidegger: fits as nihilism in Being's history (Vol. IV) — all three hold it. (2) The Will to Power as compilation — naturalist: fits and needs it; Nehamas: indifferent; Heidegger: cannot accommodate (Vol. I §2 builds on it). (3) Origin vs. use — naturalist: fits (II:12); Nehamas: fits as reinterpretation; Heidegger: fits as will to power's reinterpretation (metaphysical). (4) The asymmetry — naturalist: fits (I:2, 10); Nehamas: fits; Heidegger: fits, but reads 'good/evil' as valuation in Being's history. (5) The doer fiction — naturalist: fits (I:13, BGE 21); Nehamas: fits (a thing is the sum of its effects); Heidegger: fits as the end of the subject — then reverses it (Nietzsche as the last subjectivist, Vol. IV §§19–20). (6) Guilt as debt moralized — naturalist: fits with one deflation (II:2); Nehamas: fits, with II:2 as self-authorship; Heidegger: no reading of GM II in the four volumes — cannot accommodate by silence. (7) The will to truth — naturalist: fits (III:24, GS 344), and the reflexive problem is answered Clark's way (weakly); Nehamas: fits and answers the reflexive problem (strongly, at cost); Heidegger: fits as 'truth as justice' (Vol. III §21). (8) BGE 36 — naturalist: deflates ('suppose'; psychological doctrine); Nehamas: deflates (no metaphysics); Heidegger: holds the metaphysical register, cannot accommodate the 'suppose'. (9) Recurrence as test — naturalist: fits (Janaway), cannot use the cosmology; Nehamas: fits as integrity; Heidegger: holds the unity thesis on WM 617 — cannot accommodate the published-works rule. (10) The biologism objection — naturalist: concedes the cosmological/biological, keeps the psychological; BGE 259 unexplained; Nehamas: indifferent; Heidegger: the argument is his. Each cell must cite the section and give one clause of verdict; the figure carries the table, the prose argues the contested cells only (2, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10).
3. **Where the naturalist wins.** The Genealogy as one argument in three installments (the priest's redirection closing the victory gap; the three-stage guilt; the doer removed) — every step a mechanism with graded evidence; the Antichrist as the method applied and its limits graded by the method's own standard; the fable and perspectivism as anti-foundationalism with ordinary truth kept. State it as the result: the critique of morality is naturalism, and the reading states it as arguments — the design intent's test passed.
4. **Where it strains (four, not three).** The sovereign individual (II:2 underdetermined; deflation cheaper given I:13, at the cost of one paragraph); value creation (GS 301 read three ways; the drive rank-order underdetermined between causal and normative — the sovereign-individual verdict returning); the drive rank-order itself (Leiter 2002 vs. 2015 vs. Clark & Dudrick — BGE 19's political vocabulary); and the fourth, found by the audit: BGE 259's biological register, which the biologism objection converts from 'deflate' to 'cannot explain'. Leiter's retraction as the reading correcting itself on the third: the hypothesis is live, not settled — 'a rare, citable instance of a canonical commentary correcting itself' (dossier).
5. **Where the rivals hold text.** Nehamas: the reflexive problem answered (an opinion as good as its author) and GS 290 / EH's subtitle read as the doctrine — at the price of the historical theses' truth-value; Clark's 2018 counter stated. Heidegger: the unity of will to power and recurrence, the metaphysical register of BGE 36 and Z's 'Life' held as doctrine, the fable as twisting free — at the price of the published-works rule and with his own closing doubt (Vol. II Part Two: is stamping Becoming with Being itself revenge?) and Krell's audit as internal rebuttals. State each as fact with its cost.
6. **The five questions as the open ledger.** For each of Janaway and Robertson's five: the course's answer as fact, tagged 'verdict' or 'underdetermined'. Scope: authority and jurisdiction (Robertson) — verdict. Positive ideal: the higher type as affirmation + spiritualization + style, one ideal or many flourishings — underdetermined (Reginster vs. Leiter). Metaethics: created not found; anti-realism / fictionalism / constructivism — underdetermined, with fictionalism weakest (Thomas). Moral psychology: the society of drives with command; causal vs. normative order — underdetermined (BGE 19). Naturalism and the revaluative project: the critique is naturalist; the positive doctrine's standing is the open question — verdict on the first half, underdetermined on the second. Leiter's Ch. 9 open problems (suffering and creativity; elitism; politics) and Clark Ch. 8 §5 (life as end vs. means) as the remaining items. Close by naming what is established and what remains, as fact; no peroration.

## Pitfalls / subtleties to flag

- The audit is a synthesis lesson: no new primary material; every cell cites a section the course has read. The lesson must not introduce a new thesis to score.
- 'Verdict' and 'underdetermined' are the only two tags; no 'probably'.
- The naturalist reading's wins are stated as facts about the texts and the reconstructions; the design intent asked that the hypothesis be tested, and the lesson reports the test.
- Janaway & Robertson is on disk with page numbers — cite them; Leiter's Postscript is toc-only: the retraction is stated in the terms Leiter's own restatements verify and marked for the human.
- No closing flourish; L10(c) — what is established, what remains, stated as fact.

## EXERCISE PLAN (floor: ≤1 warm-up; ≥2 of boundary / refute / derivation / research; one FORWARD — for the final lesson, the FORWARD reaches beyond the course to the primary text)

1. (warm-up, retrieval) Fill three cells of the scorecard from memory (theses 4, 5, 8) with the siglum and verdict for each reading; then check against the lesson.
2. (refute) "The audit shows the naturalist reading is simply correct." Break it with the four strains and Leiter's own retraction; state precisely what the audit does establish (the critique of morality as naturalist argument) and what it leaves underdetermined (the positive doctrine's standing), and name the sentence of Nietzsche's (BGE 19's 'commonwealth', or II:2's 'sovereign') that keeps it open.
3. (derivation) The audit finds a fourth strain (BGE 259) that the hypothesis did not predict. Derive why: the hypothesis's three predictions concerned the positive doctrine; BGE 259 is in the critique's register — state what that shows about the boundary between 'critique' and 'doctrine' the course has used since genealogy-as-method, and whether Robertson's scope solution moves BGE 259 across it.
4. (boundary probe) Suppose a reading that combined Nehamas's reflexive answer with the naturalist's historical theses (values as authored; history as true). Is it consistent? State what each half claims for its own standing and whether Clark's ordinary truth already occupies the position — or whether the combination pays both prices.
5. (research-shaped) Take Janaway and Robertson's five questions and, for each, find the single passage of Nietzsche's the course cited as decisive or as the site of underdetermination; write the five sigla and, for the underdetermined ones, the two readings' sentences; report which question the course answered with the least textual support.
6. (comparison, rivals) Heidegger's closing doubt asks whether stamping Becoming with Being is itself revenge; Krell's 'decadence of redemption' turns the Antichrist's redeemer-type on the reading. Reconstruct both as arguments against Heidegger's own reading and state whether either is available to the naturalist as well (it is — the genealogical method applied to a reading), and what that shows about the symmetry of unmasking noted in ascetic-ideal-across-types.
7. (FORWARD → beyond the course: GM read whole) The course read the Genealogy in an order of its own (Preface + II:12–13 first). Read the three treatises once more in Nietzsche's order, in one sitting, and record where the reading order changed a verdict — the solution says this is the Preface §8's 'rumination', and that the course's scorecard is a hypothesis about the book, to be tested against it the way the book tests morality.

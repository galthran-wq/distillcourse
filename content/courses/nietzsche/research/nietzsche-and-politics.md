## ARC (L10)
- **Spine step (13, "Taking stock"):** "Taking stock: Nietzsche's contempt for the state, nationalism and antisemitism sits beside his praise of rank and caste and does not add up to a political programme; the Nazi reading was a distortion built by his sister's Archive and the regime out of a compiled book and clipped quotations, with a residue of hard passages it could use; assembled, the argument holds in its critique, strains in its reply, and achieves a new way of holding values up"
- **Inherits:** from order-of-rank, the BGE 257-258 reconstruction (aristocratic society, "slavery in some sense", the pathos of distance, society as scaffolding) and A 57's "inequality of rights" — this lesson does NOT re-derive them; it asks whether rank is a politics. From herd-morality-in-modern-dress, BGE 202-203 (democracy as heir of Christianity; "rearing and educating" the new commanders) and the Skirmishes' diagnosis of liberal institutions.
- **Contributes:** the political question answered from the texts across all three periods: the state as idol and enemy of culture; the good European against nationalism and antisemitism, with BGE 251 and D 205 read whole; the Law of Manu as the affirming counter-image to Christianity that is nonetheless "terrible"; "great politics" as a war of spirits and as a caste ruling Europe with one will; the two readings named by content, and the residue each leaves. Introduces aristocratic-radicalism and good-european.
- **Hands off:** to the-nazi-appropriation, the whole inventory of texts — the ones that refute the regime's reading (state, nation, antisemitism) and the ones it could use without forgery (BGE 251's "let no more Jews come in", 257-258, 208, A 57, the breeding notes) — so that the application lesson can read the artifacts against them. To the-system-assembled, the verdict that the political residue is a weak link of the reply, not of the critique.

## STANCE (binding)
- **Favorable:** Nietzsche saw in 1878 (HH 475) that nationalism was an artificial condition serving dynasties and commercial classes, and in 1886-87 (BGE 251, GS 377) that antisemitism and "racial self-admiration" were the symptoms of a weak people; he said so when both were respectable, and he saw that the modern state would consume culture ("power makes stupid", TI Germans 1). The lesson shows that his anti-nationalism follows from his psychology of ressentiment (GM III 14, 26: antisemites as ressentiment's "latest speculators in idealism"), not from liberal sentiment, which he rejected in the same breath.
- **Hard passages this lesson must quote whole** (wording and files under Primary passages): BGE 251, both edges, from "Germany has amply SUFFICIENT Jews" and "Let no more Jews come in! And shut the doors, especially towards the East" to "banish the anti-Semitic bawlers" and "the rearing of a new ruling caste for Europe". D 205 (pp. 124-125): the praise, the usury sentence ("one has to say in extenuation even of their usury that without this occasional pleasant and useful torturing of those who despised them it would have been difficult for them to have preserved their own self-respect"), and "Europe may fall into their hands like a ripe fruit". D 272 (p. 149): "we shall one day also achieve a pure European race and culture", with its premise that no race is pure. HH 475 whole. A 46 (p. 625), the Polish-Jews aside. BGE 208: "TO ACQUIRE ONE WILL, by means of a new caste to rule over the Continent". TI Improvers 3-4: the chandala edicts and "pure blood". GS 377: both the "race hatred ... scabies of the heart" sentences and "we count ourselves among conquerors ... a new slavery". EH IV 1 (p. 327) exactly. Notebooks (unpublished): 37[5] (June-July 1885, Bittner pp. 31-32) "to breed a ruling caste - the future masters of the earth"; 2[?] (1885-86, p. 71) "international dynasties ... rearing a master race"; 10[68] (autumn 1887, p. 186) whole.
- **What the words meant:** "Race" (Rasse) in his usage is a people formed by long shared conditions, a "res facta" (BGE 251), never a fixed biological kind: D 272 denies that pure races exist, D 205 hopes for intermixing with Europe's aristocracy, GS 377 calls the "good Europeans" "too manifold and mixed racially". "Breeding" (Züchtung, Zucht) denotes the long cultivation of a type by conditions, discipline and selection of valuations (BGE 203 "rearing and educating", 262 a species established by constant hard conditions, TI Improvers 3 Manu's four castes); it is set against "taming" (church) and both are "terrible" and use the holy lie (Improvers 5). "Caste" and "master" denote positions in an order of rank defined by what a type can bear and command, not an ethnic group (A 57: the three types are "natural", "the most spiritual" rule). "Slavery in some sense" (BGE 257, "in irgend einem Sinne") denotes the subordination of the many as the material condition of the pathos of distance (order-of-rank). "State" denotes the modern power-apparatus that claims to be the people (Z I 11); his praise of Manu and of Rome is praise of institutions that form a type, not of states as such (TI Skirmishes 39: modern institutions lack the "anti-liberal will" that makes institutions).
- **Objection answered from his side:** (1) The consistency objection: a thinker who praises caste, a new ruling caste for Europe and "a new slavery" cannot also be anti-political; the texts contradict each other. Reply from his side: the targets are different objects. What he despises is the state and the nation as idols that spend culture (TI Germans 4, Z I 11, D 179), and party politics as "petty politics" (GS 377, BGE 208); what he praises is the order of rank as a condition of culture, which he says has arisen as "fortunate accident" (A 3-4) and which he hopes a philosopher's valuations might prepare (BGE 203, 211). Great politics in EH IV 1 is "a war of spirits", a prediction of the effect of exposing Christian morality; in BGE 208 it is a geopolitical wish. The lesson shows both texts and says the two uses of "great politics" are not reconciled by him. (2) Foot's objection (Secondary): the world suffers from too little pity and too much egoism, so a warning against pity is perverse. Reply from his side, texts first: his complaint is about the conceptual vocabulary men are "imprisoned among" (TI Improvers 2, p. 502, "imprisoned among all sorts of terrible concepts"), the over-belief in altruism and equality that makes the strong sick (D 148; A 38), not about the quantity of kindness practised; GS 377 and GM III 14 say the "humane" age is the symptom of a weakening. Gap sentence to write: he never states a criterion for how much subordination a culture's height requires, and BGE 258 ("a legion of individuals ... slaves and instruments") and A 57 read as endorsement, not only description.
- **Humanizing reading to test:** Kaufmann's "fundamentally anti-political" (Portable Intro IV, pp. 14-15; Z I 11 gloss p. 119 "Straight anti-fascism, but not in the name of any rival political creed ... anti-political"; monograph Epilogue pp. 412-414, "protestant gadfly"; GS 377 nn. 147-148 "clearly intended to dissociate him") is ONE reading to test. What the text says: EH I 3 "the last anti-political German" is his own phrase and GS 377's "petty politics" supports it; but GS 377 also says "new slavery", BGE 208 wants a caste with "one will", BGE 251 ends on "the rearing of a new ruling caste for Europe", and the 1885-87 notebooks plan "the future masters of the earth". Kaufmann's "breeding = self-cultivation" (monograph pp. 304-306, Selbstzucht) fits BGE 225 and EH, not BGE 203, 251 and 37[5]. Leiter's "esoteric moralist addressing nascent higher types" (Secondary) fits GS 381 and BGE 30; it does not account for BGE 208 and 251, which are about Europe, not individuals.
- **Facts against the popular image:** the Hillebrand letter of April 1878 ("European air and not nationalistic nitrogen!", Kaufmann monograph pp. 460-461, nietzschephi-s4-ch13-appendix.txt line 3671); the Christmas 1887 letter to his sister (Portable pp. 456-457: "It is a matter of honor with me to be absolutely clean and unequivocal in relation to anti-Semitism, namely, opposed to it, as I am in my writings ... that the name of Zarathustra is used in every Anti-Semitic Correspondence Sheet, has almost made me sick"); Notes 1885 (Portable pp. 441-442: "Being nationalistic ... would ... be for us who are more spiritual not mere insipidity but dishonesty"); the Draft for a Preface, fall 1885 (p. 442: "I wish I had written it in French so that it might not appear to be a confirmation of the aspirations of the German Reich"); the January 1889 margins (Portable p. 687: "Wilhelm, Bismarck, and all anti-Semites abolished"; Kaufmann monograph pp. 43-46 for "I am just having all anti-Semites shot"); EH Appendix 4(a) (p. 342: "The damnable German anti-Semitism, this poisonous boil of névrose nationale"). Reported because the texts say so.

## The reconstruction
- **Claim and where.** Three claims that must be held together: (a) the state is an idol and the enemy of culture (Z I 11; TI Germans 4; D 179; UM III 4); (b) nationalism and antisemitism are the symptoms of a weak, ressentiment-driven people (HH 475; BGE 251; GS 377; GM III 26; A 55); (c) every elevation of man has needed an aristocratic society, and Europe needs "one will" and a new ruling caste (BGE 257-258, 208, 251; A 57; GS 377 "new slavery"). The question is whether (c) is a politics.
- **Argument in plain steps (the aristocratic-politics reading).** (1) Culture is measured by its highest specimens (order-of-rank, higher-type). (2) Such specimens have arisen only where a long ladder of rank produced the pathos of distance (BGE 257). (3) A ladder of rank requires the many to be subordinated as the material of the few (BGE 258; A 57). (4) The present democratic levelling destroys the ladder (BGE 202-203; GS 356). (5) Therefore the conditions of culture must be re-made, by a caste with one will (BGE 208, 251), through breeding (BGE 203; 37[5]). The weak step is (5): it moves from a historical claim about conditions to a project, and Nietzsche himself says the higher type has come as "fortunate accident" (A 3-4) and that politics is for "lesser heads" (D 179).
- **Argument in plain steps (the anti-politics reading).** (1) In every period he despises the state, nationalism and party (UM III 4, Z I 11, TI Germans 4, GS 377). (2) Great culture arises in ages of political decline (TI Germans 4: Goethe and Napoleon). (3) His addressees are the few who might become higher types (GS 381, BGE 30, BGE 44), and his imperatives are to them. (4) Manu is held up as the affirming counter-image to Christianity and at once called "terrible" (Improvers 3-4; A 56-57). (5) Therefore "rank" is a scale of worth and a diagnosis, not a constitution. The weak step is (5): BGE 208, 251 and GS 377 are about Europe's future, and the notebooks name "the future masters of the earth".
- **Unstated premise.** Shared by both readings: that the valuation standard (ascending vs descending life) is itself not political, i.e. that a culture's height can be judged apart from the fate of its many. Nietzsche assumes this throughout (GM I Note: the two viewpoints of value are "opposite"); the lesson names it.
- **Strongest objection and Nietzsche's reply.** As under STANCE. The lesson's own verdict: the anti-political reading fits the state, nationalism and antisemitism texts without strain; the aristocratic residue (BGE 208, 251 end, 257-258, A 57, GS 377 "new slavery", the breeding notes) is real, is published in part, and is not a programme — no institution, law or party is named anywhere; the course.yaml verdict ("does not add up to a political programme") is stated with that residue named in one plain sentence.
- **Connections.** order-of-rank (inherits 257-258, A 57); herd-morality-in-modern-dress (BGE 202-203, Skirmishes 38-39); slave-revolt-and-ressentiment (antisemitism as ressentiment, GM III 14, 26; A 55); case-of-wagner (Bayreuth's Reich and antisemitism, EH II 5, CW 2); the-nazi-appropriation (hands off the inventory); the-system-assembled (the political residue as a weak link of the reply).

## Where the claim forks (named by content)
(a) **An aristocratic politics:** Nietzsche wants a society ordered by rank with subordination in some form, for the sake of culture, and hopes a European caste will bring it (BGE 208, 251, 257-258; A 57; 37[5]). (b) **No politics, only advice to the few:** he despises states and parties as such, addresses individuals who might become higher types, and his rank is a scale of human worth; Manu is a club against Christianity (UM III 4; Z I 11; TI Germans 4; A 56-57 read as contrast). The lesson states each at its strongest with its best text and names what each leaves unexplained: (a) cannot explain D 179, TI Germans 4 and the absence of any named institution; (b) cannot explain BGE 208's "one will" and GS 377's "new slavery".

## Pitfalls
- No political lens: the lesson neither recruits him for a side nor warns against him; it reports what the texts say and judges whether they form a politics.
- Never present BGE 251 or D 205 as simply philo-Semitic or simply antisemitic: both edges are in one section and both are quoted.
- "Great politics" has two uses (EH IV 1 war of spirits; BGE 208 geopolitics); do not fuse them, and do not let Kaufmann's note decide which is primary.
- Kaufmann's gloss on Z I 11 ("anti-political") is his, not Nietzsche's; "the last anti-political German" (EH I 3) is Nietzsche's, and is what the gloss rests on.
- Notebook passages (37[5], 2[?], 9[153?], 10[68], 11[?]) are unpublished and dated every time; lead with the published text (BGE 203, 208, 251) and bring the note as scaffolding.
- Zimmern's "slavery in some form or other" loosens "in irgend einem Sinne"; quote the course's own rendering from order-of-rank.
- Re-point every TI/A quotation to Kaufmann's Portable wording and chapter names ("What the Germans Lack", "Skirmishes of an Untimely Man", "The 'Improvers' of Mankind"); the Ludovici text is fallback only.
- The sister's Nueva Germania colony: Kaufmann monograph Ch. 1 III (pp. 42-47) and the Christmas 1887 letter; never Young.
- L13: the real, documented misconception is "Nietzsche was a German nationalist and antisemite"; refute it with HH 475, GS 377, A 55, the letters, and then present BGE 251 and D 205 whole so the refutation is not a whitewash. No "some believe" strawmen.

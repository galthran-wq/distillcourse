# Research brief: nietzsche-life-and-books (Nietzsche's life and the form of his books)

status: planned
module: the-problem (1 of 9), lesson 1 of 45
type: core (easy opener, C3: among the two most polished lessons, ending in a felt win)
goal: Explain how Nietzsche's three periods and his aphoristic form decide what counts as his view, and read a short aphorism as an argument

## ARC (L10)

- **Spine step (1, "The man and the form"):** "Nietzsche wrote in numbered sections across three periods of a short, ill, solitary working life, and his sister later published his notebooks as a book he never wrote; reading him means telling the books from the notes and reading a section as an argument"
- **Inherits:** nothing but the reader's background (Plato's two worlds, the Christian themes).
- **Contributes:** three tools every later lesson uses: (1) the dated map of the books; (2) the published/unpublished rule (a notebook entry is "unpublished, notebook n[k], date, Bittner p."; a WP number is a compilation number); (3) reading a numbered section as claim + step + unstated premise, shown on BGE 68, practised on BGE 146. Concepts: three-periods, aphorism, nachlass.
- **Hands off:** to schopenhauer-and-the-birth-of-tragedy (Leipzig 1865, Basel, the first book) and death-of-god (GS 125 read in its run; Kaufmann n.20). BGE 68's unstated premise is the whole of self-as-society-of-drives. The sister's story ends in the-nazi-appropriation; the 1888 books and the collapse are read again in ecce-homo.

## Not an apparatus lesson

The human banned a "reconstruction protocol" lesson and an editorial-history lesson. This is the life and the form told with stakes (professor at 24, wanderer at 34, silent at 44); the method is ONE worked example on one aphorism; The Will to Power is a section, not a lesson; the Nazi chapter is deferred.

## STANCE (binding)

- **Favorable:** the form was a choice with a reason: a system rests on premises it cannot question ("The will to a system is a lack of integrity", TI Maxims 26), and an aphorism "has not been deciphered when it has simply been read" (GM Pref 8). The reader does once what the course does forty-four more times, and sees that the sister's book, not the brother's, is the root of both legends, "incoherent" and "proto-Nazi" (Kaufmann Prologue). His own account of illness as a vantage (EH I 1: it taught him "to reverse perspectives") is given as his claim with his reasons, never psychologized away.
- **Hard passages this lesson must quote whole:** none of the course's list falls in this lesson's texts. The one hard-register matter is the sister: the Förster marriage (1885) and his letters against it, the forgeries (Schlechta 1956: at least 32 letters forged; Kaufmann Ch.1 pp.42-46), the Archive. Reported as fact in two or three sentences.
- **What the words meant:** "aphorism" covers a one-line maxim and a three-page numbered section alike; "free spirit" is "a spirit that has become free" (EH III HAH 1), not a free-thinker; "Nachlass" is everything left unprinted, from a finished manuscript (AC, EH) to a line in a notebook, ranked by D'Iorio's five categories (texts/nietzschesou-ekgwb-description.txt).
- **Objection answered from his side:** a writer who refused a system and wrote in fragments cannot be reconstructed as "one connected argument"; any coherent Nietzsche is the reader's (Bittner Intro xiv-xv: "we have fragments"). Reply: GM Pref 2 (pp.15-16), ideas grow "with the necessity with which a tree bears fruit" from one "fundamental will"; GM Pref 8, the third essay is the worked exegesis of one aphorism, so the form invites reconstruction; BGE 5-6, every philosophy is "the self-confession of its author", so the unity sought is of the valuer, not a doctrine-list; EH III 1 (p.259), "I am one thing, my writings are another". Gap sentence: the unity he claims is of a person and a task, not of a set of propositions; where two published passages conflict the course says which it builds on and why.
- **Humanizing reading to test:** Kaufmann's rule that notes are "background only, never preferred to books" (Ch.2 pp.76-78) and his verdict that nothing in the Nachlass changes the picture (Appendix pp.424-458). The course keeps the rule, tests the verdict: the notebooks also hold the breeding and recurrence-as-cosmology claims, which later lessons bring as scaffolding, cited as unpublished, not as suppressed. Kaufmann's "lost inhibition, not lost ideas" on 1888 (pp.67-71) is stated as his.
- **Facts against the popular image:** EH I 3 (pp.225-226) "the last anti-political German"; letters to his sister 1885-87, "anti-anti-Semite" (Kaufmann pp.42-46); Turin, January 1889, "I am just having all anti-Semites shot" (Portable pp.685-687). Developed in nietzsche-and-politics.

## The reconstruction

- **Claim and where.** (a) The books are the evidence of record and a notebook entry is evidence of what he was trying (GM Pref 8; EH III 1; Bittner; the Chronology; Kaufmann Prologue). (b) A numbered section is a compressed argument (GM Pref 8 says so, the third essay performs it, BGE 68 is the case).
- **Argument in plain steps (on BGE 68).** (1) Two sources of belief about one's past disagree: memory reports, pride denies. (2) One is not truth-tracking: pride has a stake. (3) The one with the stake wins. (4) So belief about one's own past is settled by a contest of motives, not by evidence. The hidden step is (2)-(3): the aphorism shows contest and verdict, never mechanism.
- **Unstated premise.** No neutral self above memory and pride adjudicates; Plato's reason ruling the passions is exactly such a judge. That premise is the next module's thesis (BGE 12, 19).
- **Strongest objection and Nietzsche's reply.** In STANCE. Smaller objection: "three periods" is imposed from outside. Reply: he draws it himself (EH III, each book a step; the 1886 prefaces); the course uses it as a map.
- **Connections.** schopenhauer-and-the-birth-of-tragedy; death-of-god (GS 125 in sequence); self-as-society-of-drives (BGE 68's premise); the-nazi-appropriation (Archive, compilation, forgeries); ecce-homo (EH I 1-2, the 1888 books). The citation form for notes is set here.

## EXERCISE PLAN

Floor check: claim to refute (3), boundary probe (4), research-shaped (5); one warm-up (1); no Retrieval (lesson 1).

1. Warm-up (chronology): place BT 5, GS 125, Z Prologue 2, GM I 10, TI "Fable" in their periods from the timeline and say in a clause what the date tells you about the weight each bears.
2. Reconstruct BGE 146 as claim, step and unstated premise, as done for BGE 68 (premise: a self is not insulated from what it attends to; the image carries the step).
3. Claim to refute: "The notebooks show what Nietzsche really thought; he wrote them for himself." Two facts it ignores (the plan abandoned September 1888; the notes kept reappear revised in the books, Portable p.13) and what it would need (that revisions were concessions, not corrections).
4. Boundary probe: a note contradicts a published section written a year later; which counts, and does it change if the note is later? Rule (books first; a later note is a view in motion) and limit (AC and EH were finished for print: D'Iorio's second category ranks with books).
5. Research-shaped (sourcing drill): "WP 1067" and its notebook number 38[12] (1885; Bittner pp.38-39): what each number tells you, which survives a change of edition, what the compilation's placing it last did to its reading. The note was never "discarded" as a thought (Bittner x-xi); the book was.
6. FORWARD (reaches self-as-society-of-drives): describe the judge BGE 68 leaves out and name what plays that role in Plato's soul. Solution: a stakeless faculty with access to the facts; reason; <Ref> to self-as-society-of-drives, where BGE 12 and 19 argue no member stands above the rest.

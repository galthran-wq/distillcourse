# Research brief: on-truth-and-lies ('On Truth and Lies in a Nonmoral Sense' (1873) read whole)

status: planned
module: truth (6 of 9), lesson 32 of 45
type: synthesis (module application, L16)
goal: Assess the young Nietzsche's essay on truth with the module's late theory, identifying which of its claims survive and which depend on the thing in itself he later abolished

## ARC (L10)

- **Spine step (10, "Truth turned on itself"):** "Without a view from nowhere there is only perspectival knowing, and the unconditional will to truth turns out to be the ascetic ideal's last form; untruth, art and illusion can serve life, and the young Nietzsche's essay on truth shows which part of this he got right first and which he later abolished."
- **Inherits:** the whole module: the true world abolished with its "apparent" contrast (27); being and the doer as grammar's fictions (28); objectivity as "more eyes" (29); the will to truth's moral and social origin (30); untruth as a condition of life and art as honest illusion (31); and from consciousness-and-language, GS 354's consciousness as a herd instrument.
- **Contributes:** the module's results applied to Nietzsche's own most-quoted text on truth, read whole in German with Kaufmann's partial English beside it. The reader learns to catch a famous passage committing the error its author later diagnosed: "all truths are illusions" measures our concepts against "the enigmatic X of the thing in itself", the standard stage 6 abolished, while the essay's other claims (concepts equate the unequal; truth's binding force is social; the metaphor drive outlives science in art) are the module's results in their first form. Introduces truth-as-metaphor.
- **Hands off:** to module 7 (amor-fati): the essay ends with the Stoic walking out from under the storm in his cloak; the next module begins with the man who wants the storm.

## STANCE (binding)

- **Favorable:** at twenty-eight Nietzsche wrote down, in ten pages, three ideas the module took a career to secure: concepts are made by forgetting differences; "truth" first meant a social obligation to use the agreed designations; the mind's images are translations between unlike spheres, not copies. The images (the star, the tiger, the leaf, the coin, the Chladni plate) are the best teaching devices he ever drew. The lesson reads the essay as the seed it was and shows the one assumption the mature thought cut away: a "pure, consequence-free truth" of the thing in itself against which ours is illusion (ontruth-de.txt l.94). That is the module's method applied to its author, and he applied it himself.
- **Hard passages this lesson must quote whole:** none from the course's hard list falls here. Keep intact: "herdenweise in einem für alle verbindlichen Stile zu lügen" (l.141), Kaufmann's "to lie herd-like in a style obligatory for all" (Portable p.47, portable-early.txt l.1868); the opening fable whole (ll.7-20; Kaufmann pp.42-43, ll.1676-1690).
- **What the words meant:** "herd" (Herde) is GS 354's and GM I's word; here it denotes the society whose peace pact fixes the designations, with no evaluative weight yet. "Lie" (Lüge) in the title's "nonmoral sense" denotes any use of a sign that does not copy its object, not deception with intent; "truth" denotes the sanctioned usage. "Thing in itself" is Kant's and Schopenhauer's term, still the essay's measure of knowledge.
- **Objection answered from his side:** the critique is unfair because the essay half-sees it: to say the concept fails to correspond to the essence of things "would be a dogmatic assertion and as such as unprovable as its opposite" (l.131, own translation), and its own "nerve stimulus" and "cause" are admitted to be metaphor. Reply from the later texts: the hedge does not save the conclusion; "truths are illusions" has sense only if there is something they are illusions of, and once stage 6 removes the true world, "illusion" loses its contrast as "apparent" did (TI Fable 6; "Reason" 6; BGE 34). Nietzsche's own gloss: "Truth does not signify the antithesis of error but the status of certain errors vis-à-vis others" (34[?] p.15, unpublished 1885). Gap sentence: the mature view keeps calling our categories errors (GS 110, BGE 4) while denying the standard that made the essay call them illusions; the essay shows that silence was there from the start.
- **Humanizing reading to test:** (a) the popular reading takes the essay as Nietzsche's considered theory ("all truth is metaphor"); it was unpublished, 1873, and the books redefine knowledge rather than abolishing it (GM III 12, BGE 22, GS 374). (b) The reconciling reading says nothing is dropped; ll.94, 106 and 206-210 ("no correct perception") show the standard still in place. Present both, then read.
- **Facts against the popular image:** Nachlass, unpublished in 1873, famous only in the twentieth century; Kaufmann prints about 30% and tags it "a fragment published posthumously" (p.42 n.). EH does not mention it.

## The reconstruction

- **Claim and where.** S1 para 8: truth is "a mobile army of metaphors", illusions whose origin is forgotten; paras 4 and 9: truth as a social obligation to use fixed designations; paras 5-7: words twice removed from the thing in itself; concepts equate the unequal.
- **The essay's argument in plain steps.** (1) The intellect is a survival tool of the weakest animals; its chief power is dissimulation (paras 1-3). (2) Society needs a peace pact; language fixes designations; "truth" is using them, "lying" misusing them to harm (4). (3) A word is a nerve stimulus transposed into an image and then a sound, two leaps between unlike spheres, so words never reach the thing in itself (5-6). (4) Concepts arise by equating the unequal (7). (5) Therefore truth is a mobile army of metaphors; truthfulness is the duty to lie herd-fashion (8-9). (6) Science is the columbarium of dead metaphors; the metaphor drive lives on in myth and art; the intuitive man founds culture, the Stoic survives by concept and mask (10-12, S2).
- **Testing it with the module.** Steps 1, 2, 4 and the second half of 6 survive: the intellect as a tool of life (GS 110, BGE 3), truth's herd origin (GS 354, D 47), concepts that simplify (BGE 4, 192), art as the honest outlet (GS 107, GM III 25). Step 3's conclusion and step 5's "illusions" depend on truth as adequacy to the thing in itself; the fable abolishes that standard, perspectivism replaces it with "more eyes", BGE 34 replaces true/false with degrees of seemingness. So the late Nietzsche keeps "truth has a social origin and concepts equate the unequal" and must drop "all truths are illusions".
- **Unstated premise.** That true knowledge would be adequacy to the "enigmatic X" (ll.94, 106); the hedge at l.131 does not withdraw it, since "illusion" and "no correct perception" presuppose it.
- **The essay against itself.** Its tools (nerve stimulus, cause, inference) are metaphors by its lights, and it says so of "cause" (para 5); para 11 already denies any causal relation between subject and object and offers "an aesthetic relation", the late view's seed.
- **Strongest objection and Nietzsche's reply.** As in STANCE.
- **Connections.** real-world-became-a-fable (the X is stage 3's assumption); reason-in-philosophy (para 5's "the stone is hard", gendered nouns, "Schlange" as the first draft of "faith in grammar"); perspectivism (para 11's insect and bird); will-to-truth (para 4's utilitarian origin vs GS 344's moral vow); value-of-untruth (dissimulation; S2's art); consciousness-and-language (GS 354); nietzsche-life-and-books (reading a quotation by date and status); amor-fati (the Stoic's cloak).

## Where the claim forks (named by content)

(a) The essay as Nietzsche's considered theory of truth, continuous with the late books. (b) The essay as an early form that still measures knowledge against the thing in itself, so that its sceptical conclusion is dropped while its genealogy of concepts is kept. The lesson argues (b) from ll.94 and 106 (the standard), TI Fable 6, "Reason" 6 and BGE 34 (its removal), GS 354 and BGE 4 (what returns).

- **Section map:**

  - S1 para 4 (the peace pact; truth as sanctioned designation): consciousness-and-language and GS 354 (the herd-origin of concepts); will-to-truth (GS 344 later denies that consequences can ground an unconditional will: the essay's prudential account is what GS 344 rejects; name the reversal).
  - S1 paras 5-6 (stimulus, image, sound; Chladni; the X): real-world-became-a-fable (the X is stage 3's thing in itself; stage 6 removes it); reason-in-philosophy ("the stone is hard", gendered nouns, "Schlange": the first draft of TI "Reason" 5).
  - S1 para 7 (equating the unequal; the leaf; honesty): value-of-untruth (GS 110's "equal things"; BGE 4's "counterfeiting by numbers"; GS 111); doer-behind-the-deed (the qualitas occulta "honesty" as a doer behind honest deeds).
  - S1 paras 8-9 (the mobile army; coins; lying herd-fashion): will-to-truth and consciousness-and-language; the module's verdict on "illusions": real-world-became-a-fable and perspectivism (no contrast term left; BGE 34; 34[?] p.15).
  - S1 paras 10-12 (columbarium, dice, the camel, man as measure, no correct perception, the spider): perspectivism (insect and bird as perspectives; "no correct perception" is GM III 12's "eye turned in no direction" with the X still assumed; the "aesthetic relation" is the seed of BGE 22's interpretation-not-text); reason-in-philosophy (the camel found a mammal is TI "Four Great Errors" 3's "he always found in things only that which he had put into them").
  - S2 (columbarium; myth and art; Pascal; Athena; the Saturnalia; rational vs intuitive man; the Stoic): value-of-untruth (GS 107; GM III 25's "Plato versus Homer"); schopenhauer-and-the-birth-of-tragedy (the intuitive man's Greece is GT's Apollonian culture); amor-fati (the Stoic's mask and cloak as the posture the next module replaces).
- **Where the text goes further / departs from the module's idealisation:** (a) "all truths are illusions" needs the thing in itself the module abolished; the module's late form keeps "error" (GS 110, BGE 4) without the X, leaving the standard unstated (the value-of-untruth fork). (b) The essay derives the will to truth from utility alone (para 4); GS 344 finds a moral vow instead, a reversal. (c) No will to power and no drives: one "drive to metaphor" does the work later given to many (BGE 6). (d) The Stoic ending is endurance by mask; amor fati is not in it. (e) The essay already holds perspectivism's seed (para 11) and the herd-origin of concepts, earlier than any published text.
- **Reading order and the one sentence that states what the reader can now do:** read the star fable in full (para 1), then paras 4, 5-7, 8-9 as the argument, then 11 and S2 as the essay's reach past itself; the reader can now take Nietzsche's most-quoted sentence about truth, date it, state the premise it rests on, and say from his own later texts which half he kept and which half he abolished.

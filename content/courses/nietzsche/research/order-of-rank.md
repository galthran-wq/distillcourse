# Research brief: order-of-rank (Order of rank: aristocratic society and the pathos of distance)

status: planned
module: higher-human-being (8 of 9), lesson 40 of 45
type: core
goal: Explain Nietzsche's claim that every elevation of the type man has required an aristocratic society in which the many exist for the few, and evaluate the argument he gives for it

## ARC (L10)
- **Spine step (12, "The higher human being and the order of rank"):** "Affirmation needs a type able to bear it: the overman against the comfortable last man, the higher type who is as manifold as he is whole, the order of rank in which every elevation of man has needed an aristocratic society and the many have existed for the few, and the philosopher who creates values knowing they are made; 'What Is Noble?' is Nietzsche's own last statement of the type"
- **Inherits:** from higher-type, the described type and the premise that a culture is measured by its peaks (UM II 9, III 6); from master-morality, the pathos of distance as the source of the noble right to coin values (GM I 2) and "duties only to equals" (BGE 260); from will-to-power-as-psychology, life as discharge of strength and overpowering (BGE 13); from herd-morality-in-modern-dress, equality, democracy and socialism as herd morality in secular dress (BGE 202-203); from overman-and-last-man, "Men are not equal" (Z II 7).
- **Contributes:** the social claim built on the type, read from BGE 257-259 and A 57 whole: every elevation of man has been the work of an aristocratic society; such a society needs "slavery in some sense"; exploitation is a function of life; the inequality of rights is the first condition of any rights. The argument in steps, its unstated premise, the strongest objection and the reply. Introduces order-of-rank, aristocratic-society, exploitation. Reads the breeding vocabulary (BGE 203, 213, 251, 262; TI "Improvers" 3-5; the notebooks) as written.
- **Hands off:** to creating-values: the ladder of rank is a ladder of values, and "the determination of the order of rank among values" is the philosophers' task (GM Essay I Note); whether a made value can rank anything is the next question. To what-is-noble: BGE 257-259 are its first three sections and 265's egoism is where the module's text goes past this lesson. To nietzsche-and-politics and the-nazi-appropriation: whether rank implies a programme (anti-politics vs aristocratic politics), and how the sentences read here were clipped.

## STANCE (binding)
- **Favorable:** Nietzsche is the one modern thinker who refused the premise that the nineteenth century stopped arguing for: that a society is to be judged by the welfare of its majority. He put the rival measure in one sentence, "the goal of humanity cannot lie at the end, but only in its highest specimens" (UM II 9), and then asked, as a historian, what the highest specimens had in fact cost: every high culture so far rested on a caste that commanded and a mass that carried it, and the inner distance of the great soul grew out of the outer distance between castes (BGE 257). Whether or not the reader accepts the measure, the historical claim is a real question, and his honesty about its price ("the truth is hard"; "let us be so far honest towards ourselves", BGE 257, 259) is what makes the lesson worth having.
- **Hard passages this lesson must quote whole:** BGE 257 (bge-zimmern.txt lines 5055-5083; German bge-german.txt lines ~6105-6140) including "requiring slavery in some form or other" (German "Sklaverei in irgend einem Sinne nöthig hat", "in some sense"), the pathos of distance, "the truth is hard", and the barbarian origin ("more COMPLETE men (which at every point also implies the same as 'more complete beasts')"); BGE 258 (lines 5084-5109): "accept with a good conscience the sacrifice of a legion of individuals, who, FOR ITS SAKE, must be suppressed and reduced to imperfect men, to slaves and instruments", society "NOT allowed to exist for its own sake, but only as a foundation and scaffolding", the Sipo Matador; BGE 259 (lines 5110-5145): "life itself is ESSENTIALLY appropriation, injury, conquest of the strange and weak, suppression, severity, obtrusion of peculiar forms, incorporation, and at the least, putting it mildest, exploitation", "a consequence of the intrinsic Will to Power, which is precisely the Will to Life"; A 57 (pp.643-647, portable-late.txt lines ~7600-7720): "The order of castes, the supreme, the dominant law, is merely the sanction of a natural order" (p.645); "The inequality of rights is the first condition for the existence of any rights at all" (p.646); "A high culture is a pyramid: it can stand only on a broad base" (p.646); "When the exceptional human being treats the mediocre more tenderly than himself and his peers, this is not mere politeness of the heart, it is simply his duty" (p.647); GM II 12 (p.78): "mankind in the mass sacrificed to the prosperity of a single stronger species of man, that would be an advance"; GM II 11 (p.76): "legal conditions can never be other than exceptional conditions ... A legal order thought of as sovereign and universal ... would be a principle hostile to life"; TI Skirmishes 48 (p.553): "The doctrine of equality! There is no more poisonous poison anywhere"; GS 377 (pp.338-339): "we count ourselves among conquerors; we think about the necessity for new orders, also for a new slavery, for every strengthening and enhancement of the human type also involves a new kind of enslavement"; and the notebooks read whole and dated: 37[5] (Bittner pp.31-33; June-July 1885): "how shall the earth as a whole be governed? And for what shall 'man' as a whole, no longer just one people, one race, be raised and bred?"; 2[?] (p.71; 1885-86): "rearing a master race, the future 'masters of the earth' ... to sculpt at 'man' himself as artists"; 2[13] (pp.67-69): "try a whole, artificial and conscious breeding of the opposite type"; 9[153?] (pp.166-167; autumn 1887): "The levelling out of European man is the great process which cannot be impeded: it should be speeded up even further"; 10[50] (p.184; autumn 1887): the criminal who "belongs to the criminal race ... castrate him"; 11[?] (pp.207-208; Nov 1887-Mar 1888): "where are the barbarians of the twentieth century?"; 14[?] (pp.240-242; spring 1888): Christianity "thwarts natural selection".
- **What the words meant:** "Slavery in some sense" (Sklaverei in irgend einem Sinne, BGE 257): the phrase covers the ancient institution and every later form in which a mass carries a caste (BGE 203 says slavery "in the coarser and finer sense" is "the indispensable means of spiritual discipline and breeding"; GS 377's "new slavery"; 9[153?]'s "levelled species" as substructure); Nietzsche's "in some sense" widens the word, it does not withdraw it. "Aristocratic society", "caste", "Stand": a ruling stratum that looks down on "subordinates and instruments" and so acquires the pathos of distance; the Greeks, Rome, the Renaissance, Manu's India, the European nobility before 1789 are his instances. "Pathos of distance": the felt gap between castes that becomes the felt gap within a soul between its higher and lower states (BGE 257; GM I 2; GM III 14; A 43: "Today nobody has the courage any longer for privileges, for masters' rights ... for a pathos of distance. Our politics is sick from this lack of courage", pp.619-620). "Exploitation" (Ausbeutung, BGE 259): not a defect of a bad society but a function of every living body, "a consequence of the intrinsic Will to Power"; Nietzsche keeps the "disparaging" word on purpose. "Breeding" (Zucht und Züchtung, BGE 203, 213, 251, 262; TI "Improvers" 3-5): the shaping of a human type by laws, valuations, religion and marriage over generations, with Manu's caste law as the example; TI "Improvers" 4 calls "pure blood" "the opposite of a harmless concept"; the notebooks extend the word to a planned "rearing" of a ruling caste; the vocabulary is agricultural and legislative, and the lesson states exactly what it covers in each passage. "Race" (Rasse): in BGE 257 the "weaker, more moral, more peaceful races" attacked by barbarians; in D 272 "there are probably no pure races but only races that have become pure", with the hope of "a pure European race and culture"; in 2[?] p.71 a "master race" that is a "higher species of men" made by "self-legislation"; in BGE 251 the "rearing of a new ruling caste for Europe" crossing Jewish and Prussian stock. His "race" is a bred and mixed type, never a biology of blood, and the lesson shows that from D 272 and BGE 251 rather than asserting it. "Mediocre" (A 57): the broad base, "handicraft, trade, agriculture, science, the greatest part of art", whose happiness is "mastery of one thing, specialization"; not an insult, "the very first necessity if there are to be exceptions".
- **Objection answered from his side:** (1) Great art and thought have arisen without slavery (Dutch painting, the German music Nietzsche loved, nineteenth-century science), so the historical universal is false; (2) the premise that a culture's value is its peaks is asserted, not shown. Reply from the texts: to (1), BGE 257 claims that the caste difference produced the pathos of distance which produced the inner elevation, and that the inner distance survives the outer one only for a while: BGE 262 says the exceptional individuals of an age of relaxation appear "in tropical tempo" and then "the incurably mediocre" remain; GS 377 and 9[153?] say every enhancement needs "a new kind of enslavement" in some form; A 57's pyramid says the base is a precondition even when the top no longer commands it. To (2), the premise is argued as a view of value, not proved: GM Essay I Note states "the well-being of the majority and the well-being of the few are opposite viewpoints of value" and that treating the former as higher "may be left to the naivete of English biologists"; UM III 6 asks "how does your, the individual's life receive the highest value?" and answers "only by living for the benefit of the rarest and most valuable specimens"; TI Skirmishes 33 ranks a life by whether it is "the ascending or the descending line". The plain gap sentence: the historical claim that every elevation needed an aristocratic society is a generalisation Nietzsche illustrates and never tests against a counterexample, and the value premise under it is stated as his judgment and defended by a change of standpoint, not by an argument that would move someone who does not share it.
- **Humanizing reading to test:** Kaufmann Ch. 10 (pp.284-306): Zucht und Züchtung as "discipline and breeding", "blood" as tradition ("whoever cannot account to himself for a heritage of three thousand years"), Nietzsche's hopes for "a future mixed race", the published works far "more circumspect" than the notes, the anti-egalitarianism cultural rather than political; Leiter SEP sec. 4: no political philosophy, A 56-57's Manu "a rhetorical contrast with Christianity" (Brobjer), Nietzsche an "esoteric moralist" addressing individuals; Kaufmann Portable p.15: "fundamentally anti-political". What the text says: BGE 257 and 258 are published, in the book Nietzsche called "a school for the gentilhomme" (EH III BGE 2, p.310), and say "slavery" and "slaves and instruments" with "a good conscience"; BGE 259 makes exploitation a law of life; A 57 calls the order of castes "the sanction of a natural order" and the inequality of rights the condition of any rights, and TI "Improvers" 3-4 both admire Manu ("How wretched is the New Testament compared to Manu") and call his means "terrible", so the Manu passages carry both an ideal and its price, not a rhetorical club only; BGE 251 ends with "the rearing of a new ruling caste for Europe" ("die Züchtung einer neuen über Europa regierenden Kaste", bge-german.txt line 5797); the notebooks say "master race" and "masters of the earth". Kaufmann is right that "blood" is not biology for Nietzsche (D 272, BGE 251 and GS 377's "racial self-admiration ... doubly false and obscene" are the evidence) and right that the breeding programme is mostly notebook; the lesson says both, and then says what the published sentences say.
- **Facts against the popular image:** GS 377 (p.339) rejects "nationalism and race hatred" as "scabies of the heart" in the same breath as it speaks of "new slavery"; BGE 251 calls the Jews "the strongest, toughest and purest race presently living in Europe" and proposes expelling "the anti-Semitic bawlers", and also says "Let no more Jews come in!" as "the instinct of a people"; D 272 denies pure races; A 57's exceptional man owes the mediocre gentleness as "duty"; Portable p.456 no. 893 (unpublished, 1887): the philosopher must "protect the rule".

## The reconstruction
- **Claim and where.** BGE 257: every elevation of the type man has been, and will be, the work of an aristocratic society that believes in a long ladder of rank and "needs slavery in some sense". BGE 258-259: such a society takes the sacrifice of the many "with a good conscience" because exploitation is a function of life. A 57: the order of castes is "the highest law of life" and "the inequality of rights is the first condition for the existence of any rights". GM II 12: the sacrifice of mankind in the mass to one stronger species "would be an advance".
- **Argument in plain steps.** (1) The value of a culture lies in its highest specimens, not in the welfare or number of its members (UM II 9, III 6; GM Essay I Note). (2) The highest specimen is a soul with a great inner distance between its higher and lower states, the "self-overcoming of man" (BGE 257; higher-type). (3) That inner distance has only ever grown out of an outer one: the "pathos of distance" a ruling caste feels looking down on "subordinates and instruments" and practising command (BGE 257; GM I 2). (4) An outer distance of that kind requires a society in which a mass exists for a caste: slavery "in some sense" (BGE 257-258). (5) Life itself is appropriation and exploitation, so a society that made non-exploitation its fundamental principle would be "a Will to the DENIAL of life" (BGE 259; GM II 11). (6) Therefore the aristocratic society is not a crime against the type man but its precondition, and a healthy aristocracy accepts the sacrifice of the many "with a good conscience" (BGE 258); the mediocre majority is "the very first necessity if there are to be exceptions" (A 57).
- **Unstated premise.** Step 1 is the premise the whole argument hangs on, and Nietzsche states it as a standpoint ("opposite viewpoints of value") rather than proving it. Steps 3-4 add a second: that the pathos of distance cannot be had from any other source than caste (not from solitude, not from a discipline, not from an art), which BGE 257 asserts with "could never have arisen" and the notebooks (9[153?]) repeat.
- **Strongest objection and Nietzsche's reply.** As in the STANCE block: the counterexamples to the historical universal, and the unargued premise. Reply: BGE 262 and GS 377 on the short life of exceptions in a levelled society; A 57's pyramid; the change of standpoint in GM Essay I Note and TI Skirmishes 33. Gap sentence: the universal is illustrated, never tested; the premise is a judgment Nietzsche owns rather than a conclusion he reaches.
- **Why "slavery" and "breeding" are not softened.** The lesson reads "in some sense" as what the German says, a widening of the word to every form of a mass carrying a caste, and shows the published breeding passages (BGE 203, 213, 251, 262; TI "Improvers" 3) beside the notebooks, dated, so the reader sees which is which: the books speak of slavery, exploitation, caste and a ruling caste to be reared; the notebooks add a planned "master race" and "masters of the earth". Where a hard conclusion follows from the premises (the sacrifice of the many "with a good conscience"), the lesson shows that it follows.
- **Connections.** master-morality (pathos of distance, GM I 2); herd-morality-in-modern-dress (equality as herd morality; TI Skirmishes 48); higher-type (the premise); creating-values (ranking values); what-is-noble (257-265 read in sequence); nietzsche-and-politics (programme or not: Z I 11, TI "Germans" 4 against the state; A 57 for caste); the-nazi-appropriation (what was clipped from 257-259, 251, the notebooks).

## Where the claim forks (named by content)
- **Aristocratic politics:** the texts recommend a hierarchical social order with a caste that bears the labour, for the sake of culture (BGE 257-258, 251, 208; A 57; the notebooks 37[5], 2[?] p.71). **Anti-politics:** the texts despise the state and politics as such (UM III 4; Z I 11, "the coldest of all cold monsters"; TI "Germans" 4, culture and state as antagonists), address individuals, and "rank" is a scale of worth; the Manu passages are a contrast with Christianity. The lesson states both at full strength from the texts and gives the judgment that BGE 257-259 and A 57 are description plus endorsement ("and so it will always be"; "with a good conscience"; "merely formulates the highest law of life") while no text proposes an institution; the fork is argued out in nietzsche-and-politics.

## Pitfalls
- Translate "in irgend einem Sinne" as "in some sense"; Zimmern's "in some form or other" is looser and should be corrected inline. "Zucht und Züchtung" is "discipline and breeding"; "Züchtung" alone is "breeding" (BGE 203, 213, 251, 262); the breeding vocabulary is not in 257-258.
- A 57 begins p.643 (Manu's law as the harvest of experiment, revelation and tradition as its "double wall") and the caste passage runs pp.645-647; quote from Kaufmann's Portable, never Ludovici.
- Dated and marked: every notebook citation carries "unpublished, <notebook[fragment]>, <date>, Bittner p."; lead with BGE 257-259, A 57, GM II 12, GS 377, and bring the notes as what they are, the places where the published vocabulary is extended into a plan.
- BGE 251 is quoted with both halves (the praise and "no more Jews"); D 272 and D 241 are quoted as written; no sentence of moral distancing follows either.
- TI Skirmishes 36 is about the physician and free death, not a state eugenics; A 2's "shall perish" sentence continues "And they shall even be given every possible assistance" (Kaufmann).
- Scholars' names stay in References; the fork is named by content.
- No arithmetic; the "pyramid" is an image, not a model to compute.

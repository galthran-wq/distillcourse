# Research brief: overman-and-last-man (The overman and the last man)

status: planned
module: higher-human-being (8 of 9), lesson 38 of 45
type: core
goal: Explain the overman as a goal of self-overcoming set against the last man, and reject the reading of it as a future biological species

## ARC (L10)
- **Spine step (12, "The higher human being and the order of rank"):** "Affirmation needs a type able to bear it: the overman against the comfortable last man, the higher type who is as manifold as he is whole, the order of rank in which every elevation of man has needed an aristocratic society and the many have existed for the few, and the philosopher who creates values knowing they are made; 'What Is Noble?' is Nietzsche's own last statement of the type"
- **Inherits:** from eternal-recurrence, the question of who could will the return of "the small man" (Z III 13); from herd-morality-in-modern-dress, the herd ideal of comfort and equality that the last man fulfils ("we have invented happiness"; BGE 202-203's "pygmy with equal rights"); from will-to-power-as-psychology, self-overcoming as what life does (Z II 12); from death-of-god, the need for a "meaning of the earth" once the otherworldly meaning is gone (Z Prologue 2-3).
- **Contributes:** the first portrait of the type the reply to nihilism needs, in Zarathustra's images (illustration, not doctrine): the overman as the direction of self-overcoming and "the meaning of the earth"; the last man as the endpoint of herd morality and the likelier future. Introduces overman, last-man. Fixes the published meaning against the species reading from Nietzsche's own 1888 statements (EH III 1, A 3-4).
- **Hands off:** to higher-type: the word "overman" all but vanishes after Zarathustra (once in BGE 294, in the gods' laughter); what replaces it is a described type with named instances (BGE 200, 212; TI Skirmishes 49). To creating-values: the child's "sacred Yes" (Z I 1) is value creation, taken up there. To order-of-rank: "Men are not equal" (Z II 7) and the "new nobility" (Z III 12 §11-12) are the Zarathustra form of what BGE 257 states in prose.

## STANCE (binding)
- **Favorable:** Nietzsche saw before anyone that the comfortable, secure, equal society which the nineteenth century called progress has its own terminal type, and he drew it in 1883: the last man, who "makes everything small", has "one's little pleasure for the day and one's little pleasure for the night", and blinks (Z Prologue 5). Kaufmann's note (Portable p.116) sets it beside Huxley's Brave New World; GM I 12 states the same diagnosis in prose ("more comfortable, more mediocre, more indifferent, more Chinese, more Christian"). The overman is the answer to a problem the reader can feel: once heaven is gone, what is the earth for? The lesson shows why "the meaning of the earth" is a serious answer and not a slogan.
- **Hard passages this lesson must quote whole:** Z Prologue 5 in full (pp.128-131, zarathustra-full.txt) including the crowd's "Give us this last man, O Zarathustra ... Then we shall make you a gift of the overman!" (p.130); GM I 12 (p.44, genealogy--text.txt) from "the diminution and leveling of European man constitutes our greatest danger" to "We are weary of man"; EH IV 4-5 (pp.329-331, eccehomo-full.txt): the good as "the last men", "the good and the just would call his overman devil", the overman type "exemplifies all that is terrible and questionable" in reality, and "To consider distress of all kinds as an objection ... is the niaiserie par excellence ... almost as stupid as would be the desire to abolish bad weather, say, from pity for poor people"; Z II 7 (p.213): "Men are not equal. Nor shall they become equal! What would my love of the overman be if I spoke otherwise?"; notebook 10[17?] (Bittner p.177, unpublished, autumn 1887): the "superman" as "the secretion of a luxurious surplus from mankind" against "higher Chinesehood ... the miniaturisation of man".
- **What the words meant:** "Overman" (Übermensch): Kaufmann's note (p.115-116) explains the choice against "superman" (Shaw's coinage, the comic-book sense) and the reading of "Mensch" as human being against animal; in Zarathustra it names a type beyond present man toward which self-overcoming points, never a described individual; in EH III 1 (p.261) Nietzsche says the word designates "a type of supreme achievement, as opposed to 'modern' men, to 'good' men, to Christians and other nihilists". "Last man" (der letzte Mensch): the first edition's Prologue title was "On the Overman and the Last Man" (Kaufmann p.116); the figure is the human being with nothing left to despise in himself, the completed herd ("No shepherd and one herd!"). "Bred" (A 3: "what type of man shall be bred, shall be willed"): in Nietzsche's vocabulary the verb covers the whole cultivation of a type by its conditions, laws and valuations (TI Improvers 3-5 contrasts Manu's "breeding" with the church's "taming"); the breeding notes of the notebooks belong to order-of-rank, which reads them whole. "Chinese" (GM I 12; GS 24 with Kaufmann n.22, p.99; notebook 10[17?] "higher Chinesehood"): his byword for a settled, stable, contented civilisation without exceptions, the last man's society under another name.
- **Objection answered from his side:** the ideal is empty: a direction with no content gives no guidance, and anything can be called self-overcoming. Reply from the texts: (1) the overman is defined by contrast, and the contrast is concrete: the last man's catalogue (Prologue 5) tells the reader exactly what the overman is not; (2) Zarathustra loves the preparers, not a finished type (Prologue 4: "I love those who do not know how to live, except by going under"; Z II 2: "create the overman"), so the word names a task, not a specification; (3) by 1888 Nietzsche supplies the content himself: the higher type has "appeared often, but as a fortunate accident" (A 3-4), and EH III 1 says to look for "a Cesare Borgia" sooner than "a Parsifal", that is, for strength rather than sanctity. The gap sentence: the Zarathustra image gives the direction of the type and refuses to give its shape, and the later books fill the shape with named men (next lesson) without ever saying what the overman himself would be.
- **Humanizing reading to test:** Kaufmann Ch. 11 I (pp.307-316) reads the overman as the man who has overcome himself, the Dionysian Goethe-type, with marriage and procreation as the Zarathustra context (Z I 20, "when a saint mates with a goose", quoted Kaufmann p.311); Tanner finds the ideal empty; Leiter SEP sec. 2 calls it a rhetorical trope that drops out of the mature work. What the text says: EH III Z 6 (p.305) calls the overman "the greatest reality", "the supreme type of all beings"; EH IV 5 (p.331) says the type "exemplifies all that is terrible and questionable" in reality and "the good and the just would call his overman devil"; Z II 21 (p.255) says the same from the other side, "the overman would be awesome to you in his kindness" and "you would call my overman devil". Kaufmann's self-overcoming reading is right about Darwinism (EH III 1 is Nietzsche's own text) and selective about terror: the lesson gives both halves.
- **Facts against the popular image:** EH III 1 (p.261): "Other scholarly oxen have suspected me of Darwinism"; Carlyle's hero worship "read into it"; the word misread as "half 'saint,' half 'genius'". Z II 21 (p.255): the overman "awesome to you in his kindness". The first printed "overmen" (GS 143, p.191-192 + Kaufmann n.30) stand in a praise of polytheism as the school of individual ideals, not in a biology.

## The reconstruction
- **Claim and where.** Z Prologue 3-5: "Man is something that shall be overcome"; "the overman is the meaning of the earth"; the alternative is the last man, and the crowd chooses him. A 3-4 and EH III 1 fix what the word does and does not mean.
- **Argument in plain steps.** (1) God died, and with him the sin against God; the earth now needs a meaning that is not a reward elsewhere (Prologue 3). (2) What is great in man is that he is a bridge, not an end: the human being is the self-overcoming animal (Prologue 4; Z II 12, "I am that which must always overcome itself"). (3) Self-overcoming needs a goal beyond the present type, or it stops and what remains is the hour without contempt. (4) The name for that goal is the overman: not a being who will exist, but the direction in which the type is raised (A 4: "a kind of overman" has appeared "as a fortunate accident", never as "something willed"). (5) Without that goal the herd ideal completes itself: comfort, sameness, no longing, the last man (Prologue 5; GM I 12 in prose). (6) The crowd prefers the last man, which is why the overman has to be taught and why the teacher looks, from the market place, like a jester.
- **Unstated premise.** Steps 2-3: that self-overcoming is the human excellence and needs an ideal beyond itself (rather than a stable flourishing of the kind Aristotle describes), and step 5's dichotomy: no third type between overman and last man. Nietzsche treats the dichotomy as a prediction, not a theorem (A 3: the dreaded type was "willed, bred, and attained: the domestic animal, the herd animal").
- **Strongest objection and Nietzsche's reply.** Emptiness (above): the reply from Prologue 5's concreteness, from the "I love" litany, and from A 3-4 and TI Skirmishes 37, which deny both the Borgia and the Parsifal readings and point at the higher-type lesson. Gap sentence as above.
- **The path (illustration).** Z I 1: camel (carries, reveres, "thou shalt"), lion (wins freedom for new creation, "I will", a holy No), child ("innocence and forgetting, a new beginning, a game ... a sacred Yes"). Course map: camel = the morality of modules 3-5; lion = Nietzsche's critique; child = value creation (creating-values). Redemption (Z II 20): the will's "ill will against time and its 'it was'" is the spirit of revenge; to turn "it was" into "thus I willed it" is the overman's capacity, and the demon's demand (eternal-recurrence) restated.
- **Not a species.** A 3: "The problem I thus pose is not what shall succeed mankind in the sequence of living beings (man is an end), but what type of man shall be bred, shall be willed." EH III 1 repudiates Darwinism and Carlyle. Kaufmann n.30 to GS 143 and his Prologue note give the word's pre-history. L13 slot: the species reading is real and documented (EH III 1 names it), so the lesson refutes it by the schema, and hands its political afterlife to the-nazi-appropriation.
- **Connections.** eternal-recurrence (the small man recurs, Z III 13); herd-morality-in-modern-dress (last man as the herd ideal fulfilled; BGE 203's "pygmy with equal rights"); higher-type (the sober description); order-of-rank (Z II 7, Z III 12 §11-12); creating-values (the child); the-nazi-appropriation (the word's abuse).

## Pitfalls
- "Overman" throughout, never "superman"; explain the choice once with Kaufmann's note. Zarathustra is illustration: quote short passages, say whose voice speaks (Zarathustra is mocked by the crowd, tempted by pity, corrected by his animals), and keep the argument in plain prose.
- Z I 20's "saint mates with a goose" is in Zarathustra, not in Ecce Homo; EH III 1 has the Borgia/Parsifal line. Kaufmann's exegesis (p.311) is the translator's, not Nietzsche's.
- GS 143's "overmen" belong to a praise of polytheism; do not lift the word out of that context.
- The animals' cosmological statement of recurrence (Z III 13) is rebuked as a "hurdy-gurdy song"; the horror is the recurrence of the small man.
- Published/unpublished: lead with Z, EH III 1, A 3-4; bring 10[17?] and 11[?] as scaffolding marked "unpublished, <date>, Bittner p.".
- "Bred" in A 3 is Nietzsche's word; the lesson states what it covers (laws, valuations, conditions) and leaves the breeding notes to order-of-rank, where they are read whole.
- No scholars' names in body prose (L8a); the Huxley comparison is Kaufmann's.

## EXERCISE PLAN
Floor check: claim to refute (ex. 2), boundary probe (ex. 4), derivation (ex. 5); research-shaped (ex. 3).
1. Retrieval (E3 from eternal-recurrence and herd-morality-in-modern-dress): state the demon's question (GS 341) and the last men's motto (Z Prologue 5), and say in two sentences how each defines the other.
2. Claim to refute: "The overman is a future biologically superior race." Break it with A 3-4, EH III 1 and GS 143 in their contexts, naming the step at which the species reading fails (what "bred" and "willed" govern in A 3).
3. Research-shaped sourcing drill: Kaufmann's Prologue note (Portable pp.115-116) and EH III 1 (p.261) are two documents about one word written sixty-six years apart; apply metadata, records/argues/omits and "use, not bias" to each, and state what each can establish about the word's meaning.
4. Boundary probe: a person who has overcome everything in himself and has nothing left to overcome. Is he the overman? Answer from Z II 12 ("I am that which must always overcome itself") and Z IV 13 §15, and say what the case shows about the ideal's form.
5. Derivation: from "the overman is the meaning of the earth" and Prologue 3's "to sin against the earth is now the most dreadful thing", derive what Zarathustra must say to someone who finds meaning in the hope of heaven, and show where the derivation uses the death of God.
6. FORWARD (reaches higher-type): the word "overman" occurs once in Beyond Good and Evil (294) and then as the gods' way of laughing. Write down what a prose description of a higher human being would have to contain to replace the image, and test your list against one sentence of BGE 212 (Zimmern, line 3486).

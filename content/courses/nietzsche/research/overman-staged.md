# Research brief: overman-staged (The Overman and the Last Human Being, Dramatized)

status: planned
module: affirmation
type: core (module application, L16)
goal: Interpret Zarathustra's Prologue §§3–5, 'On the Three Metamorphoses' and 'On the Higher Man' against the higher-type thesis of the argued works and state what the overman adds that no argument in them supplies.

## ARC (L10)

- **Inherits:** The positive doctrine reconstructed from the argued works (affirmation, the recurrence test, the higher type, value creation with its problem) and the dramatization rule applied twice; the reader's forward question — what a dramatization of the legislator must show.
- **Contributes:** The overman mapped: Prologue §§3–5 (the meaning of the earth; the rope over the abyss; the last human being) onto BGE 257–262, A §§3–4, TI 'Anti-Nature'; 'On the Three Metamorphoses' (camel, lion, child) onto TI's spiritualization, GS 290, BGE 211 — with the child's 'sacred Yes' as the step no argument reaches; 'On the Higher Man' (Z IV: 'how shall man be overcome' vs. 'preserved') onto BGE 62 and A §§3–4; the proto-fascist misreading refuted from Z 'On the New Idol', BGE 251, A §4's 'lucky accident'; and the departure recorded: the overman's near-absence from the argued works (GM I:16's Napoleon; A §4's 'type') and the one instruction the argued works never give — 'lose me and find yourselves'. The Sign and pity as last sin in a starred section.
- **Hands off:** To Module 7: life-as-literature (Nehamas reads the self-creating character as the doctrine), heidegger-one-thought (the overman as one of the five rubrics).

Spine step: "The positive doctrine: nihilism splits into disorientation and despair, affirmation is tested by eternal recurrence, the higher type is defined by pathos of distance and sublimation — and value creation is where a naturalist must say how values can be made rather than found; the overman and the last human being are its dramatization."

**APPLICATION spec (L16).** Artefact: Zarathustra's Prologue §§3–5, 'On the Three Metamorphoses' (Z I), 'On the Higher Man' (Z IV), with 'On the Bestowing Virtue' §3 as the frame — the most quoted and most weaponized Nietzsche texts in circulation. Module results used: the higher type (BGE 257–262; TI 'Anti-Nature'; GS 290; A §§3–4), affirmation and its test (GS 276, 341), value creation (BGE 211, GS 335), plus BGE 62's harm charge and the last human being as the failure of will to power's third condition (no resistance sought). Departure from the idealisation: the overman is a doctrine in Zarathustra only — the argued works use 'Übermensch' twice (GM I:16 of Napoleon; A §4 of a type 'as a lucky accident') and never as a goal for humanity; the Prologue gives the ideal a name, an image (the rope), a goal ('the meaning of the earth') and a counter-image (the last human being), and 'On the Three Metamorphoses' gives it a developmental sequence whose last step (the child's Yes) no argued passage states as a mechanism; the argued works never instruct the reader to reject the teacher, and the drama does. The lesson records each as a fact about the two registers, and refutes the breeding-programme reading from the texts.

## Key points the lesson must cover

1. **Prologue §§3–5 mapped.** 'I teach you the overman. Human being is something that must be overcome' — BGE 257's 'self-overcoming of man'; 'the meaning of the earth… do not believe those who speak to you of otherworldly hopes' — the fable, 'On the Hinterworldly'; 'a rope fastened between animal and overman — a rope over an abyss' — humanity as transition (no argued counterpart: the image is the addition); the last human being: 'we invented happiness', blinking; 'no shepherd and one herd'; 'a little poison now and then… much poison at the end, for a pleasant death' — BGE 62's 'what ought to perish' preserved, BGE 203's 'the animalization of man into the dwarf animal', and will to power's third condition failing (no resistance sought — Reginster). The crowd's response ('give us this last human being!') as the dramatized audience partition (BGE 30).
2. **'On the Three Metamorphoses' mapped.** The camel: bearing the heaviest ('thou shalt' as load) — the morality of custom, the ascetic discipline (GM III:8's conditions); the lion: 'I will' against the dragon 'Thou shalt' whose scales are values 'a thousand years old' — the revaluation, BGE 211's legislator's No, 'On Old and New Tablets'; the child: 'innocence and forgetting, a new beginning, a game, a self-propelled wheel, a first movement, a sacred Yes' — affirmation (GS 276), creation (GS 301) — and the step no argued passage gives as a mechanism: how a No becomes a Yes without ressentiment. State the gap: the argued works describe the affirmer (BGE 56) and the test (GS 341) but not the transition; the drama asserts it as a metamorphosis.
3. **'On the Higher Man' mapped.** 'God died: now we want the overman to live'; 'how shall man be overcome?' against 'how are human beings to be preserved?'; 'the happiness of the greatest number' rejected — BGE 228's Mill, A §§3–4's 'what type one should breed' (the verb is Nietzsche's — 'züchten'), BGE 62; 'you higher men… are you failures?' — the higher men of Part IV as still transitional (pity, resignation, the ass festival). Map; note that Z IV was privately printed (Private Publications in the eKGWB) and that its speaker addresses 'higher men' who are not the overman.
4. **The proto-fascist misreading refuted (L13, genuine and prevalent).** Claim: 'The overman is a superior race to be bred by a state.' Rejection from the texts: Z I 'On the New Idol' (the state as 'the coldest of all cold monsters'; 'where the state ends, there begins the human being'); BGE 251 (the 'good European', against antisemitism); A §4: the higher type 'has appeared often — as a lucky accident, never as willed'; 'züchten' in A §3 is the same word Nietzsche uses of religions and moralities as breeders (BGE 61–62), i.e. cultural discipline, not eugenics — and the passage says the type is not to replace mankind. Replacement: an ideal for individuals and cultures, dramatized as a figure; the Archiv's compilation (will-to-power-the-book) as the channel of the misreading. Discriminating example: the overman is defined against the state in the same book that introduces him.
5. **The frame: 'lose me and find yourselves'.** 'On the Bestowing Virtue' §3: 'now I bid you lose me and find yourselves; and only when you have all denied me will I return to you' — reprised as Z II's epigraph. The argued works never say this; the drama makes it the condition of following. State it as the departure that matters most for how the doctrine may be cited: a teaching whose teacher demands rejection is not a creed, and the course reads it accordingly.
6. **The overman in the argued works.** Two occurrences: GM I:16 (Napoleon as 'synthesis of Unmensch and Übermensch' — a historical individual, ambivalent) and A §4 ('a kind of overman' as a type that has appeared). Never as a goal for humanity; never argued. The departure: the drama gives the ideal a proper name and a future; the argued works give it a type and a past. Kaufmann's Ch. 11 thesis (overman and recurrence inseparable — the recurrence undercuts any future-directed reading of the overman; Clark Ch. 8 §4's Zarathustra valuing life as a means) as the analytic bridge back to eternal-recurrence.
7. **★ The Sign (starred).** Z IV's end: the higher men leave; the lion and the doves; 'pity for the higher man' as 'my last sin' overcome; 'up now, up, you great noon'. Map to GM Preface §5 and A §7 (pity); note it resolves the plot the Prologue's sun opened; one starred section.

- **The rope over the abyss** — 'the single most reproduced conceptual image from Zarathustra' (dossier); draw it labeled: animal — rope (humanity, a crossing) — overman; the abyss beneath.
- **The three metamorphoses** as a three-stage progression (dossier and Beckman); the dragon's scales as inherited values.
- **The last human being's speech** ('we invented happiness') quoted — the photographic negative.
- **'Where the state ends, there begins the human being'** ('On the New Idol') as the refutation's anchor.
- **'Lose me and find yourselves'** as the frame line.
- **The two argued-work occurrences** of Übermensch, quoted, dated.
- **Prologue §4's catalogue mapped to Reginster's creators**: 'I love the one who…' lines in one column, what each loves (an activity, never an end-state) in the other — the overman as the direction of a love, not its object.
- **Faust's wager** (Reginster 6.I.2): 'if I ever lie down upon a bed of ease, then let that be my final end' — the literary text that says what 'the meaning of the earth' asks for.

## Pitfalls / subtleties to flag

- 'Züchten' (A §3) must be handled with its own usage (BGE 61–62 on religions as breeders); the refutation is textual and must be exact, not reassuring.
- The lesson does not claim the overman is harmless or liberal; it claims the breeding-programme reading is not the text's, and records BGE 257–259 as the passages a political reading must answer (higher-type-and-sublimation did).
- 'Superman' appears only as a translation datum (key-terms-in-translation).
- Heidegger's overman is Module 7's; one paragraph.
- The Sign is starred; nothing after it depends on it.

## EXERCISE PLAN (floor: ≤1 warm-up; ≥2 of boundary / refute / derivation / research; one FORWARD)

1. (warm-up, mapping) Complete the thesis map for Prologue §§3–5's six key lines with the argued section or 'none'.
2. (refute) "The overman is a biological superman Nietzsche wanted a state to breed." Break it with 'On the New Idol', A §4 and BGE 251, and with the usage of 'züchten' in BGE 61–62; then state what remains in the record that a political reading must still answer (BGE 257–259) and where the course answered it.
3. (derivation) Derive from Reginster's three conditions why the last human being is will to power's failure case (no determinate end beyond comfort; no resistance sought; no overcoming), and state what that makes the overman on the psychological doctrine (the type that seeks resistance) — and why that is not yet the child's 'sacred Yes' (affirmation is a further condition).
4. (boundary probe) Suppose the child's metamorphosis were replaced by the lion's alone (a No without a Yes). What would the resulting figure be on GM I:10's definitions? State what the drama claims the child adds (creation from innocence, not from negation) and whether any argued passage supplies a mechanism for it — or only a description (BGE 56, GS 276).
5. (research-shaped) Count the occurrences of 'Übermensch' in the eKGWB's published works outside Zarathustra (GM, JGB, GD, AC, EH — the staged German files; EH counts as authorized manuscript) and report each with its sentence; then state what the count shows about the doctrine's evidential base and how the published-works rule applies to a Zarathustra-only doctrine.
6. (dramatization, frame) Read 'On the Bestowing Virtue' §3 and Z II's epigraph; state the instruction, and write what it does to any exercise that cites a Zarathustra speech as Nietzsche's view — then apply it to your own answer to exercise 2.
7. (FORWARD → life-as-literature) Zarathustra bids his disciples lose him; GS 290 bids one give style to one's character; EH's subtitle is 'how one becomes what one is'. Write what reading these three together would make of Nietzsche's philosophy if its defence is not that it is true but that it is one person's coherent self-creation — and state the objection that reading must answer (what then is the standing of the Genealogy's claims?); the solution says Nehamas's reading, and the closing module, begin there.

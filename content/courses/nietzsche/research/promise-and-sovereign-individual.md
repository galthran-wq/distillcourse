## Key points the lesson must cover

1. **II:1: the breeding problem.** 'To breed an animal with the right to make promises — is this not the paradoxical task nature has set itself with regard to man?' Forgetting as an active, positive faculty (a doorkeeper, 'psychic assimilation' like digestion); memory as the counter-faculty, 'an active desire not to rid oneself' — the memory of the will. Reconstruct: (P) promising requires holding a will fixed across time against forgetting; (P) forgetting is the animal's default and is healthy; (C) the capacity to promise had to be bred against nature's grain — it is an achievement with a history.
2. **II:2: the morality of custom and its fruit.** Man made 'calculable, regular, necessary' by the 'morality of custom' and the 'social straitjacket'; at the end of the process the sovereign individual — 'autonomous and supra-ethical', 'liberated again from the morality of custom', with the right to say yes to himself, whose consciousness of his power and freedom becomes his 'dominating instinct' — 'he calls it his conscience'. Daybreak §9 supplies the definition: under the morality of custom the free individual is the evil one; 'sovereign' is the reversal.
3. **II:3: the mnemotechnics of pain.** 'Only what does not cease to hurt remains in memory'; the catalogue (stoning, breaking on the wheel, boiling, flaying, the Germanic punishments); asceticism as mnemonic. Grade the evidence as the course grades all of Nietzsche's evidence: the catalogue documents cruelty in punishment; the inference to 'memory was burned in for the sake of promising' is a hypothesis about function — flag the origin/use rule (genealogy-as-method) against Nietzsche himself here.
4. **The two readings, stated with their evidence.** Leiter: the sovereign individual is what the breeding process produces at its end, a description of a type, not an ideal; 'free', 'autonomous' are persuasive definitions — Nietzsche relabels a bred regularity with honorific words, and the passage is continuous with I:13 (no doer; a type that keeps its word because it is that type). Anderson: the passage's vocabulary of achievement, rank, 'the right', 'the privilege of responsibility', and the sovereign individual's 'measure of value' for others describe an ideal of self-legislation that the drive psychology must accommodate. The sentences each must explain: 'liberated again from the morality of custom' (Anderson's) and 'the ripest fruit' of a process (Leiter's).
5. **The tension with I:13, stated as the naturalist reading's first strain.** If freedom is a fiction, a 'free' individual is a doer behind a deed; if II:2 is sincere, I:13 must be read as denying only the metaphysical (causa sui) freedom, leaving a bred, type-level responsibility. BGE 19's feeling of freedom and BGE 21's strong wills are the reconciling texts. The lesson states both routes and does not decide; guilt-reconstructed decides.

# Research brief: recurrence-as-cosmology (Eternal recurrence as cosmology)

status: planned

## ARC (L10)
- **Spine step (11, "Saying yes"):** "The reply to nihilism is affirmation: loving what is necessary, tested by the demon's offer to live this life again forever, a test whose force does not depend on the physics; a self that can pass it is achieved, not given, by giving style to one's drives, and Ecce Homo is Nietzsche putting his own life to the test"
- **Inherits:** from eternal-recurrence, the test as a conditional and the one sentence saying he also tried to prove it; from nietzsche-life-and-books, books before notebooks and the concept nachlass (this lesson's primary material is almost all unpublished).
- **Contributes:** whether he believed it literally: in the notebooks he argued from finite force and no final state; the argument needs a premise he never states (finitely many states); three wheels show it false. A self-contained exercise in finding an unstated premise, with a demo the reader loses against. Introduces cosmological-recurrence. Shows from the books that Nietzsche held the plain cosmology at arm's length (the dwarf's "too easy", the animals' "hurdy-gurdy song") while claiming the title "teacher of the eternal recurrence".
- **Hands off:** back to the core path at becoming-who-you-are: "to imprint upon becoming the character of being" (7[54], unpublished) names what the cosmology was for, and the next core lesson gives the one published version of that stamping, a self given unity by style. The test survives the failed proof because its force never rested on it.

## STANCE (binding)
- **Favorable:** Nietzsche's instinct was right about what the hypothesis would need (no final state, finite force, infinite time), he reasoned from the conservation of energy a decade before recurrence theorems were proved for bounded systems, and he wanted the hypothesis because it would be "the most scientific" form of a thought that was first "the most extreme form of nihilism" (5[71]). The lesson gives the argument at full strength before it breaks it and treats the failure as instructive, not as a verdict on the thought.
- **Hard passages this lesson must quote whole:** none in the stance's sense; the texts carry nothing on rank, race, women or cruelty. Quoted whole for another reason: 38[12] (Bittner pp.38-39, unpublished), so the reader sees cosmology and will to power asserted in one breath and knows it was never printed.
- **What the words meant:** "force" (Kraft): the physicist's conserved quantity, which the notes supplement with an "inner world" (36[31], unpublished); "final state"/"goal": an equilibrium or end, impossible on pain of already having been reached; "eternal novelty": an unbounded capacity for new configurations, denied to a finite force and identified with "the beloved old, infinite, boundlessly creative God" (36[15]); "hurdy-gurdy song" (Leier-Lied, Z III 13): a tune ground out mechanically, Zarathustra's word for the cosmology stated as a formula.
- **Objection answered from his side:** the objection is the lesson's result: finite force does not entail finitely many states, so a finite system in infinite time need not repeat (wheels at rates 1, 2 and 1/pi never realign). Two replies on his side. First, no book stakes the thought on the proof: GS 341 is a conditional, BGE 56 an ideal, Z marks the plain cosmology as glib, EH gives no argument. Second, the one argument Zarathustra gives (Z III 2 §2) is not state-counting: an infinite past plus things "knotted together" so that each moment "draws after it all that is to come", a claim about causal closure, which fails differently (an infinite past does not entail that this moment has already occurred); one sentence says so. Gap: TI "Ancients" 5 signs "the teacher of the eternal recurrence" and EH III BT 3 claims Heraclitus taught it; he did not keep test and cosmology apart, and the lesson does not pretend he did.
- **Humanizing reading to test:** Kaufmann's verdict (Portable p.348, note to Z IV 19) of a "mistaken belief that science compels us to accept the hypothesis", and his monograph's reading (Ch. 11 II) that the thought was an experience first and the proof an afterthought left unpublished. The text supports the second half (no proof in any book) and complicates the first: 5[71] sec. 6 calls it "the most scientific of all possible hypotheses" and 5[54] deduces it from the conservation of energy in one line (both unpublished). The lesson reports the verdict as Kaufmann's and shows the notes.
- **Facts against the popular image:** the Ludovici "Eternal Recurrence" appendix (twilightof.txt) presents 1881 notebook material as if it were a treatise; it is Nachlass arranged by the Archive's translators, the route by which The Will to Power was made. Reported because the text's history says so.

## The reconstruction
- **Claim and where (unpublished).** 36[15], 5[54], 5[71] sec. 6, 38[12]: the world is a finite, determinate force in infinite time with no final state; therefore it recurs; "the most scientific of all possible hypotheses".
- **Argument in plain steps.** (1) The total force is finite (36[15]: an infinite force is "incompatible with the concept of force"). (2) Time has no beginning ("behind us lies an eternity", Z III 2 §2). (3) If a final state were possible it would already have been reached, since infinite time has passed; it has not; so there is none (36[15], 5[71] sec. 6, 11[72]). (4) A finite force "lacks the capacity for eternal novelty" (36[15]). (5) So the sequence of states repeats. (6) Each state fixes the next; once a state repeats, the whole history repeats ("knotted together"). Conclusion: identical recurrence, infinitely often.
- **Unstated premise.** From (4) to (5): that a finite force admits only finitely many states (or that "identical cases" recur, 14[98]). Never stated or argued; the "every possible combination" wording belongs to notes outside the volumes read (WP 1066, to verify). A finite quantity can be distributed in a continuum of ways.
- **Simmel's counterexample.** Three equal wheels on one axle, a point marked on each, aligned at the start; the second turns twice as fast as the first, the third at 1/pi the speed of the first; since 1/pi is irrational the marks never realign, though the system is finite and runs forever; step (5) fails (Kaufmann p.327).
- **Second weak point.** Exact identity: "this life" recurring needs exact sameness, assumed ("identical cases") rather than shown.
- **Nietzsche's own attitude.** Proof in notes only; the 1882 plan to study natural science (Kaufmann Ch. 1); the books present a conditional (GS 341), an ideal (BGE 56), a vision and teaching (Z), a "formula of affirmation" (EH); Zarathustra rebukes the dwarf and the animals; yet TI "Ancients" 5 and EH III BT 3 claim the doctrine. Verdict: the force is practical; the cosmology a hope reasoned toward in private, neither staked in the books nor disowned.
- **Connections.** eternal-recurrence; nietzsche-life-and-books (nachlass; the Archive's compilations); will-to-power-as-psychology (38[12]'s "and nothing besides", the cosmic extension BGE 36 states only conditionally); becoming-who-you-are (7[54]); the-system-assembled (the cosmology as a named gap).

## EXERCISE PLAN
Floor check: claim to refute (2), derivation (3), boundary probe (4), research-shaped (5).
1. Warm-up (the demo task's one computation): wheels at 1, 2 and 3 turns per hour from an aligned start; the first realignment time, and what property of the ratios makes one exist.
2. Claim to refute: "A finite system running forever must eventually repeat itself exactly." Counterexample and the assumption the claim needs.
3. Derivation: if states were finitely many and each fixed the next, the history is periodic from some point; mark where finiteness and where determinism are used, and which of the two the notes supply.
4. Boundary probe: suppose a final equilibrium were possible. What does 36[15]'s "it could not fail to have been reached" assume about the past, and does the inference hold if the approach to equilibrium is asymptotic?

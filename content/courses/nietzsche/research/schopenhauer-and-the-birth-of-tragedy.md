# Research brief: schopenhauer-and-the-birth-of-tragedy (Schopenhauer's pessimism and The Birth of Tragedy)

status: planned
module: the-problem (1 of 9), lesson 2 of 45
type: core (second easy opener, C3)
goal: Explain Schopenhauer's case that existence is suffering and how The Birth of Tragedy accepts the suffering while rejecting the verdict through art

## ARC (L10)

- **Spine step (2, "The first answer"):** "Schopenhauer taught that existence is suffering and the wise man stops wanting; the young Nietzsche accepted the suffering and refused the verdict, answering that Greek tragedy shows how art can justify existence, and his 1886 self-criticism names what in that answer he kept and what he dropped"
- **Inherits:** from nietzsche-life-and-books, period 1, the 1865 bookshop find, the habit of reconstruction, and the rule that a 1886 preface is the later Nietzsche judging the earlier.
- **Contributes:** the course's question in its first form (how is existence borne once we see what it is) and the first answer (art, for a world-artist). Introduces will-to-live, schopenhauers-pessimism, apollonian-and-dionysian, aesthetic-justification; reinforces three-periods. Sets two phrases the course reuses: "is there a pessimism of strength?" (Self-Crit 1) and morality as "the danger of dangers" (Self-Crit 5).
- **Hands off:** to death-of-god (the early answer needed a world behind the world; the mature Nietzsche keeps the question, drops the metaphysics) and nihilism (Self-Crit 5's "will to perish"; the Dionysian as anti-nihilist symbol, GT Appendix 1). Apollo/Dionysus return in case-of-wagner (GS 370 redefines "Dionysian") and value-of-untruth (GS 107); Schopenhauer returns in will-to-power-as-psychology (boredom), against-pity, amor-fati; "danger of dangers" titles danger-of-dangers.

## STANCE (binding)

- **Favorable:** what the 25-year-old saw that the optimistic tradition missed: the Greeks at their strongest wanted tragedy, so pessimism need not be a symptom of decline (Self-Crit 1), and a culture's art can be read as the answer to a need (GT 3: "To be able to live, the Greeks had, from direst necessity, to create these gods"). The 1886 self-criticism gets its full say as his own verdict, sharper than any critic's.
- **Hard passages this lesson must quote whole:** GT 18 (early-gt.txt l.3369ff): Alexandrian optimism "requires a slave class" while denying it; "There is nothing more terrible than a barbaric slave class, who have learned to regard their existence as an injustice, and now prepare to take vengeance, not only for themselves, but for all generations." GT 9 (l.1733ff): Prometheus as the "Aryan" myth of active sin against the "Semitic" Fall as passive, feminine sin.
- **What the words meant:** "slave class" (GT 18) denotes the labouring base every high culture has rested on, which the modern "dignity of labour" refuses to name; first statement of BGE 257-258's "slavery in some sense" (order-of-rank). "Aryan/Semitic" (GT 9) is 1872 philology's vocabulary for the Indo-European and Semitic language families and their myth-stocks, used here as a typology of guilt (a crime committed versus a seduction suffered). "Pessimism" names the verdict that non-existence is better, not a mood. "Dionysian" here is the 1872 sense (intoxication, the individual dissolved); GS 370 redefines it against romanticism.
- **Objection answered from his side:** the aesthetic justification is the spectator's, or the world-artist's who "contrives to redeem himself only in appearance" (Self-Crit 5), not the sufferer's; and tragic grandeur says nothing of the quotidian. Replies in order: GT 7 (art as "saving enchantress" for the Dionysian man's nausea); GT 25 (the old Athenian); then the later turn in which the sufferer becomes the artist of his own life: GS 107 (pp.163-164, existence "bearable" as aesthetic phenomenon), GS 276 (p.223, amor fati as making beautiful), GS 290. Gap sentence: in 1872 the reply does not exist; the justification is the god's and we are his "pictures and artistic projections" (GT 5), and Nietzsche's 1886 verdict is that the "metaphysical comfort" was romanticism, to be replaced by "the art of earthly comfort" (Self-Crit 7).
- **Humanizing reading to test:** that BT already teaches art as "a strong organism's response to suffering", a human health-claim (Kaufmann Ch.4 I pp.129-133). The text (GT 4-5, Self-Crit 5) makes it a claim about the suffering Primordial Unity; the health reading is what Nietzsche made of it in 1886-88 (EH III BT 1-2; TI Ancients 4-5). Keep the dates apart.
- **Facts against the popular image:** the "pro-Wagner" book already contained, by its author's account, its own refutation (Self-Crit 6-7).

## The reconstruction

- **Claim and where.** Schopenhauer: existence is worse than non-existence because wanting is pain, satisfaction only relief, want's absence boredom; the cure is to stop willing (WWR I §§56-59). Nietzsche 1872: the description is right, the verdict wrong; "only as an aesthetic phenomenon is existence and the world eternally justified" (GT 5, 24; Foreword; Self-Crit 5).
- **Argument in plain steps.** Schopenhauer: (1) behind appearances one blind striving, the will to live, and we are it (Kant in one paragraph: appearance and thing-in-itself, renamed will); (2) willing starts from lack, and lack is pain; (3) satisfaction only removes a pain; (4) with nothing lacking, boredom; (5) life swings between pain and boredom; (6) so non-existence is preferable and the wise deny the will (art briefly; asceticism and compassion lastingly). Load-bearing: (3). BT: (1') accept (1)-(5) as Silenus (GT 3); (2') the Greeks, who knew it, lived more brilliantly than anyone, so a people can know the worst and live; (3') by art: the Olympians as mirror, then tragedy, the Dionysian insight made bearable in Apollonian form (GT 3, 7-8, 21); (4') the suffering ground itself needs appearance for its "continuous salvation" (GT 4), so art is the world's own activity, not consolation; (5') existence is justified, aesthetically, from the artist-god's side (GT 5, 24).
- **Unstated premise.** Schopenhauer: (3), pleasure never positive. BT: (4'), the metaphysics of the Primordial Unity, which GT 4 calls an "assumption" and never argues, and which Self-Crit 5 calls "arbitrary, idle, fantastic, if you will". Without it the justification is a spectator's consolation, which is the objection.
- **Strongest objection and Nietzsche's reply.** In STANCE. Second objection, to Schopenhauer: pleasures not preceded by lack exist (coffee on waking, uncraved), so (3) falls; Nietzsche's own later route runs through boredom instead: we want resistance to overcome (will-to-power-as-psychology; jail cell, Reginster p.125).
- **Connections.** death-of-god (metaphysics dropped, question kept); nihilism (Self-Crit 5's "will to perish"; GT 7's loathing as what nihilism feels like); case-of-wagner (GT 16, 19-22; GS 370); danger-of-dangers (Self-Crit 5 a year before GM Pref 6); will-to-power-as-psychology, against-pity, amor-fati (Schopenhauer as foil); value-of-untruth ("all life rests on appearance"); real-world-became-a-fable (BT's artist-god as the last world-behind-the-world he held).

## Pitfalls

- Keep Schopenhauer to will, pendulum, resignation, compassion; compassion returns in against-pity; Kant only where (1) needs him.
- BT is not his mature view, and the 1886 preface is not a repudiation of the question: Self-Crit 1-2 keep it, 6-7 drop the formulae and the Wagnerian hopes.
- Two "Dionysians" (1872: intoxication, needs Apollo; post-1886: superabundance against romantic hunger, GS 370 n.124-126). Say it once; hand to case-of-wagner.
- The justification is the world-artist's (GT 4-5, Self-Crit 5). Never write "worth beholding and making" as BT's claim; it is the later reply, dated.
- Foreword dated end of 1871; the Wagner hopes are GT 16, 19-22.
- Haussmann is stiff and sometimes wrong; give the German beside the load-bearing sentences, "verify against eKGWB".
- WWR not staged: verify every quoted sentence against Payne or paraphrase.
- GT 18 and 9 quoted whole, explained by what the words denoted; no distancing sentence.
- "One reading says" where Tanner's objection or Kaufmann's health reading is used; names in References only.

## EXERCISE PLAN

Floor check: claim to refute (3), derivation (4), boundary probe (5); one warm-up (1); ex. 1 reuses lesson 1's dating rule.

1. Warm-up: Foreword (Basel, end of 1871), Self-Criticism (Sils-Maria, August 1886), Appendix (1888): which speaks for the mature Nietzsche, and which sentence of Self-Crit 5 could not have been written in 1871.
2. Analyze a scene: given Oedipus's blinding, what the Apollonian and the Dionysian each contribute to why the audience is not simply depressed (GT 7-9, 21); GT 9's "wisdom is a crime against nature" as the hero's Dionysian content.
3. Claim to refute: "Nietzsche disagreed with Schopenhauer about how much people suffer." Show from GT 3 the disagreement is about the verdict; name the premise BT adds (GT 4).
4. Derivation: from "only as an aesthetic phenomenon is existence justified", derive what an ordinary life (no tragedy, no art) would need for justification on BT's terms; state the problem and quote GT 5's conceding clause ("pictures and artistic projections").
5. Boundary probe: a pleasure that is not relief from a lack. Which step falls, does the verdict survive, and does BT need the step to fall? (No: BT accepts the description.)
6. FORWARD (reaches will-to-power-as-psychology): Schopenhauer explains boredom as nothing to want; using the jail cell (bored while wanting out), propose another account. Solution: absence of resistance, not of objects; <Ref> to will-to-power-as-psychology, where BGE 13 and A 2 build on this ("the feeling that power is growing, that resistance is overcome").

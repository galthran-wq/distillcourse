# Research brief: self-as-society-of-drives (The self as a society of drives)

status: planned
module: the-human-being (2 of 9), lesson 6 of 45
type: core
goal: Explain Nietzsche's claim that a person is an organized plurality of drives rather than a unified soul, and the argument that supports it

## ARC (L10)

- **Spine step (4, "The drive-self"):** "Before asking where values came from, Nietzsche asks who values: not a unified soul that sees itself clearly, but a society of drives with a thin conscious surface made for talking to the herd, and grammar's invented doer whom morality holds responsible."
- **Inherits:** from nietzsche-life-and-books (the only prerequisite): BGE 68 read as an argument with an unstated premise (no judge above memory and pride); from the reader's background: Plato's soul with three parts and reason in command.
- **Contributes:** the picture of the person the course runs on: drives as dispositions that interpret and want; the self as an order of rank among them with no commander outside them; unity as an achievement. Introduces `drives`, `soul-as-commonwealth`. Every later mechanism (ressentiment, internalization, sublimation, giving style) is a rearrangement of drives.
- **Hands off:** to consciousness-and-language: if the person is mostly drives at work below awareness, what consciousness is and why it feels like the whole self. To will-to-power-as-psychology: what principle runs through the drives.

## STANCE (binding)

- **Favorable:** Nietzsche decomposed the self into competing, interpreting sub-agents with no central ego decades before psychoanalysis and a century before modular accounts of the mind, from introspective evidence anyone can check (BGE 68, D 109), with a theory of why the unity nonetheless feels given (lessons 7-8). The lesson shows the picture as the economical hypothesis it is.
- **Hard passages this lesson must quote whole:** none of the race, women or slavery passages belong here. The hard vocabulary is political, applied inside the soul, quoted as written: BGE 19 (bge-zimmern.txt line 624ff; the body "a social structure of many souls", the willer as ruling class, "L'effet c'est moi"); BGE 6 (line 277ff; the order of rank of a philosopher's drives); BGE 158 (conscience truckling to "the tyrant in us"); GM II 1 (p.57, the organism "an oligarchy"); EH II 9 (pp.253-255, "an order of rank among these capacities"). The order-of-rank lesson applies the same words to society (BGE 257, distance "within the soul itself"); say once that the two uses are one vocabulary.
- **What the words meant:** Trieb (drive) is the word in BGE 6, 12, 13, 36 (Zimmern often writes "instinct"); Instinkt and Affekt overlap; define drive once. "Soul" is kept as a hypothesis: BGE 12 refuses only soul-atomism and asks for "mortal soul", "subject-multiplicity", "social structure of the drives and affects" (Gesellschaftsbau der Triebe und Affekte). "Order of rank" (Rangordnung), "command", "aristocracy" name relations of authority among drives: who gets to interpret a situation.
- **Objection answered from his side:** if drives fight, who arranges them, and who could master or style them; a heap cannot be weak-willed or self-critical. Reply, texts first: the one who combats a drive is itself a drive (D 109, p.65; BGE 117); willing is already command and obedience among parts with no commander outside (BGE 19); knowing is "a certain behavior of the instincts toward one another" ending in "a kind of justice and a contract" (GS 333, pp.261-262); the ruling unity grows from below and "trains all subservient capacities before giving any hint of the dominant task" (EH II 9, p.254). The gap, in one sentence: Nietzsche never says what makes a coalition of drives one person rather than several; his notebook poses the question ("A kind of aristocracy of 'cells' in which mastery resides?", unpublished, notebook 40, Aug-Sep 1885, Bittner p.46).
- **Humanizing reading to test:** two readings reinstate a self. One treats the soul as a "political order" refined from Plato's tripartite soul with a will to value above the drives (Clark & Dudrick); BGE 12 names Boscovich, never Plato's three parts. The other reads Geist as the drive to reason winning mastery (Kaufmann, Ch. 8 I); Z I 4 (p.146) calls spirit "a little instrument and toy" of the body, and BGE 6 denies a drive to knowledge as the father of philosophy. The minimal-self reading (a higher-order structure that can evaluate its own attitudes) may be built as the strongest friendly reconstruction and named as one.
- **Facts against the popular image:** none carried by these texts.

## The reconstruction

- **Claim and where.** BGE 12: the soul survives as "social structure of the drives and affects". BGE 19 and EH II 9 add that the plurality is ordered; D 119 and D 422 supply the plurality without the order.
- **Argument in plain steps.** (1) Inner conflict is observed: memory against pride (BGE 68), a craving against the wish to be rid of it (D 109). (2) The side that fights is itself a motive with an aim (D 109-110). (3) Introspection never finds a motive-free arbiter, only further motives (BGE 117; GS 333). (4) The economical hypothesis: a person is the organized set of these dispositions, each interpreting in its interest and seeking to prevail (BGE 6; D 119). (5) What says "I" is whichever drive or coalition rules and identifies itself with the whole (BGE 19). (6) Stable rule with strong parts is a character; its absence is "a ball of wild snakes" (Z I 6) or the modern self-contradiction of instincts (TI Skirmishes 41).
- **Unstated premise.** Step 3 infers "not there" from "not observed"; the support is parsimony (Boscovich's knife, BGE 12) plus lessons 7-8 on why the unity feels given.
- **Strongest objection and Nietzsche's reply.** As in STANCE.
- **Connections.** consciousness-and-language; doer-behind-the-deed (BGE 19); will-to-power-as-psychology (BGE 6); sublimation (D 109, D 560); bad-conscience; higher-type and becoming-who-you-are (EH II 9; BGE 212); order-of-rank.

## Where the claim forks (named by content)

Whether anything survives the critique of soul-atomism that can be called a self: the "I" as nothing over the ruling drive of the moment (BGE 19; notebook 1[58]), or a stable higher-order structure that can evaluate its own attitudes (EH II 9; GS 290). The texts support both; the lesson builds the second and says EH II 9's "organizing idea" is Nietzsche's own name for it.

## Pitfalls

- The Plato comparison is the course's entry device; BGE 12 names Boscovich. Never write that Nietzsche "refines Plato's tripartite soul".
- Daybreak has plurality and no ruling drive; the hierarchy is BGE 6, 19, EH II 9.
- The aristocracy of cells is a question; cite notebook 40, Bittner p.46, unpublished; never WP 490.
- GM II 1's "oligarchy" sits inside the account of forgetting: an image, not a thesis.
- Define drive once; note that Zimmern renders Trieb as "instinct"; one sentence says the political vocabulary names dispositions, not homunculi.
- No invented scenes; examples are Nietzsche's (D 109, D 119) or the reader's own observed conflict.

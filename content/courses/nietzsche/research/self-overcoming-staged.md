goal: Interpret 'On Self-Overcoming', 'On the Despisers of the Body' and 'On the Hinterworldly' against the module's reconstructed arguments and state where the drama asserts what BGE 36 fails to prove.

## ARC (L10)

- **Inherits:** The module's three results — the society of drives (BGE 12, 19), will to power sorted (BGE 36 begs the question; the psychological doctrine survives), the fable (no true world) — and the dramatization rule from tarantulas-staged.
- **Contributes:** Three speeches mapped: 'On Self-Overcoming' (Life's testimony: 'I am that which must always overcome itself'; 'not will to life… will to power'; 'the will to truth' as 'a will to the thinkability of all being' — the wisest's will to power) onto BGE 13, 36, 259, 22 and GM II:12; 'On the Despisers of the Body' onto BGE 12 and 19; 'On the Hinterworldly' onto the fable. The departure recorded: Life's speech delivers the cosmological doctrine as a character's testimony precisely where BGE 36's argument fails; 'On Poets' as the frame's warning.
- **Hands off:** To Module 6 (nihilism-and-affirmation): the will's self-overcoming is the material of affirmation; to heidegger-one-thought, which reads 'On Self-Overcoming' as doctrine.

Spine step: "The psychology beneath the genealogy: no soul-atom but a society of drives, willing as command among under-wills, will to power as the principle — defensible as psychology, question-begging as cosmology — and the two-world metaphysics that had to become a fable first; Zarathustra's 'Life' speaks the doctrine the argument cannot prove."

**APPLICATION spec (L16).** Artefact: three speeches of Zarathustra (Z II 'On Self-Overcoming'; Z I 'On the Despisers of the Body'; Z I 'On the Hinterworldly'), the texts through which the will-to-power doctrine entered the culture as a slogan. Module results used: BGE 12 and 19 (the drive-self and command), BGE 13 (against will to life), BGE 36's seven steps with the question-begging step, BGE 22 (interpretation), the fable's stage six, GM II:12 (reinterpretation). Departure from the idealisation: the argued works offer the cosmological doctrine as an experiment in the conditional; the speech has 'Life itself' assert it in the first person and Zarathustra report it as heard — testimony replaces argument; the personification supplies the authority BGE 36 lacks and drops the 'suppose'. 'On the Despisers of the Body' adds an image (the self as 'a great reason', the ego as its instrument) that BGE 19 argues; 'On the Hinterworldly' adds a motive (suffering) that the fable's stage six lacks. 'On Poets' ('the poets lie too much… Zarathustra too is a poet') is the text's own note on its evidential status. The lesson records each as a fact about the two registers.

## Key points the lesson must cover

1. **'On Self-Overcoming' mapped sentence by sentence.** 'Will to truth… a will to the thinkability of all being' — BGE 22 (physics as interpretation), GM III:24 (the will to truth as the wisest's will to power); 'you want to create the world before which you can kneel' — BGE 9 (the Stoics imposing their morality on nature); 'where I found the living, there I found will to power; and even in the will of the serving I found the will to be master' — BGE 13, 259, GM II:12; 'the weaker serves the stronger… but the smaller surrenders to the greater so that it may have… power over the smallest' — BGE 19's command and obedience among under-wills; 'Life itself spoke to me: I am that which must always overcome itself' — BGE 36's conclusion in the first person; 'not will to life… will to power'; 'whoever must be a creator in good and evil must first be an annihilator' — Z 'On a Thousand and One Goals', BGE 211. A two-column table; the residue column marks what has no argued counterpart (Life's 'secret', the personification).
2. **The departure at the centre.** BGE 36: 'suppose… would be will to power and nothing besides' (conditional; question-begging at step 1). 'On Self-Overcoming': 'Life itself spoke… I am that which must always overcome itself' (indicative; a character's report of a personification's testimony). State the difference as a fact about evidential status: a speech by a character citing a personification is not an argument, and the text's own 'On Poets' says so. What the drama adds: authority and the first person; what it drops: the 'suppose' and the conscience of method; what it undercuts: itself (Zarathustra 'too is a poet').
3. **'On the Despisers of the Body' mapped.** 'Body am I entirely, and nothing else; and soul is only a word for something about the body'; the body as 'a great reason, a multiplicity with one sense'; the 'I' as the body's instrument; 'behind your thoughts and feelings… stands a mighty commander, an unknown sage — his name is self' — BGE 12 (society of drives), BGE 19 (command), GS 354 (consciousness as the smallest part), BGE 17 ('it' thinks). Residue: the speech names the self a 'commander' and a 'sage' — a unity BGE 19's plurality of under-wills does not assert; note it for value-creation and life-as-literature (the unity of the self).
4. **'On the Hinterworldly' mapped.** 'It was suffering and incapacity that created all hinterworlds'; the sick body's 'leap' beyond itself; Zarathustra's own former hinterworld — the fable's stages 1–2 with a motive (suffering) that Twilight's 'Reason' §6 thesis 3 states ('revenge on life') and the fable itself does not argue. Residue: the confession ('I too once cast my delusion beyond man') — the speaker inside the error, as in tarantulas-staged.
5. **The frame.** 'On Poets': 'the poets lie too much'; 'we know too little and are bad learners: so we must lie'; 'Zarathustra too is a poet'. The animals' 'hurdy-gurdy song' in 'The Convalescent' (forward, eternal-recurrence) as the same device. The rule: the speeches are evidence of what Nietzsche staged; the argued works are evidence of what he argued; the course cites the second for the first's content.
6. **Application verdict.** Adds: personified authority; a motive for the two-world error; an image of the self's unity. Drops: the conditional; the method; the evidence. Undercuts: its own speaker. Heidegger's reading (Vol. III Part Three §2) takes the speech as doctrine — the servant's will to master read as a metaphysical law; the course records this as the reading Module 7 evaluates.

## Pitfalls / subtleties to flag

- The lesson must not argue that Zarathustra is 'wrong'; the point is evidential status — what a staged speech may be cited for. Keep the verdict to adds / drops / undercuts.
- 'Life' is a personification; do not gloss it as 'nature' or 'biology' — the Heideggerian and biological readings each do that, and the course's job here is to record that the text does not.
- The 'commander… sage… self' of 'Despisers' is a real residue; do not smooth it into BGE 19.
- Three speeches and one frame passage; no Zarathustra tour.
- Del Caro's 'overman' and his footnotes are the course's; 'superman' does not appear.

## EXERCISE PLAN (floor: ≤1 warm-up; ≥2 of boundary / refute / derivation / research; one FORWARD)

1. (warm-up, mapping) Complete the map for the four lines of 'On Self-Overcoming' the lesson leaves blank, with the argued section or 'none'.
2. (refute) "'On Self-Overcoming' is Nietzsche's proof that life is will to power." Break it on evidential status: name the speaker, the source of the speaker's claim (a personification's speech), and the frame ('On Poets'); then say which argued passage carries the claim and in what mood (BGE 36, conditional) — and what the speech may therefore be cited for.
3. (derivation) Derive from BGE 19 what 'even in the will of the serving I found the will to be master' would mean as psychology (the under-will's obedience as its own command over what is below it), and state whether the speech's next lines ('the smaller surrenders to the greater so that it may have power over the smallest') say the same — then say what Heidegger's reading (the servant dominating through indispensability) adds that neither text states.
4. (boundary probe) Suppose the speech were rewritten in the argued register with BGE 36's conditional restored ('suppose life were that which must overcome itself'). What would it lose as drama and what would it gain as evidence? State what that shows about why Nietzsche staged the doctrine at all (the reader's forward hook to affirmation: a doctrine one must be able to will, not only assert).
5. (research-shaped, corroboration) Compare 'On the Hinterworldly' with Twilight 'Reason' §6 thesis 3 ('inventing another world… revenge on life') as two sources for the claim that the two-world error has a motive: which gives the motive as a confession, which as a thesis, and which the course may cite; report whether either argues it.
6. (translation comparison) Del Caro's 'overcome' for 'überwinden' and 'self-overcoming' for 'Selbst-Überwindung'; compare with 'surmount' or 'conquer' and state which keeps the link to the 'over/across' imagery of 'Übermensch' (key-terms-in-translation) and to GM III:27's 'self-overcoming' law — and why that link matters for reading the speech as the same doctrine.
7. (FORWARD → nihilism-and-affirmation) Life says 'I must be struggle and becoming and purpose and the contradiction of purposes'. Write what a will that 'must always overcome itself' would need in order not to end in 'everything is empty' (the Soothsayer's line, Z II) — a value it can affirm in the overcoming itself; the solution says Module 6 opens with that problem under the name nihilism.

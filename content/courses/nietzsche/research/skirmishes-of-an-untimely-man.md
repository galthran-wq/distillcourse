## ARC (L10)

- **Spine step (8, "What morality does to us"):** "What this morality does to us: pity multiplies suffering and spends the strong, the whole system rests on false beliefs about the self and harms the highest human beings, and it survives the death of God in the secular dress of equality, democracy, socialism and emancipation, which Twilight's Skirmishes diagnose as the modern form of decline"
- **Inherits:** from against-pity, the three charges and A 7's "pity preserves what is ripe for destruction"; from danger-of-dangers, the charge that morality harms the highest and GM Pref 6's "type man"; from herd-morality-in-modern-dress, BGE 202-203 and GS 377; from will-to-power-as-psychology, A 2's "the feeling that power is growing, that resistance is overcome"; from nihilism and case-of-wagner, decadence (A 6; WA Preface) and GS 370's "hunger or superabundance".
- **Contributes:** the module's three charges run in Nietzsche's own order over nine consecutive sections of a book he finished, and the standard they silently used named in the first of them: whether a person or culture "represents the ascending or the descending line of life" (33). Introduces ascending-and-descending-life.
- **Hands off:** to order-of-rank (38's aristocratic commonwealths, 39's will to tradition, 40's "if one wants slaves"); to nietzsche-and-politics (39's institutions, Russia and the Reich); to real-world-became-a-fable, which opens the next module: 37's "in itself, no morality has any value" asks what a value "in itself" would have needed, and the next lesson tells the history of that world.

## STANCE (binding)

- **Favorable:** in nine sections Nietzsche does what the module has been doing one claim at a time, and does it with one measure in the open: the worth of egoism, of pity, of softened manners, of freedom, of democracy is read off the line of life a person or an age represents. The lesson shows the economy of this — a single canon yielding every verdict — and shows what he saw before his century: that "progress" in manners can be read as a symptom of a weaker constitution (37), that liberal institutions change their effect the moment they are won (38), that a class whose preconditions have been destroyed will experience its existence "as an injustice" (40), and that a culture's instincts, not its ideas, decide whether it can still build (39).
- **Hard passages this lesson must quote whole:** all nine are read whole from `portable-late.txt` (pp.533-546); the ones the stance names in particular: 33 (pp.533-534): "If he represents the descending development, decay, chronic degeneration, and sickness ... then he has small worth, and the minimum of decency requires that he take away as little as possible from those who have turned out well. He is merely their parasite." 36 (pp.536-538) entire: "The sick man is a parasite of society. In a certain state it is indecent to live longer"; "The physicians, in turn, would have to be the mediators of this contempt — not prescriptions, but every day a new dose of nausea with their patients"; "To create a new responsibility, that of the physician, for all cases in which the highest interest of life, of ascending life, demands the most inconsiderate pushing down and aside of degenerating life — for example, for the right of procreation, for the right to be born, for the right to live"; "To die proudly when it is no longer possible to live proudly"; "When one does away with oneself, one does the most estimable thing possible: one almost earns the right to live." 37 (pp.538-541): "cowardice", "wretchedness", "old ladies' morality" as the older names for our virtue; "Our morality of sympathy, against which I was the first to issue a warning ... l'impressionisme morale"; "'Equality' ... is an essential feature of decline". 38 (pp.541-543): "The human being who has become free ... spits on the contemptible type of well-being dreamed of by shopkeepers, Christians, cows, females, Englishmen, and other democrats"; "That one is prepared to sacrifice human beings for one's cause, not excluding oneself"; "The free man is a warrior." 39 (pp.543-544): "Democracy has ever been the form of decline in organizing power"; modern marriage founded "on the property drive (wife and child as property)". 40 (p.545): the "Chinese type" of worker; "if one wants slaves, then one is a fool if one educates them to be masters." 41 (pp.545-546): "under iron pressure at least one of these instinct systems be paralyzed".
- **What the words meant:** "egoism"/"self-interest" (Selbstsucht, 33) is "worth as much as the person who has it" — the type decides the value, as module 2's type-facts said. "Ascending" and "descending line of life" (aufsteigende/niedergehende Linie, 33) is the physiological standard A 6 defines negatively (an organism is corrupt "when it chooses, when it prefers, what is disadvantageous for it") and EH I 1 claims a nose for ("the signs of ascent and decline"). "Parasite" (33, 36) is the one who "takes away" from those who have turned out well; "degenerating life" (entartendes Leben, 36) is life on the descending line, and 36's "sick man" is defined by the state in which "the meaning of life, the right to life, has been lost", not by illness as such (EH I 2: Nietzsche counted himself sick and healthy at bottom). "Decadence" (35, 37, 39, 41) carries A 6's definition and TI Socrates 11's formula, "to have to fight the instincts". "Altruistic" (35) is Nietzsche's quotation of Comte's word, read as the fig leaf for "I no longer know how to find my own advantage". "Pathos of distance" (37) is master-morality's term, here "the will to be oneself, to stand out". "L'impressionisme morale" (37) is the morality of sympathy as "physiological overexcitability". "Freedom" (38) is "the will to assume responsibility for oneself", measured "according to the resistance which must be overcome" — A 2's happiness turned into a political measure; "liberal institutions" are what "cease to be liberal as soon as they are attained"; "herd-animalization" is BGE 202's herd read into liberalism. "Chinese type" (40) is GS 24's "Chinese happiness" and EH IV 4's "Chinese stagnation": a modest, self-sufficient, unquestioning class. "Slaves" in 40 names such a class by its function, as BGE 257's "slavery in some sense" does; "masters" are those educated to question. "Laisser aller" (41) is the abandonment to instincts that, in a decadent, "contradict, disturb, destroy each other"; "pruned ... whole" is BGE 212's "as manifold as whole" seen from the educator's side.
- **Objection answered from his side:** the strongest philosophical objection is the standard itself: "ascending or descending life" does the work of every verdict in these nine sections and is nowhere argued; by Nietzsche's own rule in TI Socrates 2 (p.474) — "judgments of value concerning life, for it or against it, can, in the end, never be true: they have value only as symptoms" — his own standard is a symptom too. Reply from the texts: TI Anti-Nature 5 (pp.490-491) accepts the self-application and claims the only standpoint there is, "beyond good and evil", from which a condemnation of life by the living is seen as symptom; A 6 gives the standard a non-moral content (an organism's choosing what harms it is a fact about it, not a valuation); 37 insists "in itself, no morality has any value", so the standard is not a rival morality but life's own measure; GS 370 (p.329) supplies the method, "the backward inference from the work to the maker ... from every way of thinking and valuing to the commanding need behind it". Gap sentence (the Portable reader's own pedagogy warning): "ascending life" versus "declining life" is treated in TI 33 and A 6 as an objective physiological standard and is never argued as one; the lesson says so in one sentence and points to order-of-rank, where the course tests it. Second objection, on 36: it licenses a state programme of eugenics. Reply from the text: 36's addressees are the physician and the sick man himself; its centre is free death ("Death freely chosen, death at the right time"), its target is the Christian "rape of the conscience" at the deathbed, and its agent is "Society — what am I saying? — life itself"; no state appears. The text also says what it says: "a new responsibility, that of the physician ... for the right of procreation, for the right to be born, for the right to live", and the lesson shows that this follows from 33's canon (if the descending man's "minimum of decency" is to take as little as possible, then life's "highest interest" may push him aside) rather than treating it as an excess. Gap sentence: how "degenerating life" is to be recognised in a case is nowhere stated, and 33's own example of a decadent who was also a beginning (EH I 1) shows the canon has no rule of application.
- **Humanizing reading to test:** that 36 "is about the physician's responsibility and free death", not eugenics for the state, and that Cesare Borgia is nowhere Nietzsche's ideal (Kaufmann, Portable Intro V, pp.16-17; the Portable reader's note). What the text says: 37 does deny that Borgia is "to be represented after my manner as a 'higher man', a kind of overman" and in the same section calls the Renaissance "the last great age" and "that lavishly squandering and fatal age"; 36 is about the physician and free death and also demands the physician's "new responsibility" over "the right to be born". The lesson gives both halves of each.
- **Facts against the popular image:** in the lesson's text: 37's denial about Borgia; 39's "German Reich" as a hybrid of "the form of decline of the state"; 38's "Englishmen, and other democrats" beside "Christians" — the targets are a type, not a nation. Reported because the text says so.

## The reconstruction

- **Claim and where.** TI Skirmishes 33: "Every individual may be scrutinized to see whether he represents the ascending or the descending line of life. Having made that decision, one has a canon for the worth of his self-interest." The nine sections apply the canon to egoism (33), revenge (34), altruism (35), the sick (36), the softening of manners (37), freedom (38), democracy (39), the worker (40) and the instincts (41).
- **Argument in plain steps.** (1) A living thing is a line, not an atom: the individual "is the whole single line of humanity up to himself" (33), so its worth is the worth of what it carries forward. (2) The line goes up or down, and this is a physiological fact (A 6; EH I 1), not a moral judgment. (3) Everything the module called a vice of morality — revenge dressed as justice (34), altruism (35), pity (36-37), equality (37), liberalism (38), democracy (39), laisser aller (41) — is what descending life prefers; "Instinctively to choose what is harmful for oneself" is "the formula of decadence" (35). (4) Everything the module called the cost — hardness (37), danger (38), rank and distance (37-38), discipline of the instincts (41) — is what ascending life needs. (5) Therefore the module's verdicts were one verdict: the morality of pity, equality and comfort is the morality of the descending line, and its spread is the spread of decline (37, 39). (6) The remedy is not a new morality ("in itself, no morality has any value", 37) but conditions under which ascending life can prevail: danger (38), institutions founded on an anti-liberal will (39), the pruning of instincts (41), and, where life has lost its meaning, the physician's contempt and free death (36).
- **Unstated premise.** That there is a fact of ascending and descending life independent of any valuation (step 2), and that one can tell which line a person represents; the text supplies no rule, and Nietzsche's own case (EH I 1-2) is both at once.
- **Strongest objection and Nietzsche's reply.** As in STANCE: the standard as symptom (reply: Anti-Nature 5, A 6, 37's "in itself", GS 370; gap sentence); 36 as programme (reply: physician and free death, no state; the sentence that does follow from 33 shown to follow; gap sentence on recognition).
- **Connections.** against-pity (35-37), danger-of-dangers (33's canon; 37's "no morality has any value"), herd-morality-in-modern-dress (34, 37-40), slave-revolt-and-ressentiment and ascetic-priest (34's "it must be somebody's fault"), will-to-power-as-psychology (38's measure of freedom; A 2), self-as-society-of-drives and sublimation (41's instinct systems; "pruned ... whole"), higher-type (38's Caesar; 49's Goethe), order-of-rank (38-40), nietzsche-and-politics (39), eternal-recurrence and amor-fati (36's free death beside Z I 21), real-world-became-a-fable (37's "in itself").

## Where the claim forks (named by content)

Two readings of what "ascending and descending life" is. One: a biological fact about an organism's instincts (A 6's definition; EH I 1's nose), so that Nietzsche's verdicts are diagnoses, not valuations, and TI Socrates 2's rule does not touch them. Two: a valuation like any other, dressed as physiology, so that the critique of morality is one morality attacking another and the "So what?" question returns. The text supports the first as Nietzsche's intention (33, A 6, Anti-Nature 5) and gives the second its opening (37's admission that a different constitution "necessarily generates a morality rich in consideration" makes every morality the symptom of a constitution, his own included). The lesson names both by content and leaves the fork open for order-of-rank.

- **Reading order and the one sentence:** read 33 first and write out the canon; then 34-37 as the module's three charges in his order (revenge, altruism, pity, manners), checking each verdict against the canon; then 38-41 as the step past morality into institutions and instincts; then TI Socrates 2 and A 6 to test the canon on itself. The reader can now take any of Nietzsche's late verdicts — on a virtue, an institution, a movement — and say which line of life he is reading it as a symptom of, what premise about that line the verdict needs, and where the text leaves the premise unargued.

## Pitfalls

- Read all nine whole and in order; the lesson is the text, not a selection, and the "sentences most often quoted against him" (33's parasite, 36's physician, 38's warrior, 40's slaves) are read in their sections.
- 36 is quoted whole, read for what it says, and shown to follow from 33; neither shrunk to "free death" nor inflated into a state programme the text does not mention.
- The standard is never argued in TI or A; one plain sentence says so (the gap), no gloating.
- Kaufmann's Skirmishes numbering follows the German (51 sections); do not use Ludovici's `twilightof.txt`.
- "Decadence" is defined at first use from A 6; "Chinese type" is glossed from GS 24 and EH IV 4, as Nietzsche's type-word, not a remark about China.
- 37's Borgia sentence is read as written: he is not Nietzsche's "higher man", and the Renaissance is "the last great age".
- HH 472 (cited in 39) is outside the staged HH selection; cite by section with "verify" or from the Portable if present.
- The marriage passage of 39 belongs to this lesson's reading; it is cross-referenced to herd-morality-in-modern-dress, not repeated there.
- Scholars' names in References only.

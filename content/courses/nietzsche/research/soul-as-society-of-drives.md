# Research brief: soul-as-society-of-drives (The Soul as a Society of Drives)

status: planned
module: psychology-beneath-genealogy
type: core
goal: Formulate Beyond Good and Evil 12, 16–19 and 23 as Nietzsche's positive psychology — the soul as a society of drives, willing as command — and evaluate the epiphenomenalism dispute between Leiter and Clark & Dudrick on that text.

## ARC (L10)

- **Inherits:** The Genealogy as one argument whose every step explains a value by a type; I:13's removal of the doer; BGE 19 previewed in doer-behind-the-deed; the Antichrist's 'redeemer type' as a drive-constitution asserted without the psychology.
- **Contributes:** The psychology stated: BGE 12's reopened soul (mortal, multiple, a society of drives and affects); BGE 16–17's cogito dismantled (the reader has it; now as the negative half); BGE 19's anatomy of willing in full; BGE 23's programme (psychology as morphology of the will to power, 'queen of the sciences'); Z 'On the Despisers of the Body' in one paragraph. The Doctrine of Types in full (type-facts, the tomato plant, causal essentialism vs. fatalism). The dispute: Leiter's epiphenomenalism (retracted in 2015 to a narrower claim after Katsafanas) vs. Clark & Dudrick's normative rank-order of drives (a 'space of reasons'), with Riccardi's primate-hierarchy rebuttal — adjudicated on what BGE 19 says.
- **Hands off:** To will-to-power-as-psychology (BGE 23's 'will to power' is the principle the society runs on — and the cosmological extension is the next question); to value-creation-and-normativity (the rank-order dispute is where a naturalist's account of 'good' lives).

Spine step: "The psychology beneath the genealogy: no soul-atom but a society of drives, willing as command among under-wills, will to power as the principle — defensible as psychology, question-begging as cosmology — and the two-world metaphysics that had to become a fable first; Zarathustra's 'Life' speaks the doctrine the argument cannot prove."

## Key points the lesson must cover

1. **BGE 12: the soul hypothesis reopened.** Materialistic atomism refuted (Boscovich), 'soul atomism' — the soul as indestructible, eternal, indivisible, a monad — to be expelled; but 'there is no need to give up the soul itself': 'mortal soul', 'soul as subject-multiplicity', 'soul as a society constructed out of drives and affects'. Reconstruct: (P) the Christian soul-atom is a fiction (I:13's doer at the metaphysical level); (P) a psychology still needs a subject-term; (C) the subject is a plurality with a structure — a society. Z's 'body am I entirely' as the same claim in the dramatized register: the self is 'a great reason' behind the ego, 'an unknown sage' — the body's own ordering.
2. **BGE 16–17 as the negative half (recap, two sentences with a Ref).** The 'I think' bundle; 'it thinks'; even 'it' too much.
3. **BGE 19 in full: the anatomy of willing.** (i) A plurality of feelings: the feeling of the state away from, toward, of 'from' and 'toward' themselves, and the muscular feeling; (ii) thinking — 'in every act of the will there is a commanding thought'; (iii) 'above all' an affect — the affect of command: 'I am free, "he" must obey'. The feeling of freedom is the commander's feeling of superiority over the under-wills that obey; 'l'effet c'est moi' — the ruling class identifying itself with the commonwealth's success; 'our body is only a social structure composed of many souls'. Reconstruct: willing is a relation of command and obedience among drives, and 'free will' names the commander's affect — the doer fiction (I:13) explained rather than merely denied.
4. **BGE 23: the programme.** 'Psychology as morphology and the doctrine of the development of the will to power'; 'the queen of the sciences'; the moral prejudices that have blocked it (the affects of hatred, envy, greed, lust for power as 'conditions of life'). This is the working hypothesis's own manifesto in Nietzsche's hand: name it as such.
5. **The Doctrine of Types in full.** Type-facts: each person's fixed psycho-physical constitution, which with environment causally explains values and behaviour; the tomato plant (nourished well or badly, never corn); causal essentialism (certain facts primary and bounding) vs. classical fatalism (every detail fixed); BGE 231's 'granite of spiritual fate' as the textual anchor. State the doctrine as Leiter's with Ch. 3 and the Postscript as pointers; note that it was stated as the hypothesis in corpus-and-protocol and is now given its text.
6. **The dispute: does the society have a government or only a physics?** Leiter (2002): conscious values and reasons are epiphenomenal — the drives cause, consciousness reports; the Doctrine of Types is the whole story. Leiter (2015 Postscript): retracted as too strong after Katsafanas; the narrowed claim concerns specific aspects (the felt experience of willing — BGE 19's 'affect of command' is real but not the cause). Clark & Dudrick: the drives arrange themselves in a rank-order that is normative, not causal — a space of reasons; what makes one a person is the capacity for values, and will to power is the second-order drive that does the ranking; persons are bodies (Z) but bodies with a politics. Riccardi's rebuttal: the authors' own comparison of the rank-order to primate dominance hierarchies is a causal order, and the 'space of reasons' has no textual purchase beyond the political metaphor. Adjudicate on BGE 19: the text says 'command' and 'obey', 'commonwealth', 'ruling class' — political vocabulary — and says the affect of command is 'above all' what willing is; it does not say the order is one of reasons. Verdict as fact: BGE 19 underdetermines; the naturalist reading fits it at the price of calling the political vocabulary a metaphor; Clark & Dudrick fit it at the price of adding 'reasons' the text does not name. Leiter's retraction as the reading correcting itself — good raw material, the dossier notes, for reading secondary literature as live rather than settled.
7. **What the psychology gives the Genealogy.** A type explains a value (the priest; the redeemer): the causal claim now has units (drives) and a structure (rank-order); ressentiment, bad conscience and the ascetic ideal are configurations of the society — say so with three Refs and no re-teaching.

- **BGE 19 dissected as a parts list** — the dossier calls it 'the single richest passage for a lesson on Nietzsche's model of agency'.
- **The society-of-drives image** (BGE 12; Z 'a great reason') drawn as a polity — the dossier's suggestion.
- **The tomato plant** — the analogy drawn as a range, a ceiling, no unique outcome.
- **BGE 68 ('I did that, says my memory… memory yields')** — carried over from corpus-and-protocol as the two-line demonstration of a drive editing consciousness.
- **Leiter's retraction** as a dated two-line story (2002 claim → Katsafanas → 2015 narrowing).
- **Riccardi's primate hierarchy** as the rebuttal figure — the dossier's 'genuinely usable classroom dialectic'.
- **Boscovich's atom and Nietzsche's drive** (Clark & Dudrick 6.3–6.4): extensionless force-centres → force-centres in a normative order — the master analogy drawn as two panels.
- **The heritability table** (Knobe & Leiter pp. 92–99): three numbers with their denominators (heritable share of variance; shared-environment share; the social-psychology effect sizes) — the Doctrine of Types' empirical case in one figure, with the frame that a heritable trait is a type-fact only on a further assumption the lesson names.
- **Two primates, two conclusions**: de Waal's chimpanzees as Clark & Dudrick read them (a political order without homunculi) and as Riccardi reads them (a dominance hierarchy, hence causal) — the dispute located in one premise.
- **The pharaoh's pyramids** (7.2): 'L'effet c'est moi' as borrowed credit — the phenomenology of freedom explained by the commanding drives' identification with the commonwealth's successes.

## Pitfalls / subtleties to flag

- Clark & Dudrick is on disk in full: quote 6.2 and 7.3 directly. Leiter's Postscript remains toc-only — state the retraction in the terms Leiter's own restatements verify and mark it for the human; the Doctrine of Types is quoted from Knobe & Leiter 2007, not from reviews.
- BGE 19's political vocabulary is Nietzsche's; the lesson must not decide that it is 'only' a metaphor or 'really' a space of reasons — the verdict is underdetermination with costs.
- Do not import contemporary philosophy of mind beyond the terms the dispute itself uses (epiphenomenal; causal vs. normative order).
- Z 'On the Despisers of the Body' is dramatization; one paragraph, mapped.
- GS 354's claim is about consciousness as communication; it supports Leiter's narrowed claim, not the 2002 one — say which.

## EXERCISE PLAN (floor: ≤1 warm-up; ≥2 of boundary / refute / derivation / research; one FORWARD)

1. (warm-up) List BGE 19's three components of willing with the sentence each comes from, and state which one Nietzsche says willing is 'above all'.
2. (refute) "Nietzsche eliminates the self: there are only drives." Break it with BGE 12's 'no need to give up the soul itself' and Z's 'a great reason'; state what is eliminated (the atom) and what is kept (a structured plurality), and which of Leiter's two positions (2002 or 2015) the refutation also touches.
3. (derivation) Derive from BGE 19 why the feeling of freedom is reliably present in willing even if it is not the cause of the act: two premises about the commander's position (it issues the command; it identifies with the outcome) and the conclusion — then state whether this derivation is available to Clark & Dudrick as well, and what each reading adds to it.
4. (boundary probe) Consider a society of drives with no rank-order — all drives of equal strength. On BGE 19 and BGE 12, could such a being will at all? State what 'command' requires and what the case shows about whether rank-order is constitutive (both readings) or normative (Clark & Dudrick only).
5. (research-shaped) Reconstruct Riccardi's rebuttal from the review in research/texts/thesoul-notes.txt as an explicit argument (the primate-hierarchy analogy; causal vs. normative order), then Clark & Dudrick's own pre-emptive use of de Waal's chimpanzee politics (7.4) — the same primate evidence cited for opposite conclusions; state which premise the two sides actually disagree on (whether recognition of authority is a causal fact).
6. (translation comparison) 'Gesellschaftsbau der Triebe und Affekte' (BGE 12) and 'Unterwillen' (BGE 19): compare Norman's renderings with the German and, if verified, Kaufmann's ('social structure' vs. 'society constructed'); state which rendering makes the political reading (Clark & Dudrick) easier and whether the German settles it.
7. (FORWARD → will-to-power-as-psychology) BGE 23 calls psychology 'the morphology and doctrine of the development of the will to power'. Write what the society of drives needs 'will to power' to mean for BGE 19's command-and-obedience to be an instance of it (each drive seeks to command), and find the one aphorism in BGE (§36) that extends the claim from drives to 'the world'; the solution says the next lesson reconstructs that extension and finds where it fails.

# Research brief: sublimation (Sublimation: spiritualizing the drives)

status: planned
module: the-human-being (2 of 9), lesson 10 of 45
type: core
goal: Explain how sublimation redirects a drive toward a new object instead of extirpating it, and why Nietzsche ranks it above both indulgence and castration of the passions

## ARC (L10)

- **Spine step (5, "Will to power"):** "One principle runs through the drives: each wants to grow and overcome resistance, not merely to survive or to stop wanting; that will to power can be spiritualized rather than extirpated, and the Four Great Errors show the whole psychology turned on morality and religion." This lesson carries the middle clause.
- **Inherits:** from will-to-power-as-psychology (prerequisite): drives that want to discharge strength and overcome resistance, so that a drive's force is separable from its present object; from self-as-society-of-drives (read): one drive mastering another (D 109), the gardener (D 560).
- **Contributes:** the third treatment of a drive beyond indulging and extirpating it: keep its force, change its object and form. Introduces `sublimation`, `spiritualization` (Vergeistigung). Mechanism behind culture (BGE 188, 229), the critique of "anti-nature" morality, bad conscience read as inward redirection, giving style and the higher type.
- **Hands off:** to four-great-errors (next): "Morality as Anti-Nature" 4-6 already reads a morality as the symptom of the life that holds it; the Four Great Errors generalize the move. Later: cruelty-and-culture (BGE 229-230), bad-conscience (GM II 16-18), ascetic-ideals-in-artists-and-philosophers (GM III 8), becoming-who-you-are (GS 290).

## STANCE (binding)

- **Favorable:** Nietzsche used "sublimation" in its modern psychological sense a generation before psychoanalysis and framed the three fates of a drive (discharge, extirpation, spiritualization) as a diagnosis of two thousand years of moral practice; he saw that cutting out a passion cuts out the strength it carried and produces the weak, not the good.
- **Hard passages this lesson must quote whole:** TI "Morality as Anti-Nature" 1-6 (Portable pp.486-492; portable-late.txt lines ~1783-1940: "castratism"; "radical means are indispensable only for the degenerate"; "the ideal eunuch"; a condemnation of life by the living "a mere symptom ... of declining, weakened, weary, condemned life"; "an idiosyncrasy of degenerates"); BGE 189 (bge-zimmern.txt line 2519; German line 3230; the sexual drive sublimated "only under the pressure of Christian value judgments"); HH I 107 (Zimmern pp.107-108; "Good acts are sublimated evil"); D 202 (p.121; "every 'guilty person' is a sick person"); BGE 229 (line 3993; the cruelty thesis, read whole in cruelty-and-culture).
- **What the words meant:** sublimiren comes from chemistry, a solid passing to vapour and condensing purer; MA 1 calls ideals "sublimations in which the fundamental element appears almost evaporated" and asks for "a chemistry of the moral, religious, aesthetic ideas". Vergeistigung is Kaufmann's "spiritualization"; Geist is intellect, not soul. "Castratism" (Kastratismus), "extirpation", "degenerate" (Entartung), "anti-nature" are his names for the church's method, for a type that can no longer impose moderation on itself, and for a morality that turns against the instincts of life; stated as positions in his argument.
- **Objection answered from his side:** if every virtue is a refined vice (love refined lust, honesty refined cruelty), the account is unfalsifiable and deflating. Reply, texts first: the thesis is continuity, not unmasking: "the most beautiful colours have been obtained from base, even despised materials" (MA 1); good things may be "knotted, crocheted" to the evil ones (BGE 2); "all good things were formerly bad things" (GM III 9, p.113); and it makes a testable comparative claim: the strongest achievements come from strong drives under long constraint ("long obedience in the same direction", BGE 188; Goethe who "disciplined himself to wholeness", Skirmishes 49). Who does the sublimating: another drive (D 109), the organizing idea (EH II 9). The gap, in one sentence: that the spiritualized form is "higher" presupposes a ranking the mechanism does not supply, and the hydraulic picture (force must go somewhere) is a nineteenth-century model asserted, not shown.
- **Humanizing reading to test:** the reading that makes sublimation the master key, so that will to power is at bottom self-overcoming and the blond beast and Borgia are "ideograms of unsublimated passion, not ideals" (Kaufmann, Ch. 7 II-III, pp.216-227). The text: Anti-Nature 2 and 6 rank the strong who can impose moderation above the degenerate who need castration and say nothing against the strong who do not spiritualize; EH III 1 (p.261) prefers "even a Cesare Borgia" to "a Parsifal"; BGE 189 credits Christian pressure, not a healthy nature, with the sublimation; GS 357 (p.307) describes Christian truthfulness "translated and sublimated into a scientific conscience", a sublimation that produced module 1's nihilism; a 1888 notebook suspects that "under the semblance of sublimation" drives are "denaturalised" (14, Bittner pp.254-255, unpublished). The lesson states that sublimation is a mechanism ranked above extirpation, not the meaning of will to power, and that the unsublimated strong are preferred to the castrated.
- **Facts against the popular image:** none directly; the word history (HH 1878) precedes Freud's use, stated as a date, not as influence.

## The reconstruction

- **Claim and where.** TI Anti-Nature 1-3: the church attacks the passions at the root, "its practice is castration"; the better way is spiritualization. HH 107 gives the formula, BGE 189 the one BGE use, D 560 the claim that one can do it deliberately.
- **Argument in plain steps.** (1) Drives are the source of all strength, including the strength to do anything good (BGE 23; HH 107). (2) A drive cannot be destroyed without destroying the strength it carries; extirpation is chosen by those too weak to moderate themselves and produces the eunuch (Anti-Nature 1-2, 4). (3) A drive discharging on its nearest object is often stupid and destructive (Anti-Nature 1). (4) A drive's force is separable from its object, because what it wants is to discharge and overcome (lesson 9): the same hostility can fight a rival or value an enemy (Anti-Nature 3). (5) So the best treatment keeps the force and changes object and form; under long constraint a drive "purifies and sharpens itself" (BGE 189; 188). (6) Cultures and persons that do this produce greatness (Skirmishes 49; BGE 229); moralities that extirpate produce the weak and call it virtue (Anti-Nature 4-6).
- **Unstated premise.** Step 4's detachability plus the hydraulic picture; the ranking that makes the spiritualized form "higher"; and that another drive does the redirecting (D 109), supplied only by EH II 9.
- **Strongest objection and Nietzsche's reply.** As in STANCE.
- **Connections.** will-to-power-as-psychology; four-great-errors (Anti-Nature 5 generalized); cruelty-and-culture (BGE 229-230); bad-conscience (GM II 16-18); ascetic-priest; ascetic-ideals-in-artists-and-philosophers (GM III 8); becoming-who-you-are (GS 290; EH II 9); higher-type (BGE 212); nihilism (GS 357).

## Pitfalls

- D 109 does not contain "sublimation"; the D uses are D 202, 248, Preface 4.
- BGE 189 is the only BGE passage with the verb; cite the sentence and note that it credits Christian constraint; BGE 58 is the minor second use.
- "HH II 95" is MOM 95 (Portable p.65); lead with MA I 1 and 107.
- Z I 19 (p.180) is about revenge, not sublimation; the Z passages are I 5 and II 15.
- Kaufmann's chapter is "Morality as Anti-Nature"; quote the Portable, not Ludovici.
- "Only the castrated man is a good man" (old brief, WP 383) is known only by compilation number; omit or supply the KSA number.
- Sublimation is not repression; repression is extirpation, what it is contrasted with. Freud: dates only.
- The lesson does not say that will to power is sublimation; lesson 9 settled the stance.

## EXERCISE PLAN

Floor check: claim to refute (ex. 3), boundary probe (ex. 4), derivation (ex. 5).

1. Retrieval (E3 from will-to-power-as-psychology): state the overcoming-resistance form and say why it makes a drive's force separable from its present object.
2. Apply (claim–evidence–reasoning): envy discharged, extirpated and spiritualized, naming object and form in each and the TI sentence that fits.
3. Claim to refute: "Sublimation is Nietzsche's word for self-control by willpower." From D 109 and BGE 117, who does the controlling; what EH II 9 puts in its place.
4. Boundary probe: a drive so weak there is nothing to redirect; what Anti-Nature 2 says such a person must do, and whom the alternative is for.
5. Derivation: Nietzsche's verdict on a monastic rule forbidding all anger, and what a spiritualizing rule would require (Anti-Nature 3).
6. FORWARD (E7, reaches four-great-errors): Anti-Nature 5 calls a condemnation of life "a mere symptom of a certain kind of life". Rewrite one moral verdict ("luxury destroys a people") as a symptom, stating cause and effect. Solution: the Four Great Errors begin with exactly this reversal, Cornaro's diet (`<Ref>`; TI "Errors" 1-2).

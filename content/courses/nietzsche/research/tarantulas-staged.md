goal: Interpret 'On the Tarantulas' and 'On a Thousand and One Goals' against the First Treatise's theses and state what the dramatization adds, drops, and undercuts.

## ARC (L10)

- **Inherits:** The First Treatise reconstructed: the asymmetry, ressentiment, the workshop, the doer fiction, and the gap (victory unexplained). The reader has not yet opened Zarathustra.
- **Contributes:** The course's reading rule for Zarathustra, established on its first case: a speech is mapped sentence by sentence onto theses argued elsewhere; what it adds (an affective diagnostic — revenge felt), drops (the historical mechanism — priestly caste, the inversion's dating), and undercuts (Zarathustra bitten; 'On Poets') is stated as the departure from the argument. 'On a Thousand and One Goals' as master valuation's origin story in parable ('man is the esteemer').
- **Hands off:** To the Second Treatise (promise-and-sovereign-individual), which begins the victory mechanism; the dramatization rule returns in self-overcoming-staged and overman-staged.

Spine step: "First Treatise: two modes of valuation with an asymmetry — 'good' coined first by the noble, 'evil' first by ressentiment — the slave revolt as the inversion, and the doer-behind-the-deed fiction that morality's first presupposition needs; Zarathustra's tarantulas stage the diagnosis."

**APPLICATION spec (L16).** Artefact: two speeches of Zarathustra — 'On the Tarantulas' (Z II) and 'On a Thousand and One Goals' (Z I) — real texts in the world, with a reception history of being quoted as doctrine. Module results used: the asymmetry (GM I:2, 10), ressentiment's definition (I:10), the workshop (I:14), BGE 195/202 on herd morality and equality, the doer fiction (I:13) for 'the preachers of equality' who need the powerful to be blameworthy. Departure from the idealisation: the speech asserts the diagnosis (equality-preaching is revenge) without the history that GM I supplies, adds an affective test the history lacks (the bite), and frames its own speaker as infected — the argued works never undercut themselves this way. The lesson states each departure as a fact about the two texts.

## Key points the lesson must cover

1. **The reading rule, stated once.** Zarathustra is a character; his speeches are testimony from a character in a narrative that shows him misunderstood, laughing at, collapsing, and warning his disciples off. A speech is therefore evidence of what Nietzsche staged, not of what he argued; the course maps each speech onto the argued works and records the residue. 'On Poets' ('the poets lie too much… Zarathustra too is a poet') and 'On the Bestowing Virtue' §3 ('lose me and find yourselves') are the text's own instruction.
2. **'On the Tarantulas' mapped.** The tarantula's hidden revenge ('your soul is full of revenge… we want to raise our clamour for equality'); 'preachers of equality' as tarantulas; 'humans are not equal, and they shouldn't become so'; 'that man be delivered from revenge: that is for me the bridge to the highest hope'; the bite and the dance. Map: the hidden revenge → I:10's ressentiment; equality-preaching as the powerless's weapon → I:14's workshop and BGE 202's herd morality; the 'good and just' → I:13's lambs; 'not equal' → BGE 257's pathos of distance; deliverance from revenge → the forward pointer to 'On Redemption' (eternal-recurrence).
3. **'On a Thousand and One Goals' mapped.** Each people has its tablet of goods; 'humans first placed values into things' — 'man is the esteemer'; the Greeks', Persians', Jews', Germans' tablets; 'a thousand goals there have been, for there are a thousand peoples; only the yoke for the thousand necks is still lacking'. Map: peoples as value-creators → GM I:2's noble self-naming at the scale of a people; the tablets → BGE 260's typology; the missing 'one goal' → the Prologue's overman (forward to overman-staged). Note that this is master valuation's origin without the priest and without ressentiment — the speech dramatizes the First Treatise's first half only.
4. **What the drama adds.** The bite as an affective test: revenge is recognized by what it does to the one who touches it, not by its history — a diagnostic the argued works state (BGE 30's audience partition; GM I:10's 'needs external stimuli') but do not enact. The dance/pillar image as affirmation's counter-figure.
5. **What it drops.** No priestly caste, no dating, no inversion of an equation, no evidence — the speech has the diagnosis of I:10 and I:14 without the history of I:6–7 and I:16. Stated as the cost of the genre.
6. **What it undercuts.** Zarathustra is bitten; he must be bound to the pillar so as not to 'whirl with revenge' — the speaker is inside the diagnosis. The argued works claim a distance from ressentiment (GM I:10's 'we') that the drama denies its own speaker. This is the departure the application records.

## Pitfalls / subtleties to flag

- The speech's 'humans are not equal' is quotable out of context; the lesson must give the sentence's object (the preachers' equality as revenge) and its pairing (not equal, and not to become so — 'what would my love for the overman be if I spoke otherwise').
- Do not read the tarantula as a portrait of any historical movement; Nietzsche names none in the speech (BGE 202 does, in the argued register — cite it there).
- Heidegger's reading of revenge is deferred; one sentence of contrast at most.
- The lesson must not become a Zarathustra tour; two speeches, one frame passage, and the map.
- Del Caro's 'overman' is the course's term; 'superman' appears only in the translation chapter.

## EXERCISE PLAN (floor: ≤1 warm-up; ≥2 of boundary / refute / derivation / research; one FORWARD)

1. (warm-up, mapping) Complete the thesis map for the three lines of 'On the Tarantulas' the lesson leaves blank, giving the GM/BGE section or 'none' with a reason.
2. (refute) "'Humans are not equal' in 'On the Tarantulas' is Nietzsche's political programme." Break it: quote the sentence's object (the preachers' equality as revenge), its qualifier (love of the overman), and the frame ('Zarathustra too is a poet'); then state what BGE 257 does assert in the argued register and why that is the passage a political reading must answer.
3. (derivation) 'On a Thousand and One Goals' has no ressentiment in it. Derive from GM I:2 and I:10 what the speech would need to add to dramatize the whole First Treatise, and write the missing paragraph's premises (not its prose).
4. (boundary probe) Suppose the tarantula's bite were removed from the speech. What thesis would the speech then assert that GM I does not, and what would it lose? State whether the bite is an argument, evidence, or a diagnostic — and what each answer would mean for citing the speech.
5. (research-shaped, corroboration) Find the one passage in the argued works closest to the tarantula's 'we want to raise our clamour for equality' (BGE 202's 'religion of pity', or BGE 44's 'levellers') and compare the two texts as sources: which names its target, which gives a mechanism, which the course may cite for the claim that equality-preaching is ressentiment.
6. (translation comparison) Del Caro renders 'Rache' as 'revenge' and 'Gleichheit' as 'equality' throughout; check whether 'vengefulness' or 'sameness' would alter the map to GM I:10 (Ressentiment) and BGE 202, and state what the German 'Gleich' does that 'equal' does not (the Sleepwalker Song's 'gleich' will return).
7. (FORWARD → promise-and-sovereign-individual) The tarantula speech ends with deliverance from revenge as 'the bridge to the highest hope' but says nothing about how the powerless ever came to rule. Write the question the First Treatise leaves (how did the inversion win?) and list what a mechanism would need (a site in the strong themselves for the No to lodge); the solution says GM II's bad conscience is that site and the Second Treatise begins with the next lesson.

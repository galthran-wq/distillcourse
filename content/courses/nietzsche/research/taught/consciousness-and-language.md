# Taught: consciousness-and-language

## Problem
Opening (= course.yaml `opening`): most of what we do, think and feel goes on without consciousness; if so, why does consciousness exist at all, and what can it show us about ourselves? (ends on the question; no answer given in the opening) Developed against the inherited view (Plato's seeing reason, Christian inner conscience, Descartes — anchored as 17th-c. French philosopher, "I think" first certainty, mind better known than body): introspection as best self-evidence. Two questions: what consciousness is for, and what it can show given its job. Answer: consciousness ("becoming conscious of something", the mirror) arose under the need to communicate (GS 354); becoming conscious = taking the form of shared signs; a sign carries only what speakers share (BGE 268), words exist for extremes (D 115), so introspection shows the average, a commentary in the herd's words (D 119, GS 335, GS 355). BGE 268 runs the mechanism over generations: drift to the common (progressus in simile). Seven-step argument; conjecture = step 3 (prehistory), asserted premise = step 5 (consciousness = sign-form); steps 4, 6 checkable now.

# Taught: death-of-god

## Problem
Why does the madman tell atheists that God is dead, and why do they laugh? Answer: "God is dead" is a claim about a belief (GS 343: "the belief in the Christian god has become unbelievable"), discredited not refuted (D 95); its consequence is that "the whole of our European morality", built on that faith, has lost its foundation, through an unstated premise (these values had no other ground) that TI Skirmishes 5 half states and the genealogy must pay.

## Explained
- GS 125 read in four movements: (1) lantern in the morning market, unbelievers laugh (God as lost child/emigrant: absence a small inventory fact); (2) indictment, "we have killed him, you and I"; sea drunk up, sponge wipes horizon, earth unchained from its sun, no up or down, "an infinite nothing", night closing in, "lanterns in the morning" = loss of every standard of orientation/ranking; "God remains dead" = no resurrection; (3) "Must we ourselves not become gods simply to appear worthy of it?" = demand, answer deferred to creating-values; (4) "too early", lantern smashed, "the light of the stars requires time", deed done / news not arrived; requiem, churches "tombs and sepulchers of God" — ## The parable: GS 125 in four movements (FigureSequence madman-1..4; Task: what the crowd thinks went missing vs what the madman says went with it)
- Death of God (concept death-of-god): GS 343 (1887) gloss quoted German + English; *unglaubwürdig* = not worthy of belief (witness who can no longer be credited), not "proved false" — ## "God is dead" is a claim about a belief
- The run, each section with its job: 108 (first occurrence; Buddha's shadow in a cave) → survival of effects (how the crowd lives on as before); 109 (shadows of God outside churches: purposes in nature, lawgiver; "only necessities") + 110 (beliefs last by usefulness, not truth) → discrediting; 124 (ship, burned bridges, open sea, no land) → dread, reversed by GS 343; 153 (after 125) (playwright tied "the knot of morality", slew all gods in act four "for the sake of morality") → the killer is morality itself, which value = nihilism — ### The run around GS 125 (figure run.svg: boxes 108 | 109–110 | 124 | 125 | 153, footer "153, after it, names the killer: morality itself"; reading-procedure Ref lives here)
- Refutation of the slogan reading (L13): not a proof, not a boast; EH, 'Why I Am So Clever' 1 quoted ("I do not by any means know atheism as a result; even less as an event … from instinct … God is a gross answer … you shall not think!"): his personal atheism vs Europe's event — ### Not a proof that God does not exist
- Discrediting (concept discrediting): D 95 quoted whole (Hollingdale); "superfluous" ≠ "false"; refutation stalemate (each refuted proof invites a better one); ghost child example (looked, found nothing; she watched a horror film); two parts both needed: no independent support + explanation of belief and its weight without its truth; Remark definition — ## Discrediting / ### How an explanation can replace a proof (figure refuted-discredited.svg)
- Weight without truth: GS 110 "strength of knowledge does not depend on its degree of truth but on its age … incorporated … condition of life"; EH on HH 1 "the ideal is not refuted — it freezes to death"; Nietzsche's own explanations of the belief's origin (needs of the suffering, priests, fear of chance) named in one clause with Ref ascetic-priest; the killers = a culture explaining its own religion (historians, comparative scholars, psychologists) = GS 153's playwright — ### Weight without truth
- The argument in five steps (1 values held because God stood behind them; 2 belief discredited; 3 ground gone → value loses standing, noticed or not; 4 European morality lost its foundation; 5 most keep morality, drop God) + unstated premise between 3 and 4 ("these values had no other ground") + second premise stated in prose (why losing these values is a catastrophe, not a relief; Ref nihilism) — ## What collapses with the belief / ### The argument in five steps (figure argument-chain.svg = key image)
- George Eliot (1819–1880, Mary Ann Evans; lost faith, translated a critical life of Jesus and a study of religion as projection; morally serious novels); TI Skirmishes 5 quoted whole (Kaufmann); *Moral-Weiblein* "little moralistic females" glossed: *Weiblein* diminutive of *Weib*, "little female"; contempt aimed at a woman's moral zeal, and meant ("what a moral fanatic one is"); the argument inside the insult = inconsistency ("English consistency" irony): dropped the premise, kept the conclusion; Christianity a system; morality a command, transcendent origin, man "cannot know" what is good; English "intuition" = after-effect of the dominion, origin forgotten — supplies first half of premise (values came by command) — ### The case of George Eliot (figure george-eliot-portrait.jpg, float-right, engraving after Burton 1865)
- Shadow and starlight (step 5, why the morality survives): values persist through habit, institutions, praise/blame, grammar, running on what they were given (humility praised without asking why); GS 108 cave shadow as the image of survival; dead-star figure; KeyFact: a morality can outlive its foundation by generations; survival ≠ evidence it never needed one — ### The shadow and the starlight (figure dead-star.svg)
- Objection 1, Euthyphro (Plato's dialogue, 399 BC, holy because loved or loved because holy) applied to Christian morality; Eliot and English utilitarians as "proof"; reply from Skirmishes 5: content is specific (equal worth of souls, love of enemies, meek inherit, humility, suffering redemptive, sin/guilt), "not self-evident", Homeric heroes/ancient nobilities did not see it; left horn assumes what it must show. Gap: the argument's scope (GS 343 "built upon this faith", Skirmishes 5 "stands and falls with faith in God") reaches specific Christian content, not minimal claims nobody derived from God (don't torture a child for amusement); whether Nietzsche himself holds such prohibitions is stated as a separate question this argument does not answer (Ref cruelty-and-culture); "no other ground" premise is a promissory note for genealogy — ### Values never needed God (figure euthyphro.svg); Aside: 1958 Catholic philosopher (Anscombe, unnamed in body), "law conception of ethics", "as if the notion 'criminal' were to remain…"
- Objection 2, genetic fallacy (defined: inferring falsity from origin); recalls lesson 1's "origin doesn't settle validity"; reply: D 95 infers superfluity, not falsity; argument carries own support vs belief held on upbringing/authority/need, which the explanation accounts for wholly; condition: the explanation must be good (no genealogy mention here) — ### Discrediting is a genetic fallacy
- Dread and dawn: madman + GS 124 (infinity awesome, no land) vs GS 343 second paragraph quoted ("new dawn", "the sea, our sea, lies open again", horizon free); images of 125 return with sign reversed; dread for the many who need a fixed point, permission for the few; the bridge spelled out: with the standard gone, either live without ranking (= infinite nothing) or take over the standard's work and rank oneself (= permission) — that is what "must we become gods?" asks; Ref creating-values; neither doom-prophet nor cheerful-atheist reading alone — ## Dread and dawn

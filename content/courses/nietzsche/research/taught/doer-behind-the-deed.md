# Taught: doer-behind-the-deed

## Problem
Opening (= course.yaml `opening`, verbatim): the court asks whether the man who broke another's jaw could have held back and says he deserves to suffer; what does the verdict assume stands behind the blow, and is anything there? Running case: the tavern striker; a falling roof tile is removed, not blamed. Bridge from consciousness-and-language's closing (grammar puts a subject before every verb; its Ex 7 "the lightning flashes"): the striker stands to his blow as lightning to its flash, plus "could have not struck". Questions: what the verdict presupposes; can a drive-self supply it. Answer: it presupposes a self behind the deed ultimately responsible for it; two arguments remove that self (grammar; causa sui), with the grammar argument's step 4 backed by BGE 19 (willing) and D 129 (deliberation), which explain the felt self without a doer: a strong case, not a proof; the compatibilist objection is answered via D 129 / GS 360 with a stated gap; retributive blame and guilt's premise fall on the desert premise; own-character responsibility never refuted, only denied the power to make suffering deserved (### The gap says so: it grounds restraint, not desert); strong/weak will, character, rank, restraint without desert stand.

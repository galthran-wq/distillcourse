# Taught: drive-psychology-and-experiment (type: extra, off-path)

## Problem
Opening (= course.yaml `opening`, verbatim): Nietzsche made empirical claims (reasons told after the fact; character set more by constitution than upbringing); a century of experimental psychology tested claims of that shape. Developed: the module argued them from texts (D 129, TI Errors/Cornaro), which cannot settle a question of fact; Nietzsche asked for the test (GS 319); the obvious move (find a finding, declare him a prophet) fails twice: no falsifiable statement, and no check that what was measured (scores, attitudes, population variance) is what he meant (a drive in one person). Answer: four testable claims; negative psychology (confabulation, weak principles) supported in his own form; type-claim supported for populations by heritability, and its reading as a floor (from the texts) fits the movement/aggregation evidence but is not established by it — heritability cannot tell a floor from total fixity; drives/will to power untested; situations bend single acts more than he allowed, and he gives no method for telling a type-fact from a habit.

- Heritability is population- and environment-relative (stated in full ONLY here); says nothing about changeability (height rose in 20th-c. Europe); reaches individuals only if typical — ### A population statistic, not a share of a person
- Situation: 1973 Princeton seminarians (hurry: ~6 in 10 vs 1 in 10 helped; Good Samaritan talk made no reliable difference); hurry ≈14% of differences in helping, computed later by correlating condition with helping; 1920s honesty studies (~11,000 children, test after test of cheating/lying/stealing): one test predicts another ≈5%; D 326 "variable quantity" quoted — ### The situation
- No method for type-fact vs habit; GM III 16 "a presupposition that I do not first have to demonstrate", "more probably … belly"; TI Skirmishes 33 quoted (individual scrutinized for ascending/descending line; "merely their parasite") needs individual type-facts the numbers can't supply → skirmishes-of-an-untimely-man — ### No method for telling a type-fact from a habit
- Objection (drives ≠ traits; population stat; situationism) — ## The strongest objection: these findings test something else
- Reply: plasticity in the texts: BGE 231 "Learning alters us", D 560 (Ref sublimation #the-trellis), D 534 small doses, D 462 slow cures, GS 290 quoted ("second nature added … original nature removed … the ugly that could not be removed is concealed"); type-claim = floor (a reading of the TEXTS); a floor allows conduct to bend above it — ### The reply, texts first (figure floor.svg; bracket: 'a floor, not a ceiling'; neither caption nor figure pre-states the heritability argument)
- Heritability cannot decide floor vs total fixity vs full plasticity: non-genetic half can be fixed early (womb, chance brain development, an illness/shock marking one child), genetic half can change (height); evidence that bears is what moves (seminarians; need-for-closure form) vs what holds — ### What heritability cannot decide
- Aggregation: battery-to-battery honesty r ≈ .5–.6 ≈ a quarter to a third of the variation (five+ times one test; checked against Hartshorne & May's tables via Rushton et al. 1983 — the 81% in the secondary literature is NOT used); 1970s day-by-day studies: more occasions averaged, closer prediction; type-claim about the shape of a life ("each cardinal problem"; Cornaro over decades); movement + aggregation rules out conduct fixed act by act and a person with nothing stable = the shape of a floor; does NOT show whether the stable part was there at birth or laid down by habit (Ref back to #no-method-for-telling-a-type-fact-from-a-habit); floor fits the evidence, not established by it (the ONE body statement of this verdict; also in the Conclusion) — ### The structure of a life, not the single act (figure variance.svg placed here, after all its numbers are set up; key image)
- Unstated premise: drives = traits (or traits measure drives); point standing: trivial situations decide single acts; he was wrong there — ### What the reply leaves open
- Verdict = scorecard figure only (scorecard.svg; row 3 "supported, for populations; floor open"; caption is a fact about the table: only claim 1 unqualified, 2–4 narrowed); the one summary is the Conclusion — ## The verdict, claim by claim

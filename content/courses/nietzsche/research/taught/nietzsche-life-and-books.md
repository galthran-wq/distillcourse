# Taught: nietzsche-life-and-books

## Problem
How do you tell what Nietzsche thought, when his books are numbered sections and his famous "main work" is a posthumous compilation? Answer: settle three things about any passage before using it: its date (period), its kind (book / finished manuscript / notebook), and its argument (claim, steps, gap, unstated premise).

## Explained
- Opening case: "This world is the will to power, and nothing besides!" = notebook 38[12], summer 1885, made the last section (1067) of WP by editors; published version is BGE 36 (1886), hedged as hypothesis ("supposing", "granted") — intro paragraphs
- Three periods defined up front, before any use (concept three-periods): 1 = 1869–1876, young professor writing as Wagner's ally; 2 = 1878–1882, the "free spirit"'s cool, experimental, psychological sections; 3 = 1883–1888, Zarathustra to the long argued works; 1876–77 = the turn between 1 and 2 — opening paragraph of ## Professor at 24…
- The life, dated: b. 1844, pastor father d. when he was 4; grew up in Naumburg (near Leipzig) with mother and sister Elisabeth; philology at Leipzig (Röcken, Pforta, Bonn, grandmother/aunts NOT mentioned). Own beat: Schopenhauer's main work found 1865 in a Leipzig bookshop (glossed: existence is suffering, the wise man stops wanting); met Wagner 1868 (music already revered); Schopenhauer gave BT its problem, Wagner the artist in whom its last sections hope to see tragedy reborn (Ref → schopenhauer-and-the-birth-of-tragedy) — ## Professor at 24…, before the first ###
- Basel chair 1869 at 24, doctorate granted without exam (Ritschl letter quoted); 1870 medical orderly, dysentery + diphtheria, health broken; migraines/eyes thereafter; Tribschen; BT 1872, UM 1873–76 — ### Period 1, Wagner's ally (1869–1876)
- Bayreuth 1876 (cult around Wagner), leave, Sorrento winter 1876–77; HH 1878 "A Book for Free Spirits"; free spirit = "a spirit that has become free, that has again taken possession of itself" (EH); freed from "idealism" = seeing ideal things (genius, saint, hero) where there is only the human, all too human (EH gloss of the title); in EH applied to his own life: serving Wagner as genius, philology as duty; resigns 1879 at 34 with pension; wandering: Genoa/Nice winters, Sils Maria summers; D 1881, GS 1882 — ### Period 2, the free spirit (1878–1882)
- Z I written in ~10 days at Rapallo winter 1882–83; Z introduced as a narrative, not a treatise: the prophet Zarathustra (after Zoroaster) preaches in parables; Z IV private 1885 (40 copies, 7 given); BGE 1886, GM 1887, GS V 1887; 1888: CW, TI, A, EH, NcW; collapse Turin early Jan 1889; letters signed Dionysus / The Crucified (Burckhardt letter quoted: "rather be a Basel professor than God"); Overbeck fetches him; 11 silent years, Naumburg then Weimar (1897); d. 25 Aug 1900; syphilis diagnosis never proven — ### Period 3, from Zarathustra to Turin (1883–1888)
- Three periods' pedigree: He drew the map himself: 1886–87 prefaces to BT, HH, D, GS; EH's book-by-book review; GM Pref 2 dates ideas to Sorrento 1876–77. Map, not wall — ### Three periods, drawn by himself
- "Dionysian" as dating example: BT = intoxicated dissolution of the individual; 1888 = affirmative attitude that keeps and masters passions (only asserted, not argued) — same subsection; KeyFact: a term used across periods is checked against that period's published books, never against a note
- Biography dates turns, doesn't settle arguments (origin ≠ validity); EH I 1 quoted: illness as vantage, "reverse perspectives" — given as his claim, assessment deferred to ecce-homo — ### Illness dates the turns
- Why numbered sections: illness explanation partial; TI Maxims 26 "will to a system is a lack of integrity" quoted; reason rebuilt in two steps: (a) a system lays its premises down as foundations, built on, never questioned (axioms printed on page one so nothing after has to argue for them; Spinoza's Ethics as the treatise example); (b) BGE 6: those foundations carry the author's values (philosophy = "confession of its originator"; moral purpose = vital germ; morality testifies "in what order the deepest impulses of his nature stand to each other", Triebe = drives); together: the values sit where the method exempts them from inspection — ## Why numbered sections
- Objection answered: "a system states its premises, a section hides them, so hiding is less honest" → what matters is whether the premise is open to question, not whether written: a system's stated premise = foundation, where argument stops; a section claims no foundation, its premise must be found by asking what would make the claim true, which already puts it up for testing — ### What a numbered section asks of its reader
- Aphorism (concept aphorism) = any numbered section, one line to several pages; not a fragment (arranged books; BGE, GM continuous); GM Pref 8 quoted (decipher, exegesis, third essay as commentary on one aphorism — which one disputed: Z epigraph or GM III 1, given as a fork, cow/rumination); GS 381 cold baths — ### What a numbered section asks of its reader
- Worked example BGE 68 (German + revised Zimmern, "I cannot have done that"): steps (two reports; memory aims at facts, pride is an interest — "inexorable"; "Eventually —" = pressure over time); claim: belief about one's own past is settled by a contest of motives; gap: no mechanism given; unstated premise: no stakeless judge in the mind; Plato's reason is that judge; KeyFact: BGE 68 presupposes, does not prove, the no-judge premise; the argument comes from BGE 12, 19 → self-as-society-of-drives — ## Reading a section as an argument (Example + 4-frame sequence bge68)
- Reading procedure (5 steps): state claim as general thesis; list steps; find the gap (image/joke/dash where a step should be); name unstated premise; check against other sections, never alone. Cautions: strong reading but only what the words support; say which reading and why; D Pref 5 "slowly, deeply, looking cautiously before and aft" — ### The procedure
- Nachlass (concept nachlass) = everything unprinted; notes as quarry for books; after 1885 the gap grows (Overbeck letter, 2 July 1885, "no longer communicable, at least not in print") — ### Notes as a quarry
- WP plan: announced GM III 27; 374-text list early 1888 (12[1]); "a torment" (Feb 1888); abandoned early Sept 1888; material to TI, A = first book of "Revaluation of All Values" — ### The Will to Power: a plan abandoned
- The sister: Elisabeth (b. 1846), married Bernhard Förster (antisemitic leader) 1885, N. opposed it in letters; Nueva Germania, Paraguay; Förster's suicide 1889; she takes the papers, founds the Archive (Naumburg, then Weimar). Three decisions: WP 1901 (483 sections) and 1906 (1,067), under a discarded four-line plan of 1886–87, ~¼ of his 1888 list omitted, texts cut/split, already-used notes mixed in; delays (EH 1908; A 1895 as "Book One of WP"); at least 32 letters to her shown (1956) to be her compositions — ### What the sister did (NOT told: her later Nazi ties, Hitler's visits — left to the-nazi-appropriation)
- Five kinds of text (critical edition, Colli–Montinari, not named in body), listed in order in the prose: published books; private prints (Z IV); manuscripts finished for print, unprinted before the collapse (A, EH, NcW); early writings finished and kept back (the 1873 truth-and-lie essay); notebook fragments. Ranked by how far he carried each toward release (top: finished, revised, signed, released; bottom: a note = what he thought that day); KeyFact (stated once, only there): the line falls at *finished*, not *printed in his lifetime* (categories 1–3 = "the books"); a note stays a draft whatever its date — ### Ranking the texts (ladder figure)
- Citation form: book abbr + section (same in every edition); GM III 27 (essay), TI Maxims 26 (chapter); notes as "notebook 38[12], June–July 1885, unpublished" (KGW chronological numbering); WP numbers = editors' placement only — same subsection (citation figure)
- Rule vs verdict: "notes are background only" kept; "nothing in notes changes the picture" rejected (the physical proof of recurrence is notebook-only); notes usable as scaffolding, labelled unpublished, never as settled view or secret doctrine — same subsection
- Aside: TI in press late 1888, out Jan 1889; EH revised "on the gold scales" (8 Dec 1888), publisher found it printable; WP has no manuscript of his
- Objection "fragments can't be one argument" (stated in the course's own words, no quoted secondary text); reply in four parts: GM Pref 2 tree-and-fruit (quoted), form invites reconstruction (GM III), unity of a valuer (BGE 6), what he rejects is a premise placed beyond question, not a premise written down: a reconstruction writes premises down as claims to test, never as axioms; gap: unity of person and task, not propositions; BGE 6's "order of the deepest impulses" quoted for the unity of a valuer; where published passages conflict a reader must say which he builds on (usually later, more argued) — ## Can numbered sections add up to one argument?

# Taught: nihilism

## Problem
Opening (= course.yaml `opening`): when a culture stops believing in God, why should the values that belief held up turn against the people who still hold them, instead of standing on their own or quietly lapsing? Developed as the market atheists' expectation (values stand on their own, or lapse like etiquette) vs Nietzsche's 1887 forecast of despair and destruction; two open questions from death-of-god: who killed God, and what in the values turns their loss into an attack. Answer: truthfulness, a Christian virtue trained for two thousand years, discredited the faith (GS 357) and must now turn on the morality (GM III 27); nihilism is the resulting condition ("the goal is lacking; an answer to the 'Why?' is lacking … the highest values are devaluated", 9[35]); it becomes despair rather than mere disorientation only through a second premise, that the highest values were life-negating (measured this world by an invented one, 11[99]), stated here and owed to the genealogy.

## Explained
- Truthfulness (*Wahrhaftigkeit*) = moral duty not to lie, to others or oneself; taught by Christianity (lying a sin, God sees the heart, confession); sublimated into the scientific conscience; GS 357 quoted German + English (incl. nature/history/own life no longer read as providence; "feminism" glossed as *Feminismus* = effeminacy, a womanish giving way, ranked with weakness and cowardice); "two thousand years of discipline for truth that … forbids itself the lie in faith in God" — ## The killer was a Christian virtue / ### Two thousand years of training in truthfulness (FigureSequence truthfulness-1..4; Task)
- Dogma vs morality (dogma = what Christianity says is the case; morality = what to do/value); GM III 27 quoted: dogma destroyed by its own morality, morality must perish too, truthfulness's last inference against itself ("what is the meaning of all will to truth?"); why step 5 of the death-of-god argument cannot last — ### From the dogma to the morality; KeyFact: faith destroyed by its most faithful practitioners = "the highest values devalue themselves"
- Lenzer Heide note (notebook 5[71], "European Nihilism", Lenzer Heide 10 June 1887, 15 §§, unpublished; Lenzerheide anchored as Swiss mountain village on road from Chur): §1 quoted (three gifts; "great antidote to practical and theoretical nihilism") → practical nihilism glossed (despising oneself, taking sides against life) vs theoretical (despairing of knowledge); Christianity cured an older nihilism, glossed provisionally as "finding nothing worth living for" (Silenus's threat); §2 quoted (needs planted now "needs for the untrue"; "the value for whose sake we endure living"; "process of dissolution"); the ill believer case (can't go back: conscience; can't go forward: need remains) — ### The note from Lenzer Heide (figure lenzer-heide.svg: five groups §§1–3, 4–5, 6–8 grey, 9–12, 13–15; eternal recurrence only glossed in caption as existence repeating endlessly without a goal)
- Nihilism (concept nihilism): 9[35] three-line definition; highest values = those that give the others their point (salvation, absolute worth of soul, God's truth); lower goods lose their "why"; Remark definition — ## Nihilism is a condition, not an opinion / ### What "highest values" are
- L13 refutation of "nihilist = believes nothing matters": popular sense from Russia (Turgenev's Fathers and Sons 1862; 1881 assassination of Alexander II); GS 347 "belief in unbelief even to the point of martyrdom", "need for a faith"; slogan fails because indifference would follow; Z IV 9 Shadow quoted ("Nothing is true, all is permitted" is the Shadow's, then lament "O eternal — in vain!"); 11[99] "cannot endure this world which one yet does not want to deny" — ### Refuting the slogan (figure russian-nihilists-1881.jpg placed: 1881 bomb engraving, float-right)
- Disorientation (values no longer bind, no standard; GS 125 "up or down") vs despair (values still bind, judged unrealizable; Lenzer Heide §§4–5 quoted: extreme replaced by its converse; "In vain!" most paralysing thought); GS 346 Either/Or quoted German + English, "reverences" that "perhaps made it possible for us to endure life"; both horns nihilism because both take the old reverences as the only possible measure; the question mark leaves room for a third possibility, a new measure — ## Why the loss is a catastrophe / ### Two ways to lose a value (figure value-realizability.svg 2×2: faith / despair (+ unnoticed shadow, "too early") / disorientation / no stable case)
- Hidden second premise: obvious answer (values were good, losing hurts) unavailable since Nietzsche thinks them bad; an ordinary bad value is a relief to lose (running case: the Friday meat rule on God's authority, a command that lapses when its source goes); so the question is which kind of bad value survives its ground; *hostile to life* defined before use = asks of this world what only another world could give and ranks this world by its shortfall; after 11[99], the contrast: a command has God as source, a life-hostile value is a standard with God as the place it was met; truthfulness removes the belief it is met, not the standard (demand came first, invented world posited to meet it; needs stay planted, ill believer of §2), so the measure survives its world and condemns the only one left; highest values life-negating, measured this world by another (11[99] quoted: purpose, unity, being = what the world was required to have: goal, whole, something lasting beneath change; Christian rankings are cases of that demand; "measured the value of the world against categories that refer to a purely invented world"); examples soul>body, eternal>passing, painless hereafter>life, selfless>self; GM II 24 "curse" of the reigning ideal, nihilism "bound to grow out of it"; Schopenhauer as the case in one mind (GS 357: "Has existence any meaning at all?", answer "stuck in … Christian-ascetic moral perspectives"); pessimism = verdict, nihilism = condition in which the verdict seems forced — ### The hidden premise (figure two-premises.svg = key image; KeyFact: premise 2 carries the account and is not yet shown; evidence = genealogy of the ascetic ideal, Ref ascetic-priest)
- Antidote was itself a will to nothingness: GM III 28 quoted (meaninglessness of suffering the curse; ascetic ideal's meaning; "man would rather will nothingness than not will"); A 7 "one does not say 'nothingness' but 'beyond' or 'God,' or 'true life'"; collapse uncovers life-negation, doesn't create it — ### The antidote was itself a will to nothingness
- Objection (aspiration vs expectation: keep values as aspirations; unfulfilled aspiration ≠ evil) and reply in three steps: Lenzer Heide §2 + §4 (needs not policies; extreme → converse); GM III 28 (aspiration gives suffering no meaning); §§9–12 quoted whole (morality as consolation of the oppressed; contempt for masters' will to power; "nothing to life that has value except the degree of power — assuming … life itself is the will to power"; those who come off badly ruined; self-destruction, "breeding one's own executioners"; "European form of Buddhism"); words glossed (will to power one clause + Ref; masters = type; *die Schlechtweggekommenen* "above all physiologically", §14; European Buddhism = weary No); KeyFact §15 "the most moderate" = Nietzsche grants objection for the strong few; gap: psychological claims, owed to self-as-society-of-drives and slave-revolt — ## The strongest objection: lower the bar
- Passive vs active nihilism (concept passive-and-active-nihilism): 9[35] quoted; notebook-only distinction; passive = spirit weakened below goals, values war, what numbs (religious/political/aesthetic), Z II 19 soothsayer; active = spirit grown past goals, "violent force of destruction", "annihilation by the hand" (note winter 1887–88, p. 225), Z III 12 §20 "what is falling, we should still push"; "passivist" (named under A as active's weary opposite, Buddhism) and "passive" (named under B for the weakness behind it) glossed in two sentences, "passive" covers both; winter 1887–88 note quoted with its subject: "Nihilism is the state of strong spirits and wills … the No of the deed springs from their nature"; gentler reading (criticism only) tested and rejected; GS 370 two kinds of destruction (overflow vs hatred of the ill-constituted; anarchists) — ## Passive, incomplete, active / ### The two named responses
- Incomplete nihilism (Nietzsche's label, notebook 10[42], autumn 1887 = WP 28, quoted Kaufmann/Hollingdale in full incl. "Incomplete nihilism; its forms: we live in the midst of it") = attempts to escape nihilism without revaluing the old values; plainest form keep values, drop ground (market atheists, George Eliot Ref); ordinary state of the educated in 1887; why worse, not only later, in four numbered steps: (1) keeps truthfulness at work (GM III 27), (2) keeps the measure/premise 2 unchallenged, (3) feeds the planted needs and teaches no other valuing (§2), (4) collapse finds measure and needs at full strength, nothing to replace them; "shadow on the cave wall" Ref'd; complete nihilism = seeing nihilism follows necessarily from the old ideals — ### The third response (figure three-responses.svg)
- Decadence (concept decadence): word of French critics of the 1880s; A 6 quoted (corrupt = prefers what is disadvantageous; supreme values = decadence/nihilistic values); TI Socrates 11 "To have to fight the instincts — that is the formula of decadence"; nihilism "just the logic of decadence" (note spring 1888); Remark — ### Decadence
- Is Nietzsche a nihilist?: five forms enumerated = two states (disorientation, despair) + three responses (passive, incomplete, active); pressed by Zarathustra's "push" and notebook 11[411] (Nov 1887–Mar 1888, drafted preface = WP Preface 3, quoted: "the first perfect nihilist of Europe who, however, has even now lived through the whole of nihilism, to the end, leaving it behind, outside himself"); incomplete ruled out (refuses revaluation); despair/passive rest on 9[35]'s pathological "inference that there is no meaning at all", he accepts premise (no true world), rejects inference (GS 346 "far from claiming that the world is worth less"); active nihilism draws no such inference but per 9[35] is "insufficient to productively posit for oneself a new goal", "pathological intermediate state … productive forces are not yet strong enough": a No that alone would end in disorientation; for him a stage/tool for revaluation (Z III 12 §1 "new tablets half covered with writing", §20 "a prelude of better players"); "perfect" = complete (vs incomplete); active alone ends in disorientation (fifth form ruled out with it); 9[41] quoted ("measure of force is how far we can admit to ourselves illusoriness, the necessity of lies, without perishing. To this extent nihilism, as the denial of a true world, of a being, might be a divine way of thinking"): Nietzsche and the despairing nihilist share the denial of a true world; the test is what the admission does; KeyFact: the sickness is holding that belief while still measuring by the true world; for one strong enough to stop, "a divine way of thinking", the sense of "leaving it behind" — ### Is Nietzsche a nihilist?
- Revaluation (concept revaluation): two exits (restore: closed, GS 347 "faith … coveted … where will is lacking"; revalue by standard of ascending vs declining life); TI Skirmishes 32: philosopher who judges man by his ideals finds nothing behind them ("If a philosopher could be a nihilist…"), error = judging the real by the merely wished-for, corrected by "What justifies man is his reality"; title history (GM III 27 subtitle; A as first book; Ref lesson 1); GM II 24 "antinihilist"; Remark — ## After nihilism / ### A new standard

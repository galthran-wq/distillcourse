# Taught: self-as-society-of-drives

## Problem
Opening (= course.yaml `opening`): Plato argues from the thirsty man who does not drink that reason, a part with no wants of its own, rules the soul; what happens to self-control and unity if no such ruler is found? Three problems open: what are the parts, what is self-control with nothing above the passions, how unified a person can be with nothing holding the parts from above (character vs crowd). Answer: the parts are drives (BGE 12 "soul as social structure of the drives and affects"); whatever fights a drive is another drive (D 109, BGE 117); no motive-free arbiter is found (GS 333, TI Socrates 9) and economy says none is needed; "I" is the title of whichever drive or coalition governs (BGE 19, 158); how unified a person is = the order of rank among drives, achieved, not given (EH 'Clever' 9). Six-step argument assembled after all three answers (step 4 = BGE 68's presupposition; bridge 3→4 = introspection premise, supported by economy). Objection "who arranges the drives?" answered from the texts; gap = membership: why these drives belong to one society at all, a question distinct from (and prior to) how unified the society is; left open (notebook 40 only shows plurality still an open hypothesis).

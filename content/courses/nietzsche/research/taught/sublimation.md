# Taught: sublimation

## Problem
Opening (= course.yaml `opening`, verbatim, stated as the problem): a dangerous drive cannot be argued out of wanting; the church destroyed it ("If thy right eye offend thee, pluck it out"); is anything lost when it dies, can its force be kept without its danger. Developed with Matthew 5:29 context and monastic discipline; one-clause Ref bridge to will-to-power (every drive wants to discharge strength against resistance); two offers: let it out / cut it out. Three questions: what is lost when a dangerous drive dies; how a drive keeps its force while its aim changes, and who changes it; whether the refined form is more than the old vice in better clothes. Answer: extirpation removes the strength the vice used (the only strength there is, on the drive picture) and is chosen by those who cannot moderate; spiritualization keeps the force and changes object and form, possible because a drive's satisfaction lies in overcoming (layer 2), produced by a strong drive under long constraint (BGE 189's fast), directed by a ruling goal that wants the drive strong; the unmasking objection answered by continuity + a comparative claim (BGE 75: crude-drive strength measured before refinement sets the ceiling of the refined form); gap: "higher" needs the ascending/declining standard, the hydraulic model is assumed, and no outcome-independent measure of drive strength is given; sublimation is a mechanism ranked above extirpation and crude discharge, not the meaning of will to power; strength governed but unsublimated still beats extirpation (sooner even a Borgia than a Parsifal).

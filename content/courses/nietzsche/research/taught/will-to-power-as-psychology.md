# Taught: will-to-power-as-psychology

## Problem
Opening (= course.yaml `opening`, verbatim; problem only, no answer): most of what a living thing does keeps it alive, so Darwin's readers took survival as its aim and Schopenhauer took every want to aim at relief; why does a climber take the north face when a cable car runs to the summit, and what is a living thing after if neither explains it? Developed as: Darwin and Schopenhauer anchored; bridge: the will to live never stops wanting, each single want aims at relief (pendulum step 3, Ref); both rivals name the aim as a *state*; the cable car is safer and painless, so neither fits; bridge from doer-behind-the-deed's closing (GS 360 dammed-up energy, the steam; GM I 13 "thirst for enemies and resistances and triumphs"): if no self moves the drives, what do they want? Stakes: Schopenhauer's verdict rested on pendulum step 3; case-of-wagner's ascending/declining standard lacked content. Difficulties: the phrase misread as lust to rule; a one-motive principle risks explaining nothing. Answer: will to power = drive to discharge strength and grow by overcoming resistance in pursuing an end; survival and pleasure are results; beats self-preservation (squandering when safe) and the will to live (satisfaction is overcoming, not relief: pendulum step 3 denied); covers mastery over others and over oneself, neither the "real" one; testable in its hedged published form, untestable in the universal notebook form; scope fork psychology / biology / world, the world-thesis published only under "supposing" (BGE 36).

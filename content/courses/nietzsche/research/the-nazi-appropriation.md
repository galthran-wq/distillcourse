## ARC (L10)
- **Spine step (13, "Taking stock"):** "Taking stock: Nietzsche's contempt for the state, nationalism and antisemitism sits beside his praise of rank and caste and does not add up to a political programme; the Nazi reading was a distortion built by his sister's Archive and the regime out of a compiled book and clipped quotations, with a residue of hard passages it could use; assembled, the argument holds in its critique, strains in its reply, and achieves a new way of holding values up"
- **Inherits:** from nietzsche-and-politics, the whole inventory — the texts that refute the regime's reading (state, nation, antisemitism) and the texts it could use unaltered (BGE 251 "let no more Jews come in", 257-258, 208, A 57, GS 377 "new slavery", the breeding notes) — and the two uses of "great politics". From order-of-rank, the BGE 257-258 reconstruction and A 57. From nietzsche-life-and-books, the published/unpublished rule and the sister's Will to Power. From master-morality and slave-revolt-and-ressentiment, the blond beast (GM I 11) and GM I 5's ethnology. From overman-and-last-man and will-to-power-as-psychology, what "overman" and "will to power" denote.
- **Contributes:** the module's application (L16): real artifacts — the Archive, the compiled Will to Power, Oehler's clipped HH 475, the sister's altered letter, the Oehler pedigree, the cut drafts of Ecce Homo — read against the texts with the sourcing drill. No new concepts; reinforces nachlass, aristocratic-radicalism, good-european, will-to-power, higher-type, order-of-rank.
- **Hands off:** to the-system-assembled, the verdict on the political residue (the hard passages are real, published in part, and belong to the reply's weak links, not to the critique) and the "write two paragraphs" exercise (the old brief's closing task moves to lesson 45).

## STANCE (binding)
- **Favorable:** the lesson tells the appropriation as the distortion it was — by Elisabeth Förster-Nietzsche, who married an antisemitic agitator, built the Archive, delayed Ecce Homo so her biographies had authority (Kaufmann, Note on Publication, EH pp. 213-214), compiled notes under a plan her brother abandoned, altered letters, and by a regime whose exegetes clipped sentences from their contexts — and it shows that the published books say, at full strength, the opposite of what the regime needed on nation, race-hatred, antisemitism and the state. It also names, without softening, what the regime could quote unaltered.
- **Hard passages this lesson must quote whole:** HH 475 (Portable pp. 61-63; Zimmern pp. 346-348, early-ma.txt lines 478-545) with the German flanking sentences from early-ma-de.txt: "Unangenehme, ja gefährliche Eigenschaften hat jede Nation, jeder Mensch; es ist grausam, zu verlangen, dass der Jude eine Ausnahme machen soll" — "vielleicht ist der jugendliche Börsen-Jude die widerlichste Erfindung des Menschengeschlechtes überhaupt" — "Trotzdem möchte ich wissen, wie viel man bei einer Gesammtabrechnung einem Volke nachsehen muss ... dem man den edelsten Menschen (Christus), den reinsten Weisen (Spinoza), das mächtigste Buch und das wirkungsvollste Sittengesetz der Welt verdankt" (verify eKGWB MA-475). A 2 (p. 570) in Kaufmann's wording: "The weak and the failures shall perish: first principle of our love of man. And they shall even be given every possible assistance. What is more harmful than any vice? Active pity for all the failures and all the weak: Christianity." GM I 5 (pp. 30-31) whole (blond Aryan conquerors, dark pre-Aryan aboriginals, democracy as the suppressed race's "counterattack"). GM I 11 (pp. 40-42) with its list and the p. 42 disclaimer. BGE 251 both edges; BGE 257-258 ("slavery in some form or other"; "slaves and instruments"); A 46 (p. 625); A 57 (p. 646); TI Improvers 4 (p. 504, "pure blood"); TI Skirmishes 36 (pp. 536-538, "The sick man is a parasite of society"); GS 377 both edges; D 205, 272, 241 whole. Notebooks (ALL unpublished, dated): 37[5] (June-July 1885, Bittner pp. 31-32) "to breed a ruling caste - the future masters of the earth"; 2[?] (1885-86, p. 71) "international dynasties ... rearing a master race"; 9[153?] (autumn 1887, pp. 166-167) "The levelling out of European man ... should be speeded up even further ... Not merely a master race"; 10[50] (autumn 1887, pp. 183-184) "assuming he doesn't belong to the criminal race. In that case war should be declared upon him even before he's done anything hostile (first step once one has him in one's power: castrate him)"; 11[?] (1887-88, pp. 207-208) "barbarians of the twentieth century"; 14[?] (1888, pp. 240-242) Christianity "thwarts natural selection"; 10[?] (pp. 190, 204-205) the anti-Jewish register inside a polemic against Christianity.
- **What the words meant:** "Breeding" (Züchtung; Zucht und Züchtung): the long cultivation of a type by conditions, discipline and valuations, inheritable because he assumed, with Lamarck, that acquired character is inherited (GS 99; Kaufmann monograph pp. 293-295); set against "taming" (TI Improvers 2-5), explicitly compatible with race-mixture (D 272, BGE 251's proposal to cross Jewish "genius for money and patience" with the Prussian officer caste, GS 377 "mixed racially"). "Race" (Rasse): a people formed by conditions, "res facta" (BGE 251), with no pure races (D 272); "blood" (Geblüt): tradition and inherited discipline (Kaufmann pp. 305-306 on the "parenthesis for asses" note; unpublished). "Master" (Herr), "master race" (Herren-Rasse): the ruling type in an order of rank, a position defined by what it can command and bear (BGE 257-258; A 57), in the notebooks a projected European caste "built upon the harshest self-legislation" (2[?] p. 71), never a nation. "Blond beast": the lion, the unsublimated predator at the root of every nobility, Roman and Japanese as much as Germanic (GM I 11; TI Improvers 2 makes the Teuton its caged specimen "improved" by the church). "Overman": the type that overcomes man and creates values (Z Prologue 3; EH III 1 rejects Darwin, Carlyle and the "half saint, half genius" reading). "The weak shall perish": a statement of what active pity obstructs, with the next sentence turning it into a maxim of assistance (A 2); what "perish" denotes is not stated, and the lesson says so. Stated as what he meant; not as excuse.
- **Objection answered from his side:** the strongest serious objection is not that he was misquoted but that the regime did not need to misquote: BGE 257-258, 208, 251 (one edge), A 2, A 57, GS 377, the breeding notes and "morality for physicians" were in print or in the sister's compilation, and an order of rank that subordinates the many is usable by any ruling caste that calls itself the few. Reply from his side, texts first: (1) the texts fix what the "few" are — the most spiritual, solitary, value-creating type (BGE 212; A 57 "the most spiritual"), which excludes a party and a nation by construction, and he says so of the Germans in particular (EH III CW 4; TI Germans 1 "power makes stupid"); (2) the state is excluded by name in every period (Z I 11; TI Germans 4; D 179), and nationalism and race-hatred are diagnosed as symptoms of weakness (GS 377; BGE 251 "a people whose nature is still feeble"); (3) the breeding he describes works through valuations and conditions over "many generations" (37[5]), never through a state's apparatus. Gap sentence to write: the texts give no criterion for who the few are that could not be claimed by anyone strong enough to claim it, and BGE 258's "slaves and instruments" and 10[50]'s "castrate him" are his sentences; the reader owns them as his.
- **Humanizing reading to test:** Kaufmann's "unequivocally anti-Nazi; worth is a function of creativity, not race; breeding is self-discipline (Selbstzucht); blond beast and master morality are not racial; the Antichrist is not antisemitic" (monograph Ch. 10 pp. 284-306; GM I 11 n. 3 pp. 40-41; I 16 n. 2 p. 53; GS 377 nn. 147-148; Portable Intro IV pp. 14-15) is ONE reading. What the text says for it: HH 475, GS 377, A 55, TI Maxims 19, EH Appendix 4, the letters. What the text says against its sufficiency: GM I 5's Aryan/pre-Aryan ethnology is in the published Genealogy; BGE 251's "let no more Jews come in" is published; D 241 and A 46 are published; 10[50] and 37[5] are his hand. Kaufmann concedes the Polish-Jews aside (Portable AC Editor's Preface pp. 565-568) and that "resentment clings to him like a leech" (EH Intro p. 208).
- **Facts against the popular image:** the Christmas 1887 letter (Portable pp. 456-457); EH Appendix 4(a) "The damnable German anti-Semitism, this poisonous boil of névrose nationale" (p. 342); the deleted draft "Jews among Germans are always the higher race — more refined, spiritual, kind" (EH III 2 n. 1, p. 262); the sister's altered letter of October 1888 (Kaufmann monograph pp. 442-443: the Kaiser's stand "against anti-Semitism and the Kreuzzeitung. Do likewise, my brave Llama!" cut and replaced by "The will to power as a principle would surely make sense to him"; "Original burnt at my mother's request"); Max Oehler's 1938 pedigree proving Nietzsche racially pure against Nietzsche's own claim of Polish descent (EH I 3 n., p. 225); the January 1889 margins (Portable p. 687); Stefan George refusing Goebbels in 1933 and the Stauffenberg brothers at his deathbed (Kaufmann Epilogue pp. 417-418). Reported because the documents say so.

## The reconstruction
- **Claim and where.** The Nazi reading needed five things: will to power as domination and biology; the overman as a race; the blond beast as the German; antisemitism supplied and the good European erased; the state glorified and the Nachlass preferred to the books. The published texts refuse each by name (BGE 13 and 36 conditional; EH III 1; GM I 11 list and TI Improvers 2; HH 475, GS 377, A 55, TI Maxims 19; Z I 11, TI Germans 4; EH Pref 1 and the published/unpublished rule). The machinery that supplied them: the Archive, the compilation under an abandoned title, altered letters, clipping.
- **Argument in plain steps.** (1) The sister controlled the Nachlass from 1894, delayed Ecce Homo and published The Will to Power in 1901 and 1906 under a plan he had abandoned in September 1888, omitting about a quarter of the texts he had listed and cutting others (Bittner; Kaufmann Prologue). (2) The regime's exegetes quoted sentences out of their contexts (Oehler's HH 475; Härtle's omission of the "parenthesis for asses"; Brinton's six references traced to Härtle), while the sister altered letters (the October 1888 letter) and a relative manufactured a racial pedigree against his own claim of Polish descent. (3) Read whole, the clipped passages say the opposite: HH 475 argues for dissolving nations and calls the Jew "as useful and desirable an ingredient as any other"; A 2's next sentence; GM I 11's Romans, Arabs and Japanese. (4) Nonetheless some passages carry the hard content unaltered: BGE 257-258, 208, 251 (one edge), A 57, A 2, GS 377 "new slavery", TI Skirmishes 36, GM I 5, D 241, and the notebooks' "future masters of the earth" and "castrate him". (5) What these denote in his vocabulary (breeding by valuations, race as res facta, master as a rank-position) is not what the regime meant, and he says so (the Germans "impossible", TI Germans; antisemites "lie as a matter of principle"); but the subordination of the many and the hardness toward the "failures" are his, published, and follow from his premises (order-of-rank). (6) Verdict: a distortion that needed compilation, forgery and clipping at its core, with a residue of hard passages the reader owns as his.
- **Unstated premise.** Of the appropriation: that a thinker's notes outrank his books and that a sentence means what it means out of its paragraph. Of the course's refutation: that the published/unpublished rule and the whole-paragraph rule are the right rules — the lesson states this premise and shows what it costs (37[5] and 10[50] are still his hand).
- **Strongest objection and Nietzsche's reply.** As under STANCE.
- **Connections.** nietzsche-and-politics (the inventory; two uses of great politics); order-of-rank (BGE 257-258; A 57); master-morality and slave-revolt-and-ressentiment (blond beast; GM I 5; antisemitism as ressentiment); will-to-power-as-psychology (BGE 13, 36 vs the compiled "This world is the will to power — and nothing besides", 38[12], unpublished, discarded); overman-and-last-man (EH III 1); nietzsche-life-and-books (the Nachlass rule; the sister); ecce-homo (Pref 1; IV 1 "prevent people from doing mischief with me"); the-system-assembled (the residue as a weak link of the reply).

## Pitfalls
- No moralizing, no exoneration by adjective, no "danger to the reader" framing: facts, documents and quotations; the residue section is written to the same standard as the forgery section.
- Never argue "not racial" by assertion: show GM I 11's list AND GM I 5's ethnology, BGE 251's both edges, D 272 AND D 205; Kaufmann's notes are one reading, printed beside the text.
- Every notebook passage is unpublished and dated each time it appears; "WP 942" and the like are compilation numbers — cite notebook[fragment] and Bittner page where known, otherwise say "compilation number; concordance check owed".
- A 2 is quoted in Kaufmann's wording; Ludovici's "botched" is a historical artifact of the reception, shown as such if at all.
- "Blond beast" in TI Improvers 2 is the caged Teuton the church "improved"; it is not praise of Germans.
- TI Skirmishes 36 is about the physician's responsibility and the sick man's free death; quote it whole and say what it does and does not say about the state.
- Dates of the Archive, visits, funeral and the Nazi books come from Aschheim/Macintyre (References) and Kaufmann; never from Young.
- Images of Hitler at the Archive are not public domain; the Villa Silberblick and the 1906 title page are.
- L13: the documented misconception ("the Nazis took him at his word") is refuted by the refutation schema — correct claim, misconception once, the documents, the replacement model, GM I 11 as the discriminating example — and then the residue is named so the refutation is not a second clipping.

## APPLICATION spec (L16)
- **The text read whole:** not one text but four artifacts read against the texts: (1) HH 475 whole (Portable pp. 61-63; Zimmern pp. 346-348; German) against Oehler's clipping (Kaufmann pp. 289-290); (2) the sister's altered letter of October 1888 (Kaufmann Appendix VI pp. 442-443); (3) The Will to Power as an artifact: the abandoned plan, 12[1]'s 374 texts, the 1901/1906 editions (Bittner Intro pp. x-xii; Kaufmann Prologue pp. 3-9; D'Iorio); (4) A 2 (p. 570), GM I 11 (pp. 40-42) and GM I 5 (pp. 30-31) read whole as the test of what context does and does not change.
- **Section map:** the Archive and the compilation use nietzsche-life-and-books (the Nachlass rule) and recurrence-as-cosmology (what the compiled "proof" and "This world is the will to power" are); the clipped quotations use slave-revolt-and-ressentiment and master-morality (blond beast, GM I 5), jesus-and-paul and nietzsche-and-politics (A 2, A 55, HH 475, GS 377); the residue uses order-of-rank (BGE 257-258, A 57), higher-type (who the few are), will-to-power-as-psychology (what "power" denotes), creating-values (values made by valuers, and by whom they can be made for a thinker).
- **Where the text goes further / departs from the module's idealisation:** the reconstruction does not clear him: GM I 5 is in the published Genealogy, BGE 251's second edge is published, 37[5] and 10[50] are his hand; the course's rule that the books outrank the notes is a rule the regime rejected and a reader may question, and the lesson says what it costs to hold it.
- **Reading order and the one sentence:** machinery (Archive, compilation, letters) → the clipped quotations read whole → what the texts refute → what they could be used for, with what the words denoted → verdict. The reader can now take any quotation attributed to Nietzsche, locate it, say whether it is published, unpublished, clipped, altered or misattributed, and state what it means in its paragraph.

# Research brief: value-creation-and-normativity (Value Creation and the Naturalist's Normativity Problem)

status: planned
module: affirmation
type: core
goal: Analyze the claim that values are created rather than discovered (GS 301, Z 'On a Thousand and One Goals') and evaluate the anti-realist, fictionalist and constructivist readings by what each lets a naturalist say is good.

## ARC (L10)

- **Inherits:** The genealogy's result (values have a history and a type); the fable (no value in being); the drive psychology (the soul as a rank-order); the higher type as the subject of the normative charge; the is–ought bridge flagged in the Antichrist, will-to-power-as-psychology and higher-type; the scope problem from genealogy-as-method; disorientation's metaethical answers from nihilism-and-affirmation.
- **Contributes:** The normativity problem stated as the naturalist's own: having explained where 'good' came from, how can he call anything good? GS 301 ('we are the ones who create what has value'), BGE 211 (philosophers as commanders and legislators), BGE 186 (against 'grounding' morality), Z 'On a Thousand and One Goals' ('man is the esteemer'), GS 335 ('give themselves laws'). Three readings reconstructed with what each must say about GS 301: anti-realism/skepticism (Leiter), fictionalism (Hussain), constructivism/subjective realism (Thomas, Reginster, Anderson) — plus Railton's normative/evaluative split and Robertson's scope reframing. The verdict deferred to the audit with the question sharpened: which reading the drive psychology of BGE 12–19 supports, and whether the sovereign-individual dispute already decided it.
- **Hands off:** To overman-staged (the ideal's dramatization); to Module 7 (Nehamas's aestheticism as a fourth answer; the audit).

Spine step: "The positive doctrine: nihilism splits into disorientation and despair, affirmation is tested by eternal recurrence, the higher type is defined by pathos of distance and sublimation — and value creation is where a naturalist must say how values can be made rather than found; the overman and the last human being are its dramatization."

## Key points the lesson must cover

1. **The problem, stated as the naturalist's own.** The genealogy explains every value by a type and a history; the fable removes value from being; the psychology is causal. Then 'the higher type is good' or 'MPS harms' is either (a) a report of a type's perspective — no better than the priest's, on its own terms — or (b) a claim with some standing the theory has not supplied. This is the normativity problem (J&R's Ch. 1 framing: the volume's question). State it as an argument: (P) all valuations are type-expressions; (P) Nietzsche's valuations are valuations; (C) Nietzsche's valuations are type-expressions — and then the question: is that all he claims for them?
2. **The texts on creation.** GS 301: 'we… are the givers' — value 'given, granted' by us, the 'contemplatives'; BGE 211: 'genuine philosophers are commanders and legislators: they say "thus it shall be!"' — 'their "knowing" is creating, their creating is a legislation, their will to truth is — will to power'; Z 'Thousand and One Goals': 'humans first placed values into things… only through esteeming is there value'; 'On Old and New Tablets': creators as breakers of tablets; GS 335: 'give themselves laws'. Three puzzles (SEP §3.1): is creation fiat or constrained achievement (BGE 211 vs. GS 290's 'constraint of a single taste')? is art the paradigm? is revaluation (of the ascetic ideal's values) the model rather than creation ex nihilo?
3. **Anti-realism / skepticism (Leiter; Berry).** Value claims are expressions of a type's affects; there are no value facts; Nietzsche's own valuations are his (and his type's), offered to those 'for whom' they are; the critique's force is rhetorical (genealogy-as-method) and the normative charge is 'MPS harms this type' — a causal claim, not a value fact. What it must say about GS 301: literally true, and the giving is causal. Cost: Nietzsche's 'higher' and 'lower' become type-relative; BGE 30's audience partition as the textual support; Reginster's elitism objection as the textual cost.
4. **Fictionalism (Hussain).** Nietzsche's higher types are 'honest illusionists': they create values knowing them to be fictions — 'we have art in order not to perish from the truth' (WP 822, Nachlass) and GS 107 ('honesty would lead to nausea… art as the good will to appearance'). What it must say about GS 301: the giving is a pretence the giver sees through. Thomas's rebuttal (J&R Ch. 6): as Nietzsche-interpretation it cannot accommodate the seriousness of BGE 211; as a position it is unstable (one cannot value what one knows to be nothing). Cost and reply stated.
5. **Constructivism / subjective realism (Thomas; Reginster's subjectivism; Anderson; Silk).** Values are real as constituted by the valuer's affects and will — 'subjective realism' — and creation is constrained by the valuer's constitution (the drive-order of BGE 12–19; GS 290's 'single taste'); Anderson's emergent self as the valuer; Clark & Dudrick's 'capacity for values' as personhood. What it must say about GS 301: the giving makes value real for the giver and is answerable to the giver's constitution. Cost: what standing has a value 'real for' a type against another type's — the relativism worry (Railton) returns.
6. **Railton and Robertson: two ways to shrink the problem.** Railton: naturalism undermines deontic normativity (rule, obligation, right/wrong — the 'thou shalt') but can keep evaluative concepts (good, noble, base); Nietzsche's ideal gives reasons by inspiration and emulation of exemplars, not by obligation — BGE 260's noble 'value-creating' vs. Z's 'thou shalt' dragon. Robertson: the target of the critique is morality's claim to authority and universal jurisdiction, not evaluation as such — Nietzsche's own values claim neither, so the scope problem (genealogy-as-method) is solved by what they do not claim. Both are readings within the naturalist camp; state what each buys (a Nietzsche who evaluates without moralizing) and the residue (whether 'higher' still ranks types across perspectives — GM I:17's prize-essay note says it does).
7. **The verdict deferred and sharpened.** What each reading needs from BGE 12–19: anti-realism needs the drive-order to be causal only (Leiter's side of the epiphenomenalism dispute); constructivism needs it to be normative (Clark & Dudrick's side; Anderson's sovereign individual). The sovereign-individual dispute (guilt-reconstructed) was underdetermined; the same underdetermination returns here at the level of the whole doctrine. State it, hand it to the audit, and name Nehamas's aestheticism as the fourth reading Module 7 adds (the value of a valuation is the character of its author).

## Set pieces and worked examples to recreate

- **GS 301 in German and English** — the sentence every camp accepts and reads differently.
- **BGE 211's 'commanders and legislators'** with 'their will to truth is — will to power' — the strongest creation text.
- **The Stoics** (BGE 9; Clark & Dudrick Ch. 3) — 'according to nature' as reading one's values into nature and finding them there: the worked example of normative commitment disguised as discovery — the course's caution against doing the same with Nietzsche.
- **The three readings on one sentence** — a three-column figure.
- **Railton's split** — deontic (struck) vs. evaluative (kept), with Z's dragon 'thou shalt' and BGE 260's 'value-creating' as the two anchors.
- **Hussain vs. Thomas** as a live disagreement inside one volume — the dossier's 'good raw material for a lesson structured as a live debate'.
- **Railton's Table 1** (p. 26) redrawn: normative-proper vs. evaluative concepts on four features — the split that lets a naturalist deny obligation and keep 'noble'.
- **The water and the mug** (Railton pp. 27–31): getting a drink after teaching, without deliberation, yet evaluable — the worked case of agency without a free will; then the mariner in the storm (pp. 31–32) for admiration tracking skill.
- **Harold Skimpole** (Blackburn pp. 291–293, Bleak House ch. 61): the free spirit who 'floats above morality and plays' (GS 107) taken literally — the discriminating example against the play reading.
- **'John is short' and the kindergarten hallway** (Hussain 2012 p. 113): language that expresses an attitude vs. language whose meaning IS the attitude — the cognitivism/non-cognitivism line drawn on two sentences.
- **Reginster's four strategies** (Ch. 4.I) as a four-cell table: voluntarism, fictionalism, naturalism, internalism — each with its claim and its failure mode; the lesson's map of the readings, with Hussain 2007 and Thomas 2012 placed in it.
- **Clark & Dudrick's three stages** (2007 pp. 193–226): HA → GS → GM as a timeline of Nietzsche's metaethics, beside Clark 1990's three stages on truth — the same developmental method twice.

## Pitfalls / subtleties to flag

- Every secondary source here is on disk except Leiter 2015 (toc-only): quote Railton's Table 1, Hussain's semantic criterion, Thomas's revaluation objection, Blackburn's Skimpole, Reginster's four strategies directly; state Leiter's anti-realism in the terms his 2007 Introduction and the reviews verify.
- Do not decide the metaethics; the course's job is to state what each reading must say about the same sentences and what each costs. The audit judges.
- WP 822 ('we have art in order not to perish from the truth') is Nachlass; the published near-equivalent is GS 107 — cite that.
- The relativism worry is not the relativist misreading (refuted in Module 2 and 4): the worry is internal to the constructivist reading and is stated as Railton's.
- No metaethics primer beyond what the readings need; define 'anti-realism', 'fictionalism', 'constructivism' in one sentence each at first use (L11c: three terms, three paragraphs).

## EXERCISE PLAN (floor: ≤1 warm-up; ≥2 of boundary / refute / derivation / research; one FORWARD)

1. (warm-up) State the normativity problem as a three-line argument and name the three course results that supply its premises.
2. (refute) "Because Nietzsche says values are created, he holds that any value is as good as any other." Break it with GM I:17's prize-essay note, BGE 211's 'thus it shall be', and GS 290's 'constraint of a single taste'; then state which reading (constructivism) has the hardest time with the refutation and why (Railton's relativism worry).
3. (derivation) Reconstruct Thomas's objection to fictionalism as an argument (a value known to be a fiction cannot be valued; BGE 211's legislator is not pretending), and derive what a fictionalist must add to survive it (a distinction between believing and acting-as-if that Nietzsche's texts on 'honesty' — BGE 227, GS 107 — either support or block; say which).
4. (boundary probe) Consider a valuer who creates a value and holds it only for himself, claiming nothing for others (BGE 43: 'my judgment is my judgment'). Does the normativity problem arise for him? State what Robertson's reframing (authority and jurisdiction) says about such a valuer, and whether Nietzsche's 'higher' and 'lower' (GM I:17) fit the case.
5. (research-shaped, corroboration) Read BGE 9 (the Stoics) and Clark & Dudrick's use of it (research/texts/thesoul-full.txt, Ch. 3) and then apply the same test to A §2 ('what is good? all that heightens the feeling of power'): is Nietzsche reading his values into nature, and by his own BGE 9 standard what would show it? 6. (translation comparison) GS 301's 'Werth… geschenkt' and 'die Schenkenden': compare 'given' / 'bestowed' / 'granted' and 'givers' / 'bestowers'; state which rendering makes the constructivist reading (value real for the giver) easier and which the fictionalist (value as gift-pretence), and whether the German settles it.
7. (FORWARD → overman-staged) BGE 211 says the philosopher of the future legislates; GS 335 says the ideal gives itself laws; neither says what the law is. Write what a dramatization of the legislator would have to show that the argued works cannot (a figure, a goal, a 'meaning of the earth'), and find Zarathustra's Prologue §3 sentence that supplies it; the solution says the next lesson maps that speech and records what the figure adds that no argument gives.

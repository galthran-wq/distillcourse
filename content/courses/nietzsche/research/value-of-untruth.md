# Research brief: value-of-untruth (The value of untruth: art and illusion)

status: planned
module: truth (6 of 9), lesson 31 of 45
type: core
goal: Evaluate Nietzsche's claim that untruth can be a condition of life and that art is the honest form of illusion

## ARC (L10)

- **Spine step (10, "Truth turned on itself"):** "Without a view from nowhere there is only perspectival knowing, and the unconditional will to truth turns out to be the ascetic ideal's last form; untruth, art and illusion can serve life, and the young Nietzsche's essay on truth shows which part of this he got right first and which he later abolished."
- **Inherits:** from will-to-truth, truth as a measure of strength; from perspectivism, BGE 34's "degrees of seemingness"; from schopenhauer-and-the-birth-of-tragedy, the aesthetic justification and Apollo's veil (GT 3-4, 24-25) with the Primordial Unity that carried it.
- **Contributes:** the positive side of the critique of truth. Some false beliefs are indispensable (enduring things, equal things, substances, free agents, "that what is good for me is also good in itself"); "the falsity of a judgment is not yet an objection to it"; art is the illusion that knows itself, "the good will to appearance", which keeps truthfulness from "nausea and suicide". Introduces untruth-as-condition-of-life and honest-illusion; brings back The Birth of Tragedy's question without its metaphysics; holds the counterview inside Nietzsche (EH: "the decadents need the lie").
- **Hands off:** to on-truth-and-lies, which tests the late view against the 1873 essay, written when he still measured our "illusions" against a thing in itself.

## STANCE (binding)

- **Favorable:** Nietzsche saw, before evolutionary epistemology had a name, that the mind's basic categories were selected for use, not accuracy, so a creature's deepest certainties are the oldest errors it cannot live without; and he saw what follows for a truthful creature: honesty about this would be unbearable unless something taught it to love appearance knowingly. Art as "the good will to appearance" is the one place where illusion and honesty coexist without one lying about the other: "Plato versus Homer".
- **Hard passages this lesson must quote whole:**
  - TI "The 'Improvers' of Mankind" 5 (p.505, portable-late.txt ll.2441-2451): the pia fraus, "the heirloom of all philosophers and priests who 'improved' mankind. Neither Manu nor Plato nor Confucius nor the Jewish and Christian teachers have ever doubted their right to lie ... all the means by which one has so far attempted to make mankind moral were through and through immoral."
  - A 56-57 (pp.643-646, ll.7519-7563): "The 'holy lie'—common to Confucius, the law of Manu, Mohammed, the Christian church—is not absent from Plato"; "it was with an opposite feeling that I read the law of Manu"; the lie that sanctions the caste order as "a natural order", quoted with its caste framing. A 23 (pp.590-591): against "the 'holy lie' even more than against any other lie".
  - BGE 188 ("stupidity as a condition of life"), 107 ("a will to stupidity", bge-zimmern.txt l.2121), 59 (l.1811: piety as "the fear of truth", "untruth at any price").
- **What the words meant:** "holy lie" (pia fraus) denotes a legislation passed off as revelation or nature so that it cannot be questioned; A 57's "opposite feeling" toward Manu is not a retraction: he ranks the Manu lie as affirmative (it says Yes to life and rank) against the Christian lie as negating, and the lesson shows the ranking, where "untruth in the service of life" meets the order of rank. "Stupidity" (BGE 107, 188): the narrowing of view any long obedience requires, the unflattering name for the life-serving untruth GS 110 calls error. "The decadents need the lie" (EH III BT 2): "lie" there is the ideal, the untruth that denies being one, the opposite of art's.
- **Objection answered from his side:** incoherence: one cannot believe something while believing it false, so "useful and false" beliefs cannot be held knowingly. Reply: GS 110's errors are "incorporated", held below reflection ("sense perception and every kind of sensation worked with those basic errors"), so believing and knowing sit at two levels; GS 54's dreamer who "must go on dreaming lest I perish" and GS 107's "good will to appearance" show that knowing engagement with known illusion is possible, because art does it daily. Assess: the reply works for art and the incorporated categories; it strains for beliefs acted on reflectively, and the lesson says so. Second objection: pragmatism (true = useful). Reply: "Life is no argument. The conditions of life might include error" (GS 121); "Man's truths are ultimately his irrefutable errors" (GS 265); the notebook keeps error as error. Gap sentence: Nietzsche never specifies the standard by which the useful beliefs are false; the two readings of BGE 4 are the two ways of filling that silence.
- **Humanizing reading to test:** the "new language" reading, on which "false" in BGE 3-4 means only "lacks a standard beyond man". The text says "the falsest opinions (to which the synthetic judgments a priori belong) are the most indispensable", "a constant counterfeiting of the world by means of numbers", and GS 110 names the errors; the notebooks say "error" flatly. Present it as one option and show the sentences it must reinterpret.
- **Facts against the popular image:** EH argues the opposite of "untruth is fine": "They are not free to know: the decadents need the lie" (III BT 2, p.272); "The condition of the existence of the good is the lie" (IV 4, p.329). The reconciliation is honest illusion versus the lie that denies being one.

## The reconstruction

- **Claim and where.** BGE 4: falsity is not yet an objection; some false judgments are indispensable; untruth is a condition of life. GS 110: the errors named. GS 107, GM III 25: art is the illusion that knows itself.
- **Argument in plain steps.** (1) To act, an organism must treat different things as the same, changing things as enduring, itself as one agent (GS 110, 111). (2) Strictly, nothing is equal, nothing endures unchanged, and the agent is grammar's fiction (lessons 10, 28). (3) So the beliefs indispensable to action are, strictly, false. (4) They were kept because they worked; they are "incorporated" (GS 110). (5) Therefore falsity alone cannot be a reason to reject a judgment one cannot live without; the question is what it does for life (BGE 4). (6) A truthful creature that sees this risks "nausea and suicide" (GS 107). (7) Art embraces appearance knowingly, so truthfulness and life coexist; it is the ascetic ideal's genuine opposite because in it "the lie is sanctified" without pretending to be truth (GM III 25).
- **Unstated premise.** Step 2's standard of "strict" truth: the beliefs are false only against a world of pure becoming (GS 109), asserted, not argued; this is where the fork lies.
- **Strongest objection and Nietzsche's reply.** As in STANCE.
- **The Birth of Tragedy returned without its metaphysics.** GT needed a Primordial Unity that suffers and needs appearance (GT 4); GS 107 keeps the function and drops the metaphysics (n.57: bearable, not justified); "Reason" 6's fourth thesis gives the late formula.
- **Connections.** schopenhauer-and-the-birth-of-tragedy; perspectivism (degrees of seemingness); will-to-truth (BGE 39 and 9[41] govern how much illusion one may use); creating-values (values as honest illusions, one reading); becoming-who-you-are (GS 299); order-of-rank (A 57's holy lie); on-truth-and-lies.

## Where the claim forks (named by content)

(a) The falsification thesis: our basic beliefs really are false, against a world of becoming, and life needs them anyway (GS 110, BGE 4, the notebooks in their plainest sense). (b) The "new language" reading: "false" in BGE 3-4 means only "without a standard beyond man". Each must explain something: (a) against what standard, once the true world is gone; (b) "the falsest opinions" and GS 110's list.

## Pitfalls

- L13 target (documented): "Nietzsche holds a pragmatist theory of truth." Refute with GS 121, GS 265 and the notebook's "error": useful and false, not true because useful.
- Do not say "truth doesn't matter": GS 2, BGE 39, EH Pref 3 govern how much illusion a strong spirit may use.
- BGE 4's German is "noch kein Einwand", weaker than Zimmern; the lesson states this as course.yaml does. GS 110 lists six errors. BGE 57 is not about untruth; use BGE 94.
- Mark every notebook line unpublished; lead with GS 110 and BGE 4. EH's counterview is the second half of Nietzsche's position, not an inconsistency to apologize for. 1872 "justified" and 1882 "bearable" differ (n.57). Zarathustra is illustration. No scholars' names in the body.

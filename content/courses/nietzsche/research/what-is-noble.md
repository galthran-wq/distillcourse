## STANCE (binding)
- **Favorable:** Part IX is Nietzsche's answer to his own question, and it is a psychologist's answer, not a politician's: nobility is found neither in actions nor in works but in a soul's relation to itself (287), it shows in small signs (272: counting one's prerogatives among one's duties; 284: "To have, or not to have, one's emotions, one's For and Against, according to choice"), it is wounded by communicability (268) and by pity (269), it wears masks (270, 278, 289), and it ends in laughter (294) and in a god who loves man enough to want him harder (295). The lesson shows a reader why these forty sections are the most personal statement of the type in the published work, written by the man who told Ecce Homo that the book was "a school for the gentilhomme" (EH III BGE 2, p.310).
- **Hard passages this lesson must quote whole:** BGE 257 (lines 5055-5083), 258 (5084-5109), 259 (5110-5145), as read in order-of-rank and now in sequence; BGE 265 (lines 5463-5500): "egoism belongs to the essence of a noble soul, I mean the unalterable belief that to a being such as 'we,' other beings must naturally be in subjection, and have to sacrifice themselves ... 'It is justice itself'"; "HE KNOWS THAT HE IS ON A HEIGHT"; BGE 262 (5316-5395): the "incurably mediocre" as survivors; BGE 272 (5668): "never to think of lowering our duties to the rank of duties for everybody"; BGE 273 (5673): "means ... obstacle ... resting-place"; BGE 293 (5889): the master's pity against "the cult of suffering"; BGE 295 (5921-5990): Dionysus: "I like man, and often think how I can still further advance him, and make him stronger, more evil, and more profound ... also more beautiful"; BGE 251 is Part VIII and is read in order-of-rank and nietzsche-and-politics, not here.
- **What the words meant:** "Noble" (vornehm): a quality of soul, not a title; 287 says so against "the little word 'von' and the Gotha Almanac" (the phrase is a notebook's, Kaufmann p.305; the published 287 says "not his actions ... neither is it his 'works'"). "Egoism" (265): the noble soul's "unalterable belief" that others are "in subjection" to it as "the primary law of things", with the counterpart that it honours itself in its equals and exchanges rights with them; a description of a type's self-feeling, offered "at the risk of displeasing innocent ears". "Reverence for itself" (Ehrfurcht vor sich, 287): a "fundamental certainty" a soul has about its rank, "not to be sought, not to be found, and perhaps also not to be lost", which the text calls a "BELIEF" (Glaube) using "an old religious formula with a new and deeper meaning". "Ignoble" (268): the communicable, the average, what "words" can carry because they stand for frequent, shared experiences; nobility is what resists translation. "Mask" (270, 278, 289): the protection of deep suffering and of every profound spirit; "every word is also a mask". "Slave" and "master" in 260: two types of valuation found "even within one soul". "Stronger, more evil, and more profound" (295): "evil" in the master's sense of 260, the dangerous and fear-inspiring, not the wicked of slave morality.
- **Objection answered from his side:** Part IX does not argue; it asserts a type's self-feeling and calls it rank. Reply from the text: the Part is built as a tour of signs by which the type "betrays himself" (287's own question), and its method is the psychologist's of 269-270: read the man from the work and not the work from the man, distrust success ("success has always been the greatest liar", 269), read suffering as the maker of distance (270); 287 makes rank a faith on purpose, because actions and works are "always ambiguous", so the demand for an argument is the demand that nobility be provable from outside, which 287 denies in advance. The plain gap sentence: the Part's claim that the noble soul's self-certainty fixes rank is stated as a faith, and nothing in the forty sections could tell a reader who does not share it from one who does; Nietzsche accepts that cost in 287 and the lesson says so.
- **Humanizing reading to test:** the reading that takes Part IX as an ethics of self-cultivation (nobility as inner discipline, "slavery" in 257 as a figure, 265's egoism as self-respect) against the text: 257-259 are the Part's foundation and say slavery, scaffolding and exploitation; 265 says "subjection" and "sacrifice themselves" and calls it "justice itself"; 272 refuses to lower duties "to the rank of duties for everybody"; 293 calls the sufferers' pity "the cult of suffering" and ranks "the master's" pity above it; 295's god wants man "more evil". The self-cultivation reading is right about 284, 287 and 290-292 (good conscience as enjoying one's soul as simple; the philosopher as one who "constantly experiences, sees, hears, suspects, hopes, and dreams extraordinary things") and silent about 257-265; the lesson's section map gives both.
- **Facts against the popular image:** the Part ends not with a conqueror but with a laughing god and the author mocking his own thoughts (296: "some of you, I fear, are ready to become truths"); 269 reads Jesus with pity ("a poor soul insatiated in love, who had to invent hell for those who would not love him"); 270 makes suffering the maker of nobility; 293's "gai saber" is the amulet against the cult of suffering.

## The reconstruction
- **Claim and where.** The Part's title question, "What is noble?", is answered in 287: not actions or works but "some fundamental certainty which a noble soul has about itself"; the Part's foundation (257-259) says what society that soul has needed, and its close (295) says what a god would want from man.
- **Argument in plain steps (how the Part moves).** (1) Every elevation of man has rested on an aristocratic society and the pathos of distance (257-259). (2) Two basic types of valuation have arisen, master and slave, now mixed even within one soul (260-261). (3) The master type's self-feeling is egoism, instinct for rank and reverence (262-265). (4) Nobility is wounded by what makes men communicable, by pity, and by success; suffering and masks protect it (268-270, 276, 278, 284). (5) It shows in signs (272-284) and is fixed by a faith (287). (6) It ends in laughter and in a Dionysus who wants man stronger, more evil, more profound, more beautiful (294-295). The steps are a descent from society to soul to god, not a deduction.
- **Unstated premise.** That a soul's rank can be real without being provable (287), which rests on the module's premise that value is bestowed by a kind of life (creating-values) and on order-of-rank's premise that distance is the condition of elevation.
- **Strongest objection and Nietzsche's reply.** As in the STANCE block; the gap sentence stands.
- **Connections.** master-morality and slave-revolt-and-ressentiment (260-261); consciousness-and-language (268); against-pity (269, 293); perspectivism (289); higher-type (268-270, 273, 276); order-of-rank (257-259, 265); creating-values (260-261, 287); becoming-who-you-are (287's faith against achievement); overman-and-last-man (294); the-nazi-appropriation (what was clipped).

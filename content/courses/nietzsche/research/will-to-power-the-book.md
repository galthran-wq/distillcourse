# Research brief: will-to-power-the-book (The Will to Power as an Editorial Construction)

status: planned
module: reading-protocol
type: core (module application, L16)
goal: Demonstrate from dated notebook fragments in the critical edition that The Will to Power is a compilation, and state what its use as a source does to an argument.

## ARC (L10)

- **Inherits:** corpus-and-protocol's evidence ladder and published-works rule, stated but not yet exercised; the siglum convention; the reader's forward exercise on WP 1067 vs. BGE 36.
- **Contributes:** The rule run on the hardest case — a 'book' that Heidegger, Baeumler and a century of quotation treated as Nietzsche's system — shown from dated fragments to be an editorial object; what quoting it does to an argument (a premise with no author-authorized location); and, in a starred section, the political appropriation the same editorial hands enabled, refuted from a published aphorism and a letter.
- **Hands off:** To genealogy-as-method (the Genealogy read as the authored, sequenced argument the compilation is not); to heidegger-one-thought (Module 7), whose evidence base is exactly this compilation.

Spine step: "The method: a corpus of numbered sections, ordered in time, read published-works-first, by a stated reconstruction protocol — and the naturalist reading fixed as the hypothesis to test; the compiled book that is not Nietzsche's shows why the evidence rules matter."

**APPLICATION spec (L16).** Artefact: the Digital Critical Edition (eKGWB) at nietzschesource.org, in which every Nachlass fragment sits in notebook order with a siglum, set against the 1906 Will to Power's thematic arrangement. Module results used: the corpus chronology (which notebook belongs to which book's period), the published-works rule, the protocol's step 4 (second-passage check). Result reproduced: Kaufmann's (1964 article; Appendix) and Montinari's demonstration that the compilation is an editorial construction — shown here from the fragments themselves (NF-1885,2; NF-1888,14) rather than asserted. Departure from the idealisation: the rule says 'published works ground'; the deployed scholarship of 1901–1960 (Heidegger's four volumes included) grounded on the compilation, and part of what the course must later evaluate (Module 7) inherits that base.

## Key points the lesson must cover

1. **What the artefact is.** The eKGWB prints ~6,600 corrections from the KGW commentary volumes inline; every fragment is numbered notebook[fragment] in the physical order of writing; no thematic arrangement exists. The tooltip capture shows one correction in place; the point is that the edition records what the print editions got wrong, sentence by sentence.
2. **'Der Wille zur Macht' as a title, never a book.** NF-1885,2: the heading over an outline about the origin of value-tables, with 'Leben ist Wille zur Macht' two sentences after 'was sind unsere Werthschätzungen… selber werth?' — the Genealogy and the will-to-power idea cohabiting one undated page two years before GM. NF-1888,14: four incompatible tables of contents under the same title in one year. Krell's count of ~140 plans. Nietzsche's letters of 1888 abandoning the four-book plan for the Antichrist as 'the whole Revaluation' (BVN-1888; Hollingdale's translator's note in research/texts/twilightof-.txt).
3. **How the compilation was made.** One of the discarded outlines used as skeleton; fragments assigned to its four books by theme; 483 (1901) then 1,067 (1906). Kaufmann's Appendix and Heidegger Vol. I §2 agree on the facts and disagree on what to do with them: Heidegger declares the compilation his text; Clark's rule excludes it as ground.
4. **What quoting it does to an argument.** WP 1067's 'this world is will to power — and nothing besides' has no published equivalent in the indicative; BGE 36 says 'suppose'. A premise drawn from WP is a premise with no author-authorized location: it may date from 1885 or 1888, may be a draft Nietzsche rejected (he rejected most of what he drafted), and its neighbours in the compilation were chosen by an editor. Worked: the protocol's step 4 run on WP 1067 → BGE 36 (the reader's forward exercise, now completed).
5. **The philology of one word (A §29).** 'Idiot' deleted until 1954; a critic's inference from the gap to Nietzsche's incipient madness; Kaufmann's tracing to Dostoevsky's Prince Myshkin. A compact case of how an editorial deletion manufactures an interpretation.
6. **★ The appropriation and its refutation (starred).** The Archiv's Nietzsche and the 1930s reading; Baeumler's edition; Heidegger's Vol. I §4 refutation of Baeumler on other grounds. Refutation from the texts the reader can check: BGE 251 (against antisemitism, for the 'good European'), GM I:16's 'Rome against Judea' as a symbol of two valuations, not a racial programme; the Fritsch letter; Z I 'On the New Idol' (the state as the coldest monster). Bounded: the refutation is textual and does not make Nietzsche a liberal (BGE 257–259 stay on the table for higher-type-and-sublimation).

## Set pieces and worked examples to recreate

- **The four 1888 tables of contents side by side** (NF-1888,14) — four columns under one title, no two alike.
- **NF-1885,2[190] read as a page**: the question about value-tables, then 'meine Formel dafür lautet: Leben ist Wille zur Macht' — the fragment that shows GM's question and the WP idea born together.
- **WP 1067 vs. BGE 36** as the worked protocol run (indicative vs. conditional).
- **The 'idiot' case** as a three-step story (deletion → inference → source).
- **Krell's catalogue** (~140 plans) as a single number with its frame: more outlines than months.
- **Two readers, one fragment**: WM 617 as Baeumler quotes it (first sentence only) beside the full note with the 'twofold falsification' clause (Heidegger III p. 213) — the compilation's harm demonstrated on a single citation, not asserted.
- **The sister's forgery with content** (Kaufmann App. VI): five dots of ellipsis in the letter to the Kaiser replaced with 'The will to power as a principle would surely make sense to him' — the one documented case where editing changed a philosophical claim, and Kaufmann's argument that no serious reader was fooled.
- **Kaufmann's three deletions table** (App. III): what was cut from The Antichrist's first edition, when restored, and why none is sinister — against the 'suppressed manuscripts' legend in the other direction.
- **Zucht und Züchtung** (Ch. 10): a title written once in a notebook, picked posthumously for Book IV — the smallest unit of the compilation's editorial politics.

## Pitfalls / subtleties to flag

- The eKGWB is German-only; every quoted fragment needs the course's own translation beside the German, siglum cited.
- Do not overclaim: the compilation's fragments are Nietzsche's words; what is editorial is the selection, the order and the title. The lesson says both.
- Heidegger's reliance on WP is a fact the course uses in Module 7; state it here without evaluating the reading.
- The starred section must stay dry: no 'Nietzsche would have been horrified' speculation. Quote BGE 251 and the letter; let them carry it.
- The sick-Nietzsche photograph is optional and could read as sensational; if used, its only caption is the date and the fact that the Archiv commissioned it. The human decides.
- Do not let this become a history of the Archiv; the Archiv appears only as the agent of the compilation.

## EXERCISE PLAN (floor: ≤1 warm-up; ≥2 of boundary / refute / derivation / research; one FORWARD)

1. (warm-up) State the three facts that make The Will to Power an editorial object (selection, order, title) and the one that does not (the words are Nietzsche's).
2. (research-shaped) On nietzschesource.org, open NF-1888,14 and count the distinct table-of-contents drafts headed 'Der Wille zur Macht'; then find the 1888 letter in BVN-1888 where the four-book plan is dropped. Report the fragment numbers and the letter's date.
3. (refute) "Heidegger's reading can be dismissed because it rests on a forged book." Break the claim: the fragments are authentic; state precisely what the compilation's editorial status licenses one to reject (its arrangement as an argument) and what it does not (any individual fragment as a dated thought).
4. (derivation) Take WP 1067 and write the published-works reconstruction of the same thesis from BGE 36 alone, preserving its conditional mood; then list what a reader of WP 1067 would take to be asserted that BGE 36 does not assert.
5. (boundary probe) Suppose a fragment exists in the Nachlass with no published counterpart and a date after the last published book (late 1888). Under the published-works rule, what may it be cited for? Give the answer for NF-1888,25 and explain why the rule's 'corroborate, never ground' handles the case.
6. (sourcing / translation) Translate the Fritsch letter's two sentences and BGE 251's sentence on antisemites; state what each can and cannot establish about the appropriation (a letter to a publisher vs. a published aphorism), following the records / argues / omits pass.
7. (FORWARD → genealogy-as-method) NF-1885,2[190] asks 'what are our value-tables themselves worth?' two years before the Genealogy's Preface asks the same. Reconstruct the fragment's question as a premise and predict what evidence a 'real history' would need to answer it; the solution says the Preface §§3–6 states that demand and genealogy-as-method reads it.

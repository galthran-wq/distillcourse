# Research brief: will-to-truth (Science as the Ascetic Ideal's Latest Form: The Will to Truth)

status: planned
module: third-treatise
type: core
goal: Formulate GM III:23–27 with GS 344 and BGE 1–4 as the argument that science and modern atheism still rest on an unconditional faith in truth, and state what the argument does to the Genealogy's own method.

## ARC (L10)

- **Inherits:** The priest's mechanism and III:28's conclusion; perspectivism's redefinition of objectivity; from madman-argument the unexplained cause of the death of God; from ascetic-ideal-across-types the trap Clark found in the treatise's opening.
- **Contributes:** The Third Treatise's last move reconstructed: science is not the ascetic ideal's antagonist but its 'latest and noblest form' because it rests on the unconditional value of truth (III:24), which is a faith (GS 344) — Plato's and Christianity's ('God is the truth'); Christianity destroyed its own dogma by its own truthfulness (III:27) — the mechanism madman-argument lacked; BGE 1–4's 'the value of truth is a problem'. The reflexive result: the Genealogy is written by the will to truth it has just genealogized. Clark Ch. 6: what this proves (a counter-ideal is needed) and does not (that truth or argument must be abandoned).
- **Hands off:** To antichrist-applied-genealogy (the machinery of all three treatises run on a case); to nihilism-and-affirmation (the counter-ideal); to life-as-literature and the audit (the reflexive charge as the self-refutation problem).

Spine step: "Third Treatise: the ascetic ideal is what a declining life does with suffering — the priest redirects 'someone is to blame' onto the sufferer — and modern science is its latest form because it rests on an unconditional faith in truth; the critique reaches Nietzsche's own will to truth, and The Antichrist runs the machinery on Jesus and Paul."

## Key points the lesson must cover

1. **III:23: the expected antagonist.** 'Science today has absolutely no faith in itself'; where it is at its best it is the ideal's 'own most recent and noblest form' — the reversal of the reader's expectation set up since III:1 (the trap sprung: Clark Ch. 6 §2 → §4).
2. **GS 344 reconstructed as the argument III:24 cites.** (P) Science admits no convictions, only hypotheses under 'police supervision'. (P) But the rule 'no convictions' is itself a conviction — that truth is worth more than anything, that nothing is more needful. (P) That conviction cannot come from utility: truth and untruth have both been useful throughout. (P) So 'I will not deceive, not even myself' is a moral ground — and 'we stand on moral ground'. (P) The morality that forbids deception affirms 'another world than that of life, nature and history' — the one in which truth is divine. (C) Our faith in science is still a metaphysical faith, 'the Christian faith which was also Plato's, that God is the truth'. Walk every step; the passage is one of the corpus's few explicit chains.
3. **III:24: the demand.** The will to truth 'requires a critique' — 'the value of truth must for once be experimentally called into question'; the 'free spirits' who deny the ideal in metaphysics still affirm it in truth; 'the unconditional will to truth' is the ideal's core. Reconstruct: (P) the ideal's core is the valuation of one thing unconditionally; (P) science values truth unconditionally; (C) science is an expression of the ideal — its critics included.
4. **III:27: the mechanism of the death of God.** 'All great things bring about their own destruction through an act of self-overcoming'; Christianity as dogma perished of its own morality; Christian truthfulness, 'refined confessional subtlety', drew the conclusion against the Christian God; now the same truthfulness must ask 'what is the meaning of all will to truth?' — 'morality will gradually perish now'. This closes the loop opened in madman-argument: the missing cause was truthfulness bred by the religion it destroyed. State it as the completed argument.
5. **BGE 1–4: the value of truth as a problem.** The Sphinx and Oedipus; 'why not rather untruth?'; the metaphysicians' faith in opposite values; 'the falseness of a judgment is not an objection'; untruth as a condition of life. Reconstruct BGE 4 and mark its premise (life-value is the standard) as the one the naturalist reading needs and the one Clark's reading must bound (it does not say all judgments are false).
6. **What the reflexive result proves and does not (Clark Ch. 6 §§4–5).** Proves: the Genealogy is written under the will to truth; Nietzsche says so (III:24 'we knowers'); its own valuation of truth needs a counter-ideal to stand on. Does not prove: that truth-claims must be abandoned — the critique of the value of truth is not a denial of truth (perspectivism's result carried over), and a genealogy of the will to truth is itself a truth-claim Nietzsche means. The rival reading (Clark & Dudrick: keep both wills in 'magnificent tension') stated in one paragraph. The lesson ends with the demand for a counter-ideal — 'a will to nothingness — but it is and remains a will' — as the problem Module 6 takes up.

## Pitfalls / subtleties to flag

- The reflexive result is the course's most misquoted move; the lesson must state precisely what is turned on what (the value of truth, not truth), or the reader arrives in Module 7 with 'Nietzsche refutes himself' as a settled result.
- GS 344 is German only on disk; translate carefully — 'Wille zur Wahrheit', 'Ueberzeugung', 'Glaube', 'Polizei des Misstrauens'.
- III:27's 'morality will perish' is a prediction, dated (the next two centuries); state it as such.
- Do not preview Module 6's counter-ideal beyond III:28's 'a will' and Clark's 'counter-ideal needed'.
- BGE 4's 'life-value as standard' is where the is–ought question lives; name it, do not solve it (value-creation-and-normativity).

## EXERCISE PLAN (floor: ≤1 warm-up; ≥2 of boundary / refute / derivation / research; one FORWARD)

1. (warm-up) Write GS 344's chain in six premises and a conclusion, each with its German anchor phrase.
2. (refute) "Nietzsche's critique of the will to truth refutes itself: if truth has no value, his claim has none either." Break it: distinguish 'truth has no unconditional value' from 'no claim is true'; cite III:24's 'we knowers' and BGE 22's last sentence; state what the reflexive charge does establish (the need for a counter-ideal to ground the valuation) and what it does not.
3. (derivation) III:27 states a 'law of life' (self-overcoming) and applies it to Christianity. Derive its next application as Nietzsche states it — morality perishing by the same law — as a three-premise argument, and state what the argument predicts about the Genealogy's own fate (the will to truth questioning itself) and whether that is a refutation or an instance.
4. (boundary probe) Suppose a science that held truth valuable only conditionally (as useful). On GS 344's account, could it exist? State which premise of §344 rules it out (utility cannot ground the unconditional rule, and a conditional rule would admit useful falsehoods) and whether a 'pragmatic science' would be inside or outside the ascetic ideal.
5. (research-shaped, corroboration) Compare GS 357's sentence on Christian morality 'translated into scientific conscience' with III:27 as two statements of one mechanism; report the differences (what each names as the agent) and which one madman-argument's reconstruction needed.
6. (translation comparison) 'Wahrhaftigkeit' (III:27), 'Redlichkeit' (BGE 227), 'Wille zur Wahrheit': compare Samuel's and Norman's renderings ('truthfulness', 'honesty', 'probity'); state whether the mechanism of III:27 reads as a virtue turning on its origin or as a habit, and why Clark's reading needs the first.
7. (FORWARD → antichrist-applied-genealogy) III:27 says Christianity perished of its own morality. The Antichrist §§39–47 tell the reverse story for its origin: Paul turning a practice into a doctrine. Write the two claims as premises of one history (a religion made by the priest's redirection, unmade by the truthfulness it bred) and mark what evidence each would need; the solution says the next lesson reads Nietzsche's own application and grades its evidence.

# Research brief: barres-and-maurras-integral-nationalism (Barrès and Maurras: the earth and the dead, and integral nationalism)

status: planned
module: crowd-race-nation
type: core
goal: Analyze Barrès's nationalism as acceptance of a determinism and Maurras's integral nationalism with its 'politics first' and hereditary monarchy, and evaluate them as answers to Renan

## ARC (L10)

- **Inherits:** From `renan-what-is-a-nation` (the prerequisite): `daily-plebiscite` — a
  nation is "a soul, a spiritual principle" of two parts, a legacy of memories and present
  consent — and `shared-forgetting`; the objection Renan himself wrote in 1869 (a nation
  "cannot depend on a vote … it is an idea, superior to particular wills"); the two-domain
  reading of his racial hierarchy. From `burke-reflections`: `prejudice` (inherited
  judgment whose reasons are latent) and the partnership of generations. From
  `maistre-and-bonald`: `no-such-thing-as-man` and `counter-revolution` (and, from that
  brief's objection section, the genealogy that runs from Maistre to Maurras's Action
  française — recalled in one clause). From `taine-and-le-bon-revolution-and-crowd`:
  `esprit-classique` (reasoning about abstract man) — Barrès's "intellectual" is this type
  renamed — and `crowd-psychology`. From `chamberlain-and-grant` and
  `gobineau-inequality-of-races`: `racial-theory-of-history`, the position both men will
  say they do NOT hold and repeatedly use.
- **Contributes:** The one move: two answers to Renan from the same camp, which differ in
  kind. Barrès keeps Renan's formula and changes one word — the nation is "the common
  possession of an ancient cemetery and the will to continue to make that undivided
  heritage count" — so that the will becomes the ACCEPTANCE of what the dead and the soil
  have already made of a man: "Nationalism is the acceptance of a determinism"
  (`la-terre-et-les-morts`). Maurras turns the same acceptance into a method and a regime:
  if the nation is the highest political good and is a natural fact one is born into, then
  every question is to be decided by what serves it ("politics first" — first in time, not
  in dignity: `politique-d-abord`), and the regime that serves it, by a chain he calls a
  theorem, is a hereditary, anti-parliamentary monarchy (`integral-nationalism`).
- **Hands off:** (i) The test: both doctrines were made in and for the Dreyfus Affair, and
  the course.yaml notes hold the Dreyfus material for `dreyfus-affair-as-test` — this
  lesson states the doctrines and their antisemitic component as doctrine, and leaves the
  case (Barrès at Rennes, Déroulède's "France is innocent", treason judged by consequences,
  the forged document) to the application. Barrès's definitions of treason and of "the
  intellectual" are taught here only so far as the doctrine needs them. (ii) Forward:
  Maurras's later course — the "divine surprise" of 1940, Vichy, the 1945 conviction — is
  stated as fact in one paragraph and carried into `the-right-after-1945`. (iii) The
  organized minority that integral nationalism presupposes (who decides what "serves the
  nation"?) is the question `mosca-ruling-class` asks in general form. (iv) Later echoes,
  Ref only: `schmitt-concept-of-the-political` (friend and enemy; Maurras's wolf-and-god
  passage), `hazony-virtue-of-nationalism`, `de-benoist-european-new-right`.
- **Spine step:** "The late nineteenth century looks beneath institutions for what moves
  peoples: the crowd (Taine, Le Bon), the race (Gobineau, Chamberlain, Grant), the nation as
  will or as inheritance (Renan, Barrès, Maurras) — and the Dreyfus Affair puts them to the
  test"

## EXPLANATION PLAN (L0)

Opening (course.yaml, verbatim): "Renan said a nation is a daily choice. A French novelist
who had made his name with a cult of the self answers that the self is an illusion: a man
thinks with his dead and lives from his soil, and his nation is not a choice but a
formation he can only accept. A younger writer from Provence goes further — if the nation
is the highest political good, then its government should be chosen by what serves it, and
the answer is a king. The lesson follows both arguments and the case each made against the
Republic."

Anchors (L11): Maurice Barrès (1862–1923), born in Lorraine (the part left to France in
1871; Metz, an hour away, was German), novelist, deputy for Nancy (1889) on the Boulangist
ticket, coined the political sense of "nationalisme" in 1892 (Note 136–138 of the book —
verify the Figaro date); Scènes et doctrines (1902) collects his campaign pieces of
1892–1901. Charles Maurras (1868–1952), from Martigues in Provence, deaf from adolescence,
poet and critic turned journalist; from 1899 the doctrinal head of Action française, a
movement and (from 1908) a daily newspaper; Mes idées politiques (1937) is an anthology of
his writings arranged by a disciple, with a new Preface. The Third Republic (the French
regime since 1870–75: an elected Chamber and Senate, a weak president, ministries that
fell every few months — the "Republic" both men attack). Boulanger (a war minister whose
1886–89 movement nearly toppled the parliamentary republic; it collapsed when he fled
abroad in 1889). Déroulède (poet of the 1870 defeat, head of the Ligue des patriotes —
needed only for §30).

The newcomer's problem: Renan (Ref) made the nation a choice renewed daily. If that is
true, a Frenchman may un-choose France, a province may secede, and a naturalised man is as
French as a Lorrainer whose family has been there for centuries. Barrès and Maurras say all
three conclusions are false, and must say what a nation is instead — without falling back
on race, which both deny.

1. **Can the self choose at all?** Barrès's own route, which is the argument: he began
   with the "cult of the self" (Un Homme libre, 1889 — anchor in a clause), the effort to
   make the self by will. Analysed to the bottom, the self turns out to be made by others:
   "There are no personal ideas; even the rarest ideas … are general ways of feeling and
   are found in all beings of the same organism besieged by the same images" (§5 l.494);
   "There is not even freedom of thought. I can only live according to my dead. They and my
   soil command a certain activity in me" (l.415). The obvious attempt (Renan's, and the
   liberal's): reason is independent and can judge one's inheritance. Barrès's answer:
   that is the illusion of "masters … the Taines, the Renans [who] believed in an
   independent reason in each of us" (l.494) — he names them as the faith he gave up.
   Seam: this is Burke's prejudice (Ref) without Burke's confidence that the reasons are
   still there to be found; and Maistre's "I have never met man" (Ref) turned inward — I
   have never met my own independent reason either.
2. **Then what is a nation?** Barrès's definition: "A nationalist is a Frenchman who has
   become conscious of his formation. Nationalism is the acceptance of a determinism" (§2
   l.321). The patrie is "la Terre et les Morts" — the soil and the dead (l.301). Show the
   Antigone passage (§3 l.352–360): Creon, "a master come from abroad", applies the laws
   "with his intelligence — intelligence, what a small thing at the surface of ourselves";
   Antigone obeys "her deep heredity". The newcomer must see that "determinism" here is not
   physics but inheritance: what a man is was settled by his ancestors' lives and the
   place they worked, and freedom consists in knowing and accepting it. Then the side-by-side
   (Figure 1): Renan's "the common possession of a rich legacy of memories … the will to
   continue to make the most of the heritage received undivided" against Barrès's "the
   common possession of an ancient cemetery and the will to continue to make that undivided
   heritage count" (§19e l.2325). One word changes: memories become a cemetery; and with
   it the will, which in Renan could say no, becomes the will to continue. Say the
   expectation and break it: from Renan's critic one expects a rival definition; Barrès
   keeps Renan's sentence and changes its object.
3. **Is this race by another name?** The obvious suspicion — and the reader has just come
   from Gobineau and Chamberlain (Ref). Barrès denies it in so many words: "Let us say it
   once and for all: it is inaccurate to speak in the strict sense of a French race. We are
   not a race but a nation; it continues every day to make itself" (§6 l.538). His image is
   geological, not biological: the conglomerate, stones of many origins bound by a mortar
   made from their own wear (l.538–560). And his best institutional definition: "a
   national conscience … is the agreement of people who have been united for several
   generations in the same social institutions to affirm common moral interests" (ch. IV
   §24a l.4326). Then the record, at full length, with no comment beyond the reading: the
   same lecture that defines the patrie by soil and dead says of the naturalised foreigner
   that "the blood persists in following the order of nature against oaths, against laws" —
   he is "our guest", owed "security" but not "a place in the powers of the country" (§18e
   l.1940); the Programme de Nancy would give the naturalised private rights only
   (Appendix 41). The reader is asked to state where the conglomerate's "mortar" stops and
   "blood" begins — the line the Dreyfus lesson will test. Do not resolve it for them.
4. **What follows for politics? — "by relation to France".** Barrès's method: "Nationalism
   is to resolve each question by relation to France" (§18e l.1766); "They [the
   'intellectuals'] judge everything by the abstract. We shall judge each thing by relation
   to France" (§18d). The intellectual defined as the man "who persuades himself that
   society must be founded on logic and fails to see that it rests in fact on necessities
   anterior and perhaps foreign to individual reason" (§13 l.1020) — this is Taine's
   classical spirit (Ref `taine-and-le-bon-revolution-and-crowd`) given a new name and a
   new enemy. And the principle that will matter next lesson, stated abstractly: treason is
   "measured socially not by the intentions of its author but by the consequences of the
   act" (§7 l.626). Hand the reader the question: what does a judge do with an individual's
   innocence if each question is resolved by relation to France? (Answer held for
   `dreyfus-affair-as-test`.)
5. **Maurras: society before the individual.** Problem: Barrès gives a feeling and a
   method; he does not say what regime follows. Maurras starts lower, with a scene (Preface
   I pp. 15–17): the chick breaks its shell and runs; the human newborn "lacks everything",
   must be "drawn from its mother, washed, wrapped, fed" before it has a will; the group it
   enters has "no pact possible, nothing that resembles a contract"; its first law is
   "inequality without measure and necessity without reserve" — "protective inequality".
   The obvious attempt it answers: Rousseau's and the Revolution's contract (Ref
   `rousseau-inequality-and-general-will`). Maurras's distinction (Preface II): ASSOCIATION
   (natural, from need and from the complementarity of unequals) comes first; CONTRACT is a
   later device added to secure an association's duration — "One must associate to live; to
   live well one must contract". And the consequence for the nation (Part VII): "one does
   not choose one's fatherland … any more than one chooses father and mother" (p. 262);
   "one enters a party, but one is born in a nation" (p. 267); "forty million living, but a
   thousand million dead — there is the true cement" (p. 260). Seam: Renan's two halves
   come back with their order reversed — the dead first, and the living as their debtors.
6. **Politics first.** Problem: if the nation is a natural fact, why does it need politics
   at all, and why first? Maurras's maxim, defined exactly as he defines it (p. 139): "When
   we say 'politics first', we mean politics the first, first in the order of time, in no
   way in the order of dignity" — the bow and arrow before the target. The obvious
   misreading (that Maurras ranks politics above religion or morality) is refuted by his
   own sentence; the real claim is causal: no economic, moral or religious reform lasts
   unless the political order that carries it is set right first. Then what "set right"
   means: the "common denominator" of every question is "the interest of the Nation" (p.
   284); the Roman temple to the goddess Roma built on the Acropolis — "to the most perfect
   beauty, to the most sacred right, Rome knew how to prefer the safety of Rome" — as
   Maurras's image of the virtue he wants: "an equal religion of the goddess France".
7. **The theorem: from nationalism to the king.** The chain as Maurras states it (pp.
   283–285), rebuilt as a `<FigureSequence>` (Figure 3): (a) the patriot wants French
   strength united against foreign rivals; (b) the Republic, by its own principles, lives on
   the conflict of parties ("where opinion governs, no one governs"; "if Number governs,
   Money will govern behind it"); (c) so the patriot who stays republican "is republican
   without admitting the consequences of the Republic"; (d) the central power must
   therefore not depend on election or party: it must be hereditary, independent ("absolute"
   in the sense of not answerable to parties, not despotic: old France "bristling with
   liberties"), and decentralised below; (e) crises need a dictator, but a dictator needs a
   counterweight in the public interest — the king as the hereditary dictator whose
   interest is the dynasty's survival and therefore the nation's (p. 282); (f) hence "the
   necessity of monarchy is demonstrated like a theorem … if you have resolved to be a
   patriot, you will be a monarchist of necessity" — and royalism "called itself integral
   nationalism" because it alone satisfies every postulate of nationalism. Close with his
   own modest version: monarchy as "the lesser evil, the possibility of the good" (p. 286),
   and "The republic is a religion. The monarchy is a family" (p. 289).
8. **The doctrine's enemies within.** Problem: if France is a natural society, what
   explains a France divided against itself? Maurras's answer is part of the doctrine and
   is taught as such, quoted at the length needed: nationalism defends the moral
   inheritance "against the foreigner of the interior" (pp. 271–272); the "four confederate
   states — Jewish, Protestant, Masonic, métèque" (p. 221, with his footnote dating the
   phrase to the Dreyfus quarrel of September 1898); the great modern crises as "oriental",
   "Jewish in personnel" (Preface V pp. 50–51). State it as the record: the course.yaml
   key_idea is "the antisemitic thesis in Maurras as part of the doctrine". Then the facts
   of its later consequences, one paragraph, without adjectives: Action française's
   campaigns of the 1930s (the riots of February 1934; the call against Léon Blum); the fall
   of France in 1940 welcomed by Maurras as a "divine surprise"; support for Pétain's Vichy
   regime, which passed statutes excluding Jews from public office (1940); his conviction
   in January 1945 for intelligence with the enemy, a life sentence, release on health
   grounds in 1952. Facts from the dossier's survey (Fawcett pp. 262–263) — verify dates
   and the wording of the charge against a standard history; Ref forward to
   `the-right-after-1945`.
9. **The OBJECTIONS at full strength, and where the lesson stands.** Each from where it
   comes:
   (a) **From Renan's side (liberal):** acceptance is itself a choice. Barrès chose
       nationalism after a "cult of the self" — his own biography is a counterexample to
       "there are no personal ideas"; and if the dead decide, Alsatians born German after
       1871 should accept Germany, which Barrès refuses (Livre III §34 l.6934: the annexed must
       "remain a pebble of France under the invader's boot" — "Demeurez un caillou de
       France sous la botte de l'envahisseur"). The determinism is applied
       selectively: to the Alsatian's Frenchness, not to his German schooling.
   (b) **From the conservative side (Burke, Oakeshott later):** Maurras's restoration is a
       revolution — a regime designed by deduction ("a theorem") against a republic with a
       century of roots; the empiricist who mocked the tabula rasa ("a programme of
       amnesia") proposes to sweep away the institutions France actually inherited. The
       survey literature states this objection (Fawcett, References); the course states it
       as its own, from Maurras's text: his "organising empiricism" (Part IV) vs the
       theorem (p. 285).
   (c) **From within the camp:** Barrès stayed a republican deputy and never accepted the
       king; the "theorem" did not compel its most famous reader. Barrès's own reply is in §19f ("Les dîners de l'Appel au
       soldat", l.2402ff.), l.2697: he praises Maurras's Enquête sur la monarchie as
       unequalled political reasoning and then says "Je ne suis pas monarchiste" — "we
       meet not on the idea of monarchy" but on the appeal to the soldier.
   (d) **From the record:** "politics first" and "the interest of the Nation" as the only
       denominator leave no standpoint from which a court can say the nation's interest
       was served by a lie — the Dreyfus lesson will put this to the test with a forged
       document Maurras defended. State the objection here in abstract form only.
   Where the lesson concedes: the newborn argument is strong against a contract theory of
   ORIGINS — no one consents to being born into a family or a language, and Renan's own
   definition concedes the legacy is received; Barrès and Maurras show that consent cannot
   be the whole story. What their argument does not show, and the lesson says so plainly,
   is the step from "received, not chosen" to "therefore a hereditary king" or "therefore
   the naturalised hold private rights only" — those steps need further premises (about
   blood, about parties, about who embodies the nation), and the reader is asked to name
   them (Exercises 3, 5).

Headings as claims (suggested): "The self is made by its dead"; "Renan's sentence with a
cemetery in it"; "Not a race, but a nation — and then blood"; "Every question by relation
to France"; "Society begins with a newborn, not a contract"; "Politics first in time, not
in dignity"; "From patriotism to the king, as a theorem"; "The foreigner of the interior";
"What acceptance cannot prove".

## Set pieces and worked examples to recreate

1. **Renan's formula and Barrès's rewrite** (Renan l.101; Barrès §19e l.2325): typeset as a
   two-line `<Quote>` pair in French with the course's English, the one changed phrase in
   amber. The single most efficient way to show "an answer to Renan".
2. **Antigone and Creon** (§3 l.352–360): Barrès's reading of Sophocles — the foreign
   master who "judges with his intelligence" vs the girl who draws on "her deep heredity" —
   with one clause recalling what happens in the play (Antigone buries her brother against
   Creon's decree and dies for it).
3. **The conglomerate** (§6 l.538–560): the pudding-stone image read as an argument against
   both race (stones of many origins) and contract (the mortar is made by wear, over time,
   not by agreement); the stone that comes loose "rolls fast, wears down, becomes dust" as
   the fate of the déraciné (define: the uprooted man, Barrès's word and a novel's title).
4. **The Chambière cemetery at Metz** (§18e l.1858–1902): 7,200 French dead of 1870 among
   German graves, the German inscription "God was with us!" and Prussian bugles from the
   drill ground — Barrès's scene of what "the earth and the dead" means; then the
   naturalisation sentence four pages later (l.1940). Scene, then consequence.
5. **The newborn** (Maurras, Preface I pp. 15–17): the chick and the infant; worked as an
   `<Example>`: what the infant receives, from whom, on what terms, and why no contract is
   possible ("for these moral agreements two are needed; the morality of one of them does
   not yet exist", l.198).
6. **"Politique d'abord" defined** (p. 139): the definition quoted whole, with the bow and
   arrow, and the misreading it rules out.
7. **The goddess Roma on the Acropolis** (p. 284): Maurras first took the Roman temple
   beside the Parthenon for a profanation, then for "a sublime audacity" — the image of
   preferring the city's safety "to the most perfect beauty, to the most sacred right".
8. **The theorem** (pp. 283–285): the chain rebuilt step by step (Figure 3), with the
   sentence "if you have resolved to be a patriot, you will be a monarchist of necessity".

## Pitfalls / subtleties to flag

- **Terms (L11), and where:** déraciné/uprooted (set piece 3); "la terre et les morts"
  (link 2: keep the French as the name, gloss "the soil and the dead"); determinism (link
  2: inheritance, not physics); Third Republic (anchors); Boulanger and Boulangism
  (anchors, one clause); "intellectuel" (link 4: the word was new in 1898 — Barrès credits
  Clemenceau's L'Aurore with the coinage, §14 l.1037 — leave the story for the Dreyfus
  lesson); Action française (anchors: founded 1898–99 by Vaugeois and Pujo, Maurras its
  doctrinal head — verify; the dossier's survey says 1898, Barrès §19b gives its
  declaration of 15 November 1899); positivism and Comte (link 5 or 7, one clause);
  métèque (link 8: the Athenian metic, a resident foreigner without citizenship — Maurras
  revived the word as an insult; say only what it meant and that he used it of
  naturalised and resident foreigners); "pays légal / pays réel" (link 7 or 8: the legal
  country of parties and institutions vs the real country of families, trades and
  provinces; the Spanish text has "país legal/real", Preface VI p. 54); Vichy, Pétain,
  February 1934 (link 8). Three new terms per section at most — link 8 is the risk.
- **Translation:** Barrès is French, Maurras is in Spanish. The lesson gives English and
  the French beside key phrases: "acceptation d'un déterminisme", "la Terre et les Morts",
  "Il n'y a pas d'idées personnelles", "Nous ne sommes point une race, mais une nation",
  "par rapport à la France", "politique d'abord", "nationalisme intégral", "inégalité
  protectrice", "on ne choisit pas sa patrie", "l'étranger de l'intérieur". Where the
  French of a Maurras sentence is not verified, the lesson renders the Spanish and marks it
  in References ("translated from the Spanish edition of 1962"). The "billón de muertos"
  trap (Spanish billón = 10¹²; Maurras wrote "milliard", 10⁹) must not reach the page as
  "a billion" without the gloss.
- **Copyright:** Barrès public domain (d. 1923); Maurras's French text public domain in
  France since 2023 (verify); Irazusta's translation in copyright — short quotations only,
  and the course's own English.
- **Real misreadings (L13):** (1) "Politique d'abord means politics matters more than
  religion or morality" — refuted by Maurras's own definition (first in time, not in
  dignity). (2) "Barrès's nationalism is racial" — he denies race in §6 and his conglomerate
  is anti-racial; the record of §18e and of the Rennes chapter shows blood returning. The
  lesson presents both texts and lets the reader see the tension; it does not settle it as
  "really racial" or "really not". One slot each, no strawmen beyond these.
- **Do not teach the Affair here.** The course.yaml notes hold it for the application.
  Name it as the occasion (both books were made in it: Barrès's subtitle-material and
  Maurras's "four states" footnote date from 1898–99) and stop; no bordereau, no Rennes, no
  "France is innocent".
- **Register:** The course passes no verdict of its own on any author or idea: no
  condemning or approving labels in its own voice. Judgement comes only through argument
  and evidence; a label may appear only inside a quotation, with who applied it and when.
  The antisemitic passages are quoted as doctrine, with their date and context, at the
  length the argument needs; no adjective in the course's voice either way. Where to class
  the two men among the later movements of the right is a scholars' debate; if mentioned,
  it is one sentence naming it as a debate, the scholars in References.
- **Do not re-teach** Renan's elimination, Burke's prejudice, Maistre's "no such thing as
  man", Taine's classical spirit, Le Bon's crowd — each a clause and a `<Ref>`.
- **Numbers (L12):** keepers: 1882 (Renan), 1899 (Barrès's "La Terre et les Morts"
  lecture; Maurras's "Dictator and King"), 1937 (the anthology), and Maurras's "forty
  million living, a thousand million dead". The 7,200 dead at Chambière only inside the
  scene.
- **Dossier thin:** Barrès's novels and Maurras's Enquête sur la monarchie are not in the
  dossier; the Maurras text is a translation of a late anthology (extracts of different
  dates arranged by a disciple), so a quoted sentence's original date is often unknown —
  say "in the 1937 anthology" unless the date is printed (the dictatorship piece: 1899).

## EXERCISE PLAN (E6/E7 + playbook-history mix)

1. **Retrieval and chronology (E3, warm-up):** place in order and date to the decade:
   Fichte's Addresses, Gobineau's Essay, Renan's lecture, Barrès's "La Terre et les Morts"
   lecture, Maurras's "Dictator and King", Mes idées politiques; then state in one line
   what each says a nation is.
2. **Document reasoning (sourcing drill):** Barrès §19e (l.2325). Metadata: Barrès,
   reporting his own inaugural talk to the "Association nationaliste de la Jeunesse" at the
   café Voltaire in the Latin Quarter, c. 1900 (the section follows a letter of February
   1900), months after the Rennes verdict. Predict how he will use Renan; read; then: what does the passage record
   (the formula and its audience), argue, omit (Renan's "present consent")? What can it be
   used to establish about Barrès's debt to Renan, and what can it not?
3. **Claim to refute (E6b):** "Barrès's nationalism is simply a racial theory like
   Gobineau's." Refute with §6 (not a race; the conglomerate) and §24a (the institutional
   definition), then state what §18e's naturalisation sentence adds that the refutation must
   still account for. The model answer argues both sides with the texts and names the
   premise on which the reader's verdict turns.
4. **Boundary probe (E6a):** apply "la terre et les morts" to a child born in Metz in 1880
   to French parents who stayed after the annexation and sent him to German schools. Whose
   dead, which soil? Show that the formula gives two answers and that Barrès needs an extra
   premise (the dead count more than the schooling; Livre III's "remain a pebble of
   France") to pick one.
5. **Derivation the lesson skipped (E6c):** Maurras says monarchy follows "like a theorem"
   from the will to preserve France. Write out the chain and supply every premise it needs
   that is not in "preserve France" (e.g. that parties necessarily divide; that hereditary
   power aligns the ruler's interest with the nation's; that no non-hereditary independent
   power is possible). Mark which premise a republican nationalist like Barrès rejects.
6. **Synthesis / counter-argument (E3, Burke and Maistre):** "Maurras is Burke in French."
   Name two facts the claim must account for (Maurras's restoration is a deduction against
   a century-old regime — the method Burke condemned; Maurras's positivism and lack of faith
   vs Burke's Church) and one it gets right (the inheritance against the contract; the
   newborn argument as a form of the partnership of generations). Ref `burke-reflections`
   and `maistre-and-bonald` (the genealogy to Action française).
7. **FORWARD (E7) → `dreyfus-affair-as-test`:** using only Barrès's two principles — every
   question "by relation to France" (§18e) and treason "measured by consequences, not
   intentions" (§7) — decide what a nationalist must say about a court-martial verdict
   against an officer when serious evidence later points to another man, and whether the
   officer's innocence settles anything. The solution derives that innocence becomes
   secondary to the army's authority and the nation's unity, and says in one sentence,
   with a `<Ref>`, that the next lesson reads this principle against the Dreyfus record,
   where Barrès applied it himself.

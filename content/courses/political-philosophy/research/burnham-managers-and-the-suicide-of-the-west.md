# Research brief: burnham-managers-and-the-suicide-of-the-west (Burnham: the managerial revolution and the suicide of the West)

status: planned
module: postwar-conservatism
type: core
goal: Analyze Burnham's thesis that a managerial class was replacing capitalists as the ruling class, and his later argument that liberalism is the ideology of Western contraction, and judge both against the record and Burnham's own test

## ARC (L10)

- **Inherits:** From `michels-and-pareto-oligarchy-and-elites` (prerequisite): the
  `iron-law-of-oligarchy` (a party of workers produces a permanent leadership) and the
  `circulation-of-elites` with Pareto's lions and foxes and `residues-and-derivations`. From
  `mosca-ruling-class`: the `ruling-class` (an organized minority always rules) and the
  `political-formula` (the principle by which it justifies itself). From `marx-manifesto`: class
  defined by ownership of the means of production, and the prediction that the proletariat will
  abolish classes. From `jouvenel-on-power`: `growth-of-power` and the state's alliance with the
  common man. From `kirk-and-nisbet-american-conservatism` (prerequisite): the founding of the
  National Review movement, Kirk's naming of the "managerial revolution" as the shape of the planned
  state, and Nisbet's state that absorbs the functions of groups. From
  `oakeshott-rationalism-in-politics`: rationalism in politics (Burnham uses Oakeshott to define the
  genus to which liberalism belongs). From `what-conservatism-is`: `positional-ideology`. From
  `spengler-decline-of-the-west`: civilizations as organisms that grow and die (Burnham's atlas
  invokes Spengler's "decline" and refuses the word). Each a `<Ref>` and one clause.
- **Contributes:** One question asked twice, twenty-three years apart, by the same method: WHO RULES,
  and what story does the ruling group tell? In 1941 Burnham answers that control of production, not
  legal ownership, makes a ruling class, and that the people who run factories, ministries and armies
  were becoming one under capitalism, fascism and communism alike (`managerial-revolution`). In 1964
  he answers that the West, which in 1914 ruled nearly the whole earth and still had the power to keep
  it, was withdrawing, and that liberalism — the creed of the educated, verbal and administrative
  class — is the ideology that makes the withdrawal feel like justice
  (`liberalism-as-ideology-of-contraction`). What connects them is the theory of ideology the first
  book states and the second applies: ideologies express the interests and condition of a class, and
  are not refuted by facts. The lesson reconstructs both theses, tests the 1941 forecasts against
  1945, and turns Burnham's own test of an ideology — "what evidence might conceivably prove you
  wrong?" — on his 1964 thesis, and judges both (see `## VERDICT`): 1941's war forecasts failed and
  its description of the managed corporation and the administrative state held, established; the
  1964 thesis is not settled by 1989–91, open, with the evidence that would settle it named.
- **Hands off:** (i) `udhr-read-against-the-right` (next): Burnham's 39-sentence test of liberalism
  ends with eight sentences taken from the 1948 Declaration — the module's application reads that
  document article by article. (ii) `lasch-revolt-of-the-elites`: thirty years later, a critic from
  the left describes the same professional-managerial class as having seceded from the nation. (iii)
  `the-cognitive-elite`: whether the new class is selected by measured ability. (iv)
  `america-and-the-english-decline` uses Suicide of the West as evidence for the decline thesis;
  `murray-strange-death-of-europe` restates the guilt argument for Europe in 2017;
  `deneen-post-liberalism` the charge that liberalism's elite governs in its own interest.
- **Spine step:** "After 1945 fascism has discredited the right, and it rebuilds in new registers:
  Jouvenel's history of Power, Oakeshott's disposition, Strauss's natural right, Voegelin's diagnosis
  of Gnosticism, Kirk's and Nisbet's American conservatism, Burnham's managers — tested on the
  Declaration of 1948"

## VERDICT

(From `research/verdicts/REGISTER.md`, Module 9, lesson verdict. Binding: carry each part and its
grade exactly.)

- **(1941) Claim: the managers will replace owners as the ruling class, and the managerial powers will
  win the war. → The war forecasts failed; the description of the managed corporation and the
  administrative state held. Grade: established.** Germany was defeated, Russia did not split, and
  capitalism was restored in western Europe (the edition's own DMR glosses: "This was Burnham's big
  mistake"); the state grew, administrative agencies took over rule-making, and salaried managers
  gained control of the large firm.
- **(1964) Claim: liberalism is the ideology of Western suicide. → Not settled by 1989–91. Open.**
  Burnham's own test reads: "if the contraction of the past fifty years should cease and be reversed,
  then the ideology of liberalism, deprived of its primary function, will fade away"
  (`C/research/texts/burnham/suicide-of-the-west.txt` l.2573). His "liberalism" is a specific
  ideology — his nineteen propositions, guilt, accommodation in place of rollback — not liberal
  democracy. The reversal of 1989–91 came under governments that had rejected détente in his sense.
  So "liberal states won the Cold War" scores the test in the wrong sense of the word, and the growth
  of the former imperial states after 1950 is beside a thesis he defined by territory ruled. Neither
  reading of 1989–91 decides it.
- **What would settle it:** a series on elite opinion on Burnham's propositions — rollback against
  containment, decolonisation and guilt — from 1964 to 2000, to test whether those positions weakened
  before the reversal (which would fit Burnham: the ideology faded and the contraction stopped) or
  only after it (which would fit his test's prediction of fading once the function was gone, but
  leave the causal claim unshown), or did not weaken at all (against him). The series is not in the
  course; the lesson says so.
- **Strongest objection to the verdict:** the critics' reading — the West reversed the contraction
  while remaining liberal, so the thesis is refuted, not open. It does not overturn the verdict,
  because it scores the test with "liberal" meaning liberal democracy, which Burnham's thesis is not
  about; on his definition the governments that won the last decade of the Cold War had rejected the
  liberal positions he named. From the other side, the heirs' reading — the reversal came despite
  liberalism and so confirms him — needs the same missing series to show the positions had weakened
  first.
- **What would overturn it (move it off open):** the elite-opinion series above.

## EXPLANATION PLAN (L0)

Opening (course.yaml, verbatim): "In 1941 the world appeared to offer three systems — capitalism,
fascism, communism — and a former Trotskyist philosophy professor argues they are converging on one:
a society ruled not by owners but by the people who run the factories, ministries and armies.
Twenty-three years later, the same man, now an editor of the leading American conservative magazine,
counts the square miles the West had ruled in 1914 and in 1964 and asks why it was letting itself
shrink. The lesson follows the two theses and what connects them."

Anchor (L11): James Burnham (1905–87), son of a railway executive, taught philosophy at New York
University, spent the 1930s in Trotsky's movement and broke with it in 1939–40 when Trotsky defended
the Soviet attack on Poland; The Managerial Revolution (1941) was a best-seller; after the war he
argued for "rolling back" Soviet power rather than containing it, and helped found National Review in
1955 (Ref `kirk-and-nisbet-american-conservatism` for the magazine). Note for the writer: Burnham does
not count square miles — he leafs through a 1921 school atlas and says the West's dominion "has been
cut to less than half of what it was in 1914"; the lesson keeps the opening and makes the measure
precise in the first Suicide section (see Pitfalls).

**Part one — 1941: who rules?**

1. **What kind of revolution is the war?** In 1941 nearly everyone agreed the war was also a social
   revolution, and no one could say of what kind. Burnham's method (ch. 3): only three theories of the
   coming society exist — capitalism will last; socialism will replace it; a third society will — and
   only one can be true, so refuting two makes the third probable. Define social revolution by its
   three parts (institutions, beliefs, the group at the top) and its rate. Name the method now
   (argument by elimination) because link 6 tests it.
2. **What makes a ruling class?** Obvious attempt, from Marx (Ref `marx-manifesto`): ownership of the
   means of production. Burnham keeps the question and changes the variable: a ruling class is the
   group with (a) control of ACCESS to the instruments of production and (b) PREFERENTIAL treatment
   in the distribution of their products; legal property is only the form this control usually takes
   (ch. 4). The test is empirical — who gets the largest incomes and the right to say who uses the
   plant. Seam to `mosca-ruling-class`: Mosca's ruling class was a political minority held together
   by organization; Burnham's is an economic one held together by control.
3. **Why not socialism?** The socialist prediction needs two premises: socialism is the only
   alternative to capitalism, and abolishing private property abolishes classes. Russia tests the
   second: property abolished, yet a privileged group controls access and takes the larger share;
   classes can rule through corporate positions, as priesthoods did. The episode of workers' control
   (factory committees in 1917, reduced to a veto, then abolished in 1938) is Michels's iron law at
   the scale of a revolution (Ref `michels-and-pareto-oligarchy-and-elites`). The syllogism "if not
   capitalism then socialism" is valid; its premise is the whole dispute.
4. **Who are the managers?** The worked example (ch. 5): an automobile company with four groups —
   the managers who direct production; the finance-executives who watch profit; the bankers who
   lend; the stockholders who own. A car is technically the same whatever it sells for or whatever
   interest the bank charges, so only the first group is technically necessary. The managers'
   position rests on the technical nature of production, the capitalists' on capitalism: so the
   managers can do without the capitalists, and not the reverse. Against the 1932 finding that
   ownership and control had separated in large corporations, Burnham insists ownership IS control
   ("if you cannot keep others out, you are not the owner") — the partial separation he does grant
   is unstable. The historical analogy: the Mayors of the Palace, servants of the Merovingian kings,
   ran the kingdom for generations before one of them took the crown. Define `managerial-revolution`
   here.
5. **Where does the managerial society already exist?** Three roads (chs. 12–14): Russia (owners
   destroyed first, the masses curbed after), Germany (the masses curbed first, using the owners, who
   are reduced after), the United States of the New Deal (the slow way, through government agencies
   and war production). Sovereignty moves from parliament, which bargains like a stock exchange, to
   the administrative bureau that issues rules (the "alphabet agencies"; Congress lost the
   war-making decision in 1940). The ideologies change with the class: state, people or race instead
   of the individual; planning instead of initiative; duties instead of rights; jobs instead of
   opportunity — and "the anticapitalist pages of fascist and communist analyses could usually be
   interchanged". Seam to `jouvenel-on-power`: Jouvenel's growth of Power, read as a class taking it.
6. **What 1945 did to the forecasts — and the OBJECTION to the method.** Build a scorecard (figure 4):
   forecast in 1941 → record. Germany would win or at least not be defeated (wrong — the edition's
   own gloss: "This was Burnham's big mistake"); Russia would split between a European and an Asian
   centre (wrong); three super-states would divide the world (partly — two blocs and a third world
   came instead); capitalism would not be restored in Europe (wrong for the West); the state would
   grow and administrative agencies take over rule-making (right); in large firms salaried managers
   would gain control over owners (largely right as a description of the postwar corporation;
   contested as a claim about who RULES). The objection, stated as the course's own (Orwell's, in
   References): Burnham predicted "a continuation of the thing that is happening" — whatever was
   winning in 1940 would win — and underrated what a democracy can do in war. Then apply his own
   standard (ch. 15: a theory is refuted only by a more probable rival) to his own method: argument
   by elimination is only as good as the list of alternatives, and Burnham's three-way list commits
   the fault he found in the socialists' two-way one — the mixed economy of the postwar West, with
   owners, managers and voters sharing control, was a fourth case the list left out. Where the
   lesson holds: the ruling-class definition is testable and the managerial description of the large
   corporation and the administrative state survived; what failed was the forecast of who would win.

**Bridge — from managers to ideology.** Burnham's 1941 theory of ideology (ch. 11): ideologies are
not controlled by facts; they express a class's interests in universal terms ("humanity", "the
people") and give it a cement. In The Machiavellians (1943) he added that all societies, "including
societies called democratic, are ruled by a minority", and that liberty survives where minorities
check one another — the elite theorists read as defenders of freedom (Ref
`michels-and-pareto-oligarchy-and-elites`; name the book in one sentence, References). After the war
he became the movement's strategist of the Cold War. The 1964 book applies the 1941 theory of
ideology to the creed of the class he now opposed. One plain sentence makes the seam; no roadmap.

**Part two — 1964: why does the West withdraw?**

7. **Is the West shrinking, and why?** The atlas premise (ch. 1): in 1914 the West ruled or dominated
   nearly the whole land surface outside Russia's and China's interiors; by 1964 Russia (1917), China
   (1949), eastern Europe (1945–48), the Indian subcontinent (1947), most of the Arab world, Suez
   (1956) and Africa (from 1957) had passed out of Western control — "a film winding in reverse".
   Burnham insists on the neutral word "contraction" and leaves aside whether it is good or bad. Then
   the elimination: the West did not lack resources — in 1914 and again in the years of the American
   nuclear monopoly it held overwhelming economic and military superiority — and no external power
   could have forced the withdrawal; so the causes are internal and non-material, what he calls "the
   will to survive". Hence the word "suicide", defined in four cognitive steps (l.488). The thesis:
   liberalism is not the cause but "the ideology of Western suicide" — it "motivates and justifies the
   contraction, and reconciles us to it". The replies Burnham anticipates (decolonization is just;
   the West grew stronger without empire; Western ideals spread) are stated here at full strength,
   because link 10 returns to them.
8. **What is liberalism, on this account?** Obvious attempt: a party programme. Burnham: a syndrome —
   nineteen propositions (L1–L19) a liberal is logically committed to, from "human nature is changing
   and plastic" (L1) and the remediable causes of evil (L3) through equality (L14–L16) to the duty
   of government to guarantee everyone a decent living (L19). The reader works the 39-sentence test
   on a few items (Burnham reports self-described liberals never scored below 85 per cent "Agree");
   note that the last eight sentences are articles of the 1948 Declaration (forward). The value
   ladders (ch. 9): nineteenth-century liberals ranked Freedom, Liberty (national independence),
   Justice, Peace; the nuclear-age liberal Peace, Justice, Freedom, Liberty — the slide of Liberty to
   last is, for Burnham, the contraction written as a scale of values.
9. **Why should a creed reconcile a civilization to its end?** Three mechanisms: guilt — the liberal
   believes evils come from bad institutions, so the West's own institutions stand accused and
   reform has no end point (ch. 10); "no enemy to the left" — the preferred enemy is always the right
   (ch. 11); and the social base (ch. 13) — the creed is strongest among the verbal and administrative
   professions, weakest among soldiers, owners and farmers; through Pareto (Ref), a governing class of
   "foxes" that manages by persuasion and combination and has lost the "lions'" readiness to use force
   (§2178–2179). The function (ch. 16): "a swan song, a spiritual solace"; the sick man who calls the
   fever "the glow of health returning". Define `liberalism-as-ideology-of-contraction` here.
10. **The OBJECTIONS at full strength, and the test Burnham supplied.**
    - *Decolonization as consent, not suicide:* the peoples ruled in 1914 had not consented to that
      rule, and the end of empire was in large part their doing; the former imperial states of
      Western Europe grew richer after 1950 than ever before; what spread in the same years —
      constitutions, universities, science, the language of rights, the United Nations itself — was
      Western, so the West contracted only on Burnham's chosen measure, acreage under rule.
      Burnham's reply, from ch. 1: he chose the measure deliberately, the replies are the ideology
      speaking, and power not used is power lost.
    - *The ideology charge turned around:* Burnham calls liberal faith "practically shock-proof"
      (ch. 4) and asks of any ideology what evidence could prove it wrong (ch. 6). Ask it of his own
      thesis. He supplied an answer himself: if the contraction stops and reverses, liberalism "will
      fade away". Between 1989 and 1991 the Soviet bloc collapsed and much of eastern Europe joined
      Western institutions; liberalism did not fade. Readings: for Burnham's heirs, the reversal came
      despite liberalism, through the rearmament and resolve he called for; for his critics, liberal
      states won the Cold War and the test refutes the thesis. Both are set out here at full strength;
      link 11 weighs them.
    - *The managerial thesis as description without a test:* the ruling-class definition is testable
      (incomes, control of access), but Burnham's own figures (Soviet income shares, the German state
      share of national income, "half or more" of Americans dependent on government) are asserted
      without sources — the reader should know which numbers are his.
11. **The weighing: the managers came, the managerial powers lost, and the suicide thesis is open.**
    Set both theses against their strongest objections and the record and reach the verdict of
    `## VERDICT`, claim → evidence → bearing (L10e). (i) 1941: the scorecard of link 6 decides it —
    the war forecasts failed and the description of the managed corporation and the administrative
    state held — **established**. (ii) 1964: the premise (contraction by acreage) is true on its own
    measure; the thesis that liberalism is the ideology of the contraction is scored by Burnham's own
    test (l.2573). Read the test with his definition of liberalism (the nineteen propositions, guilt,
    accommodation in place of rollback), not with liberal democracy: the reversal of 1989–91 came
    under governments that had rejected détente in his sense, so "liberal states won" scores it in
    the wrong sense of the word, and the postwar growth of the former imperial states is beside a
    thesis defined by territory ruled. The heirs' reading needs evidence that the positions weakened
    before the reversal; the critics' needs the word to mean what Burnham did not mean. **Open** — and
    say what would settle it: a series on elite opinion on rollback against containment and on
    decolonisation and guilt, 1964–2000, showing whether those positions weakened before or after the
    reversal. The course does not have the series and says so.

**Conclusion** (one or two short paragraphs, before Exercises): the verdict in plain words with its
grades — established: Burnham's 1941 description of who runs the large organization and the
administrative state held, and his forecasts of who would win the war failed; open: whether
liberalism, in his sense, is the ideology of the West's contraction — 1989–91 does not settle it, and a
series on elite opinion from 1964 to 2000 would. Then the hand-off: the last eight of his thirty-nine
sentences are articles of the 1948 Declaration, which the next lesson reads against the module's
critics (`udhr-read-against-the-right`).

Headings as claims (suggested): "Three theories, one survivor"; "A ruling class is defined by control";
"Abolishing property did not abolish class"; "Only the managers are technically necessary"; "Three

## Set pieces and worked examples to recreate

1. **The automobile company** (MR ch. 5 l.2181–2330): four circles — managers, finance-executives,

   answered step by step; Henry Ford as the late case of one man holding all four functions.
2. **The Mayors of the Palace** (MR ch. 6 l.2720–2734): servants who govern for generations before
   one of them is crowned (Pepin, 751) — "simply put in a formal way what had already happened". One
   clause of anchor: the Merovingians, the first Frankish royal house.
3. **The ruling-class definition applied** (MR ch. 4 l.1547–1587): run it on a feudal manor (lord:
   access to land, the larger share), a 1900 steel firm (owner), a 1941 Soviet factory (director and
   party: access without legal title) — the reader fills a 3×2 table.
4. **The scorecard of 1941** (MR chs. 10, 13; DMR glosses l.6872): six forecasts against the record,
   right and wrong marked, with the edition's own "big mistake" gloss quoted.
5. **The atlas** (Suicide ch. 1 l.416–438): the old school atlas, the double-page 1914 map, the
   imagined maps of 1920, 1945, 1949, the 1950s — "a film winding in reverse". The single sentence
   with "less than half of what it was in 1914" (l.474) is the measure.
6. **The 39 sentences** (Suicide ch. 2): the reader marks five of them (e.g. 14 colonialism is wrong;
   20 independence by majority; 28 the vote; 32 and 35, from the Declaration), then reads Burnham's
   claim about the scores and his reply to "but these are just reasonable".
7. **The value ladders** (Suicide ch. 9 l.1650–1700): four orderings of Freedom, Liberty, Justice,
   Peace — the reader ranks the four for himself before seeing Burnham's (a self-test Burnham
   himself proposes; ch. 9 fn 2).
8. **The sick man** (Suicide ch. 16 l.2569–2573): "Ah, the glow of health returning!" — and the
   last line, "Perhaps this book is one of them", which is the test of link 10.

## Pitfalls / subtleties to flag

- **Register rule:** The course judges arguments, not people: no condemning or approving labels for any author in its own voice; a label may appear only inside a quotation, with who applied it and when. It does pass verdicts on arguments: where the lesson stages a dispute it has the evidence to settle, and in every module's closing lesson, it says which side wins, how firmly (established / strong / on balance / open) and why, as recorded in research/verdicts/. "Open" is a finding, stated with what would settle it, never a way to avoid one. The same tests apply to the left and the right.
- **The opening's "square miles":** Burnham gives no square-mile or percentage counts; his measure is
  the colours of an atlas and the phrase "cut to less than half of what it was in 1914". The opening
  is fixed; the first Suicide section states the measure exactly as Burnham does and says it is his
  estimate. If the writer wants a number, it must come from a sourced dataset of territory and
  population under Western rule in 1914 and 1964 (not in the dossier) — otherwise none. Recommended
  course.yaml fix reported to the caller.
- **Terms to anchor (L11), at most three per section:** Trotskyist (follower of Leon Trotsky, the
  Bolshevik leader exiled by Stalin, who held that the Soviet Union was a degenerated workers' state);
  the New Deal (Roosevelt's programmes of the 1930s); "alphabet agencies"; finance-capitalist;
  Mayors of the Palace; Merovingians; rollback vs containment (the American policy, from 1947, of
  holding Soviet power within its existing limits); National Review; Suez 1956 (Britain and France
  forced by the United States to withdraw from an invasion of Egypt); decolonization; "syndrome"
  (Burnham's word: a cluster of symptoms whose cause is left open); lions and foxes (recall,
  `michels-and-pareto-oligarchy-and-elites`).
- **Do not re-teach** Mosca, Michels, Pareto, Marx's class theory, Jouvenel, Oakeshott's rationalism
  — clause plus `<Ref>`. The new matter is the CONTROL definition of class, the four-group example,
  the scorecard, and the ideology-of-contraction function.
- **The real misreading (L13):** "Burnham was a defender of the managerial society he described." In
  1941 he disclaims any programme or morality ("no program and no morality") and calls planning
  morally neutral; by the 1950s he opposed the planned state from the right. Present the 1941 book as
  a forecast, not an endorsement — and Orwell's "power worship" charge as the objection to that very
  neutrality.
- **Race and atrocity material in Suicide of the West:** the atlas chapter's list of "symbols"
  (l.~452: flight of Europeans, atrocities in central Africa, insolence to Western envoys), the
  sentences on race and "civilizing potential" (test sentence 30, L16), and the NYU anecdote in ch.
  10 (l.1909) are Burnham's evidence or rhetoric. Quote none of the atrocity list; state L16 as one
  item of his syndrome, with one clause that the evidence on group differences is the subject of the
  heredity module (`hereditarian-case-iq-and-nations`) — no verdict here either way. The anecdotes are
  his testimony, not data; say so if any is used.
- **Dated and partisan framing:** both books are period pieces (the 2014 foreword says so) — Kennedy-era
  cases, the Cold War, Algeria and the Congo. Use no more than two of the 1960s cases, each anchored
  in a clause; the argument's structure is the lesson, not the docket.
- **Numbers (L12):** keeper numbers: "less than half" (1914 → 1964, Burnham's measure); 85 per cent
  (the test score Burnham reports); the 1932 corporate figures only if set up in one sentence (the 200
  largest non-financial corporations; Burnham reports that about two-thirds of them, holding about
  four-fifths of their assets, were classed as management-controlled) — and labelled as Burnham's
  report of the study. Burnham's Soviet and German statistics are unsourced; keep them out of the
  body.
- **Burnham's own changes of mind:** Trotskyist to anti-Stalinist (1939–40), anti-interventionist to
  interventionist (Pearl Harbor), rollback later softened (Fawcett: "though he later changed his
  mind"). One sentence; do not make the biography the argument.
- **Balance:** the 1941 thesis and the 1964 thesis each at full strength first (the automobile company
  and the atlas are persuasive and should feel so), then the objections from the democratic record,
  from the decolonized side, and from Burnham's own falsification test. It does pass verdicts on arguments: where the lesson stages a dispute it has the evidence to settle, and in every module's closing lesson, it says which side wins, how firmly (established / strong / on balance / open) and why, as recorded in research/verdicts/. ## EXERCISE PLAN (E6/E7 + playbook-history mix)

1. **Retrieval warm-up (the only mechanical one):** match each of the four groups in Burnham's
   automobile company to its function and say which one his argument needs to be technically
   necessary, and why; then name the earlier theorist whose minority the managers most resemble —
   Mosca's, Michels's or Pareto's — with one reason (Ref).
2. **Document reasoning (sourcing drill):** the atlas passage (Suicide ch. 1 l.416–438). Author (an
   ex-Trotskyist, National Review editor, Cold War strategist), date (1964), audience (American readers
   in the year of the Goldwater campaign), purpose; what it RECORDS (a sequence of transfers of rule),
   what it ARGUES (that they are one process), what it OMITS (who in the ruled territories wanted the
   transfer); what it can be USED to establish and what it cannot.
3. **Boundary probe (E6a):** apply Burnham's ruling-class definition to a firm in which one family
   founds, owns, finances and manages (his own Ford example) and to a cooperative whose workers own
   the plant and elect the manager. Where does "control of access" sit in each, and what does the
   definition say at these two degenerate cases about the separation it relies on?
4. **Claim to refute (E6b):** "Burnham predicted in 1941 that the managers would come to rule through
   the capitalist corporation." Name the step that fails (state ownership as the managerial base; the
   instability of the partial separation; "10 times 10% state capitalism… equals 0% capitalism") and
   state what he did predict.
5. **Derivation the lesson skipped (E6c):** Burnham's 1941 theory of ideology says a ruling class's
   ideology speaks in universal terms and serves the class. Derive from it what he must say in 1964
   about the social base of liberalism, and check the derivation against his list of who is and is not
   liberal (Suicide ch. 13).
6. **Counter-argument (synthesis):** "The end of empire after 1945 was the West's suicide." Name two
   facts the claim must account for (the colonized peoples' own movements for independence; Western
   Europe's postwar prosperity and the spread of Western institutions) and state what would have to be
   true for the claim to survive (that territorial rule is the measure of a civilization's life —
   Burnham's premise, which he chose deliberately).
7. **Research-shaped task (E6d):** design the test of Burnham's 1964 thesis that he himself implies
   ("if the contraction should cease and be reversed, liberalism… will fade away"): what would you
   measure for "contraction" and for "liberalism" between 1964 and 2000, what result would confirm and
   what would refute it, and why the events of 1989–91 do not settle it without a further premise
   (whose doing the reversal was).
8. **Attack the verdict.** The lesson graded Burnham's 1964 thesis open. State the strongest objection
   in its best form — that the West reversed its contraction in 1989–91 while remaining liberal, so the
   thesis is refuted — and then the strongest objection from the other side — that the reversal came
   from leaders who had rejected détente, so the thesis is confirmed. For each, name the premise it
   needs that the course's evidence does not supply, and describe the series (whose opinion, on which
   of Burnham's propositions, which years) that would move the verdict off open in its direction.
9. **FORWARD (E7) → `udhr-read-against-the-right`:** the last eight of Burnham's 39 sentences are
   articles of the 1948 Declaration (e.g. "The will of the people shall be the basis of the authority
   of government"; "Everyone, as a member of society, has the right to social security"). For three
   of them, name the L-proposition each one expresses and say what Burnham's use of them assumes about
   the document. The solution says in one sentence that the next lesson reads the Declaration article
   by article against the module's critics and asks which articles — among them Article 29 on duties —
   the critics' reading leaves out, with a `<Ref>`.

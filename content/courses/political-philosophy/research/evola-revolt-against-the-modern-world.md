# Research brief: evola-revolt-against-the-modern-world (Evola's Revolt against the Modern World: tradition and the dark age)

status: planned
module: conservative-revolution
type: core
goal: Analyze Evola's traditionalist case that the modern world is the last stage of a regression from a sacred hierarchical order, and evaluate the method that supports it

## ARC (L10)

- **Inherits:** The module so far: Sorel's myth that moves a class because it is not a prediction (`sorel-reflections-on-violence`, `political-myth`); Jünger's front and his Worker, a new ruling type made by technology (`junger-storm-of-steel-and-the-worker`, `gestalt-of-the-worker`); Moeller's revolution "to create things worth conserving" and the name "Third Reich" (`moeller-van-den-bruck-third-reich`, `conservative-revolution`). From the previous module: Spengler's Cultures, his Culture/Civilization pair, Caesarism and the West's winter (`spengler-decline-of-the-west`, `culture-vs-civilization-spengler`, `caesarism`). From much earlier, the material Evola re-reads: Plato's three classes and justice as each doing his own work (`kallipolis-and-the-philosopher-king`, `three-classes`) and Plato's decline of regimes (`decline-of-regimes`, `regime-cycle`); Aristotle's natural slavery and its admitted difficulty (`aristotle-political-animal-and-natural-slavery`); Rome's civil theology and the altar it lost to the Church (`constantine-and-representation`, `altar-of-victory`); law descending from God's reason (`aquinas-law-and-kingship`); Maistre's providential counter-revolution (`maistre-and-bonald`); Carlyle's heroes (`carlyle-heroes`, `hero-worship`).
- **Contributes:** One move: the most radical answer the right gave to "what standard condemns the present?" Every critic so far measured modernity against something historical — the ancien régime, the Church, the nation, the Kaiser, the front. Evola measures it against a type that no documented society fully embodied, Tradition, and reads all recorded history as one regression from it. The lesson reconstructs the standard (two natures, authority from above, caste as degrees of being), the law of history that follows (`regression-of-castes`), and the method that supports both (myth as a truer record than documents, truths "remembered", not debated) — and then evaluates the method at full strength. It introduces `primordial-tradition` and `regression-of-castes`.
- **Hands off:** (i) Evola's own conclusion that the way back is "doubly blocked", and the problem it leaves every thinker of the radical right: a doctrine of sacred hierarchy found no regime to embody it, and the regimes that claimed hierarchy were mass movements, which his own law classifies as the rule of the lowest caste. What German thinkers did when such a movement took power is `the-right-in-1933` (next lesson, the module's application). (ii) Evola's postwar refusal of politics ("ride the tiger") and his readers among the young right are picked up in `the-right-after-1945` (the fate of the Conservative Revolution's authors). (iii) Culture before power — his withdrawal from politics into "testimony" — is the seed of the European New Right's metapolitics (`de-benoist-european-new-right`, which names Evola in its own lineage).
- **Spine step:** "The radical right turns from conserving to revolution — Sorel's myth, Jünger's front and Worker, Moeller's Third Reich, Evola's Tradition — and in 1933 its thinkers have to choose: Schmitt and Heidegger join, Spengler refuses"

## EXPLANATION PLAN (L0)

Opening (course.yaml, verbatim): "Most critics of modernity in the 1920s wanted to save something from the nineteenth century — the nation, the Church, property, the monarchy. An Italian former Dadaist painter wants none of it: in his view the whole of recorded history since the sacred kingships of the ancient world has been a fall, and the nation, the Church and the bourgeoisie are stages of the same decline. The lesson asks what standard could condemn all of history at once, and reconstructs the one he offers."

The problem as the newcomer feels it: every critic of the modern world the course has read stood somewhere. Burke stood on the English constitution, Maistre on the Church and the king, Moeller on a nation still to be made. A critic who rejects the nation, the Church and the bourgeoisie together seems to have nowhere left to stand. The chain:

1. **Where can a critic stand who rejects everything that exists?** Obvious attempt: in an earlier period — the Middle Ages, Rome, archaic Greece — known from documents. Why it fails for Evola: by his own dating the decline is visible from the 8th–6th centuries BC, the very moment documented history begins (Foreword, l. 508); every period a historian can document is already inside the fall. So the standard cannot be a period. Answer: a **type**. Modern and traditional civilization are "two universal types and … two a priori categories of civilization" (Foreword); traditional societies happen to lie in the past, "merely accidental". Because: a period could be refuted by the record; a type cannot, and that is both its strength and (link 6) its problem. Name the concept here: `primordial-tradition` — a sacred, hierarchical order of which all later history is a regression. Seam to earlier lessons: Spengler had already denied a single world history and given every Culture its own life (Ref `spengler-decline-of-the-west`); Evola agrees with the diagnosis and refuses the relativism — not many Cultures each with its winter but one Tradition and its loss ("dualism", not "relativism", of civilizations). One sentence: he translated Spengler and faulted him for having no "transcendent" reference point.
2. **What does the type contain?** The two natures — a realm of being (immortal, unchanging) above a realm of becoming (ch. 1); from it, authority that "comes from above", never conferred from below (ch. 2); the king as *pontifex*, a "builder of bridges" between the two realms (l. 634), so that kingship and priesthood were originally one; law from above, so that "natural rights" are "a mere fiction" and the people, unformed, is matter that needs form (l. 884). Because: if reality is ranked, a legitimate order must copy the ranking; an order built from below copies nothing. Set it against the reader's earlier answers to "where does authority come from?": Aquinas's law descending from God's reason (Ref `aquinas-law-and-kingship`), Maistre's "sovereignty and infallibility" (Ref `maistre-and-bonald`), Burke's prescription — authority from duration (Ref `burke-reflections`). Evola keeps Aquinas's direction and refuses his Church: the priest who claims to stand above the king is the usurper (chs. 10–11; the pope's very title a "usurpation", l. 1510) — a Ghibelline, not a Guelph (anchor: the medieval parties of emperor and pope). And he refuses Burke's test: duration proves nothing; only the source does.
3. **Who rules, and how is he known?** The caste. Obvious objection, the reader's own: a hierarchy of birth is arbitrary. Evola's reply, the core of ch. 14: "birth does not determine nature, but nature determines birth" (l. 1760) — one is born into a caste because one already is, "transcendentally", of that nature; the castes are "a higher application of the principle *suum cuique*" (to each his own). Initiation actualises what birth confers. Because: this closes the gap Aristotle admitted — nature "often fails" to give the natural slave the slave's body and the free man the free man's soul (Ref `aristotle-political-animal-and-natural-slavery`); if nature fixes birth, birth cannot misplace anyone. Set beside Plato's three classes and justice as each doing his own work (Ref `kallipolis-and-the-philosopher-king`, `three-classes`): Evola reads the Republic as a late echo of the caste order (ch. 3, ch. 14; Mussolini drew the same comparison for Evola's three races). Mark the cost in the same link, at full strength: the reply is unfalsifiable by construction — no observation of a misplaced person can count against it, since the doctrine defines placement by birth. The lesson states that this is the price of closing Aristotle's gap, not a slip.
4. **How did the order fall?** By a law: `regression-of-castes`. Power passed "from sacred leaders, to a warrior aristocracy, to the merchants, and finally, to the serfs" (ch. 35, l. 4676), and each phase has its own bond — sacred *fides*; the warrior's loyalty and honour (the great monarchies); the social contract (parliaments and "the kings of the coal, oil, and iron industries", l. 4688); collectivised labour (1917, "a socialist civilization of labor"). Wars and nations are "secondary and contingent". Because: if the castes are degrees of being, history can only be a descent through them — there is no lower caste to rise from and no higher one to rise to. Seam: the reader has met three descents already — Plato's aristocracy-to-tyranny (Ref `decline-of-regimes`), Polybius's cycle (Ref `polybius-anacyclosis-and-rome`), Spengler's Culture-to-Civilization (Ref `spengler-decline-of-the-west`). From these one would expect a cycle that turns back up; Evola's law runs one way, and its present is the rule of the last caste. Here the four ages enter in one paragraph (ch. 22: Hesiod's metals, the Hindu yugas — *Kali Yuga*, the "Dark Age" — Daniel's statue) as the same law told in myth. A `<FigureSequence>` carries the descent (Figure 2).
5. **What does the law say about the modern world in particular?** It explains why Evola rejects everything the rest of the right defends. The nation: a "demos, not ethnos" artifice (ch. 36), the merchant's state. The Church: the "lunar" pole that usurped the king's. The bourgeoisie: the third caste. Socialism and communism: the fourth. Russia and America: "two ends of the same pair of pincers" closing on Europe (l. 4882) — the planned state and the religion of productivity as one phenomenon. Modern work: "if there ever was a civilization of slaves on a grand scale, the one in which we are living is it" (l. 1958). And Fascism and National Socialism, by the law, are mass movements — the fourth caste in uniform. This is where the reader sees why Evola's critique of the Fascist regime came "from the right" (link 7): it is derived from the law, not from taste. Part Two's Hyperborean and Atlantic origins and the North–South polarity are reported here in one paragraph as Evola's claims, with one sentence on what the record says (see Pitfalls); the lesson does not argue them point by point.
6. **What supports all this? The method — and the strongest objection.** Evola's case, at full strength: (a) myth is a truer record of what a civilization took to be real than its documents — "the Rome of legends speaks clearer words than the historical Rome" (l. 544); (b) modern historical method carries modern assumptions (linear time, causality, "evolution"), so to judge Tradition by it begs the question; (c) such truths are not debated but recognised — "either they are or they are not … It is only possible to remember them" (l. 546). Give (b) its due: the reader met a version of it in Spengler's claim that every Culture's categories are its own. **Objection, at full strength, from three sides.** (i) The liberal and scientific objection: a claim that admits no test cannot be told apart from an invention; and Evola's material is itself drawn from modern scholars (Bachofen, Fustel de Coulanges, Frazer, Guénon) selected where they fit — the Short Introduction and the notes show it. (ii) The internal objection: the North/South, solar/lunar binary decodes any datum either way — Bachofen read the "mother" cults as the earliest stage that patriarchy overcame; Evola reads the same evidence as a later Southern intrusion (ch. 27, fn. 13); identical evidence, opposite order, and nothing in the method chooses. (iii) The objection from the structure of reaction (stated as the course's own; Lilla in References): the story — a harmonious order, alien ideas, a betrayal, a remnant who remember — has the shape of every narrative of loss, and "nostalgia is irrefutable". Where the lesson concedes: given its premise — that there is a non-empirical knowledge of the past — the system is coherent and the method follows; the disagreement is about the premise, and no argument inside the book can settle it for a reader who does not share it. Evola says as much: the book is "testimony" for "the differentiated man". Tone: evaluated, not mocked (course.yaml note).
7. **What could such a doctrine do in politics?** Two problems that were Evola's own. First, the regime: he wanted a sacred empire and got a mass party. He would be "fascist" exactly "to the extent that Fascism follows these principles" (the 1930 "Identity Card"); his journal was suppressed; he called the regime's leaders "caricatures"; the SS, studying his German lectures in 1938, judged him a "reactionary Roman" with "Utopian" plans and recommended stopping his public activity — Himmler agreed (quote the dossier's document, attributed and dated). Second, race: in 1938–42 he offered a "race of the spirit" against biological racism (ch. 9: blood and purity "merely 'material' factors") and was attacked by the head of the regime's race office, Guido Landra, for allowing "an Aryan … the soul of a Jew" — the doctrine was hierarchical without being biological, and that put him against the state's racial law from the right while he still wrote antisemitic texts himself (the republished *Protocols*, ch. 35). State both halves; do not resolve them into a verdict. The postwar coda in two sentences: paralysed by a bomb in Vienna in 1945; tried in 1951 for "glorification of Fascism" and acquitted, his defence that Plato, Aristotle and Dante would be guilty too; his last word on politics was *apoliteia* — to "keep standing amid a world of ruins" and "ride the tiger" (Conclusion). Close on the problem handed on: a doctrine whose own law says that every modern mass movement is the rule of the lowest caste can find no movement to join — and yet the movements of the 1930s spoke its words (hierarchy, empire, sacrifice). What German thinkers did when one of them took power is the next lesson.

Headings as claims (4–6 sections; core lesson, 40 min): "A standard outside history"; "Authority from above and the king who builds bridges"; "Nature determines birth"; "Four castes, one descent"; "Why Tradition rejects the nation, the Church and the bourgeoisie"; "Myth as evidence, and the case against it"; "A doctrine without a regime".

Worked example of Evola's procedure: one etymology, one ancient witness, one Nordic saying ("May our leader be our bridge"), and a general law drawn. Flag (one clause) that the etymology of *pontifex* is uncertain in modern philology; the point is how the argument is built, not the word's history.
3. **"Nature determines birth"** (ch. 14, l. 1760) set against Aristotle's admission (Ref): a two-row comparison showing how the metaphysical reply closes the empirical gap — and what it costs.
4. **The kings of coal, oil and iron** (ch. 35, l. 4688): the third phase in one sentence; then the reader maps 1789, 1848, 1917 onto the four castes (Exercise 1 warm-up).
5. **The pincers** (ch. 37, l. 4882): Russia and America as one civilization of production. 8. **"Keep standing amid a world of ruins"** (Conclusion, l. 5106) and **"riding a tiger"** (l. 5114): the end of the book as the end of the argument — no politics follows. One sentence each.

## Pitfalls / subtleties to flag

- **Terms a newcomer will not know (L11), each anchored where it first appears:** Dadaism (the anti-art movement founded in Zurich in 1916; Evola painted in it c. 1916–21) — at the opening; Guénon (French esotericist, 1886–1951, whose *Crisis of the Modern World* Evola translated) — once, in link 1; Tradition with a capital T (Evola's term of art: not "custom" in Burke's sense but a supra-historical order — say so explicitly, since the reader's "tradition" is Burke's); *pontifex* (Roman high priest; "bridge-builder") — link 2; Ghibelline and Guelph (the medieval parties of the emperor and of the pope) — link 2; caste (the hereditary orders of ancient India: brahmins/priests, kshatriyas/warriors, vaishyas/merchants and farmers, shudras/serfs) — link 3, with the Sanskrit names given once and then English; *dharma* (one's own law or duty in the order) — link 3; *suum cuique* (to each his own: Roman law's definition of justice) — link 3; Kali Yuga (the last of the four Hindu world-ages, the "Dark Age") — link 4; Hyperborea (the Greeks' land "beyond the north wind") — link 5, one clause; *La Torre* (Evola's journal, 1930) — link 7; the SS (the Nazi party's elite guard under Himmler) — link 7; Landra (head of the Italian regime's race office, co-author of the 1938 race manifesto) — link 7; *apoliteia* (Evola's term for inner detachment from politics) — link 7. No more than three new terms per section: split link 2 and link 3 into separate sections.
- **The translation is the 1969 text** (see Sources). Never quote a passage referring to the postwar world as if written in 1934. When the date matters, say "in the revised edition of 1969".
- **Copyright:** short quotations (a sentence) only; Evola's Italian is protected until 2044 and the Stucco translation is © 1995. The lesson budget is about eight short quotations in all.
- **Evola is not Guénon's copy, and not Spengler's.** Two real differences the lesson needs: against Guénon, he puts the warrior's action on a level with the priest's contemplation and the king above the priest (the Translator's Preface and chs. 10–11; Sedgwick for the dispute, References); against Spengler, one regression instead of many cycles, and a transcendent standard instead of a biological life-span.
- **Race: state his position exactly, in both directions.** He rejected biological racism as "material" (ch. 9; the 1931 article) and was attacked by the Fascist state's race theorists for it; he also republished the *Protocols of the Elders of Zion* and wrote antisemitic passages, including ch. 35's use of Marx's "On the Jewish Question" to make "secularized Judaism" the agent of the merchants' age. Report both as his, dated, once; no summarising label in the course's voice; the Short Introduction's defence ("the style of the times") is the introducer's, attributed. The ch. 35 passage is not quoted in the lesson; it is reported in a clause.
- **Misogyny and the "Civilization of the Mother"** (chs. 20, 27): Evola's metaphysics of the sexes is part of the system (the solar/lunar, masculine/feminine binary) and cannot be cut without misrepresenting the binary; report it in one sentence as the binary's application ("he classes the feminine with becoming and the South"). Do not build a section on it.
- **Empirical claims in Part Two** — a polar homeland of humanity, Atlantis as a historical centre, the tilt of the earth's axis ending the Golden Age, evolution as "a democratic myth": state them as Evola's, once, and say in one sentence that geology, archaeology and genetics record none of them (the heredity module later reads what ancient DNA does show about the Indo-European migrations, `reich-ancient-dna-and-the-indo-europeans` — a forward Ref in a clause, not an argument here). Do not spend the lesson refuting Atlantis; the evaluation belongs to the method (link 6), where Evola himself places it.
- **Do not mock** (course.yaml note: "its method is evaluated, not mocked"). No ironic framing of the polar myths; no sentence in the course's voice that turns on "esoteric" or "occult" as a dismissal. The register rule: The course passes no verdict of its own on any author or idea: no condemning or approving labels in its own voice. Judgement comes only through argument and evidence; a label may appear only inside a quotation, with who applied it and when.
- **Do not re-teach** Plato's three classes, Aristotle's natural slavery, Spengler's morphology, Maistre's providence, the Altar of Victory. Each is a `<Ref>` and a clause. The one place the lesson spends more than a clause on an earlier text is the Aristotle comparison in link 3, because the closing of Aristotle's gap IS the argument.
- **Fascism and Evola — the record is mixed and the dossier's account is an admirer's.** Never a party member; suppressed in 1930; critical of the regime in print; consulted by Mussolini on race in 1941–42; with the Salò republic's circles after 1943 (verify the 1943 Rastenburg episode in Sedgwick or Furlong before using it; otherwise omit). Do not let the SS dossier or De Felice "acquit" him and do not let the antisemitic texts "convict" him in the course's voice: the reader weighs.
- **Postwar influence** (MSI youth, the "years of lead" debates, de Benoist, later readers) is contested and belongs to `the-right-after-1945` and `de-benoist-european-new-right`; here one sentence at most, and no list of later admirers.
- **Where the dossier is thin:** nothing on the 1934 first edition's text or on its German reception (1935); nothing beyond the Short Introduction on the biography; no secondary study of Evola is staged. Say so in References and keep the biography to what the Short Introduction documents (its quoted documents, not its judgements).
- **L8a:** the body names Evola and the thinkers he reads (Plato, Aristotle, Hesiod, Spengler, Guénon once); never Hansen, Stucco, Lilla, Muller, Sedgwick, Drake, De Felice. "The objection that every such story has the shape of a lost paradise" — not "Lilla argues".

## EXERCISE PLAN (E6/E7 + playbook-history mix)

1. **Retrieval and chronology (warm-up).** Place on Evola's four-caste ladder the following, with the bond each represents: Charlemagne's empire, Louis XIV's monarchy, the Third Republic of 1880 with its industrial magnates, the Soviet Union of 1925. Then say which earlier thinker in the course would have accepted the second placement and why (Bodin or Hobbes: absolute sovereignty is the warrior's phase for Evola, the summit of order for them — Ref `bodin-sovereignty`).
2. **Document reasoning — sourcing drill.** The 1938 SS report (quoted in the Short Introduction, ll. 294–308): author (Himmler's personal staff), date, audience (internal), purpose (whether to support Evola's German lectures and his proposed "secret international order"). Records / argues / omits; what it can be used to establish (that the regime's organs saw his doctrine as aristocratic and reactionary rather than National Socialist; that he was not supported) and what it cannot (what Evola believed; whether his doctrine was compatible with Fascism in principle — the report judges utility, not doctrine).
3. **Claim to refute.** "Evola's hierarchy is racial hierarchy under another name." Break it at the step that fails, from the text: ch. 9 calls blood and purity "merely 'material' factors" and the regime's chief race theorist attacked him for it; caste rests on "nature determines birth", a metaphysical, not biological, claim. Then state what survives of the claim (the "race of the spirit" still ranks peoples and he still wrote antisemitic texts) — the solution keeps both halves.
4. **Boundary probe.** Evola's reply to Aristotle's difficulty ("nature determines birth") makes misplacement impossible. Take the degenerate case: a king's son who is cowardly and stupid. What does the doctrine say (the quality "virtually conferred" at birth must be actualised by initiation; failure is a failure of initiation, or a sign of the Dark Age's "mixing")? Show that every outcome is absorbed, and say why that is exactly what makes the reply proof against Aristotle's objection and against any other.
5. **Derivation the lesson skipped.** From the regression law alone, derive Evola's judgement on a twentieth-century mass party that claims to restore hierarchy, empire and sacrifice. (It is the fourth caste's rule in the warrior's costume; hence his "too bad for Mussolini" and the 1935 verdict on National Socialism as "semi-collective nationalism".) Then say what the law cannot tell him: whether to collaborate with such a party for lack of a better one — the decision the law leaves open and his career shows him taking both ways.
6. **Synthesis — claim–evidence–reasoning (E3: earlier lessons).** "Evola is the most consistent of the right's critics of modernity, because he alone refuses to conserve anything modern." Argue for or against with three pieces of evidence: Burke's prescription (Ref `burke-reflections`), Maistre's Church (Ref `maistre-and-bonald`), Moeller's "create things worth conserving" (Ref `moeller-van-den-bruck-third-reich`). State the warrant and the cost (consistency bought by a standard no living society meets).
7. **Research-shaped task.** Design the test that would distinguish Evola's account of the four ages from the ordinary historian's: what evidence would each predict about the Mycenaean palaces and the polis that followed them (Ref `greek-miracle`: the palace falls, the agora opens)? State what Evola would say about any evidence you name (it is "historical" and so of the lower order) — and conclude what kind of disagreement this is.
8. **FORWARD (E7).** Evola ends his book with no political programme: "keep standing amid a world of ruins" and turn the destructive forces against themselves. Suppose a group of his readers in the 1960s concludes that, since elections and parties belong to the last caste, the right should work on ideas, schools and journals for a generation before it seeks power. State what they keep from Evola and what they must give up (the claim that the truths cannot be "discussed"). The solution says, in one sentence with a `<Ref>`, that this is the programme of the European New Right — "metapolitics" — read in `de-benoist-european-new-right`.

## Nietzsche cross-course links

- `nietzsche/master-morality` — one Ref at link 2 or 3, for the contrast: Nietzsche's noble values are the self-affirmation of a ruling type; Evola's rank is participation in being "from above". The two hierarchies are founded in opposite ways (immanent self-affirmation vs transcendent order); the Short Introduction's own insistence that Evola's hero is not Nietzsche's superman or a Titan (l. 250) can be paraphrased as Evola's claim.

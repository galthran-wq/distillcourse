# Evidence review: why measured mean cognitive scores differ between groups

Research dossier for the heredity module (`hereditarian-case-iq-and-nations`,
`reich-ancient-dna-and-the-indo-europeans`, `gould-and-harden-on-heredity`,
`national-data-audited`). Compiled 2026-11-01; revised the same day after the human
staged full texts of most items first read as abstracts. Not lesson text: a record of
what the primary literature shows, how strongly, and what it cannot show, so that the four
lessons state no claim beyond its evidence in either direction.

The question: is the US Black–White gap in measured cognitive scores, and the spread of
national averages, caused by genetic differences, environmental differences, or both —
and in what proportion?

## How to read this dossier

Each citation carries a reading tag.

- **[full]** — the full text (or the whole relevant section) was read. Staged texts are in
  `research/texts/heredity-papers/` (file named in the citation where useful).
- **[abstract]** — only the abstract was read. Numbers so tagged are the abstract's own;
  methods and caveats were not checked.
- **[secondary]** — known only through another source's account; that source is named.
- **[not read]** — cited because the debate cites it; nothing verified. Listed at the end.

Perplexity (sonar-deep-research) was used for leads on 2019–2026 work. Every claim from it
that enters this file was checked against the paper at the tag given; several leads were
wrong (the 2024 PNAS paper on within-group heritability is by Schraiber & Edge, not
"Rosenberg et al."; the staged "Flynn, What Is Intelligence?" is in fact the Russian
translation of Nisbett's *Intelligence and How to Get It*, 2009). Nothing is cited to
Perplexity.

Register: the course passes no verdict of its own on any author. Where a source applies a
label to another ("scientific racism", "fringe science", "pseudo-quantitative"), it is
quoted as that source's label, with its date.

### What changed in the revision (full texts read)

- **Overall answer:** the earlier draft said the evidence "leans toward environmental
  factors, weakly". That sentence is withdrawn. It rested on an asymmetry (environmental
  effects of gap size are demonstrated, genetic ones are not), but the full texts add
  evidence on both sides that the abstracts hid: measured parental education and income
  account for about 45–65% of the child WISC gap and leave about 6 points (Weiss &
  Saklofske 2020); the one within-family admixture test has a positive but
  non-significant point estimate (Fuerst et al. 2023); the adoption and Eyferth nulls are
  small and confounded. The revised status is "open, no direction established", with two
  limits that are established: the gap is not wholly genetic as it stands (it moved and
  varies with place), and no evidence excludes a genetic share.
- **What would settle the admixture question (§4):** the earlier draft said a
  sibling-difference admixture design "removes every family-level confound including …
  colourism". Schraiber & Edge (preprint, read in full) point out that colourism can act
  on individuals within a sibship; the design removes family-level confounds only. Revised.
- **Numbers corrected or added:** Dickens & Flynn (gap widens with age: Black IQ 90 at 4,
  79 at 23 in 1972); Murray 2007 (≈0.5 SD narrowing from 1920s to late-1960s cohorts, then
  flat); Weiss & Saklofske (WISC-V 11.6, WAIS-IV 14.5 points); Murray 2021 (0.85 SD,
  2010s); Kirkegaard & Jensen 2025 (85 uncorrected, 81.5 corrected; cohort not a
  significant moderator); Rindermann et al. 2020 (N = 86, mean 49% genetic); Bratsberg &
  Rogeberg (rates); Bird 2021 (the bound and its assumptions); Lasker et al. (SES = parental
  education only; colour predicted from genotype); Tizard 1974 (OCR of the scan).
- **Eyferth 1961:** still unread; two secondary sources disagree on which group scored
  96.5 and which ~97 (both say no difference).

## 1. Within-group heritability of intelligence, by age; SNP heritability; the gap

**Status: settled in direction and rough size for Western samples (twin estimates);
DNA-based and within-family estimates are lower and still moving. None of it, by itself,
bears on between-group differences (Schraiber & Edge 2024; Sorjonen & Melin; §8).**

- Haworth et al. 2010, *Molecular Psychiatry* 15:1112–1120 [full]. 11,000 twin pairs,
  six studies, four countries. Heritability of g rises "from 41% in childhood (9 years) to
  55% in adolescence (12 years) and to 66% in young adulthood (17 years)". Limitation: rich
  Western samples; equal-environments assumption.
- Bouchard 2013, *Twin Research and Human Genetics* 16:923–930 [full]. Heritability
  "reaches an asymptote at about 0.80 at 18–20 years"; shared environment ~0.10 from then.
  Scope stated by the author: "Westernized industrial democracies".
- Tucker-Drob & Bates 2016, *Psychological Science* 27:138–149 [abstract]. Gene × SES
  interaction (lower heritability at low SES) "moderately sized" in US studies, "zero or
  reversed" in Western Europe and Australia.
- Pesta et al. 2020, *Intelligence* 78:101408 [full]. 16 US samples (84,897 White, 37,160
  Black, 17,678 Hispanic): heritabilities "consistently moderate to high" and not different
  across groups. The authors caution that equal heritability does not show the gap is
  genetic (as Sorjonen & Melin also note). Critique: Giangrande & Turkheimer 2022 (§8).
- DNA-based estimates:
  - Howe et al. 2022, *Nature Genetics* 54:581–592 [full]. 178,076 siblings, 19 cohorts.
    Within-sibship shrinkage: cognitive ability 22% (95% CI 6–37%), educational attainment
    (EA) 47%, height 10%. SNP heritability of cognition 0.24 (population) vs 0.14
    (within-sibship); cognition n ≈ 27,600, wide CIs.
  - Tan et al. 2025 (medRxiv 2024), "Family-GWAS reveals effects of environment and mating
    on genetic associations" [full, first 100k characters]. Cognitive performance: SNP
    heritability 0.188 (direct) vs 0.186 (population) — does not replicate Howe's
    reduction. EA: direct/population correlation 0.689; direct-effect heritability about
    half the population figure, attributed to assortative mating and "likely uncorrected
    population stratification".
  - Okbay et al. 2022 (EA4), *Nature Genetics* 54:437–449 [full, supplementary notes 5–7].
    Direct/population ratio: cognitive-performance index on cognition 0.824; EA index on EA
    0.556.
  - Lee et al. 2018 (EA3), *Nature Genetics* 50:1112–1121 [abstract]: scores explain
    "7–10% of the variance in cognitive performance".
  - Kong et al. 2018, *Science* 359:424–428 [abstract]: non-transmitted parental alleles
    predict the child's EA at 29.9% the effect of transmitted ones ("genetic nurture").
  - Plomin & von Stumm 2018, *Nature Reviews Genetics* 19:148–159 [secondary, search
    summaries].

The gap: adult twin heritability 0.5–0.8; SNP heritability ~0.15–0.25; current scores
predict roughly 5–10% of cognitive variance in European-ancestry samples. How the
shortfall divides among untagged variants, rare variants and inflated twin estimates is
unresolved.

What within-group heritability implies for groups: nothing by itself. Schraiber & Edge
2024, *PNAS* 121:e2319496121 [full, checked against the bioRxiv v2 preprint,
`2023-11-06-565864v2-full.txt`] show that aggregate within-group data "cannot separate
among-group differences into genetic and environmental components", covering
between-group heritability, Q_ST–F_ST, and admixture-based methods. Sorjonen & Melin,
"Group differences in intelligence due to environmental factors do not require differences
in genetic heritability within groups" (preprint, `h2iq-preprint.txt`) [full]: simulations
in which an adverse environment lowers one group's mean by up to 30 points leave its
heritability unchanged — equal heritability is not evidence for a genetic gap.

What would settle the open part: large within-family GWAS of cognition (not EA) in several
ancestries. That settles direct effects within populations; it does not by itself settle
anything between them.

## 2. Size and trend of the US Black–White gap; measurement invariance

**Status: the gap is real on the tests; in the main US datasets the tests measure the same
latent abilities in both groups (moderate–strong evidence; partial non-invariance in one
referred urban sample). Size: about 0.75 SD in recent child norms, about 1 SD in adult
norms, about 0.85 SD in 2010s achievement data; it widens with age. Trend: narrowed by
roughly 0.3–0.5 SD between cohorts born early in the 20th century and those born around
1970 (settled in direction for achievement data; size disputed for IQ); flat or slightly
wider for later cohorts on most series; maths gaps widened after 2020.**

Gap size:

- Neisser et al. 1996 (APA task force), *American Psychologist* 51:77–101 [full]: Black
  mean "typically about one standard deviation (about 15 points)" below White; possibly
  diminishing (Stanford-Binet 1986 norms: 13 points younger, 10 older children; NAEP
  17-year-old maths gap 1.1 SD in 1978 to .65 SD in 1990).
- Dickens & Flynn 2006, *Psychological Science* 17:913–920 [full]. Nine standardization
  samples: WISC-R/III/IV (1972, 1989, 2002), WAIS-R/III, AFQT (1980, 1997), Stanford-Binet
  (1985, 2001). Black gain on Whites 0.184 IQ points per year under age 25, about 5.5 points
  1972–2002. The gap widens with age: Black IQ was 90 at age 4 and 79 at age 23 in 1972;
  95.5 and 84.5 in 2002. Reply: Rushton & Jensen 2006 [not read]; Murray's Woodcock-Johnson
  analysis found gains of similar size at an earlier period (Nisbett et al. 2012 [full]).
- Murray 2007, *Intelligence* 35:305–318 (`murray2007.txt`) [full]. Woodcock-Johnson
  standardizations by birth cohort 1920–1991: the gap in IQ and Gc fell "on the order of
  half a standard deviation" from cohorts born in the 1920s (≥1.3 SD on his reading) to the
  mid-1960s/early 1970s, but no narrowing for IQ or Gc among those born from the late 1940s
  if testing under 40 only; slight widening after the 1960s. Other series he cites agree on
  a stop in the 1970s (NAEP narrowest for 1969–79 cohorts; WAIS 1.14 SD for 1920s cohorts,
  0.73 for 1971–75; AFQT 1.21 to 0.97 SD), with one exception he names: the WISC, 1.08 SD
  for cohorts born 1973–83 to 0.78 for 1986–96.
- Weiss & Saklofske 2020, *Personality and Individual Differences* 161:109962 [full].
  Norm samples: WISC-V White 103.5, Black 91.9, gap 11.6 points (0.77 SD); WISC-IV 11.5
  (0.77); WAIS-IV 103.2 vs 88.7, 14.5 points (0.97). Mediation (§ "environment" below).
- Murray 2021, *Facing Reality* ch. 3 (`murray-facing-reality.txt`) [full, ch. 3]. Fifty-nine
  estimates 1972–2019 from IQ norms, federal longitudinal surveys and NAEP: large narrowing
  in the 1970s–80s, trendline "nearly flat" for 1983–2019, lowest 1987 (0.69 SD); for
  2010–2019 (nine of ten measures are NAEP achievement tests) 0.85 SD, which he converts to
  White 103.35, Black 90.60. Murray sets causes aside: the chapter does not discuss them.
- Kirkegaard & Jensen 2025, *Mankind Quarterly* 66:290–310 (`meta-analysisofamerican…txt`)
  [full]. 139 US studies 1918–2017: random-effects Black mean 85 (95% CI 83.9–86.1,
  I² = 98%); 81.5 after a small-study correction; cohort, age and number of subtests were
  not significant moderators (cohort coefficient +0.037/yr, SE 0.024). Their "strong
  evidence against convergence" for post-1960 cohorts is argued in the discussion from
  other sources (Murray 2007; SAT/ACT), not shown by their own moderator test.

Achievement data:

- NAEP long-term trend 2023 [full, highlights page]: age-13 maths, Black −13 vs White −6
  points since 2020; gap 35 → 42.
- Reardon, Kalogrides & Shores 2019, *American Journal of Sociology* 124:1164–1221
  [abstract]: district gaps from "nearly zero" to more than 1.5 SD; local SES, segregation
  and schooling explain 43–72% of the geographic variation.

Measurement invariance:

- Dolan 2000; Dolan & Hamaker 2001 (WISC-R, K-ABC) [secondary, via Wicherts et al. 2004,
  full]: invariance between US Black and White children tenable.
- Dolan, Roorda & Wicherts 2004, *Intelligence* 32:155–173 [full]: in Dutch (GATB) and
  South African (JAT) data invariance "must be rejected" — it has to be tested each time.
- Lasker et al. 2019, *Psych* 1:431–459 (PNC) [full]: strict invariance held; gap
  equivalent to 14.72 IQ points, 75.6% attributed to g.
- Hu et al. 2019, *Psych* 1:240–261 (NLSY97) [abstract]: strict invariance tenable.
- Hajovsky & Chesnut 2022, *Journal of School Psychology* 93 [abstract]: WJ-IV construct
  equivalence; "little to no predictive bias".
- Graves, Smith & Nichols 2021, *Contemporary School Psychology* 25:157–169
  (`graves2020.txt`) [full]: 1,184 children referred for special education, 55% Black;
  WISC-V Picture Span and Figure Weights "not invariant by race" (partial metric
  invariance). Limitation: referred, not representative.
- Murray 2021 ch. 3 [full] and Neisser et al. 1996 [full] agree tests do not under-predict
  Black performance; Neisser: the differential is "apparently not due to any simple form
  of bias in the content or administration of the tests".

What invariance means: the test measures the same abilities in the same way; it says
nothing about why the latent means differ. Warne 2021 [full] counts it as one line of
evidence for a partly genetic gap but writes that invariance "also does not conclusively"
establish cause; Schraiber & Edge and Giangrande & Turkheimer reject the inference.

What would settle the trend: one representative IQ battery re-normed on the same design
each decade, analysed by birth cohort.

## 3. The Flynn effect and its reversal; what it implies for group gaps

**Status: settled that mean scores rose about 3 IQ points per decade in the 20th century
and that the Norwegian rise and decline are within-family, hence environmental (strong).
Settled that cohort gains and the US Black–White gap differ in measurement structure
(moderate; MGCFA comparisons). It shows environments can move scores by a gap-sized
amount; it does not show what causes the gap.**

- Pietschnig & Voracek 2015, *Perspectives on Psychological Science* 10:282–306
  [abstract]: 271 samples, ~4 million people, 31 countries; annual gains 0.41 (fluid),
  0.30 (spatial), 0.28 (full-scale), 0.21 (crystallized); smaller in recent decades.
- Bratsberg & Rogeberg 2018, *PNAS* 115:6674–6678 (`bratsberg2018.txt`) [full]. Norwegian
  conscripts born 1962–1991, brothers compared (e.g. 355,438 men in families with two scored
  brothers). Model estimates: rise 0.20 IQ points/year within families vs 0.18 across
  (1962–75); decline 0.33/year within vs 0.34 across (1975–91); ratios 1.14 and 0.98. The
  decline is therefore not compositional (not dysgenic fertility or immigration). The
  simpler fixed-effects estimate recovered less of the late decline (−0.08 vs −0.23/yr),
  which the authors attribute to selection into scoring.
- Wicherts et al. 2004, *Intelligence* 32:509–537 [full]: cohort invariance "untenable" in
  five datasets; the US Black–White MGCFA studies supported invariance; so "the nature of
  the Flynn effect is qualitatively different from the nature of B–W differences".
- Ritchie & Tucker-Drob 2018, *Psychological Science* 29:1358–1369 [abstract]: 1–5 IQ
  points per extra year of education (quasi-experimental, >600,000 people).
- Neisser et al. 1996 [full]: the Flynn effect "shows that environmental factors can
  produce differences of at least this magnitude, but that effect is mysterious in its own
  right".

How each side reads it: environmental accounts take it as proof that environments shift
means by 1 SD in two generations. Hereditarian accounts accept it as environmental but
argue it is a different kind of difference (not invariant, weak on g); Wicherts et al.
2004 — not a hereditarian source — supports that half. Neither side has shown which
environmental differences between US Black and White children, of what size, produce a
latent difference of the observed size.

What would settle it: an identified set of environmental causes whose measured group
difference times causal effect reproduces the gap on an invariant measure.

## 4. Admixture studies

**Status: open. Within self-identified US Black samples, more European genomic ancestry
goes with slightly higher scores (r ≈ 0.09–0.14; under 1% of variance). The association is
reduced but not removed by parental SES, and not removed by the colour measures used so
far (genotype-predicted). The one within-family test is underpowered and inconclusive.
Old blood-group studies found no association with weak tests. Latin-American studies that
control social conditions find the association small or gone, on other outcomes.**

Older studies:

- Loehlin, Vandenberg & Osborne 1973, *Behavior Genetics* 3:263–270 [full]. Two twin
  samples of 40 and 44 Black adolescents; European-typical blood-group alleles were not
  associated with higher scores. The authors: it "may not … be a very strong test",
  because blood-group and ability genes assort independently over generations. Nisbett
  2009 [full, Russian translation] gives r = 0.01 in one sample and −0.38 (ns) in the other.
- Scarr, Pakstis, Katz & Barker 1977, *Human Genetics* 39:69–86 [secondary, via Nisbett
  2009]: 144 Black twin pairs, r = 0.05 between IQ and estimated European ancestry,
  −0.02 controlling skin colour and SES.
- Eyferth 1961 [secondary]: German children of US soldiers; no difference by father's race.
  Neisser et al. 1996 [full] and Nisbett 2012 [full] cite it as evidence against a genetic
  account (Nisbett, via Flynn 2008, adds that the groups also matched on measured g).
  Waldman et al. 1994 [full] give 96.5 (Black fathers) vs 97.2 (White fathers); Nisbett
  2009 [full, Russian] gives the opposite labelling (96.5 White, 97 Black). Limitation
  (Nisbett, Flynn 1980): Black soldiers were an IQ-screened group; Flynn estimated the
  resulting bias at no more than ~3 points.

Genomic-ancestry studies:

- Kirkegaard et al. 2019, *Psych* 1:1–25 (PING, N = 1,369) [full]. SES measures: household
  income, guardian education and occupation. With self-identified race in the model, African
  ancestry coefficient −1.58 SD for 0→100% (CI −1.98 to −1.19), Amerindian −1.15; adding
  parental SES "reduced the validity of ancestry predictors by 36–48%". No appearance data;
  the authors note SES reduction is expected under both genetic and environmental models.
- Lasker et al. 2019, *Psych* 1:431–459 (PNC) [full]. SES = parental education only
  ("the control can only be regarded as partial"). Skin colour predicted from genotype with
  HIrisPlex-S, not observed. Among monoracial African-Americans (n = 2,179): European
  ancestry B = 0.78 SD per 0→100% (β = 0.09; adjusted R² = 0.007); with predicted colour
  B = 0.83; with SES B = 0.67 (β = 0.08). The authors: "we cannot rule out many types of
  confounding environmental variables with this research design." Their naïve PGS figure:
  20–25% of the gap; a between-group heritability of 50–70% "depending on the model" from
  an admixture formula.
- Hu et al. 2019, *Psych* 1:240–261 (NLSY97) [abstract]: parent-reported European ancestry
  r = 0.137 with g among Black respondents; darker interviewer-rated skin r = −0.112; colour
  predicted g between full-sibling pairs (β = −0.176) but not within them (β = 0.027,
  225 pairs).
- Fuerst, Shibaev & Kirkegaard 2023, *Mankind Quarterly* 63:527–600 (ABCD, N = 10,245)
  (`4-fuerstgenetichypothesis-docx.txt`) [full]. Group differences in g "completely
  explained by genetic ancestry" in their models; brain volume mediates ~15% of the
  ancestry–g association. **Within-family test:** European ancestry on g within sibships
  b = 0.62 (SE 0.55; 2,291 siblings) vs 0.93 in the full sample (33% shrinkage), not
  significant; the authors: such analyses are "highly underpowered and, as such,
  unreliable". On PGS: "it is uncertain to what extent eduPGS differences correspond to
  causally relevant genetic differences".
- Warne 2020, *Mankind Quarterly* 60 (N = 193) [not read].

Latin America:

- Wehby & Trujillo 2017, *Infant Behavior and Development* 46 [abstract]: infant gaps by
  reported ancestry "largely explained" by location and household characteristics.
- Lima-Costa et al. 2018, *J. American Geriatrics Society* 66 [abstract]: highest African
  ancestry quintile β = −0.73 MMSE points at baseline, only among the least educated; no
  association with decline.
- Llibre-Guerra et al. 2024, *Alzheimer's & Dementia* 20 [abstract]: after social
  determinants, ancestry "shows no link to dementia".
- These outcomes (infant screening, MMSE, dementia) are not childhood IQ.

The confound dispute:

- Hereditarian reading (Lasker 2019; Fuerst 2023; Warne 2021, all [full]): ancestry
  predicts g within groups beyond SES and colour; PGS partly mediates it.
- Critical reading: Bird, Jackson & Winston 2024, *American Psychologist* 79:497–508
  (`confronting-scientific-racism…txt`) [full]: Lasker's colour covariate is "predicted from
  the genotype data using an unreliable method for admixed populations" and "not a
  sufficient control for colorism"; SES control was parental education only; PGS are biased
  in admixed individuals; hence "no evidence that racial IQ gaps are genetic or
  intransigent can be taken from admixture studies". Their labels for the work ("racial
  hereditarian research", "scientific racism", "pseudo-quantitative") are theirs, 2024.
  Schraiber & Edge 2024 [full, preprint]: the regression slope on global ancestry gives
  "only … the sum of the genetic and environmental effects of global ancestry, not their
  relative contribution or even their direction".

Governance note (reproducibility, not evidence about any coefficient): in 2026 the ABCD
consortium wrote that an applicant "gave misleading information" to obtain data later used
for race-difference work; NIH revoked his access (ABCD letter [full]).

What would settle it: a well-powered sibling-difference admixture regression (tens of
thousands of admixed sibling pairs) removes family-level confounds — parental SES,
neighbourhood, family-level colourism — but not treatment that responds to each sibling's
own appearance (Schraiber & Edge). Adding observed skin colour and testing local ancestry
at loci unrelated to pigmentation narrows that gap; no design fully closes it. Fuerst et
al.'s 2,291-sibling attempt has a confidence interval spanning zero to about the full-sample
value.

## 5. Polygenic scores: shrinkage, portability, population comparisons

**Status: settled that current cognitive and education scores are partly inflated by
indirect effects, assortative mating and stratification (EA strongly, cognition less), and
lose most of their accuracy in African-ancestry samples; therefore settled that mean score
differences between continental ancestries cannot now be read as genetic-potential
differences. Open whether any genetic difference exists; no current PGS study can answer.**

Portability:

- Martin et al. 2019, *Nature Genetics* 51:584–591 [abstract]: scores "several times more
  accurate" in European-ancestry individuals.
- Okbay et al. 2022 [full, supplement]: EA4 index incremental R² 1.3% (HRS, African
  ancestry, N = 2,507) and 2.3% (Add Health, N = 1,716) — "89% and 85% attenuation"; the
  loss exceeds what LD and allele frequencies predict (predicted relative accuracy 35%).
- Privé et al. 2022, *AJHG* 109:12–23 [abstract]: portability "drops off within European
  ancestries" and globally with genetic distance.
- Ding et al. 2023, *Nature* 618:774–781 [full, abstract and introduction]: accuracy falls
  individual by individual with genetic distance (r = −0.95 across traits).
- Mostafavi et al. 2020, *eLife* 9:e48376 [full, abstract]: accuracy varies within one
  ancestry by SES, age and sex.
- Fuerst et al. 2023 [full]: eduPGS validity for Black Americans about one-third lower than
  for Whites (b 0.16 vs 0.26).

Population comparisons:

- Piffer 2019, *Psych* 1:55–75 [abstract]: allele-frequency "polygenic selection factor"
  correlates r ≈ 0.9 with national IQ across 26 (and 53) populations; European GWAS
  weights, ecological.
- Lasker et al. 2019 [full]: eduPGS gap d = 1.89 between groups; "as much as 20%–25% … can
  be naïvely explained" (their word). Bird et al. 2024 report Lasker as claiming "over 60%";
  that figure corresponds to Lasker's separate admixture-formula estimate (50–70%), not to
  the PGS.

Critiques:

- Berg & Coop 2014, *PLoS Genetics* 10:e1004412 [abstract]: Q_X test.
- Sohail et al. 2019, *eLife* 8:e39702 [full, abstract] and Berg et al. 2019, *eLife*
  8:e39725 [full, abstract]: height selection signals were artefacts of stratification;
  population-level PGS differences "should be treated with caution".
- Bird 2021, *American Journal of Physical Anthropology* 175:465–476 (`bird2021.txt`)
  [full]. 1000 Genomes African and European samples: no excess PGS differentiation with
  within-family effect sizes; F_ST of cognition SNPs (0.112) not above controls. Bound: with
  Piffer's national IQs (Africa 68.4, Europe 99.2), at most 3.6% (h² 0.15), 8.4% (0.35) or
  12% (0.5) of the among-group variance is additive genetic, and the expected genetic
  difference is "at most 4.7–8.5 IQ points", in either direction. Bird's own caveats: "a
  rough calculation" without uncertainty measures, assuming neutrality and equal effects
  across populations — "best viewed as a rough upper bound". It is a bound under
  neutrality: evidence of directional selection on cognition-associated alleles (Akbari
  2026, within West Eurasia) does not test that assumption between Africa and Europe but
  shows such selection is detectable. The comparison is national/continental, not US
  Black–White.
- Blanc & Berg 2025, *Genetics* 230:iyaf071 [abstract]: residual confounding biases
  PGS–ancestry tests.
- Coop & Przeworski 2022, *Evolution* 76:846–853 [full, relevant sections]: no bright line
  between within and between ancestry comparisons; current EA scores "neither interpretable
  nor particularly meaningful" beyond prediction. Harden 2022 reply [abstract]: agrees
  present scores do not support between-group inference.
- Akbari et al. 2026, *Nature* [abstract]: ~15,836 ancient West Eurasians; one-SD changes
  over ten millennia in combinations of alleles that today predict higher cognitive
  performance; "it remains unclear how these relate to phenotypes that were adaptive in the
  past". Says nothing about present African–European differences.

What would settle it: within-family GWAS of cognition in large African-ancestry and admixed
samples, scores validated within families in each ancestry, then compared. None exists at
the needed size.

## 6. Adoption studies across groups

**Status: open; the data are few, small and confounded with pre-adoptive history, and
the original authors themselves say they cannot separate genes from environment.**

- Minnesota Transracial Adoption Study:
  - Scarr & Weinberg 1976, *American Psychologist* 31:726–739 [full]. 101 White families.
    At ~7: Black/interracial adoptees 106.3 (n = 130), adopted White 111.5, biological
    children 116.7. Two Black birth parents 96.8 (n = 29) vs one Black and one White 109.0
    (n = 68); the former were adopted at 32.3 months on average vs 8.9, with poorer and more
    numerous placements and less-educated birth mothers (10.9 vs 12.4 years).
  - Weinberg, Scarr & Waldman 1992, *Intelligence* 16:117–135 [full]. At ~17 (tests and
    norms changed; all family members including parents scored lower): biological 109.4,
    Black/interracial 96.8, adopted White 105.6, Black/White 98.5, Black/Black 89.4.
    Adoptees and biological children did not differ in IQ change. Birth mother's race
    "remained the best single predictor" of adoptee IQ, a relation that "may be due, in
    large part, to … unmeasured, social characteristics associated with biological mothers'
    race". Authors' conclusion: the social environment "maintains a dominant role" in the
    average IQ of the Black and interracial children.
  - Waldman, Weinberg & Scarr 1994, *Intelligence* 19:29–44 (`racial-group-differences-in-iq-in-the-mi.txt`)
    [full]. Reply to Levin and Lynn: the findings "do not speak directly to genetic and
    environmental etiologies" because race and adoptive experience are confounded; Flynn
    corrections should be made individually and were promised for a later paper (not found).
    Their judgment: the balance "although not conclusive, favors a predominantly
    environmental etiology".
  - Hereditarian reading: Lynn (as quoted by Neisser 1997 [full]): Black adoptees
    "registered no IQ gains"; the ordering White > mixed > Black persists in the same homes.
    Rushton & Jensen 2005 [full] count it for the genetic model.
  - Neisser 1997 [full]: no clear evidence for the genetic hypothesis, but the data "argue
    against certain versions of the environmental hypothesis". Thomas 2017, *Journal of
    Intelligence* 5:1 [full]: attrition inflated the White follow-up mean by ~3.8 points;
    adjusted, White vs Black/Black 11.7 points at 17 vs 14.7 at 7.
- Tizard 1974, *Nature* 247:316 (`tizard1974.txt`, OCR of the scan) [full]. English
  residential nurseries. Study 1 (85 children, ages 2–4): no significant difference except a
  non-verbal score favouring non-white children. Study 2 (64 children at 4.5, WPPSI): in
  institutions White 101.2, mixed 109.3, Black 105.6; adopted White 113.0 (n = 17), mixed
  119.9 (n = 7); all differences non-significant. Tizard: "genetic aspects were neither
  controlled nor adequately known". Very small samples, young age.
- Moore 1986, *Developmental Psychology* 22:317–326 [secondary, via Waldman 1994 and Nisbett
  2012]: Black and mixed children adopted by White families scored about 13.5 points above
  those adopted by Black families; mixed vs Black difference non-significant.
- Eyferth 1961: §4.
- Nisbett et al. 2012 [full]: adoption from working-class to middle-class homes raises IQ by
  12–18 points (within-group studies).

What would settle it: a large adoption-at-birth study with documented independence of
ancestry from placement quality, tested in adulthood. It will not be run; the question has
to go to genomic designs (§4–5).

## 7. National IQ datasets and the causal debate

**Status: settled that national averages of measured skills differ widely and correlate
strongly with national income (strong; corroborated by harmonized school-test data).
Settled that the lowest entries rest on unrepresentative samples and are biased low for
sub-Saharan Africa (moderate–strong; ~67–70 vs ~80–82 on UK norms). Open on cause: the
arrow runs both ways, schooling and health demonstrably move scores, and no design has
isolated a genetic component of national differences.**

- Lynn & Vanhanen 2002 [full, via the course brief]: 81 measured, 104 estimated; r ≈ 0.7
  with GDP per head.
- Lynn & Becker 2019, *The Intelligence of Nations* (Ulster Institute)
  (`the-intelligence-of-nations…txt`) [full, chs. 1–2 and the genetics section]. Based on
  NIQ dataset v1.3.1, "a revision and reissuing" of Lynn & Vanhanen; samples coded
  representative / normative / selected, averaged per country. Quotes Rindermann's regional
  cognitive-ability means (Sub-Saharan Africa 69, Northeast Asia 103) and his r = .82 with
  log GDP for 161 countries. A table of "genetic correlates" (genetic distance, Piffer's
  allele frequencies) is ecological.
- Wicherts, Dolan & van der Maas 2010, *Intelligence* 38:1–20 [full]: systematic review of
  non-Raven tests; Africans average "approximately 82" on UK norms, converging with
  achievement-based estimates (~82); measurement bias for Africans untested in most data.
  Rejoinder, *Intelligence* 38:30–37 [full]: ~80 overall including Raven; Lynn and
  Meisenberg's representativeness judgments tracked "the average IQ in the sample" rather
  than objective sampling features. Lynn & Meisenberg 2010, *Intelligence* 38:21–29
  [not read; no open copy found].
- Sear 2022, PsyArXiv [abstract]: the Lynn & Becker 2019 data are mostly unrepresentative
  convenience samples with "arbitrary adjustments"; its further claim that comparable
  measures are impossible in principle is not supported in the abstract.
- Angrist et al. 2021, *Nature* 592:403–408 [abstract]: harmonized learning outcomes, 164
  countries; human capital explains "a fifth to half" of income differences.
- Eppig, Fincher & Thornhill 2010, *Proc. R. Soc. B* [abstract]: national IQ correlates
  r = −0.76 to −0.82 with parasite stress, robust to GDP and education controls;
  ecological.
- Rindermann, Becker & Coyle 2016 [full] and 2020 [full]: §8.

Causal direction: Lynn & Vanhanen "assume" IQ is causal (p. 87). Evidence that schooling
and health raise scores is quasi-experimental or historical; evidence that skills raise
growth is the growth literature. Neither partitions national differences into genetic and
environmental parts.

What would settle it: representative samples; invariance tested across countries; national
means tracked through development.

## 8. Expert opinion and consensus statements

**Status: no consensus either way. The APA task force (1996) said no adequate explanation
was available, with less support for the genetic than for the cultural accounts; the 2012
update said there was no new direct evidence and the existing direct evidence did not
support a genetic hypothesis; surveys with low response rates split near 50/50 on average,
with very wide spread.**

- Neisser et al. 1996 [full]: "There is not much direct evidence on this point, but what
  little there is fails to support the genetic hypothesis" (citing Eyferth and the
  blood-group studies); high within-group heritability "has no necessary implications" for
  the gap; summary: cultural explanations "so far none has been conclusively supported",
  "even less empirical support for a genetic interpretation", "no adequate explanation"
  available. Neisser 1997
  [full]: whether the environmental difference suffices "has no scientific answer at
  present".
- Nisbett et al. 2012, *American Psychologist* 67:130–159 [full]: the 1996 conclusion
  "stands today: There has been no new direct evidence"; reports Dickens & Flynn's 5.5-point
  gain and a g gap narrowed by 5.13 points; gap ~5 points at age 4 and ~17 at 24 (cohort or
  age effect undetermined). Nisbett individually: the direct evidence indicates the
  difference is "entirely" environmental (Nisbett 2005 commentary [full]: the genetic
  contribution "is nil").
- Rushton & Jensen 2005, *Psychology, Public Policy, and Law* 11:235–294 [full, abstract,
  framing and reply conclusion]: ten categories of evidence favour a "50% genetic–50%
  environmental" model over "culture-only"; their reply (pp. 328–336) puts the fit at 50–80%
  genetic. Commentaries in the same issue [read: Nisbett full opening; Sternberg and
  Suzuki & Aronson abstracts]: Nisbett — the evidence "most relevant" shows the genetic
  contribution is nil; Sternberg — no policy implications follow; Suzuki & Aronson —
  cultural malleability.
- Warne 2021, *American Journal of Psychology* 134:479–501 [full]: five lines argue the gap
  is ">0%" genetic; "the data are not fully conclusive" and the degree is unknown; scope
  US-born native English speakers.
- Rindermann, Becker & Coyle 2016, *Frontiers in Psychology* 7:399 [full]: international
  differences; N = 71 (20% response); genes the largest single factor (17%), education
  largest when summed (21.6%); genes had by far the largest SD (23.9).
- Rindermann, Becker & Coyle 2020, *Intelligence* 78:101406 (`survey-of-expert-opinion…txt`)
  [full]. 265 responses (19.7% response rate); on the US Black–White gap, N = 86 rated the
  genetic share: mean 48.8% (SD 31.3); 16% chose 100% environmental, 6% 100% genetic.
  Belief in a genetic share correlated with being male (r = .50) and politically right
  (r = .48). Self-selected sample; the authors list the low response rate.
- Giangrande & Turkheimer 2022, *Perspectives on Psychological Science* 17:696–710 [full,
  preprint]: "severe … flaws in every step" of Pesta et al. 2020; their title calls it
  "fringe science" (2022). Pesta et al. 2023 reply, *Intelligence* 98:101737 [not read; no
  open copy found].
- Haier 2017, Quillette [full]: "direct evidence from modern genetic studies of group
  differences is not yet available"; a partial genetic contribution "not unreasonable" as a
  hypothesis. Turkheimer, Harden & Nisbett 2017 (Vox) [not read; site blocked].
- Lala & Feldman 2024, *PNAS* 121:e2322874121 [abstract].
- Sesardic 2000, *Philosophy of Science* 67:580–602 (philosophical defence of the
  hereditarian inference) [not read; no open copy obtained].

Reading: the camps disagree less on the data than on the default. Warne and Haier concede
the evidence is inconclusive and the size unknown; the APA task force conceded no
environmental account had been shown sufficient.

## 9. What 2022–2026 added

- Within-family genetics matured (Howe 2022; Okbay 2022; Tan 2025): direct effects on
  cognition are real and somewhat smaller than population GWAS suggested.
- Portability measured on a continuum (Ding 2023).
- Formal proof that aggregate within-group data, including admixture methods, cannot
  partition a between-group difference (Schraiber & Edge 2024).
- The first within-family admixture estimate for g (Fuerst et al. 2023): positive,
  non-significant, underpowered.
- Ancient-DNA selection scans report directional change in cognition-associated scores
  within West Eurasia (Akbari 2026).
- Achievement gaps in maths widened after 2020 (NAEP LTT 2023) — a change on a two-to-three
  year scale, which no genetic account can produce.
- No well-powered within-family admixture study or multi-ancestry within-family GWAS of
  cognition has been applied to the Black–White gap.

## Overall answer, in plain words

1. Within populations, measured intelligence is substantially heritable (about half in
   childhood, more in adulthood, in Western samples) — settled. DNA-based estimates are
   lower, and part of what polygenic scores capture is family environment and population
   structure, especially for education.
2. The US Black–White gap on IQ tests is real on the measures: about 0.75 SD in recent child
   norms, about 1 SD in adult norms, 0.85 SD on 2010s achievement tests, larger at older
   ages. It narrowed between cohorts born early in the 20th century and those born around
   1970, then stopped narrowing on most series.
3. Whether any of it is genetic is not known. No study has measured a genetic contribution
   with a design that excludes confounding; the one within-family admixture test is too
   small to tell. No study has shown that measured environmental differences account for all
   of it: parental education and income account for roughly half to two-thirds of the child
   WISC gap and leave about 6 points.
4. Two limits are established. The gap is not wholly genetic as it now stands: it narrowed
   by several points within decades, varies from near zero to 1.5 SD across school
   districts, and moved after 2020. And no evidence excludes a genetic share.
5. The indirect evidence offered for a genetic share (heritability, invariance, Spearman
   pattern, admixture correlations, PGS means) is weak about causes: some of it is logically
   uninformative, some confounded, some invalid across ancestries at present. The evidence
   offered against (blood-group studies, Eyferth, Tizard, Moore, MTRAS reinterpretations) is
   small, old, and in places confounded.
6. National averages: the association between measured skills and income is strong; the
   worst national-IQ entries are unreliable; causation runs both ways; nothing isolates a
   genetic component.
7. Status: open. The evidence does not establish a direction or a proportion. Expert
   opinion is split around an average of one half with a very wide spread, and that
   opinion correlates with the experts' politics and sex.

## Claims the lessons must NOT make

Not supported, in the hereditarian direction:

- "The gap is X% genetic" for any X — including Rushton & Jensen's 50–80% or the survey
  mean of ~49%. No design has estimated it.
- "High heritability within groups implies the gap is probably genetic", or "equal
  heritability across groups shows it". Shown invalid (Schraiber & Edge; Sorjonen & Melin).
- "Invariance / Spearman's hypothesis shows the gap is genetic."
- "Polygenic scores show lower genetic potential in Africans or Black Americans."
- "Admixture studies show genes cause the gap" — correlations under 1% of variance,
  confounded; the within-family estimate is non-significant.
- "The Minnesota study proves the gap survives a good environment" — its authors deny it;
  confounded by age at adoption and placement history.
- "Sub-Saharan African IQ is about 70" stated as a measurement.
- "The gap has never narrowed" — every cohort series shows narrowing before ~1970s cohorts.

Not supported, in the environmental direction:

- "Science has shown the gap is entirely environmental" / "genes play no role" — the APA
  task force said no adequate explanation exists.
- "Test bias explains the gap" — main US datasets are invariant and do not under-predict.
- "The Flynn effect proves the gap is environmental" — it proves environments can produce
  gaps of that size; it differs in structure from the gap.
- "SES explains the gap" — measured SES accounts for part, leaving ~6 points in children
  and more in adults; and SES is itself partly downstream of ability.
- "Admixture and adoption studies show no ancestry effect" — recent genomic studies find
  small positive associations; older nulls were weak tests.
- "Polygenic scores have shown no genetic difference" — Bird 2021 is a bound under
  neutrality and stated assumptions, not a measurement of zero.
- "The gap is closing" stated of the present — it stopped narrowing for cohorts born after
  the 1970s on most series and widened in maths after 2020.
- "National IQ differences are artefacts" — the association survives better data; what
  fails is the precision of the lowest entries and any causal reading.

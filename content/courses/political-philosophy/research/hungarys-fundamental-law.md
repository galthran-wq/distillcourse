# Research brief: hungarys-fundamental-law (Hungary's Fundamental Law of 2011 read against the post-liberals)

status: planned
module: after-liberalism
type: core
goal: Apply Deneen's, Hazony's and Vermeule's accounts of post-liberal government to the text and practice of Hungary's 2011 constitution and its amendments, and judge what the case shows about the theories

## ARC (L10)

- **Inherits:** The module's four theories, each with its own lesson: Deneen's diagnosis that
  liberalism failed by succeeding and his prescription — "Not a better theory, but better
  practices", local and pre-political, with his own warning that a degraded citizenry will cry out
  for "a strongman" and that "populist nationalist authoritarianism" is a plausible successor
  (`deneen-post-liberalism`; `post-liberalism`, `liberal-anthropology`, `anticulture`,
  `liberalocracy`); Hazony's `order-of-national-states`, the nation as tribes bound by mutual
  loyalty prior to any state, his seven principles (non-transference of powers to universal
  institutions; protection of minority nations) and the "majority nation" thesis
  (`hazony-virtue-of-nationalism`; `imperialism-of-universal-doctrine`); Vermeule's
  `common-good-constitutionalism` and `lex-and-ius` — law read as a reasoned ordinance for the
  common good, regime form left to "determination of the constitution", deference to the public
  authority, and the safeguard placed in reason and good faith (`vermeule-common-good-constitutionalism`);
  de Benoist's right to difference, his federal Europe of peoples and his rejection of "a 'Europe
  of Nations'" (`de-benoist-european-new-right`; `metapolitics`, `ethnopluralism`). From earlier
  modules: `oikophilia` and `pre-political-loyalty` (`the-nation-and-its-defenders`); Burke's
  `entailed-inheritance` and `prejudice` (`burke-reflections`); Oakeshott's suspicion of the
  written plan (`oakeshott-rationalism-in-politics`, `rationalism-in-politics`); Aquinas on
  `unjust-law-not-law` (`aquinas-law-and-kingship`); Schmitt's `state-of-exception` and Article 48
  as the course's earlier test of emergency government (`schmitt-political-theology`,
  `article-48-weimar`); Polybius's `checks-and-balances` and Madison's `faction-madison`
  (`polybius-anacyclosis-and-rome`, `federalist-one-people-and-the-extended-republic`); Tocqueville's
  `tyranny-of-the-majority` (`tocqueville-tyranny-of-majority-and-soft-despotism`); the Hungarian
  fence of September 2015 (`the-2015-crisis-read-three-ways`); the Böckenförde question
  (`bockenforde-dilemma`, `murray-strange-death-of-europe`).
- **Contributes:** One move: the module's theories taken to one real constitution, read clause by
  clause, with the record of how it was made, amended and applied, and with the formal objections
  of the bodies that judged it. The reader finds (i) where the text embodies each theory (Hazony's
  nation before the state and its refusal of universal institutions; Vermeule's purposive reading
  "in accordance with … the public good"; Deneen's family, nation and faith as pre-liberal goods);
  (ii) where it contradicts each (a state-made programme where Deneen asked for local practice; EU
  membership written into the text where Hazony forbids transferring powers; a "Europe of Nations"
  where de Benoist wants a federation of peoples; a two-thirds majority that writes the rules of its
  own review where Vermeule's safeguard is the rulers' good faith); and (iii) what the critics
  (the Venice Commission, the European Union's institutions) and the defenders (the government and
  its constitutional lawyers) each establish and each assume. No verdict on Hungary; a judgement on
  what the case shows about the THEORIES.
- **Hands off:** `the-argument-assembled` takes the module's answer — that every post-liberal
  theory needs a people already held together by something liberalism cannot produce, and that
  Hungary tried to write that something into its constitution — to the course's last open question:
  the Böckenförde dilemma, whether a free state can produce the presuppositions it lives on.
- **Spine step:** "After liberalism: Deneen says it failed by succeeding, Hazony defends the order
  of national states, Vermeule the common good, de Benoist the right to difference — Hungary's
  constitution as the case — and the whole argument is assembled"
- **APPLICATION spec (L16):**
  - *Artefact read:* the Fundamental Law of Hungary (Magyarország Alaptörvénye), adopted 18 April
    2011, in force 1 January 2012, in its official English translation — the National Avowal, the
    Foundation articles (A–U), Freedom and Responsibility (I–XXXI), The State (1–54) — and its
    amendments, chiefly the Fourth (2013), the Seventh (2018) and the Ninth (2020); the Venice
    Commission's opinions on the 2011 text (2011) and on the Fourth and Ninth Amendments (2013,
    2021); the European Union's proceedings (the 2012 Court of Justice judgment on judges'
    retirement; the European Parliament's Article 7 resolution of 2018; the rule-of-law
    conditionality regulation and its 2022 application); the national election results 2010–2022
    (votes vs seats).
  - *Module results it uses:* `post-liberalism` and Deneen's three steps (Deneen); the nation as
    prior to the state, `order-of-national-states`, principles 6 (minority nations) and 7
    (non-transference), the majority nation (Hazony); `common-good-constitutionalism`,
    `lex-and-ius`, determination of the constitution, deference, the forfeiture clause (Vermeule);
    `ethnopluralism` and the federal Europe (de Benoist); `oikophilia`, `pre-political-loyalty`
    (Scruton/Miller, reinforced).
  - *Departures from the idealisation the lesson must name:* (1) every theory speaks of a people,
    a tradition or a law; this text was written and adopted in about four months by one
    parliamentary majority, without a referendum, and claims continuity with a thousand-year
    "historical constitution" by an act of enactment — Burke's inheritance claimed through the
    instrument Oakeshott called rationalist; (2) Deneen's post-liberalism is local practice before
    theory; Hungary's is a national programme written into the supreme law; (3) Hazony's national
    state transfers no powers to universal institutions; the Fundamental Law's own Article E
    authorises Hungary's participation in the European Union and the exercise of competences
    through it; (4) Vermeule's common good is safeguarded by reasoned ordinances in good faith;
    the case includes amendments that reinstated provisions the Constitutional Court had annulled
    and repealed the Court's earlier case law — whether that is determination or will is Vermeule's
    own line; (5) the text is not the practice: elections, media and courts are governed mostly by
    cardinal (two-thirds) statutes, not by the Fundamental Law itself, and the lesson keeps the two
    apart; (6) the people's "we" in the National Avowal is the nation, including Hungarians abroad,
    while the electorate is the citizens — the theories of the module do not say which is the
    constituent people.

## EXPLANATION PLAN (L0)

Opening (course.yaml, verbatim): April 2011, the Hungarian parliament adopts a constitution whose
"National Avowal" praises the king who founded the state a thousand years ago, recognises the role
of Christianity in preserving the nation and declares the family and the nation the principal
frameworks of life together; amendments follow; post-liberals cite it as a model, critics as a
warning; the lesson reads the text against the module's theories. (See Pitfalls for one wording
problem in the opening: marriage was already defined in the 2011 text.)

Anchors before link 1 (L11), as a short paragraph and the timeline figure, not a history lesson:
Hungary, a country of about ten million in the Carpathian basin; Saint Stephen, crowned about 1000,
and the Holy Crown that bears his name; the Treaty of Trianon (1920), by which Hungary lost about
two-thirds of its territory and a large share of its Hungarian-speaking population to neighbouring
states; German occupation (19 March 1944) and the deportation of Hungary's Jews that spring and
summer; the communist constitution of 1949; the revolution of 1956, crushed by Soviet troops; the
negotiated transition of 1989, which rewrote the 1949 constitution almost entirely but kept its
name and date; the 2010 election, in which one alliance won about 53% of the list vote and over
two-thirds of the seats — the share needed to change the constitution.

1. **What kind of document is this, and how was it made?** The structure: the National Avowal (a
   preamble in the first person plural); Foundation (lettered articles A–U); Freedom and
   Responsibility (Roman-numbered rights); The State (numbered institutions); closing provisions.
   Two features the lesson needs first: (a) Article R(3) gives the Avowal legal force — the
   Fundamental Law is to be interpreted "in accordance with their purposes, the National Avowal
   … and the achievements of our historical constitution" (verify wording); (b) the two-thirds
   rules — amendment by two-thirds of ALL MPs (Article S), and some fifty matters reserved to
   "cardinal Acts" passed by two-thirds of MPs PRESENT (Article T(4)). The making: drafted by the
   governing alliance, the main opposition parties boycotting the drafting, a questionnaire to
   households in place of a referendum, adopted 18 April 2011 by 262 votes to 44 (verify), signed
   25 April, in force 1 January 2012. Because: a reader cannot judge any theory against the text
   until he sees that the preamble is law and that the majority that wrote it could change it.
2. **Who is "we"? The nation before the state.** Read the Avowal's first sentences: "God bless
   the Hungarians"; "We, the members of the Hungarian nation"; Saint Stephen "made our country a
   part of Christian Europe one thousand years ago"; "We recognise the role of Christianity in
   preserving nationhood. We value the various religious traditions of our country"; "the
   nationalities living with us form part of the Hungarian political community"; "the family and
   the nation constitute the principal framework of our coexistence"; the Holy Crown "embodies the
   constitutional continuity of Hungary's statehood and the unity of the nation" (all: verify
   against the official translation). Then Article D — responsibility "for the fate of Hungarians
   living beyond its borders". Read with `<Ref the-nation-and-its-defenders>` (`pre-political-loyalty`,
   `oikophilia`): the constituent "we" is a nation defined by memory, faith and language, not the
   body of citizens — and it includes people who are not citizens of the state and are citizens of
   other states. Then HAZONY (`<Ref hazony-virtue-of-nationalism>`): the nation as prior to the
   state; a "majority nation" whose cohesion lets it grant rights to minority nations (the Avowal's
   "nationalities", recognised by law — thirteen, verify — and Article XXIX); public recognition of
   God and a moral minimum (his Protestant construction, l.412–422). The fit is close; say so.
   The departure the seam must state: Hazony's principle 7 forbids transferring powers to
   universal institutions (l.1297), and the same text's Article E authorises the exercise of
   competences through the European Union — a national state inside the union Hazony calls
   "in effect a universal state". The 2018 amendment's answer is "constitutional identity"
   (Article R(4) and the limits in Article E as amended — verify): membership, but with a
   reserved core. Because: the text is Hazony's nation written into a member state of an empire,
   by Hazony's own definition.
3. **What does the text say law is for? Vermeule's reading.** Article 28 (verify wording): courts
   shall interpret laws "primarily in accordance with their purpose and with the Fundamental Law",
   and "it shall be presumed that they serve moral and economical purposes which are in accordance
   with common sense and the public good". Set beside `<Ref vermeule-common-good-constitutionalism>`
   — lex read inside a purposive order, the presumption of a public good. The Avowal's "the common
   goal of citizens and the State is to achieve the highest possible measure of well-being, safety,
   order, justice and liberty" against Vermeule's triptych of justice, peace and abundance. Article
   O — every person "responsible for him- or herself" and "obliged to contribute to the performance
   of state and community tasks" — against Vermeule's duties and solidarity. Article L (marriage as
   "the union of a man and a woman", 2011; the family as "the basis of the survival of the nation")
   and Article II (the life of the foetus protected "from the moment of conception") against
   Vermeule's natural institutions. The fit with the METHOD is real. Then Vermeule's own test:
   "determination of the constitution" leaves regime form open (l.737) — but determination is
   reasoned specification by an authority that acts in good faith, and arbitrary rule "is not law"
   (`<Ref aquinas-law-and-kingship>`, `unjust-law-not-law`). Here enters the FOURTH AMENDMENT
   (2013): provisions the Constitutional Court had annulled (among them, verify each: limits on
   political advertising, the recognition of churches by parliament, the criminalising of rough
   sleeping, conditions on student grants) were written into the constitution itself, and the
   Court's decisions from before 2012 were repealed. Pose the question exactly as the theory
   forces it: is a constitutional amendment that overturns the Court's reading a DETERMINATION
   (the authority specifying what the common good requires, as Vermeule's deference allows) or
   WILL (the majority removing the reasoned check — Vermeule's "law as will" in the form he
   attributed to his opponents)? The theory has no external test; its test is the authority's
   reasons. The lesson puts the government's stated reasons (from its reply to the Venice
   Commission) beside the Commission's objection and leaves the reader to apply Vermeule's own
   criterion.
4. **Is this Deneen's post-liberalism?** The pre-liberal goods are all here: family, nation, faith,
   memory across generations ("We deny any statute of limitations" for crimes against the nation;
   "our fundamental cohesive values are fidelity, faith and love" — verify), against Deneen's
   anticulture (`<Ref deneen-post-liberalism>`: nature, time, place). The departure is Deneen's
   own: his steps are no return, no new ideology, practice before theory — "Not a better theory,
   but better practices" — and a constitution that commands Christian culture and defines the
   family is theory written by the state. And his own book predicts the strongman and warns that
   "populist nationalist authoritarianism" may succeed liberalism and is "not to be wished for in
   the form it is likely to take" (l.923–925). Two readings, each stated at full strength: (a) the
   case is the strongman Deneen predicted, the demotic answer of a degraded citizenry; (b) the case
   is the national-scale protection of the local practices Deneen wants, against the "pincer" of a
   distant state (Brussels) and a global market. The lesson does not choose; it shows which facts
   each reading needs (how the institutions of local self-government fared — municipal powers were
   in fact centralised after 2011, e.g. schools taken over by the state in 2013: verify — favours
   (a); the family-policy and housing programmes, which are statutory, favour (b) only if they
   strengthened households rather than the state's reach).
5. **De Benoist's Europe or Hungary's? The theory furthest from the case.** The manifesto rejects
   "a 'Europe of Nations', a mere assemblage of national egos" (`<Ref de-benoist-european-new-right>`);
   the Hungarian government's European programme is precisely a Europe of nations. Where they meet:
   restriction of immigration (the 2018 amendment, Article XIV(1): "No alien population shall be
   settled in Hungary" — verify) and recognition of nationalities as collective cultures (Article
   XXIX; the Act on the Rights of Nationalities) — de Benoist's communitarian model applied to old
   minorities, not to new immigrants. One paragraph; the point is that "post-liberal" names at
   least two incompatible maps of Europe.
6. **The critics at full strength.** In order of what they establish:
   - **Process** (Venice Commission 2011): speed, lack of inclusiveness, the opposition's absence;
     a constitution "of one party" (state as the Commission's concern in its words once obtained).
   - **Lock-in**: cardinal acts extend a two-thirds majority's choices (family, tax, pensions,
     elections, media, courts) beyond the next election — a later simple majority cannot change
     them. Draw the three tiers (figure 4).
   - **The Court and the courts**: the Court's review of budget and tax laws limited; its earlier
     case law repealed (2013); the judges' retirement age cut from 70 to 62 in 2011–12, removing
     a large cohort of senior judges at once, which the EU's Court of Justice held unlawful
     (C-286/12) — the Commission's and the Court's findings are the record.
   - **Votes and seats**: the electoral law of 2011 (a cardinal act) — with about 45% of the list
     vote in 2014 and 49% in 2018 the alliance again held two-thirds of the seats (figure 3; data
     from valasztas.hu). Polybius's and Madison's question (`<Ref polybius-anacyclosis-and-rome>`
     `checks-and-balances`; `<Ref federalist-one-people-and-the-extended-republic>`
     `faction-madison`): which part of this constitution can block the part that holds two-thirds?
   - **History and the 1944 sentence**: the Avowal dates the loss of self-determination to 19 March
     1944 (German occupation) and its restoration to 2 May 1990 (verify wording). Critics read it as
     placing the deportations of 1944 — carried out with Hungarian state organs under occupation —
     outside the Hungarian state's responsibility, and the interwar state's anti-Jewish laws
     (1938–41) outside the account; defenders read it as a legal statement about sovereignty under
     occupation. Present both; the sourcing drill (exercise 2) is built on it.
   - **The European Union**: the Parliament's Article 7(1) resolution (2018); the conditionality
     regulation upheld by the Court (2022) and funds suspended. State what each instrument
     establishes (a political majority's finding; a court's ruling on the regulation's validity; a
     Council decision) — they are three different kinds of evidence.
7. **The defenders at full strength.** (a) Mandate: the alliance won two-thirds in a free election
   in 2010 and again in 2014, 2018 and 2022; the constitution has survived four elections. (b)
   Legacy: Hungary was the only post-communist state still governed by its communist-era
   constitution, amended but never replaced; the Avowal's "We do not recognise the communist
   constitution of 1949, since it was the basis for tyrannical rule" (verify) states the purpose.
   (c) Comparison: invocations of God and of Christianity are in the Irish, Greek, Polish and German
   texts; constitutional definitions of marriage as between a man and a woman are in the Polish,
   Croatian and several other constitutions; "constitutional identity" as a limit on EU law was
   asserted first by Germany's Federal Constitutional Court (the Lisbon judgment, 2009). (d) The
   critics' standard: the defenders say the Venice Commission and the EU institutions apply to
   Hungary standards they do not apply to older member states — Hazony's argument (l.1584) that
   moral outrage is directed at nations that "resist European standards of behavior" — and that the
   liberal objection assumes courts are neutral where the theory of the module says no institution
   is (Vermeule l.1036; de Benoist I.3 on the "republic of judges"). (e) Mandated change through
   amendment is what constitutions provide for; eleven amendments in a decade (verify) is a fact,
   not an argument.
8. **What the case shows about the theories** (the closing section before the Conclusion; L16).
   Three findings, stated as the course's judgement on the THEORIES, not on Hungary: (i) the
   post-liberal theories disagree on the case — Hazony's is the closest fit and is contradicted by
   the EU clause; Vermeule's method fits the interpretive articles and his safeguard is exactly what
   the critics say failed; Deneen's prescription is contradicted by the method and his prediction is
   the critics' reading; de Benoist's Europe is the opposite of the government's; (ii) every one of
   them assumes a check that the case tests — Hazony's cohesive majority nation that "can afford" to
   grant minorities rights, Vermeule's officials' good faith, Deneen's local practice preceding
   national theory — and the case shows how each check looks when a single majority holds the
   pen; (iii) the liberal critique assumes neutral referees (a constitutional court, the
   Commission) that the post-liberals deny exist; the case cannot settle that disagreement, because
   the referees are a party to it. Leave the judgement of Hungary to the reader; state these three
   about the theories.

Headings as claims (suggested): "The preamble is law"; "'We, the members of the Hungarian
nation'"; "The public good written into the rule of interpretation"; "Theory by the state, not
practice from below"; "Two maps of Europe"; "What the critics establish"; "What the defenders
establish"; "What the case shows about the theories".

## Pitfalls / subtleties to flag

- **The opening's wording** (course.yaml, verbatim, cannot be changed by the writer): "Its
  amendments later define marriage". In fact the 2011 text already defined marriage as "the union
  of a man and a woman" (Article L(1)); the amendments defined the FAMILY (2013) and parenthood by
  sex (2020), and the "Christian culture" clause came in the Seventh Amendment (2018) and was
  extended in the Ninth (2020). The body must state the record correctly right after the opening;
  recommend that course.yaml's opening say "define the family" and its key_ideas list the 2018
  amendment (2013, 2018, 2020) — reported in the final message, not edited here.
- **Every quotation from the Fundamental Law in this brief is from memory of the official English
  translation and is marked "verify".** The writer must take each from the official text (the 2011
  version for the original, the consolidated version for the amended) and give the article number.
  Do not quote the Venice Commission or the EU documents from this brief's paraphrase: obtain and
  quote the documents.
- **Text vs practice.** The Fundamental Law is short on elections, media and courts' organisation;
  these live in cardinal acts (the 2010 media laws; the 2011 electoral law; the 2011 judiciary acts).
  When the lesson speaks of practice, it names the statute or the decision, not "the constitution".
  Media concentration after 2018 (the foundation that absorbed several hundred outlets) is
  widely reported but outside the text and the module's theories — one sentence at most, attributed,
  or nothing.
- **Terms to anchor (L11)**, at most three per section: Fundamental Law (Alaptörvény — chosen
  instead of "constitution", alkotmány); National Avowal (Nemzeti hitvallás, literally "national
  creed"); cardinal act (sarkalatos törvény); the Holy Crown and the "historical constitution"
  (the doctrine that Hungary's constitution was the body of customary law embodied in the Crown,
  not a single document); Trianon; Fidesz–KDNP (the governing alliance of a party founded by
  students in 1988 and a Christian Democratic partner); the Venice Commission; Article 7 TEU (the
  EU's procedure for a member state's breach of the Union's founding values); the Court of Justice
  of the EU vs the European Court of Human Rights (different courts — do not confuse them).
- **Do not re-teach** Hazony, Vermeule, Deneen, de Benoist, Scruton, Burke, Oakeshott, Schmitt,
  Polybius, Madison: one clause and a `<Ref>` each; the new matter is the text and the record.
- **Balance and register.** The course passes no verdict of its own on any author or idea: no
  condemning or approving labels in its own voice. Judgement comes only through argument and
  evidence; a label may appear only inside a quotation, with who applied it and when. This covers
  "illiberal democracy" (the prime minister's own phrase of 2014 and Zakaria's of 1997 — attribute
  each), "autocratic legalism" and "electoral autocracy" (critics' terms — attribute or omit),
  "Christian democracy" (the government's later self-description — attribute), and equally
  "national sovereignty" used as a vindication. Give the defenders' case (link 7) the same length
  and care as the critics' (link 6).
- **The opening's "Western post-liberals cite Hungary as a model"**: in the dossier this is
  grounded only in Hazony (l.1584, 1631 — and he explicitly declines to put the nations he lists
  "in the same moral category") and Fawcett's report of a French magazine (l.13697–13700). Deneen's
  2018 book does not name Hungary; Vermeule's names it only as a Geneva Consensus sponsor. Do not
  attribute endorsements to Deneen or Vermeule beyond their books; if the writer wants their later
  statements, they must be obtained and cited (dossier gap — flagged).
- **Contested history** (1944; Trianon; the interwar regime) is taught as two readings with their
  evidence, never settled in the course's voice. The numbers: "about two-thirds of its territory"
  (Trianon) and "over 400,000 deported in May–July 1944" are the standard figures — use them only
  if a claim needs them, with their frame.
- **No current politics.** The lesson reads 2011–c. 2022 and the documents; the 2026 election gets
  one sentence at most (verify the result). Do not predict.
- **Numbers (L12):** keeper numbers — two-thirds (the threshold); about 45% of the vote → two-thirds
  of the seats (2014); 262 to 44 (the adoption vote, verify). Everything else to a figure.

## EXERCISE PLAN (E6/E7 + playbook-history mix)

1. **Retrieval (warm-up, E3):** For each of the four post-liberal theories of the module, name in

   `<Ref hazony-virtue-of-nationalism>`, `<Ref vermeule-common-good-constitutionalism>`,
   `<Ref de-benoist-european-new-right>`), and the one article of the Fundamental Law that comes
   closest to it.
2. **Document reasoning (sourcing drill):** the National Avowal's sentence on 19 March 1944 and 2 May
   1990. Metadata first (who drafted the Avowal, in 2011, for whom, with what legal force — Article
   R(3)); predict what a text of that provenance will say about the period 1944–1990; then RECORDS
   (the dates of occupation and of the first freely elected parliament), ARGUES (where
   self-determination was lost and regained), OMITS (the state's conduct before March 1944 and the
   deportations after it); finally, what can the sentence be USED to establish — about 1944, and
   about the Hungarian state's self-understanding in 2011?
3. **Claim to refute (E6b):** "The Fundamental Law is unique in Europe in invoking God, Christianity
   and a definition of marriage." Refute it with the comparison texts (Ireland, Greece, Poland,
   Germany; Poland's Article 18), then state what IS distinctive in the Hungarian text (the
   interpretive force of the Avowal under Article R(3); the extent of the cardinal-act tier; the
   2018 duty of every state organ to protect "Christian culture") — so the refutation does not
   overshoot into "nothing is distinctive".
4. **Boundary probe (E6a), Vermeule's criterion at its edge:** A constitutional court annuls a
   statute; the same majority then writes the statute into the constitution. Using only Vermeule's
   own terms (`<Ref vermeule-common-good-constitutionalism>`: determination; deference; "law as
   will"; the forfeiture of the right to rule; and `<Ref aquinas-law-and-kingship>` for
   `unjust-law-not-law`), state the condition under which this is a legitimate determination of the
   constitution and the condition under which it is the "perversion of law". Show that the test is
   the content of the reasons, not the procedure — and say what evidence from the Fourth Amendment
   record you would need to apply it.
5. **Research-shaped (E6d):** From valasztas.hu, take the list-vote share and seat share of the
   governing alliance in 2010, 2014, 2018 and 2022. Compute the ratio of seat share to vote share
   for each election and state, for the two elections where the vote was below 50%, what feature of
   the electoral system produced the two-thirds (single-member districts; the "winner
   compensation" rule — verify its existence and effect in the 2011 electoral act). End with an
   uncertainty statement: what the computation cannot tell you (how votes would have fallen under
   the old system).
6. **Synthesis — causal and comparative (E3: `article-48-weimar`):** In March 2020 the Hungarian
   parliament authorised the government to govern by decree for the duration of the pandemic
   emergency, without a fixed end date (verify the act and its later repeal). Compare it with the
   Weimar decrees under Article 48 (`<Ref article-48-weimar>`; `<Ref schmitt-political-theology>`,
   `state-of-exception`): who declared the exception, who could end it, and what the parliament
   retained. Conclude in a claim–evidence–reasoning paragraph which of Schmitt's two dictatorships
   (commissarial or sovereign) the Hungarian measure resembles, and what fact would change your
   answer.
7. **Counter-argument (E3: Deneen):** "Hungary is Deneen's post-liberalism put into practice." Name
   two facts the claim must account for (Deneen's "practice before theory"; his warning about
   "populist nationalist authoritarianism", l.923) and state what would have to be true for the

8. **FORWARD (E7) → `the-argument-assembled`:** The Böckenförde question (`<Ref

# Research brief: junger-storm-of-steel-and-the-worker (Jünger: the storm of steel and the figure of the Worker)

status: planned
module: conservative-revolution
type: core
goal: Analyze how Jünger turned the experience of the Western Front into a political anthropology — the front as a school, total mobilization, the Worker as the new ruling type — and evaluate what it asks of the individual

## ARC (L10)

- **Inherits:** From `sorel-reflections-on-violence` (the direct prerequisite): the
  political myth — an image of a decisive battle, taken whole, not checked against
  results — and the ethic born of a war voluntarily joined; Sorel's open question why
  the myth moves some nations and not others. From `spengler-decline-of-the-west` (the
  other prerequisite): Culture and Civilization, the machine as the Faustian soul's
  last work, Caesarism after the rule of money, and Spengler's call for a "Prussian"
  socialism of duty — Jünger writes inside that frame and Spengler himself will object
  to where he takes it (set piece 7). From `mann-reflections`: Kultur against
  Zivilisation, the war of 1914 as a war against the West's politics and the Bürger as
  the German type — Jünger declares the Bürger finished. From
  `ortega-revolt-of-the-masses`: the mass-man and the specialist; Jünger replaces both
  mass and individual with a third term. From `schmitt-concept-of-the-political`: the
  "total state" and the real possibility of killing; Schmitt himself cited Jünger's
  "total mobilization" in 1931 (Muller l.3711). From `article-48-weimar`: the republic of
  1930–33 that Jünger's book of 1932 treats as already over. From `marx-manifesto`: the
  worker as a class defined by its place in production — the definition Jünger rejects.
- **Contributes:** The module's second move: the myth Sorel found in the strike is found
  in the trenches, and turned from a class's image of victory into an anthropology. The
  lesson follows one path in three steps: (1) the memoir — the front as an ordeal that
  makes a new man, recorded "without pity and without protest" (`front-experience`);
  (2) the diagnosis — modern war as the labour of whole nations, `total-mobilization`;
  (3) the theory — Der Arbeiter (1932): the Worker as a Gestalt, not a class, who rules
  through technology and in whom the bourgeois individual is replaced by the "type"
  (`gestalt-of-the-worker`). The reader leaves able to state what Jünger asks of the
  individual at each step and where the memoir contradicts the theory.
- **Hands off:** (i) The Worker is a ruling type without a regime: Der Arbeiter calls
  dictatorship "only a transitional form" and names no party. What happens when a party
  claims to be the transition is `the-right-in-1933`'s question (Jünger refused the
  Nazis' offers; Heidegger, who read the book in seminar, joined). (ii) Jünger's
  conservatism is a conservatism with nothing left to conserve; the next lesson,
  `moeller-van-den-bruck-third-reich`, states the programme of a conservatism that must
  first CREATE what it would conserve. (iii) The postwar fate of the book and its author
  is `the-right-after-1945`'s material.
- **Spine step:** "The radical right turns from conserving to revolution — Sorel's myth,
  Jünger's front and Worker, Moeller's Third Reich, Evola's Tradition — and in 1933 its
  thinkers have to choose: Schmitt and Heidegger join, Spengler refuses"

## EXPLANATION PLAN (L0)

Opening (course.yaml, verbatim): between 1914 and 1918 a German infantry officer is
wounded fourteen times and keeps a diary; he publishes it in 1920 without pity and
without protest; twelve years later he claims that the machines that made the trenches
are making a new society in which everyone is a worker.

Anchors (L11), section 1: Ernst Jünger (1895–1998), a volunteer of August 1914 at 19,
storm-troop leader, one of the youngest officers ever to receive the Pour le Mérite,
the highest Prussian order for bravery (September 1918); the Western Front (a 700 km line of trenches
from the Channel to Switzerland — one clause and a locator in FIGURE 1); the Somme (1916)
and the German spring offensive of March 1918, each half a sentence; the Stahlhelm, the
largest veterans' league of the Weimar years, half a sentence. "Materialschlacht" —
battle of materiel — defined at first use.

1. **What did the war do to the men who fought it?** Two familiar answers: it
   destroyed them (the testimony the reader may know from the British war poets) or it
   was a crusade (the propaganda of 1914). The diary gives a third: an ordeal that
   selected and made a type. Read the memoir's own sequence: the 1914 volunteers'
   "drunken atmosphere of blood and roses" (l.325) → the first shell that strips war of
   "its mask of cosiness" → the trench as a workshop of trades → the Somme, "a war of
   materiel of the most gigantic proportions" (l.823). Then the twist the reader does
   not expect: inside the machine war the diary finds the individual MORE decisive, not
   less — "the work at decisive points had been done by no more than a few handfuls of
   men" (l.1817), "the aristocrat of the trench" (l.2135), "an ever-purer, ever-bolder
   warriorhood" (l.1425). Answer: the front is a school that selects an elite by courage
   (`front-experience`). Seam to Sorel (Ref): the myth of a decisive battle, born here not
   in a strike, and kept after the certainty of victory is gone — "we might be crushed,
   but surely we could not be conquered" (l.1063).
2. **What the diary records and what it omits.** The course's sourcing move, done in the
   body (the material demands it): the book "has no pacifist design … it is pure where
   and when … above all, what" (Introduction l.183): no causes, no politics, no war
   aims, no account of the defeat. Its ethic is stated as practice — the enemy viewed
   "without animus", sought out and killed, prisoners protected (l.751); the
   scorched-earth retreat condemned as dishonouring "economic thinking" (l.1323).
   And the text itself changed: the 1924 version added nationalist editorial — "the
   course of nations in the last resort rises and falls with the destiny of war",
   "Germany lives and Germany shall never go under!" — which the 1934 version removed;
   the English the reader is quoted is the last revision (1961). Answer: what the memoir
   can establish is the experience and the values of one storm-troop officer; what it
   cannot is any claim about the war's meaning — and it was the author who added and
   removed such claims over forty years.
3. **From experience to doctrine: why the front generation rejected the republic.**
   Obvious expectation: men who survived the slaughter become pacifists or democrats.
   Many did; a large minority did the opposite. The memoir already holds the step: the
   bourgeois at home negotiated while the soldier fought (Der Arbeiter §9 makes it a
   charge — "the bourgeois's war guilt is that he could neither wage the war in earnest …
   nor lose it", l.1133). Anchor the Weimar years in one paragraph: the 1918 defeat and
   revolution, Versailles, the veterans' leagues; Jünger as a nationalist publicist who
   "hated democracy like the plague" (1925), twice refused a Nazi Reichstag seat, never
   joined the party. Answer: the front as a standard by which every civilian institution
   — parliament, contract, the market — is measured and found to be a negotiation where
   a decision was owed. Link to Schmitt's decision against discussion (Ref
   `schmitt-political-theology`, one clause).
4. **Total mobilization: what kind of thing is a modern war?** Obvious picture: an army
   fights while the nation behind it works. Jünger's diagnosis (the 1930 essay, taught
   from Der Arbeiter and from Schmitt's 1931 summary): the war of materiel converted the
   whole of a nation's life — factories, railways, savings, newspapers, women's labour
   — into energy for the front; "war front and labour front are identical" (§33,
   l.3461); the soldier no longer "falls" but "drops out", like a part ("man fällt
   nicht mehr, sondern man fällt aus", l.3376). Answer: `total-mobilization` — modern
   war as a labour process of whole nations, which no longer distinguishes combatants
   from workers. The "because": materiel decided the battles (the diary's Langemarck,
   l.1817, and the Arbeiter's Langemarck of 1914, where "free will, education,
   enthusiasm" broke on "a few hundred metres" swept by machine guns, l.3303–3334).
   Show that the concept was taken up at once: the jurist of the friend–enemy criterion
   called it "a pregnant phrase" for the passage from the neutral to the total state
   (Muller l.3711; Ref `schmitt-concept-of-the-political`).
5. **The Worker is not a class.** The newcomer's reading of "the worker": Marx's
   proletarian (Ref `marx-manifesto`). Jünger refuses three readings by name — a fourth
   estate, the bearer of a new society, an economic organ (§§3–6, l.405) — and answers
   with the GESTALT (concept `gestalt-of-the-worker`): "a whole that comprises more than
   the sum of its parts" (§§7–8, l.888–952) — as a people is more than its members — a
   human form that stamps an age the way the knight or the Bürger stamped theirs.
   Everyone is a worker, from the general to the stoker; work is a way of life with no
   opposite, "like gunpowder" a principle (§28). The because: once war and work are one
   process (link 4), the old divisions — estate, class, profession, individual, mass —
   no longer sort men; only their relation to the total work-process does. Then the
   TYPE: the individual's uniqueness (Einmaligkeit) is replaced by unambiguousness
   (Eindeutigkeit); the 19th century ranked men by degree of individuality, the 20th
   will rank them by how far they represent the work-character (§§37–41).
6. **Freedom, rule and technology in the Worker's state.** Three claims, each the
   answer to a liberal objection the reader will raise. (a) "But that is servitude."
   Freedom as a fief: felt most where it is known to be held from a higher order (§1,
   l.303); "freedom from" becomes "freedom for" (l.7925); "the type knows no
   dictatorship, because for him freedom and obedience are identical" — dictatorship
   only a transition (§43, l.4706). (b) "Who rules, and by what right?" Herrschaft is
   not seizure but representation of the Gestalt; "seizing power" is "a phrase of the
   incapable" (§§21–22, summary). (c) "Technology is neutral." No: "technology is the way
   in which the Gestalt of the Worker mobilizes the world" (§44, l.4865); adopting it is
   "a rite of a foreign cult"; the work plan replaces the constitution (§75, l.9022).
   Seam: from Spengler (Ref) one expects the machine to mark the Civilization's old age;
   Jünger reads it as the uniform of a new ruling type — "Die Technik ist die Uniform
   des Arbeiters".
7. **What the theory asks of the individual — the thinker's own difficulty.** The human
   asks that the right's difficulties be taught as its own; here there are three, each
   stated at full strength and from the text. (i) The memoir against the theory: in the
   diary the decisive man is the few, the "aristocrat of the trench", and the killer
   keeps a remorse the state cannot take from him (l.2351); in Der Arbeiter the
   individual is a relic and freedom is obedience. Both cannot be the lesson of the same
   war. (ii) The conservative's problem: the Worker dissolves estate, peasant, Church,
   property, nation — everything the right had defended. Spengler's letter of 25
   September 1932 says so from inside the Conservative Revolution, appealing to the
   peasant; Jünger's reply is that every imperial will sacrifices the peasantry
   (l.11760–11775). A conservatism that welcomes the destruction of what conservatism
   conserved is, on the course's earlier definitions (Ref `burke-reflections`), something
   else — the radical conservatism the next lesson names. (iii) The method's problem:
   "see, do not evaluate" (§36) — the book describes and welcomes ("though dangerous …
   welcomed", 1963 preface) without a standard from which it could refuse what it
   describes. Its §70 calls it a "miscalculation" of bourgeois society not to foresee
   "that the people might one day decide against democracy" (l.8493) — written in 1932.
8. **The OBJECTION at full strength, and the record.** The charge, from the left and the
   liberal centre: the work aestheticised killing and prepared the ground for the
   regime that came four months after Der Arbeiter — a "Bible of totalitarianism", as a
   Swiss critic later put it (Korrespondenz). The record that tells against it, stated
   with equal care: Jünger refused the Reichstag seat the party offered him, stayed out of the regime's
   writers' academy, left Berlin, was
   attacked in the party paper for venturing "into the zone of head-shots", and published
   in 1939 a fable widely read as a veiled attack on the regime; the 1934 Storm of Steel
   is the version that removed the nationalist editorial. The concession: the record
   shows distance from the Nazi party, not from the total state the book called for; the
   reply Jünger gave — that neither Nazis nor opponents could use the book — is a claim
   about use, not about content. Forward: `the-right-in-1933` puts the same question to
   Schmitt, Heidegger and Spengler.

Headings as claims (suggested): "The front selects the few"; "What the diary leaves
out"; "The soldier's standard turned on the republic"; "War as the labour of whole
nations"; "A Gestalt, not a class"; "Freedom as obedience, technology as uniform"; "A
conservatism that welcomes the end of what it conserved"; "The charge and the record".

## Pitfalls / subtleties to flag

- **The register rule.** The course passes no verdict of its own on any author or idea:
  no condemning or approving labels in its own voice. Judgement comes only through
  argument and evidence; a label may appear only inside a quotation, with who applied it
  and when.
- **Which text the reader is reading.** Hofmann translates the last revision (1961), not
  the 1920 diary-book; any sentence quoted from it is quoted as "in the final version".
  The reflective, elegiac passages (l.2351, l.2487) may be later additions; the dossier
  cannot date them. Do not present them as 1920 views. The 1924 nationalist sentences
  are quoted from the Introduction (from the 1929 English translation of the 1924 text).
- **German terms to anchor, ≤3 per section (L11):** Gestalt (a form or figure that is a
  whole — keep the German, define once); Typus (type); Totale Mobilmachung; Bürger /
  bürgerlich (the bourgeois and his world — Ref `mann-reflections` for Mann's sense,
  which Jünger reverses); Herrschaft (rule, dominion); Arbeitsplan (work plan);
  Materialschlacht; Stahlhelm; Pour le Mérite; Langemarck (a Flemish village where in
  November 1914 German volunteer regiments, many of them students, attacked and were
  cut down — the "Langemarck myth" of the Weimar right — one sentence).
- **Translations are the course's own** (the book has no English edition in the dossier;
  a 2017 English translation exists — do not quote it). Give the German beside the key
  phrases: schlechte Bürger; ein Lehen; man fällt nicht mehr, sondern man fällt aus;
  Kriegsfront und Arbeitsfront; Der Typus kennt keine Diktatur; Freiheit wovon / wozu;
  Rechenfehler; Die Technik ist die Uniform des Arbeiters. Short quotations only.
- **Period-bound material in Der Arbeiter the lesson must frame** (summary 111 pedagogy): racial
  vocabulary (Rasse, "überlegene Rasse" in the Übersicht), the depopulation passage
  ("vingt millions de trop", §42 l.4642), approval of purges and of capital punishment
  (Adnoten). Neither hide nor amplify: if the depopulation passage is used, quote it
  with its context as part of what the theory licenses (link 7 iii), not as a shock.
- **Do not equate Jünger with the Nazis or acquit him.** Present the documented distance
  (no membership, refused seat, the party paper's attack, the 1939 fable) AND the
  documented content (the total state, freedom as obedience, the people deciding
  against democracy as a welcome "miscalculation"). The lesson's verdict is the
  argument in link 8, not a label.
- **"Conservative Revolution" is a later label** (fixed by Mohler, 1949, whose typology
  served partly to separate the movement from Nazism — Fawcett l.8838–8850). State this
  once (the concept is introduced in the next lesson; here say only that the name came
  later). Do not use the label as if Jünger used it of himself in 1932.
- **Total mobilization** is taught from Der Arbeiter and Schmitt's 1931 citation; the 1930
  essay is not in the dossier — say so in References.
- **Do not re-teach** Spengler's Civilization/Caesarism, Mann's Bürger, Ortega's
  specialist, Schmitt's total state, Sorel's myth — each one clause plus `<Ref>`.
- **Numbers (L12):** keepers: fourteen wounds (the opening) and the three phases of the

  (2,000) only if the sourcing section needs it. No casualty series.

  secondary literature on Der Arbeiter (only Fawcett's paragraph).

## EXERCISE PLAN (E6/E7 + playbook-history mix)

1. **Retrieval and chronology (the one warm-up):** put in order and date to the year:
   the Pour le Mérite; Storm of Steel's first edition; its nationalist revision; "Total
   Mobilization"; Der Arbeiter; Hitler's appointment as chancellor; the "quiet"
   revision. Then say which of these the English translation reflects.
2. **Document reasoning (sourcing drill):** "We might be crushed, but surely we could not
   be conquered" (Guillemont, 1916; final version). Author, date of the event, date of
   the text, audience; what it records (a unit's morale under bombardment) and what it
   argues; what the revisions history means for using it as evidence of 1916 feeling.
3. **Claim to refute (E6b):** "Der Arbeiter is a Marxist book in nationalist dress: the
   worker becomes the ruling class." Name the steps that fail (§3: not a fourth estate;
   §5: independence from the economy; the Gestalt is not a class; rule is representation,
   not class victory) and state what the claim gets right (the planned economy, the end
   of the bourgeois, Fawcett's report that critics called it "right-wing Bolshevism").
4. **Boundary probe (E6a):** "the type knows no dictatorship, because for him freedom and
   obedience are identical." Take the case of a single soldier who, like the diarist at
   l.2289, lets an enemy go against the logic of the battle. Is that act free on the
   Arbeiter's definition? What does the answer show about the definition's boundary —
   the case where obedience and conscience part?
5. **Corroboration (E3, two sources):** Spengler's call for a "Prussian socialism" of duty
   (Ref `spengler-decline-of-the-west`) and Jünger's Worker. Using Spengler's letter of
   1932 and Jünger's reply (set piece 7), state where they agree (the end of the
   bourgeois age, duty over contract) and the point on which they split (the peasant),
   and say which of the two is the more conservative by the course's earlier definition
   (Ref `burke-reflections`) and why.
6. **Derivation the lesson did not do (E6c):** from "war front and labour front are
   identical" derive what follows for (a) the distinction between combatants and
   civilians and (b) the meaning of "peace". Check the derivation against §42 (total
   inclusion, no distinction of combatants) and state the consequence for the laws of
   war that Sorel's "in war the vanquished are not killed" (Ref) had assumed.
7. **Counter-argument (synthesis):** "Jünger never joined the Nazi party, so his work bears
   no relation to the regime." Name two facts the claim fails to account for (the 1932
   welcome of the people deciding against democracy; the total state and freedom as
   obedience) and two it rightly rests on (the refusals; the 1934 deletions; the party
   paper's attack). State what would have to be true for the claim to survive.
8. **FORWARD (E7) → `moeller-van-den-bruck-third-reich`:** Jünger welcomes the end of the
   Bürger, the estates and the peasant, and still calls himself a man of the right.
   Using only this lesson, state the problem such a thinker has with the word
   "conservative" (what is left to conserve after 1918?) and propose a definition of
   conservatism that would let him keep the word. The solution says in one sentence that
   the next lesson's author answered with exactly such a definition — "to conserve is to
   create what is worth conserving" — and with a distinction between the conservative
   and the reactionary, with a `<Ref>`.

## ARC (L10)

- **Inherits:** From `carlyle-heroes`: the `cash-nexus` — Carlyle's 1843 phrase for a society whose only bond is payment, which the Manifesto borrows in 1848 — and the Condition-of-England facts (Peterloo, the workhouse, Chartism). From `hegel-ethical-life-and-the-state`: `civil-society` as the system of needs and its "rabble" (§§241–248) — the problem Hegel saw and answered with corporations and colonies, which Marx says cannot be answered inside the system. From `tocqueville-equality-and-the-township` and `-soft-despotism`: `equality-of-conditions` and the industrial aristocracy of Vol. II Pt II ch. XX. From `locke-property-and-consent`: the `labour-theory-of-property` — that labour creates value and title, which Marx turns against the owners. From `thrasymachus-and-the-ring`: justice as the interest of the stronger — the ancestor of "the ruling ideas of each age have ever been the ideas of its ruling class". From `mill-liberty-and-its-limits` (the course.yaml prerequisite) and `stephen-liberty-equality-fraternity` (the lesson just before in course order): a quarrel inside liberalism about how far coercion may go — which Marx's frame makes a quarrel inside one class; Stephen's "power precedes liberty" and parliament as "counting heads instead of breaking heads" are the nearest thing the module has yet said to "political power … is merely the organised power of one class for oppressing another" (one clause each, to show the meeting point; do not re-teach). Each a `<Ref>` and one clause.
- **Contributes:** The module's last position, taught fairly: history as the struggle of classes; the bourgeoisie as "the most revolutionary" force in history, which dissolves every bond into "callous cash payment" and builds productive forces greater than all earlier ages together; the mechanism (forces outgrow relations; crises); the proletariat as its product and gravedigger; the objections Marx answers (property, family, nation, eternal truths) and the ten measures. Then the lesson's hinge: Part III.1, where Marx despises the feudal critics of capitalism whose diagnosis he shares, and the four replies the right made to him — Tocqueville's industrial aristocracy as an exception rather than the engine; Schumpeter's class as a hotel; Hayek's social justice as a mirage; Scruton's alienation inverted.
- **Hands off:** The next lesson, `maine-and-lecky-on-popular-government`, gives the reply to Marx from liberals who had no feudal nostalgia to mock: Maine's wealth as "a continuous process of reproduction, not a granary to be divided" and the mutinous crew who feast on the stores; Lecky's fear that a wide suffrage is "a spear as well as a shield" — that the "battle of democracy" Marx wants won (Part II) and his measure 2, "a heavy progressive or graduated income tax", are exactly the danger — and Lecky's own reading of Capital (v2 ch. VIII). The forward exercise lands there. The lesson decides the Manifesto's forecasts (the political forecast failed, **strong**; the economic forecast largely held). `british-franchise` tests Marx's Chartists (Part IV) and the prediction that the proletariat's party would conquer political power against the record of who extended the vote. Whether a critique of equality can be had without the feudal nostalgia Marx mocked — and whether the demand for equality is itself ressentiment — returns in `ressentiment-and-envy`. Later: Mann's Kultur against the "politicisation" Marx and Mill both represent (`mann-reflections`); Schmitt's "state and society" (`schmitt-concept-of-the-political`); Hayek's constructivism and Oakeshott's rationalism (`oakeshott-rationalism-in-politics`); Scruton's own lesson.
- **Spine step:** "Democracy arrives as a fact and is judged: Tocqueville foresees soft despotism, Carlyle demands heroes, Mill defends liberty and Stephen answers him, Marx predicts class war, Maine and Lecky call popular government fragile — and the British franchise tests who was right"

## VERDICT (lesson verdict, from research/verdicts/REGISTER.md)

**Claim judged.** The Manifesto's predictions: proletarian revolution in the advanced countries; absolute immiseration; the old middle class sinking into the proletariat and wage dependence becoming general; concentration and recurring crises; polarisation into two camps.

**Verdict.** **The political forecast failed; the economic forecast largely held.**
- The revolutionary predictions failed: no proletarian revolution in an advanced country. Power was reached by the ballot (Labour in office 1924), and several of the ten measures, a graduated income tax among them, came by statute. The one communist revolution of the period came in an agrarian country. Grade: **strong**.
- Absolute immiseration failed after c. 1850. Grade: **on balance** until a real-wage series is bound (Feinstein 1998); **strong** once it is.
- Wage dependence became general. The old middle class ("small tradespeople, shopkeepers … handicraftsmen and peasants … sink gradually into the proletariat", staged `research/texts/thecommunist-.txt` ll. 373–375) shrank across the industrial countries. In the US the self-employed share of non-agricultural white male workers fell from one in six in 1910 to one in ten in 1970 (Fairlie & Meyer 1999), and the farm decline was larger. The world market spread. Grade: **strong**.
- Concentration and recurring crises held in outline. Grade: **on balance** until a series is bound.
- Polarisation is half right. Wage dependence became general, but income and consciousness did not polarise, because a salaried middle grew; Schumpeter's family mobility shows classes were not closed. Grade: **strong** on each half.

**What decides it.** The record of the industrial countries 1848–1945: no proletarian revolution where the Manifesto expected one; the franchise and the measures reached by statute; 1917 in an agrarian country; the self-employment series (Fairlie & Meyer); Schumpeter's family histories for mobility; a real-wage series (Feinstein) and an occupational series (Routh) once bound.

**Strongest objection.** The ballot was the class struggle by other means: the measures came because of working-class pressure, so the political forecast was fulfilled in substance. It does not overturn the verdict, because the Manifesto predicted "forcible overthrow" and a proletarian revolution following immediately on the bourgeois one (IV, ll. 1414–1438); a forecast fulfilled only in a form it explicitly rejected has failed as stated. Whether pressure from below drove the statutes is weighed in `british-franchise`.

**What would overturn it.** Falling long-run real wages and a shrinking salaried middle in the industrial countries.

## EXPLANATION PLAN (L0)

Start from the course.yaml opening verbatim: 1848, two Germans, a pamphlet; all history the struggle of classes; the middle class the most revolutionary force that ever existed, which "melts into air" everything solid, and is producing the class that will bury it; then how much of the description the right had already said, and why Marx despised them for it.

1. **What explains a world where everything solid dissolves?** The reader arrives with three explanations: Tocqueville's providence (equality advancing for seven centuries), Carlyle's great men, and Hegel's reason realising itself in the state (Refs). Marx's first move is to replace all three with one engine: "The history of all hitherto existing society is the history of class struggles" — oppressor and oppressed in every age (freeman and slave, lord and serf, guild-master and journeyman), each fight ending in "a revolutionary reconstitution of society… or in the common ruin of the contending classes". The "because": classes are defined by their place in production, so their quarrel is not a quarrel of opinions (Carlyle's sincerity, Hegel's Idea) but of material positions that cannot both be held. Define *class*, *bourgeoisie* (the owners of the means of production and employers of wage-labour) and *proletariat* (those who own nothing but their labour and must sell it) in Engels's own 1888 footnote terms.
2. **How did a class of town merchants become the master of Europe?** Burghers of the chartered towns → manufacture (the guild displaced) → Modern Industry (steam, the factory) → the world market, each step of economic growth "accompanied by a corresponding political advance" until "the executive of the modern State is but a committee for managing the common affairs of the whole bourgeoisie". The lesson pauses on the praise, because a course that teaches Marx as an opponent must show it: the bourgeoisie "has played a most revolutionary part" — it has ended "all feudal, patriarchal, idyllic relations", torn "the motley feudal ties", "drowned the most heavenly ecstasies of religious fervour… in the icy water of egotistical calculation", turned physician, lawyer, priest and poet into wage-labourers, and "during its rule of scarce one hundred years, has created more massive and more colossal productive forces than have all preceding generations together". Parse "All that is solid melts into air, all that is holy is profaned" clause by clause (set piece 1): constant revolutionising of production → disturbance of all social conditions → "everlasting uncertainty and agitation" → the end of fixed relations and venerable prejudices → "man is at last compelled to face with sober senses his real conditions of life". Note the borrowed phrase: "callous 'cash payment'" is Carlyle's cash-nexus (Ref), with the quotation marks Marx kept.
3. **Why must the bourgeoisie fall if it is so powerful?** The obvious thought: a class that strong is permanent. Marx's mechanism: the productive forces a society builds outgrow the property relations it built them in; feudal relations "became so many fetters. They had to be burst asunder; they were burst asunder" — and "a similar movement is going on before our own eyes". Evidence: the commercial crises, "the epidemic of over-production" in which society "finds itself put back into a state of momentary barbarism" because it has "too much civilisation, too much means of subsistence" — the sorcerer "no longer able to control the powers of the nether world whom he has called up by his spells". The bourgeoisie survives each crisis "by paving the way for more extensive and more destructive crises". Mark what is argument here (the form of the earlier transition, applied by analogy) and what is prediction.
4. **Who buries it, and why them?** The proletariat is the bourgeoisie's own product: labour "a commodity, like every other article of commerce", the worker "an appendage of the machine", wages equal to "the means of subsistence that he requires" (the iron law). Its history has stages — individual workers against individual masters, machine-breaking, combinations, trade unions, "the ten-hours' bill in England" — toward a national party: "every class struggle is a political struggle". Why only this class is revolutionary: it has no property to conserve, no nation ("The working men have no country"), and it is "the immense majority"; the lower middle class is reactionary, or revolutionary only in view of its "impending transfer into the proletariat"; the "social scum" a "bribed tool". And the bourgeoisie is "unfit to rule" because it cannot "assure an existence to its slave within his slavery": pauperism grows faster than wealth. Hence "its own grave-diggers… Its fall and the victory of the proletariat are equally inevitable" — Ref Hegel's rabble in one clause: the problem Hegel admitted civil society generates and could not solve from within, Marx makes the solution.
5. **What do the Communists want, and how do they answer the obvious objections?** Part II in its own form — objection and reply. Property: not property in general but bourgeois property, "the final and most complete expression" of exploitation; "capital is… a social power"; and the reductio — "in your existing society, private property is already done away with for nine-tenths of the population; its existence for the few is solely due to its non-existence in the hands of those nine-tenths". Freedom: bourgeois freedom is "free trade, free selling and buying" (Ref Locke's labour title in a clause — the labour that creates value is the labour that owns nothing). Laziness: those who work acquire nothing and those who acquire do not work. Culture, law, "eternal truths": "The ruling ideas of each age have ever been the ideas of its ruling class"; "your jurisprudence is but the will of your class made into a law for all" — Ref Thrasymachus, one clause, and state the difference (Thrasymachus: a timeless fact; Marx: a fact with an end). The family: "To this crime we plead guilty"; the "community of women" turned back on bourgeois marriage. The nation: "The working men have no country… We cannot take from them what they have not got." Then the ten measures as a table, and the transitional "despotic inroads on the rights of property", and the end state: "the public power will lose its political character", because "political power… is merely the organised power of one class for oppressing another"; "an association, in which the free development of each is the condition for the free development of all".
6. **THE HINGE — why does Marx despise the critics who agree with him?** Part III.1. The French and English aristocracies, beaten by the bourgeoisie, "took their revenge by singing lampoons on their new master, and whispering in his ears sinister prophecies of coming catastrophe". This is Feudal Socialism: "half lamentation, half lampoon; half echo of the past, half menace of the future; at times, by its bitter, witty and incisive criticism, striking the bourgeoisie to the very heart's core; but always ludicrous in its effect, through total incapacity to comprehend the march of modern history." The image: the aristocrats "waved the proletarian alms-bag in front for a banner", and the people, joining them, "saw on their hindquarters the old feudal coats of arms, and deserted with loud and irreverent laughter". Named: "the French Legitimists and 'Young England'" — anchor Young England (Disraeli and a handful of young Tory MPs in the early 1840s; *Sybil*'s "two nations… the Rich and the Poor") in a clause, and Ref Carlyle for the cash-nexus Marx has just used. Why the contempt, in Marx's terms: the feudal critic sees the wound (cash has dissolved the bonds) and wants the old bonds back — but the old bonds were a different exploitation, and the bourgeoisie that dissolved them created the only class that can end exploitation as such; to mourn the past is to misread the "line of march". Add "Clerical Socialism" ("holy water with which the priest consecrates the heart-burnings of the aristocrat"), petty-bourgeois socialism (Sismondi — acute critique, "reactionary and Utopian" aims) and German "True" Socialism (the monks writing saints' lives over classical manuscripts — "Alienation of Humanity" written over the French critique of money). The pattern Marx exposes is the lesson's finding: the right and the left of 1848 share a diagnosis and differ on the direction of the cure.
7. **REPLY 1 — Tocqueville's industrial aristocracy (1840).** Eight years before the Manifesto, the same facts: the pin-head maker who "improves" as a workman and "is degraded" as a man; the master "the administrator of a vast empire"; "the art advances, the artisan recedes"; "What is this but aristocracy?" The difference is the verdict. For Tocqueville this is "a monstrous exception" in a democratic age — an aristocracy with "members, but no body", master and man bound "as two rings at the extremities of a long chain" — "one of the harshest that ever existed… but one of the most confined and least dangerous", because equality elsewhere keeps churning its membership. Where Marx sees the engine, Tocqueville sees the channel by which inequality *might* re-enter if democracy lets it; the forecast is a warning, not a law.
8. **REPLY 2 — Schumpeter (1927): aptitude and mobility.** Class is "like a hotel or an omnibus, always full, but always of different people". The barriers between classes are real but surmountable, and by the same aptitude that forms them; the accumulation "automatism" Marx needs — capital concentrating in ever fewer hands, families fixed in place — is contradicted by the record of family rise and decline (the Brno textile families, the Krefeld silk-weavers). What rises a family is departure from routine — the entrepreneur — and what sinks it is routine; so the two-camp polarisation does not happen. The concession Schumpeter makes, and the course should keep: aptitude "confers no moral laurels" — the market rewards use, not virtue (as Hume and Mallock said before him). **REPLY 3 — Hayek: the mirage of social justice.** Marx's indictment presupposes that the distribution of a market order is *someone's* act and so can be unjust. In a market no one distributes; "only conduct can be just", and a result "no one intended" is neither just nor unjust; to demand "social justice" is to demand that someone be given the power to distribute — and full equality of opportunity would require control of the family, "a nightmare". The point against Marx: the fault he finds is a category mistake, and the cure requires exactly the concentrated power Part II's ten measures create. **REPLY 4 — Scruton: alienation inverted.** Take Marx's deepest word, alienation (from the Paris manuscripts, not the Manifesto — say so), and keep it: alienation is real and political, "the absence of society". But its cause is the industrial process and the division of labour, not private property; so changing ownership cannot cure it — the Soviet factory is as alienating as the Manchester one. Property, rightly held, is the opposite of alienation: possession of irreplaceable things as ends (the home, the heirloom) against consumption of replaceable commodities; "commodity fetishism is the pathology, not the essence, of property". And the feudal principle Marx mocked — tenure of property as the fulfilment of duty (Disraeli) — is the conservative's cure for the disease Marx named. One clause on method: "demystification mystifies" — the class-interest explanation of ideas explains everything and so nothing.
9. **The weighing — the political forecast failed; the economic forecast largely held.** First what holds and gives way as *argument*: hold — the description of bourgeois modernity (the right, Tocqueville and Carlyle agree); political power as organised class power (Thrasymachus's ghost, which the course has met in every module); the family and nation replies as rhetoric of the first rank. Give way — the inevitability (the sorcerer image is an image); the cure (Hayek: the ten measures build the power the critique feared; Scruton: ownership is not the cause). Then the Manifesto's predictions against the record, each graded as the register grades it. (i) *The revolutionary predictions failed* (**strong**): no proletarian revolution in an advanced country; power was reached by the ballot (Labour in office 1924), and several of the ten measures, a graduated income tax among them, came by statute; the one communist revolution of the period came in an agrarian country. (ii) *Absolute immiseration failed after c. 1850* (**on balance** until a real-wage series — Feinstein, *JEH* 58, 1998 — is bound; **strong** once it is). (iii) *Wage dependence became general* (**strong**): the old middle class that "sink[s] gradually into the proletariat" (ll. 373–375) shrank across the industrial countries; in the US the self-employed share of non-agricultural white male workers fell from one in six in 1910 to one in ten in 1970 (Fairlie & Meyer 1999), and the farm decline was larger; the world market spread. (iv) *Concentration and recurring crises held in outline* (**on balance** until a series is bound). (v) *Polarisation is half right* (**strong** on each half): wage dependence became general, but income and consciousness did not polarise, because a salaried middle grew; Schumpeter's family mobility shows classes were not closed. Then the strongest objection to the verdict in its best form — the measures came by statute *because* of working-class pressure, so the ballot was the class struggle by other means and the political forecast was fulfilled in substance — and the answer: the Manifesto predicted "forcible overthrow" and a proletarian revolution following the bourgeois one (IV, ll. 1414–1420); a forecast fulfilled only in a form it explicitly rejected has failed as stated, though the pressure reading is the one `british-franchise` weighs. Last, what would overturn the verdict: falling long-run real wages and a shrinking salaried middle in the industrial countries.

**Conclusion.** States the verdict in plain words with its grades: the Manifesto's political forecast failed — no proletarian revolution in an advanced country, and power reached by the ballot (**strong**) — while its economic forecast largely held: wage dependence became general and the world market spread (**strong**), concentration and crises held in outline (**on balance**), absolute immiseration failed (**on balance** until a wage series is bound), and polarisation was half right (**strong** on each half). Close on what the reader can now do, and on the next lesson: a critique of democracy and of redistribution from liberals with no feudal past to mourn (Maine, Lecky); `british-franchise` tests who extended the vote.

Headings as claims: "One engine for all of history"; "The bourgeoisie as the most revolutionary class"; "Forces outgrow their fetters"; "The grave-diggers"; "Objections answered: property, family, nation, eternal truths"; "Half lamentation, half lampoon: the feudal critics Marx despised"; "Four replies from the right"; "The political forecast failed; the economic forecast largely held".

## Pitfalls / subtleties to flag

- **Taught fairly.** Marx appears here as a position the right argued with, and the reader must be able to state it as Marx would. The praise of the bourgeoisie (link 2) is not optional; the ten measures are quoted, not summarised as "communism"; the family and nation replies are given their force before any reply.
- **Terms to anchor (L11):** bourgeoisie, proletariat, class (Engels's 1888 footnote definitions, which the Gutenberg text lacks — write them in the course's own words); feudal (the order of lords, serfs and guilds the bourgeoisie displaced); guild; manufacture vs Modern Industry (the workshop with hand tools vs the steam-driven factory); the world market; 1848 (the February revolution in Paris and the risings across Europe — the Manifesto appeared in London days before); Metternich, Guizot; the Chartists (Ref `carlyle-heroes` for the Charter; here: the party Marx names as the English ally); "Young England" and *Sybil*; the French Legitimists (supporters of the elder Bourbon line after 1830); Sismondi, Proudhon, Saint-Simon, Fourier, Owen (one clause each where named — Marx's taxonomy needs them only as labels); the ten-hours' bill (1847); the lumpenproletariat ("social scum" in Moore's rendering); Brno and Krefeld (Austrian and Prussian textile towns); Disraeli's Crystal Palace speech (1872) if used.
- **Edition limits.** Moore's 1888 translation as edited by Engels; the Gutenberg file lacks all prefaces and Engels's footnotes. Say "the Manifesto" and "Marx and Engels" where the text is joint; "Marx" where the course speaks of the doctrine; do not attribute the 1888 footnotes to the 1848 text.
- **Alienation is not in the Manifesto.** Scruton's reply and Marx's "True Socialism" jibe both turn on a word from the 1844 manuscripts; the lesson says so in a clause and does not teach the early Marx.
- **The polarisation thesis and the "middle class grew" reply** are contested empirical claims in the course's sense: state Schumpeter's and Marx's positions as arguments with the kind of evidence each needs (family histories; income shares; the self-employed share), then give the register's finding at its grade and no firmer: polarisation is half right — wage dependence became general, income and consciousness did not polarise (**strong** on each half). Absolute immiseration and concentration stay **on balance** until the Feinstein real-wage series and an occupational series (Routh) are bound; no trend is stated from them before that. Likewise "the ten measures were adopted": count only what the record shows (progressive income tax; free public schooling; central banks; state railways in most of Europe).
- **Do not equate Marx with the regimes that invoked him** as a refutation; the course's method is the argument, and Strauss's rule (Ref forward, References) is that "a view is not refuted by the fact that it happens to have been shared by" anyone. Equally, do not omit that Part II's "despotic inroads" and the industrial armies are in the text.
- **Thrasymachus and "ruling ideas"**: one clause with a Ref; the course has met the claim in every module (Carneades, Machiavelli, Hobbes's sovereign who makes justice). State the one difference (Marx historicises it).
- **Hegel's rabble**: one clause with a Ref; do not re-teach civil society.
- **Carlyle's cash-nexus**: one clause with a Ref; the point is the borrowing and the quotation marks Marx kept.
- **L8a:** Schumpeter, Hayek and Scruton are thinkers whose texts are the material of the replies — name them as such ("Schumpeter answers"); Muller, Hirschman, Fawcett, Hamilton, Cropsey — References only. "Marx argues" / "the Manifesto says" are the body's authority voices.
- **Numbers:** none are load-bearing except "nine-tenths" (Marx's own figure, a rhetorical estimate — say so) and "scarce one hundred years". Do not import wage or pauper data; `carlyle-heroes` carries the 1842 return.
- **The register rule.** The course judges arguments, not people: no condemning or approving labels for any author in its own voice; a label may appear only inside a quotation, with who applied it and when. It does pass verdicts on arguments: where the lesson stages a dispute it has the evidence to settle, and in every module's closing lesson, it says which side wins, how firmly (established / strong / on balance / open) and why, as recorded in research/verdicts/. "Open" is a finding, stated with what would settle it, never a way to avoid one. The same tests apply to the left and the right. (Marx's own labels — "reactionary", "social scum", "feudal socialism" — appear only inside his quotations, as his.)
- **Dossier:** the Manifesto is full. Tocqueville Vol. II Pt II ch. XX is summarised (28) but not staged — fetch Gutenberg #816 and verify the pin-head quotations. Schumpeter and Hayek are anthology excerpts — the Muller shard for ch. 4 and ch. 8 must be located by heading in `conservatism-anthology.txt` (the staged sidecars cover shards 1–5 and 7–11; ch. 4 is in shard 6, `conservatism-s6-.txt`). Scruton chs. 5–6 come from the partial read (index 13) — verify in the full text. *Sybil* and the 1888 preface are not in the dossier. The Strauss–Cropsey Marx chapter has landed as unsummarised OCR (ll. ~38816–40063).

## EXERCISE PLAN (E6/E7 + playbook-history mix)

1. **Retrieval (warm-up).** State the Manifesto's mechanism of historical change in three steps (forces grow; relations become fetters; fetters burst) and name the evidence Marx gives that bourgeois society is at step two (the crises of over-production).
2. **Document reasoning — sourcing drill.** Part III.1 on Feudal Socialism: author, date (early 1848), audience (the Communist League, then German readers), purpose; what it records about the 1840s right (that an aristocratic critique of capitalism existed, in France and England, and courted the workers), what it argues (that the critique is nostalgia), what it omits (that the right's Factory Acts and Ten Hours' Bill — the one Marx praises in Part I — were carried with Tory votes); what the passage can be used to establish about the shared diagnosis and what it cannot establish about the critics' motives.
3. **Boundary probe.** Set the number of classes to one: after the proletariat's victory, by Marx's own definition of political power as "the organised power of one class for oppressing another", what does "political power" mean, and what does the state become (Part II, 950–965)? Then run it the other way: in a society where Schumpeter's hotel is true (membership churns), is there a "class" in Marx's sense at all, and what happens to "the history of all hitherto existing society"?
4. **Claim to refute.** "The Manifesto says the bourgeoisie is a parasite that produced nothing." Name the failing step with Marx's words (ll. 164–268: "a most revolutionary part"; "more massive and more colossal productive forces than have all preceding generations together"), and say why the praise is load-bearing for his argument (only a class that built such forces could build the class that ends classes).
5. **Research-shaped task — computation inside source inquiry.** Take the ten measures and, for each, record whether and when Britain adopted something like it (progressive income tax 1909; free compulsory elementary schooling 1870–91; a central bank with the note monopoly 1844/1946; railways nationalised 1948; inheritance duties 1894; no confiscation of emigrants' property; no industrial armies). State the count, then the two readings: Marx confirmed (the proletariat's pressure extracted them) or Marx refuted (a "committee of the bourgeoisie" enacted them without revolution) — and what evidence would distinguish them. End with an uncertainty statement (which measures are "something like" is a judgment). The solution states the finding the count supports: the measures came by statute, without the revolution Part IV predicted, so the political forecast failed as stated (**strong**); whether pressure from below drove the statutes is `british-franchise`'s question.
6. **Counter-argument stem (synthesis).** "Hayek's 'mirage of social justice' answers Marx." Name two facts the claim must account for (Marx does not say the market is unjust — he says it is a fetter; and the ten measures are offered as the next stage, not as justice), and state what would have to be true for Hayek's reply to land (that Marx's indictment rests, as Part II's "nine-tenths" does, on a judgment about who ought to hold what — a distributive claim).
7. **FORWARD (E7) → `maine-and-lecky-on-popular-government`.** Part II says the first step of the workers' revolution is "to win the battle of democracy", after which the proletariat "will use its political supremacy to wrest, by degrees, all capital from the bourgeoisie" (ll. 899–902), and measure 2 is "a heavy progressive or graduated income tax" (l. 923). Suppose a liberal of the 1880s accepts this description of what a working-class majority would do with the vote. State what he must conclude about widening the franchise, and what institutions he would want to stand between a majority and property. The reader will produce a property qualification, a second chamber and a constitutional protection of contract; the solution says in one sentence, with a `<Ref>`, that the next lesson meets two such liberals — one of whom calls the suffrage "a spear as well as a shield" and graduated taxation "the supreme danger of democracy".
8. **Attack the verdict.** The lesson concludes that the Manifesto's political forecast failed (**strong**). State the strongest objection in its best form — that the franchise, the graduated income tax and the welfare state were won by the working class's organised pressure, so the class struggle did conquer political power, by the ballot instead of the barricade — and say what evidence would be needed to overturn the verdict rather than merely reword it: a proletarian revolution in an advanced country, or a showing that the Manifesto itself predicted the parliamentary road (against IV, ll. 1414–1438, "forcible overthrow"). Then do the same for the economic half: what series would overturn "wage dependence became general" (Fairlie & Meyer) or the failure of absolute immiseration (a bound real-wage series showing long-run decline).
9. **E3 — earlier lessons.** "The ruling ideas of each age have ever been the ideas of its ruling class": compare with Thrasymachus (Ref `thrasymachus-and-the-ring`) and with Hobbes's sovereign as the maker of justice (Ref `hobbes-sovereign-and-liberty`). State what all three share, what Marx adds (an end to the sequence), and which of the three Hayek's "no one distributes" would count as a reply to.

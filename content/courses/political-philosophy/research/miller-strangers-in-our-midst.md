# Research brief: miller-strangers-in-our-midst (Miller: the liberal-nationalist case for controlled borders)

status: planned
module: nation-and-immigration
type: core
goal: Analyze Miller's argument that states may control membership on grounds of self-determination while owing refugees and migrants specific duties, and evaluate it against both open borders and restriction

## ARC (L10)

- **Inherits:** From `carens-open-borders` the reader has the liberal case at full strength — three routes to free movement as a basic liberty, the public-order restriction as the only brake, and the conclusion that exclusion cannot be derived from liberal premises. From `the-nation-and-its-defenders` they hold Miller's own foundation: weak cosmopolitanism (everyone's interests weighed; reasons owed for unequal weight), compatriot partiality, associative obligations and the three strands of the compatriot relation. From the empire module they have `rectification`/`supersession` (`inherited-guilt-and-reparations`) — the reparation claims to admission in ch. 7 touch it. From `bodin-sovereignty` they have `sovereignty` — which Miller will say is too quick an answer. From `rawls-theory-of-justice` (via the Carens lesson's reading of §58) they know that Rawls's own international extension gave *peoples* a right "to settle its own affairs" — Miller's self-determination has a Rawlsian ancestor, and the seam may say so in one clause. From the American lessons they have the public-culture question in a concrete case — `anglo-protestant-core` against `american-creed` (`albions-seed-and-who-are-we`) — which Miller's private/public culture distinction answers in his own way. From `powell-raspail-and-camus` they have the `prophecy-of-displacement`: the worry about "the composition of the demos" stated as a forecast; Miller states the same concern as an *interest* to be weighed, not a prophecy — a contrast the seam into link 4 can make in one sentence.
- **Contributes:** One move: that one can grant Carens's premise — the equal moral worth of every person — and still derive a state's right to control membership, from a people's interest in determining its own future (its public expenditure, the composition of its demos, its public culture). The lesson builds that right from the ground (why sovereignty is too quick; territorial jurisdiction's three conditions; the park warden showing jurisdiction does not entail border control; self-determination supplying the missing link), then follows it to the duties it leaves standing — rescue for refugees, fair terms for economic migrants, rights that grow with residence, integration that is civic by requirement and cultural only by expectation — and to the shape of policy ("hard on the outside, soft on the inside"). It answers Carens's cantilever and Abizadeh's coercion argument, and ends where Miller himself admits strain: the 2015 postscript.
- **Hands off:** This lesson decides the dispute `carens-open-borders` staged: equal moral worth does not entail a right to cross borders freely (Miller over Carens, on balance; ## VERDICT). It leaves open, as a value question, how far duties of admission or of effective aid reach people above Miller's floor but far below the options in rich states. Miller's framework assumes a state that can enforce its border, sort refugees from economic migrants and integrate those it admits. The objection he does not answer is Murray's (next lesson, `murray-strange-death-of-europe`): that the European state of 2015 could do none of these, so the framework describes a "we" and a capacity that no longer exist. The module's application (`the-2015-crisis-read-three-ways`) then sets Miller's Postscript against the record he did not have and carries the module's four-part verdict (procedure, security, principle, integration); its principle part rests on this lesson's verdict, and it finds 2015 a failure of capacity and of principle, though not of Miller's principle, which was not applied. The reader should leave holding Miller's four values and the one admission in the postscript that "the distinction… is under strain", so Murray's charge lands on something precise.

## VERDICT

**Claim judged** (the dispute `carens-open-borders` stages): does the equal moral worth of persons entail a right to cross borders freely?

**Verdict: it does not.** It entails duties of rescue, fair terms for migrants and the inclusion of residents, and it leaves a people a qualified right to control its membership. **Grade: on balance.**

**What decides it.**
- The interests that free movement *inside* a state protects — against domination by one's own state (the passbook, the ghetto, forced resettlement) and for an adequate range of options — can be met in any adequately free state. So they ground a right to *some* adequate place, not to *this* one (Miller ch. 3 pp. 51–56, the generic/specific distinction and the reply to the cantilever).
- Miller himself extends refuge-type claims to anyone whose human rights cannot be protected at home (ch. 5 pp. 82–83), so the verdict does not leave the person without an adequate place unprotected.
- Carens's global original position bypasses Rawls's own placement of self-determination at the level of peoples (*Theory* §58, l. 16641–16647): Rawls ran the device a second time over representatives of nations, and its first result was a people's right "to settle its own affairs".

**Strongest objection.** Abizadeh's: border control coerces the excluded, and coercion must be democratically justified to those it coerces, because "the demos of democratic theory is in principle unbounded" (Abizadeh 2008). The point in dispute is the coercion/prevention distinction: Miller holds that barring entry removes one option and is prevention, not coercion; Abizadeh that a border regime backed by force coerces. It does not overturn the verdict, because even granting that the excluded are owed a justification, Miller supplies one of the kind weak cosmopolitanism requires (reasons the person ought to accept: an adequate place elsewhere, rescue where there is none); what Abizadeh's thesis demands in addition — a democratic voice for every would-be entrant — is the premise in dispute, not a finding. This is why the grade is on balance and not strong. The lesson must not describe the unbounded demos as a view no one defends; Abizadeh defends it.

**What stays open.** How far duties of admission, or of effective aid in its place, reach people above Miller's floor but far below the options in rich states. It is a value question that the place-premium data sharpen but cannot settle: wages for observably identical workers differ by a ratio of about 4 at the median across the US border (Clemens, Montenegro & Pritchett 2009 [ratios secondary — bind from the paper]).

**Would be overturned by:** an argument that the interests protecting internal movement cannot be met by any adequately free state other than the one a person chooses; or a case for the unbounded demos that does not presuppose the cosmopolitan conclusion it is used to prove.
- **Spine step:** "The live question: what a nation is and who may join it — Scruton and Miller on the pre-political loyalty, America as England's heir from the Federalist to Who Are We?, the prophecies of displacement from Powell to Camus, Carens and Miller on borders, Murray on Europe — and 2015 as the test"

## EXPLANATION PLAN (L0)

Start from the opening verbatim: suppose everything Carens says about moral equality is true — does it follow that a state may not choose who joins? The newcomer meets it as a test of inference: last lesson showed that *liberal premises* do not yield a right to exclude; this lesson asks whether a liberal can add one premise and get there.

1. **Why "it's sovereign" is too quick.** The obvious attempt is Bodin's (`<Ref bodin-sovereignty>`): the state is the one power bound by no law, so it controls its border. Miller: that tells us the state *has* the power, not that it is *legitimate* to use it against a person with a claim. Carens's whole point was that power needs a reason. Because: the question is the legitimacy of *selection*, not the existence of a border.
2. **What a state's territorial right actually is.** Attempt: the state owns its land, like the farmer his farm — the Nozickian move Carens already broke (`<Ref carens-open-borders>`). Miller's alternative: territorial *jurisdiction*, legitimate under three conditions — it keeps order and protects everyone's human rights; it represents the inhabitants (democracy not always required); the represented people has a right to occupy the land (it did not expel and replace another). Because: jurisdiction is a trust over a place and its people, not a title deed.
3. **Does jurisdiction give border control?** Attempt: of course — whoever makes the rules of a place decides who enters it. The park warden shows it does not: wardens enforce the park's rules on everyone in it without being able to decide who comes through the gate. Jurisdiction, control of resources and control of entry are a bundle that comes apart. Because: the authority to rule those present is logically separate from the authority to decide who is present. This is the lesson's reversal seam — from a defender of closed borders one expects jurisdiction to settle it; the material says it does not.
4. **The missing premise: self-determination.** What jurisdiction lacks, a democratic people's interest in deciding its own future supplies. Three things inflows change that a people has an interest in controlling: public expenditure (what it must provide), the composition of the demos (who will vote on everything else), the long-term plan (population size, land, sustainability). Add the trust leg (diversity can lower trust and turn deliberation into group bargaining — not inevitably, offset by integration; a cost in the ledger, not a law). Then the private/public culture distinction: a people may be indifferent to meat eaters and vegetarians but not to democrats and theocrats — what it may protect is the *public* culture, the shared terms of deliberation. The theatre company and its impresario: a company that cannot choose its members cannot decide what it performs. Because: self-government is empty if the self cannot decide who it is. Mark Miller's own limit: this is an *interest*, not a right; it must be weighed against the immigrant's claim under weak cosmopolitanism — and the strength of that claim varies (mere preference / threat to human rights / a prior promise).
5. **Two objections Miller answers.** (a) Abizadeh, at full strength: a border is coercion, coercion needs democratic justification to those coerced, so borders need justification to the excluded — a cosmopolitan democracy or no borders. Miller's two replies: not all coercion needs democratic legitimation (the child beater, the drunk friend's keys, the invading army); and exclusion is *prevention* — removing one option — not coercion, which narrows all options (the mugger vs barring a stranger from one's house); enforcement against evaders may be coercive, and must be justified as such. (b) Carens's cantilever (the FORWARD exercise of last lesson): if free movement inside a state is a human right, so is movement between states, or one must explain why Oregon to Washington differs from Washington to British Columbia. Miller: internal free movement protects minorities against domination by their *own* state — apartheid's passbooks, the ghetto, Soviet resettlement — a protection a border does not withdraw; and a human right protects generic interests (a sufficient range of options), not any specific one (Oberman's air ticket, the Tibetan monks). Because: the two movements protect against different things.
6. **Who is owed admission anyway: refugees.** Attempt: no one — self-determination decides. Miller: the duty of rescue (the passer-by) is prior to self-determination and attaches when a person presents herself at the border and makes herself vulnerable to the state's decision. Who is a refugee: wider than the 1951 Convention (persecution) — anyone whose human rights cannot be protected except by crossing a border, including state collapse and prolonged disaster; but not those whom aid could help in place. The claim is to *a* safe place, not the place preferred; so transfer, quotas, fair shares and even "trading" of obligations are permitted (the retirement home); cultural affinity is not a permitted criterion except as a division of labour between states. The duty is remedial and cost-limited; the two open problems — free-riding by other states and flows beyond absorption — leave a "morally excruciating" residue where the obligation becomes humanitarian rather than justice. Because: rescue is owed to the drowning, not to everyone who would prefer the shore.
7. **Economic migrants: mutual advantage on fair terms.** Attempt: if refugees have a claim, so does anyone poorer than us (wages for an identical worker are about four times higher across the US border at the median, Clemens et al. 2009 [secondary until bound]; Miller's own range is wider). Miller: no — economic migrants are admitted on terms of mutual advantage, which must be *fair* (the test: terms people free to consent would accept), selection must be by reasons the migrant ought to accept (skills yes; race or national origin never; political background only where numbers threaten institutions; culture by cost and cohesion), and the sending state counts (no recruiting of scarce doctors). Temporary programmes are legitimate with safeguards, against Walzer's ban. Because: a bargain is not a rescue, but a bargain can still be unjust.
8. **Rights that grow with time; integration that stops at culture.** Attempt: once admitted, equal at once — or never. Miller: human rights to everyone present, irregulars included; social justice to members; membership earned by time (5–10 years) through social membership and reciprocity; citizenship after a delay and a formal test, and nothing more (the Swiss municipalities that rejected 0 to 47 per cent show why discretion is forbidden). Integration has three dimensions: social (promoted indirectly), civic (required — language, the ground rules, no coercive practices), cultural (an expectation, never a requirement; public vs private culture; strict neutrality impossible, so a fair compromise). The reciprocal bargain. Because: a liberal state cannot have a permanent caste of strangers — the one thing all four values forbid.
9. **The shape of policy and the strain.** "Hard on the outside, soft on the inside": strong enforced borders, clear public numbers, rapid status decisions, temporary programmes that end — and full inclusion for those who stay. Then the Postscript, as Miller's own test: 350,000 recorded crossings by August 2015 and 800,000 expected in Germany; Schengen borders closing; a mixed flow in which the refugee/economic-migrant line "is under strain" (camp residents whose rights *could* be secured by funding; survival migrants; the young and educated leaving Syria); the rescue paradox (a ship must rescue; a state may need to break the rescue–admission link — Australia); his package.
10. **The weighing — Equal worth grounds a right to some adequate place, not to this one.** Set Carens's claim (`<Ref carens-open-borders>`) against Miller's and against the strongest objection to Miller, and reach the verdict in the course's voice (## VERDICT). (a) The decisive argument: what internal free movement protects can be had in any adequately free state, so moral equality yields a right to an adequate place, and Miller's wide refugee definition secures it for those who have none. (b) The Rawlsian point: Carens's global original position bypasses §58, where Rawls's own extension placed self-determination with peoples; Carens's reply (the device's critical power) shows the device *can* be run over individuals, not that it must. (c) The strongest objection, Abizadeh's unbounded demos, at full strength from his own text; the point in dispute is coercion against prevention; why it lowers the grade but does not reverse the verdict. Verdict: **equal moral worth does not entail a right to cross borders freely; it entails rescue, fair terms and inclusion, and leaves a qualified right to control membership — on balance.** Then what stays open, stated as a finding: how far duties of admission or effective aid reach people above the floor but far below rich-country options — a value question the place premium (about 4 at the median) sharpens but cannot settle.

**Conclusion.** State the verdict in plain words with its grade: a liberal can grant Carens every premise about equal worth and still hold, on balance, that a people may control who joins it, provided it rescues those whose rights cannot be protected elsewhere, sets fair terms for those it admits and fully includes those who stay; what it owes to the merely much poorer remains open. Hand-off: everything here presumes a state that can hold its border, sort the flow and integrate the admitted. Miller does not answer the charge that the state of 2015 could do none of these; the next lesson makes it, and the module's application judges it.

Headings fall out as claims: "Sovereignty says who has the power, not who may use it"; "Jurisdiction is a trust, not a title deed"; "The park warden: ruling a place is not choosing who enters it"; "Self-determination supplies what jurisdiction lacks"; "Prevention is not coercion; Oregon is not British Columbia"; "Rescue is owed to the drowning"; "A bargain can still be unjust"; "Membership is earned by time, not by culture"; "Hard outside, soft inside — and the strain of 2015"; "Equal worth grounds a right to some adequate place, not to this one".

3. **The theatre company and the impresario** (ch. 4 p. 69, l. 2663) together with "Maîtres chez nous" (Quebec, 1960s) and decolonisation: what self-determination is worth is shown by what its loss cost. Then Miller's own deflation: an interest, not a right.
4. **"Meat eaters and vegetarians, but not democrats and theocrats"** (ch. 4 p. 68, l. 2602): the private/public culture line in one sentence, then two test cases (a dietary minority; a group that rejects man-made law).
5. **Abizadeh answered** (ch. 4 pp. 72–75): a three-step reconstruction of the objection, then the two replies with Miller's cases — the child beater, the drunk friend's car keys, the invading army (coercion needing no democratic warrant); the mugger with a knife vs barring a stranger from one's house (coercion vs prevention). Make the reader locate exactly where the objection's second premise breaks.
6. **The cantilever and its fork** (ch. 3 pp. 53–56, l. 2062–2135): Oregon → Washington → British Columbia. Then Miller's reply with its three historical anchors — the apartheid passbook (South Africa, 1950s–86: internal passes restricting Black South Africans' movement), the Jewish ghetto, Soviet forced resettlement — each one clause. The point: internal free movement protects against one's own state.
7. **The passer-by** (ch. 5 pp. 78, 83–84) and the refugee defined: the 1951 Convention sentence (Art. 1A(2), "well-founded fear of being persecuted…") set beside Miller's wider definition; the three categories (a)(b)(c) as a table; the reason (c) falls out.
8. **The retirement home** (ch. 5 pp. 88–89): why paying another state to take one's refugees is not commodification if the destination's rights protection is verified and fees uniform — a worked analogy, with the commodification objection stated first at full strength.
9. **The Swiss municipalities** (ch. 7 pp. 128–129): naturalisation decided by local vote, with rejection rates from 0 to 47 per cent across communes — the one keeper number of the lesson, bound (the spread, not a mean, is the point: discretion produces bias). Hence a formal test and nothing more.
10. **Oldham 2001 and "parallel lives"** (ch. 8 pp. 130–131): the riots in northern English mill towns (Oldham, Burnley, Bradford, summer 2001) and the Cantle report's phrase; then the three dimensions of integration as the analytic reply, with Anderson's four stages (formal desegregation → spatial → formal social → informal social — "hobnob about last night's match").
11. **The Postscript's two numbers** (pp. 166–167, l. 6344–6348): 350,000 recorded crossings January–August 2015 (Miller: "the actual number may have been considerably larger"); Germany anticipating 800,000. Bind them: against a Germany of about 81 million (one per cent), against the 48,589 asylum applicants Germany had in 2010 (Murray's pair, next lesson — leave the comparison to that lesson and the application). Then the camp-resident puzzle and the rescue paradox as the two places Miller says the framework strains.

## Pitfalls / subtleties to flag

- **Terms to anchor at first mention** (L11): *territorial jurisdiction*; *demos* (the body of those who decide — Greek *dēmos*, people; the reader met *polis* and *isonomia* in `greek-miracle`, Ref); *non-refoulement* (the rule that a state may not return a refugee to a place of danger — 1951 Convention Art. 33); *the 1951 Convention* (the Geneva Convention Relating to the Status of Refugees, drafted after the Second World War's displacements, its definition tied to persecution); *UNHCR* (the UN refugee agency, 1950); *Schengen* (the 1985 agreement, in force 1995, abolishing internal border checks among most EU states — one clause here; Murray's lesson gives it); *Dublin* (the EU rule that the first member state of entry assesses an asylum claim — one clause; Murray's lesson); *irregular migrant* (Miller's term for one present without authorisation — he explains why not "illegal", ch. 7 n. 1–4); *metics* (Walzer's term from the Athenian resident alien — `<Ref aristotle-regimes-and-the-middle>` for who was a citizen); *Gastarbeiter* (German guest workers, 1961–73 — one clause); *Oldham*, *banlieues* (set piece 10); *Maîtres chez nous* (Quebec Liberal slogan, 1962); *apartheid*; *survival migration* (Betts's term); *coconut consensus* (set piece in ch. 9 — explain the image: hard shell, soft inside).
- **Do not re-teach** weak/strong cosmopolitanism, compatriot partiality, associative obligations, the airport queue or the hiker (`<Ref the-nation-and-its-defenders>`), nor Carens's three routes or the public-order restriction (`<Ref carens-open-borders>`). Ch. 3's rejection of the common-ownership and equal-opportunity routes is background: one paragraph at most, since Carens did not use them and the reader has not met Grotius or Risse — or omit and put in References.
- **Miller answers Carens and Abizadeh; he does not answer Murray.** The lesson must not supply a reply Miller did not give. The Conclusion states the charge as unanswered by Miller and names where it is judged (`the-2015-crisis-read-three-ways`: a failure of capacity, not of Miller's principle). The lesson's own verdict is on the Carens dispute, at on balance — no firmer, because the coercion/prevention point is contested.
- **The Postscript is dated** (written early autumn 2015 — it anticipates 800,000; the final 2015 count was higher; Eurostat's 1.26 million first-time applicants EU-wide is the application lesson's number). Do not correct Miller's figures in this lesson beyond saying they were written as the events unfolded; the 2015 lesson does the cross-check.
- **Contested empirical claims**: diversity–trust (ch. 4 pp. 64–66; Miller's own qualifications), the Australian tow-back's effect on deaths (p. 171 — Miller reports that flows and deaths fell; state as his report), the OECD welfare figure (Ref to the nation lesson). Present each as Miller presents it, with his hedges.
- **Miller's own labels**: he calls his position "communitarian" and "social democratic" (ch. 9 p. 161) and admits his arguments "may carry the imprint of his British/Old World context" (ch. 1 n. 38). Use the first once, plainly; the second belongs in an exercise on sourcing.
- **Where the summary and text may diverge**: page numbers in the summary are the printed book's; the text file carries them (search "Postscript 167" etc.). Spot-check every page pointer before writing it into References.
- **Thin spots**: ch. 7's reparation and desert claims (pp. 113–116) are brief and the reader has `rectification`/`supersession` — one paragraph linking them (does a colonial past create a claim to admission? Miller: weight, not a right; a just response may be in place) and no more. Ch. 6's brain-drain figures (Ghana, Zimbabwe, the £500,000 GP) are Miller's citations of 2013–15 sources; bind or drop.
- **Register.** The course judges arguments, not people: no condemning or approving labels for any author in its own voice; a label may appear only inside a quotation, with who applied it and when. It does pass verdicts on arguments: where the lesson stages a dispute it has the evidence to settle, and in every module's closing lesson, it says which side wins, how firmly (established / strong / on balance / open) and why, as recorded in research/verdicts/. "Open" is a finding, stated with what would settle it, never a way to avoid one. The same tests apply to the left and the right. Miller's "hard on the outside" and his "soft on the inside" — amnesty with redemption, full citizenship for the long-resident, no cultural requirement — are one position; present both halves with equal weight, whichever half a given reader finds congenial.

## EXERCISE PLAN (E6/E7 + playbook-history mix)

1. *Retrieval (E3, earlier lessons)*: State Miller's four guiding values and, for each, the earlier lesson where the reader first met the idea it rests on (weak cosmopolitanism — `the-nation-and-its-defenders`; self-determination — `locke-property-and-consent`'s consent and `ortega-revolt-of-the-masses`'s nation as project; fairness — `cicero-res-publica-and-natural-law`'s natural law or `aquinas-law-and-kingship`'s common good; an integrated society — `tocqueville-equality-and-the-township`'s mores). One sentence each.
2. *Boundary probe*: A state with jurisdiction but no demos. Run Miller's argument for a colonial administration that satisfies the first condition (order and rights) but not the second (representation). Show that self-determination cannot be invoked — there is no self — so no right to control entry follows, and say what this implies for the empire module's cases (`<Ref locke-and-mill-on-empire>`: Mill's tutelage could not claim a right to exclude on Miller's grounds).
3. *Document reasoning — sourcing drill*: The 1951 Convention's definition of a refugee (Art. 1A(2)), with metadata (drafted 1950–51 by states that had just absorbed Europe's displaced persons, originally limited to events before 1951 in Europe). Predict what it will cover and omit; then read it against Miller's wide definition: what does the Convention record about its drafters' situation, what does it argue about the state's duty, what does it omit (state collapse, disaster, private violence)? What can it be USED to establish about the duty of rescue — and what can it not?
4. *Claim to refute*: "Miller's park-warden argument shows that jurisdiction never includes border control, so states have no right to exclude." Name the step that fails (the warden shows jurisdiction *alone* does not entail entry control; Miller then adds self-determination, which the park lacks and a people has) and state what would have to be true for the claim to survive (that self-determination is not a legitimate interest — which is Abizadeh's and Carens's real ground).
5. *Derivation the lesson did not do*: Run the coercion/prevention distinction on *enforcement*. Miller concedes that acting against someone who has evaded the border may be coercive. Derive what justification he then owes — to whom, and of what kind — and show that it is weak cosmopolitanism's demand for reasons the person ought to accept, not Abizadeh's demand for a democratic voice. The solution notes that the deportation question this raises is judged on the record in the 2015 lesson (the removal failure, strong).
6. *Synthesis — causal ranking*: Miller names three stresses on current regimes (more would-be migrants than states admit; the premium on getting a foot inside; native anxiety that impedes integration). Rank them as necessary / enabling / precipitating causes of the 2015 breakdown as he describes it in the Postscript, and defend the ranking from the Postscript's own facts.
7. *Attack the verdict*: State Abizadeh's objection in its best form: a border regime is backed by force against those who try to cross; coercion by a state needs justification addressed to those it coerces as equals; therefore the excluded are owed a democratic say in the regime, and "the demos of democratic theory is in principle unbounded". Show where it meets Miller's coercion/prevention distinction and why the verdict depends on that distinction. Then say what evidence or argument would be needed to overturn the verdict — e.g. a showing that the interests internal movement protects cannot be secured in any adequately free state but the chosen one, or an account of the unbounded demos that does not presuppose the cosmopolitan conclusion. The solution grants that the coercion question is unsettled (which is why the grade is on balance) and shows that the verdict survives even if borders coerce, provided the justification owed is reasons, not a vote.
8. **FORWARD (E7)** → `murray-strange-death-of-europe`: Apply the coconut to Germany in autumn 2015 as Miller describes it: 800,000 expected, Dublin suspended, borders inside Schengen closing. List what the "hard outside" requires (enforced border, rapid determination, returns of those refused) and ask which of these a state in that position could do. The solution says the reader has reached the objection Miller does not answer — that the framework presupposes a capacity the state of 2015 lacked — and that the next lesson's author makes it the centre of his case.

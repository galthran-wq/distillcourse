# Research brief: murray-strange-death-of-europe (Murray's Strange Death of Europe: the restrictionist case)

status: planned
module: nation-and-immigration
type: core
goal: Analyze the restrictionist argument that mass immigration combined with a loss of faith in Europe's own beliefs is changing Europe into something else, and evaluate its evidence and its objections

## ARC (L10)

- **Inherits:** From `miller-strangers-in-our-midst` the reader has the liberal-nationalist framework and the one objection it does not answer: that it presupposes a state able to hold its border, sort the flow and integrate the admitted. From `the-nation-and-its-defenders` they have `oikophilia` and the `pre-political-loyalty`; from `carens-open-borders` the public-order restriction and the charge of feudal privilege. From `powell-raspail-and-camus` they have the `prophecy-of-displacement` and `replacement-as-argument` — Powell's 1968 forecast, Raspail's 1973 novel, the 1985 *Figaro Magazine* projection and Camus's 2011 term, each checked against the record — so this lesson does NOT retell that history; it Refs it and asks what Murray adds. From the empire module they have `hereditary-guilt` and the "tyranny of guilt" argument already stated once (`inherited-guilt-and-reparations`, where Murray ch. 10 was the restrictionist frame). From `ortega-revolt-of-the-masses` the `mass-man`; from `spengler-decline-of-the-west` the vocabulary of civilizational exhaustion; from `huntington-clash-of-civilizations` the claim that religion is the deepest cultural difference; from `albions-seed-and-who-are-we` the `anglo-protestant-core` and the question whether a people is held by a culture or a creed — Murray quotes Huntington's *Who Are We?* on exactly that point.
- **Contributes:** One move: the restrictionist case as a *conjunction* — two things at once, each perhaps survivable alone (`two-concatenations`). (1) Europe received more people, faster and from more different cultures, than ever before, through policy that was never chosen and could not be enforced (guest workers who stayed; forecasts wrong every decade; asylum law that cannot return the refused). (2) The receiving civilization had meanwhile lost faith in its own story — Christianity, then the Enlightenment's substitutes — and is "tired". The lesson reconstructs both with their evidence, follows them to the 2015 border and to the integration record from Rushdie to Cologne, recalls replacement as the argument the previous lesson tested and isolates what Murray adds to it (the second concatenation as the mechanism; his own projections and his own caveats), states the `bockenforde-dilemma` as the course's own question, and puts to the case its objections: the economists' studies Murray himself cites, the demographic projections read with their assumptions (including the projections that count mixed populations differently), Miller's framework, and the liberal reply.
- **Hands off:** What this lesson decides: "never chosen" (two verdicts, both strong) and three liberal forecasts staged as Murray's "excuses" (## VERDICT). Two things go forward. (1) Murray's record of 2015 — the pull-factor claim, the Dublin suspension, the deportation gap — is cross-checked against the public data in the module's application, the next lesson (`the-2015-crisis-read-three-ways`), which sets Carens, Miller and Murray against the same year and carries the module's four-part verdict (procedure, security, principle, integration); its judgment of Murray (his "never asked" holds for 2015; his paralysis forecast held for about six months and then failed) builds on this lesson's verdict. Powell's consent claim, left open in `powell-raspail-and-camus`, is decided here. (2) The "tiredness" concatenation is not testable as stated, and it is a diagnosis without a theory of why liberalism produced it; the Böckenförde question (`bockenforde-dilemma`, introduced here) is handed to the next module: `deneen-post-liberalism` answers that liberalism drew down an inheritance it could not replenish, and the module verdict in `hungarys-fundamental-law` judges the premise (a free state does not live on liberal principles alone: on balance).

## VERDICT

The lesson judges Murray's "never chosen" claim and hosts three **forecast checks** on the liberal side — the forecasts Murray calls the "excuses". All grades are the register's.

**1. "Never chosen" (lesson verdict).**
- *(a) The scale and permanence of post-war immigration were neither intended nor forecast, and majorities opposed it throughout the period Murray covers.* **Holds. Strong.** The Home Office forecast 5,000–13,000 a year for the UK after 2004 (Dustmann et al. 2003); Eastern Europeans in Britain reached 1.24 million by 2013; the "tens of thousands" target was missed; polls from Gallup 1968 to YouGov 2011.
- *(b) "Never chosen" in the sense that no one decided.* **Fails. Strong.** The instruments were chosen by governments and legislatures: the recruitment agreements (Germany–Turkey 1961, with a two-year rotation clause that was not kept), and in 2004 the decision not to use the transitional restrictions the accession treaty allowed — set out in the very report that made the failed forecast (Dustmann et al. 2003). That the UK, Ireland and Sweden alone opened at once [UNVERIFIED], and the British Nationality Act 1948 [UNVERIFIED], are to be bound before use.
- **Short form: chosen in its instruments, not in its scale.** This decides Powell's consent claim carried from `powell-raspail-and-camus`.
- The "tiredness" concatenation is **not testable as stated**; the Böckenförde question it raises goes to module 16.
- *Strongest objection:* from Murray's side, that a choice of instruments made in ignorance of their scale is no choice of the outcome, so "chosen" flatters the governments; from the other side, that elected governments chose and were re-elected, so "never chosen" is false outright. Neither overturns the split verdict, which states both halves: the instruments were chosen by those entitled to choose them, and the scale was neither intended nor forecast. *Would be overturned by:* policy papers showing the realised scale was projected and accepted before the instruments were chosen (against (a)); or evidence that the instruments were imposed without a government decision (against (b)).

**2. Forecast check — immigration as a net fiscal gain that helps carry an ageing population.** Dated statements: European Commission, *A European Agenda on Migration*, COM(2015) 240, 13 May 2015, §III.4 ("Migration will increasingly be an important way to enhance the sustainability of our welfare system"); the UCL headline (Dustmann & Frattini 2013: EEA migrants paid "34 per cent more in taxes than they received").
- (a) *Immigration as such is a net fiscal gain:* **fails** as a general claim. The lifetime, origin-split studies of high-welfare states find a negative balance overall, strongly negative for asylum and family migration and non-Western origins. Netherlands, per immigrant: labour +€125,000, asylum −€475,000; no second-generation group made a positive contribution (van de Beek et al. 2023). Denmark: non-Western immigrants and descendants −27bn kr in 2019 (Finansministeriet 2023). The OECD: "between ‑1% and +1% of GDP for most countries". The UK gives a positive lifetime figure for non-EEA migrants (MAC 2018). **Strong** for the Dutch and Danish type of welfare state; "small either way" for the OECD.
- (b) *Selected labour migration, and migration from rich and EU countries, is a net gain:* **holds. Strong.**
- (c) *Immigration can carry the pension burden:* **fails. Established.** Holding the support ratio would need 674 million migrants into the EU by 2050, which the UN's own report calls "totally unrealistic".
- *Would be overturned by:* lifetime studies in the same countries that include dynamic effects and reverse the sign for asylum and family cohorts; for (c), a feasible inflow that holds the support ratio.

**3. Forecast check — "they will go home" and "integration will happen by itself".**
- (a) *The guest workers would return:* **failed. Established.** Of about 870,000 Turkish recruits about half a million returned [secondary], and the policy's own authors said so in 1982 (BT-Drs. 9/1629) and 2010 (Merkel at Potsdam).
- (b) *Integration would follow by itself:* **failed** for second-generation Europeans of non-EU parentage on education, employment and felt membership (employment 68 per cent against about three-quarters; reported discrimination rose for that group); **held** for those of EU parentage, and the NEET gap is narrowing (OECD/EU 2023). **Strong.**
- (c) *Multicultural policy caused the shortfall:* **open** (Koopmans against Wright & Bloemraad and Heath & Demireva, abstracts only). What would settle it: a cross-national panel of policy indices against second-generation outcomes, with welfare generosity controlled.
- *Would be overturned by:* for (b), third-generation data at parity on employment and education.

**4. Forecast check — Muslim-origin Europeans will secularize as Christian-origin Europeans did.** **Fails. Strong.** In France 91 per cent of those raised Muslim keep the religion, against 67 per cent of those raised Catholic (INSEE 2023); across fifteen countries second-generation Muslims keep first-generation religiosity while Christians converge (Güveli & Platt 2023); German and French studies agree; only Dutch data show some decline. Murray's open question ("may be the case or may not", l. 7837–7840) is answered on the side he leaned to. The verdict says nothing about the third generation. *Would be overturned by:* third-generation data showing convergence to native levels.
- **Spine step:** "The live question: what a nation is and who may join it — Scruton and Miller on the pre-political loyalty, America as England's heir from the Federalist to Who Are We?, the prophecies of displacement from Powell to Camus, Carens and Miller on borders, Murray on Europe — and 2015 as the test"

## EXPLANATION PLAN (L0)

Start from the opening verbatim: two things happened to Europe at once — more people arrived in a shorter time than ever before, and the continent receiving them had stopped believing in its own story; either alone might have been absorbed, together they cannot be. The newcomer meets it as a claim about a *conjunction*: the lesson must show why Murray thinks neither half alone is fatal and what the "cannot" rests on.

*Length budget (the lesson stays at 45 minutes):* the verdict and three forecast checks are absorbed into the steps that stage them, not added after them. Link 1 is cut to one paragraph; link 3's economics and ageing rebuttals become forecast check 1; link 5's "wait a generation" becomes forecast check 2; link 6's demographers' objection on switching becomes forecast check 3; link 4 keeps only the mechanism (2015's detail is the next lesson's); the Böckenförde question closes link 7; link 8 is the weighing.

1. **How much change keeps a thing the same?** (one paragraph) The obvious attempt: Europe has always changed, so this is nothing new — the "nation of immigrants" reply. Murray concedes the premise with the Ship of Theseus and puts the question into *pace and difference*. Because: identity through change is a matter of rate. The scale comparison does the work: all the Huguenots now arrive every few months.
2. **Was it chosen?** Attempt: a democracy that admitted millions must have wanted to. The record Murray assembles says no: guest workers admitted as temporary who stayed and brought families (Germany–Turkey 1961–73); legislation always reactive; forecasts wrong in every decade (13,000 a year predicted for 2004; Eastern Europeans in Britain 170,000 → 1.24 million by 2013); targets missed ("tens of thousands"; net 330,000); persistent majority opposition in polls (Gallup 1968; YouGov 2011: 67 per cent "bad" vs 11 per cent "good") while criticism cost careers (Honeyford 1984). Murray's conclusion: a policy nobody planned and majorities opposed is a drift, not a decision. Mark Murray's two rival explanations — deliberate transformation (the Neather and Roche remarks) and administrative muddle — and that he allows both; the motive claims are his attributions. Set beside his record, in one paragraph, what it leaves out and the weighing (link 8) will use: the instruments that produced the scale were decided — the 1961 recruitment agreement with its two-year rotation clause that was not enforced, and in 2004 the decision not to use the accession treaty's transitional restrictions, which the same report that forecast 5,000–13,000 a year sets out (Dustmann et al. 2003).
3. **The excuses, and the kernel in each — with the fiscal and ageing forecasts checked.** Attempt: the drift was justified after the fact by four arguments. *Diversity* and *inevitability* in one sentence each (the marginal benefit of a new cuisine does not rise with numbers, and Rotherham's costs surfaced late; Japan restricts). *Economics* and *ageing* are liberal forecasts with dates, and the lesson checks them instead of only reporting Murray's rebuttal — **forecast check 1** (300–500 words of lesson). *The forecast, dated:* the European Commission, 13 May 2015 (COM(2015) 240, §III.4): migration "an important way to enhance the sustainability of our welfare system"; the UCL headline, 2013: EEA migrants paid "34 per cent more in taxes than they received" (set piece 4, the headline against the table). *The record:* the lifetime, origin-split studies — Netherlands (per immigrant: labour +€125,000, asylum −€475,000; no second-generation group positive), Denmark (non-Western −27bn kr in 2019), the UK (positive lifetime figure for non-EEA, MAC 2018), the OECD ("between ‑1% and +1% of GDP for most countries"); the UN's 674 million migrants needed to hold the EU support ratio, "totally unrealistic". *Verdict and grade:* immigration as such a net fiscal gain **fails** (strong for the Dutch and Danish type of welfare state; small either way across the OECD); selected labour and EU migration a net gain **holds** (strong); immigration carrying the pension burden **fails** (established). The OBJECTION — the economists' studies read on their own terms, not Murray's — is now inside the check: the record agrees with Murray on asylum and family migration and against him on labour migration. Because: the split, not either headline, is the finding.
4. **Why the border could not hold.** Attempt: asylum law is a system — Geneva decides who is a refugee, Dublin decides which state decides. Murray: at scale the system inverted — rescue nearer Libya lowered the smugglers' costs (the pull-factor claim, against the official denial: "Yet how could it not?"); identities could not be checked; the Dublin rule gave front-line states and migrants alike a reason not to fingerprint; the refused were almost never returned ("once you survive Lampedusa's waters you are in Europe for good"). Then the week of 25–31 August 2015 in two sentences and the deportation gap (220,000 orders, 11,300 removals by May 2016). Because: the first concatenation is not "many came" but "a law that could not be enforced met a decision that suspended it". Keep the dated record short: the next lesson tests it against the series and judges it; mark the Hungary number as one to check.
5. **The integration record, Rushdie to Cologne — and "they will go home", "it will happen by itself" checked.** Attempt: integration takes a generation; wait. First **forecast check 2** (300–500 words of lesson). *The forecasts, dated:* the 1961 recruitment agreement's clause 9 (no residence permit beyond two years in all); the cabinet decision of 1982 (BT-Drs. 9/1629: strengthen "die Rückkehrbereitschaft"); Spencer on 1997–2005, "We just believed migrants would integrate" (Murray l. 1799–1801). *The admissions:* Merkel at Potsdam, 16 October 2010 ("We kidded ourselves … 'They won't stay'"), Cameron at Munich 2011, Sarkozy 2011. *The record:* of about 870,000 Turkish recruits about half a million returned [secondary]; the second generation of non-EU parentage — employment 68 per cent against about three-quarters, fewer highly educated, perceived discrimination rising; parity for EU parentage; the NEET gap narrowing (OECD/EU 2023). *Verdict and grade:* the guest workers would return — **failed, established**; integration by itself — **failed** for non-EU parentage, **held** for EU parentage — **strong**; that multicultural policy caused the shortfall — **open**, with what would settle it (a cross-national panel of policy indices against second-generation outcomes, welfare generosity controlled). Then, compressed, Murray's quarter-century: the Rushdie affair (1989) as the early warning that *creed*, not race, was the variable — the death sentence internalised by publishers, and faith-based "community" representation (UKACIA → the MCB; "What number do I dial…?"); the Dutch dissidents (Bolkestein, Fortuyn, van Gogh, Hirsi Ali); polls on Islam moving with exposure; Cologne New Year 2015/16 and the slow official admission. Because: the claim is that elites "underestimated the numbers and overestimated the capacity to integrate", and the record is offered as evidence of the second. The OBJECTION enters here from Miller's framework (`<Ref miller-strangers-in-our-midst>`): integration has three dimensions and the civic one can be *required*; Murray's cases are failures of civic integration, which Miller's state would enforce — and Murray's reply is that no European state did or could. Tibi's Leitkultur (a core culture of rule of law, church–state separation and rights, joinable by anyone) sits between them; Murray asks who spells it out and with what sanction.
6. **Replacement: what Murray adds to a tested prophecy.** Seam: the reader has just watched the prophecies of displacement checked against the record (`<Ref powell-raspail-and-camus>`: `replacement-as-argument`, the 1985 projection's understated input and failed output); one expects Murray simply to repeat them. He adds two things, and the lesson isolates them. (a) A *mechanism*: the prophets argued from numbers; Murray argues that numbers matter because of the second concatenation — a tired culture does not assimilate, so the same arrivals that a confident culture would absorb change a tired one. That is why he can accept the 1985 projection was "not correct… numerically speaking" and still close that chapter with "the worst prophets of doom turned out to have understated their case" — the claim has moved from a count to a direction and a will. (b) His *own projections*, which the lesson reads as conditionals: the ONS "most modest" estimate and Coleman's "2060s" (ch. 2), Pew's 2050 UK Christian share and Kaufmann's Swiss 14-year-olds (ch. 16) — each with its publisher, year, variant and horizon, and with Murray's own caveats ("notoriously tricky", "rife with possible variations… they assume that Christians will continue to become non-religious whereas Muslims will not"). Because: a projection is a conditional, and the reader's task is to read the condition. The OBJECTION is the demographers', stated at full strength as the course's own: fertility differentials narrow after arrival; switching may run both ways; and the "minority" date depends on how people of mixed ancestry are counted — on the mixed-population projections, the fastest-growing group is neither "native" nor "newcomer", which changes what "replacement" could mean (dossier gap: see Sources). Murray's reply: the direction is not in doubt even where the date is, and France's ban on ethnic statistics left the question unmeasured. Then **forecast check 3** (300–500 words of lesson), on the one demographers' objection that can now be checked — that switching runs both ways, i.e. Muslim-origin Europeans will secularize as Christian-origin Europeans did. *The forecast, dated:* the assimilationist expectation the projections test (Pew 2017 builds in a 10 per cent defection rate for rising Muslim cohorts); Murray leaves it open ("which may be the case or may not", l. 7837–7840). *The record:* France — 91 per cent of those raised Muslim keep the religion, against 67 per cent of those raised Catholic (INSEE 2023); fifteen countries — second-generation Muslims keep first-generation religiosity while Christians converge (Güveli & Platt 2023); German and French studies agree; only Dutch data show some decline. *Verdict and grade:* the expectation **fails; strong**; it says nothing about the third generation; Pew's 10 per cent assumption is close to the French retention figure, so its projections are not biased by this. The other demographers' objections (fertility convergence; mixed-ancestry classification) stand as stated in `<Ref powell-raspail-and-camus>`.
7. **The second concatenation: tiredness.** Attempt: a civilization that doubts itself is only being modest. Murray: Europe lost its foundational story twice — Christianity to biblical criticism and Darwin (Pusey at Göttingen, 1825: "This will all come upon us in England"), then the substitutes (art as religion, idealism, Marxism, fascism, "progress") — and is left with human rights as a Christian conscience "uncertain of its moorings", consumption, and Delsol's Icarus who survived the fall. A culture of self-doubt is unlikely to persuade newcomers to adopt its stance; the East (Visegrád), which lacked the post-war immigration, the guilt and the fatigue, refused the quotas. Because: integration presumes something to integrate into. The guilt frame is recalled by Ref (`<Ref inherited-guilt-and-reparations>`), one paragraph: the 2015 welcome read as atonement, and the double standard (the Gulf states).
   Close the step on **the Böckenförde question**, stated as the course's own (one paragraph): *does the free secular state rest on presuppositions — a shared faith, a loyalty, a willingness to sacrifice — that it cannot itself produce or guarantee?* If yes, the second concatenation is a structural deficit; if no, institutions, law and prosperity suffice. Say plainly what this lesson can and cannot decide: the "tiredness" concatenation is not testable as stated (no measure of a culture's confidence is offered, and no outcome is named that would count against it), and the question it raises is judged in the next module (`deneen-post-liberalism` stages it; `hungarys-fundamental-law` gives the module verdict). The liberal OBJECTION (the course's own statement, Fawcett in References) in two sentences: the restrictionist's recurring themes are a style, not a policy, and a nation as ethnos cannot found a liberal state — with the liberal objector's own concession that an inclusive nation "might well require steps to limit immigration", and Murray's reply that both his routes to restriction (ch. 18: liberal and Burkean, `<Ref burke-reflections>`) stay inside the rules.
8. **The weighing — Chosen in its instruments, not in its scale.** Set Murray's "never chosen" against the record and the strongest objection, and reach the verdict in the course's voice (## VERDICT 1). (a) Not intended or forecast in scale, and opposed by majorities throughout: the 2004 forecast against 1.24 million by 2013; the missed target; polls from Gallup 1968 to YouGov 2011 — **holds, strong**. (b) "Never chosen" in the sense that no one decided — **fails, strong**: the 1961 agreement and its unenforced rotation clause; the 2004 decision not to use the transitional restrictions set out in the report that made the failed forecast. The objections from both sides (ignorance of scale is no choice of outcome; re-elected governments chose) and why the split verdict answers both. Then gather the three forecast checks in one paragraph, by their grades: the fiscal claim failed for asylum and family migration and held for selected labour migration; return failed; integration by itself failed for the non-EU second generation and held for the EU one; secularization failed. Say what the checks do to Murray's case: they bear out his record of the "excuses" where the excuses were general claims, and they count against him wherever he treats all migration as one (labour and EU migration paid; the EU-parentage second generation integrated).

**Conclusion.** State the verdicts in plain words with their grades: Europe's post-war immigration was chosen in its instruments and not in its scale (strong on each half); the liberal forecasts Murray called excuses split — the fiscal gain failed as a general claim (strong) and held for selected labour migration (strong), the pension claim failed (established), "they will go home" failed (established), integration by itself failed for the non-EU second generation (strong), and the expected secularization did not come (strong); whether multicultural policy caused the shortfall is open, and the "tiredness" concatenation is not testable as stated. Hand-off: the next lesson sets the three theories of the module against one year, 2015, and judges what it showed; the Böckenförde question goes to the next module.

Headings fall out as claims: "The Ship of Theseus: how fast can the planks change?"; "Nobody chose it: guest workers, forecasts and polls"; "Four excuses and the kernel in each"; "Why asylum law inverted at scale"; "Rushdie to Cologne: creed, not race, was the variable"; "From a count to a direction: what Murray adds to the prophecy"; "Tiredness: a story that ran out"; "Chosen in its instruments, not in its scale". Forecast-check subheads: "The fiscal gain: true for workers, false for asylum"; "They did not go home"; "Secularization did not come".

(a) Mass immigration into a civilization fully confident of its story — Murray's own counterfactual ("might have worked"): what in his account of integration changes? (b) Loss of faith with no immigration: what is left of the argument? Show that the "cannot" lives only in the conjunction, and identify the sentence in the Introduction where Murray says so.
3. *Document reasoning — the sourcing drill on a projection*: Quote the Pew 2050 projection as Murray gives it (UK Christian affiliation falling from almost two-thirds in 2010 to a minority by 2050). Metadata (a US research centre, April 2015, a global report). Predict what it assumes; then read the methodology: what does it record (2010 affiliation), argue (a trend under stated assumptions about fertility, switching and migration), omit (Muslim secularisation — Murray's own caveat)? What can it be USED to establish, and what can it not establish about 2050?
4. *Research-shaped (E6d)*: Reproduce the headline-vs-table reading. Obtain the UCL 2013 discussion paper (open access); find the EEA 2001–11 figure and the all-migrants and non-EEA 1995–2011 figures in its tables; state the population, period and counterfactual behind each; say which of the two newspaper headlines Miller records is defensible from the paper, and whether Murray's −£95 billion is the authors' number or his reading. End in a prose interpretation and an uncertainty statement.
5. *Claim to refute*: "Murray's argument is just the 1985 Figaro projection again, and since that projection was wrong, so is he." Name the step that fails (Murray accepts the number was wrong and moves the claim from a count to a direction and a mechanism — the second concatenation — which the projection's failure does not touch), and state what evidence bears on his version (integration outcomes by generation; secularisation rates among Muslim Europeans; the classification of mixed-ancestry children). The solution notes that the lesson has checked the first two (integration by itself failed for the non-EU second generation; secularization did not come) and that the third is still the demographers' open point.
6. *Synthesis — counter-argument stem*: Someone claims that Miller's framework answers Murray completely: enforce the border, require civic integration, cap refugees at a fair share. Name two facts from this lesson the claim fails to account for (the deportation gap — 220,000 orders, 11,300 removals — shows "enforce" is a capacity, not a policy; the second concatenation says there is no agreed public culture to require integration into), and state what would have to be true for the claim to survive.
7. *Derivation the lesson skipped*: Murray's 81-people parable has no stopping point; Miller's duty of rescue has a "morally excruciating" residue. Derive, from Miller's own premises, the condition under which a state may refuse the 82nd person, and say whether Murray's reductio touches it. The solution shows that Miller's limit is cost to the state's capacity to protect rights, which is an empirical threshold neither author quantifies.
8. *Attack the verdict*: Take "chosen in its instruments, not in its scale". State in its best form the case that it was chosen in its scale too: governments were told by their own research that flows could exceed forecasts, they kept the instruments after the numbers were known (the UK did not restrict after 2004; Germany did not enforce rotation after 1973), and a choice renewed with knowledge of its results is a choice of those results. Then say what evidence would be needed to overturn the (a) half of the verdict — what documents, from which years, showing what — and what would overturn the (b) half from the other side. The solution grants that renewal with knowledge narrows the gap after the first decade, and states that the verdict holds until policy papers show the realised scale was projected and accepted when the instruments were chosen.
9. **FORWARD (E7)** → `the-2015-crisis-read-three-ways`: Murray says rescue nearer Libya "pulled" migrants ("Yet how could it not?"). Write down what pattern in the monthly arrival numbers for Italy before, during and after Mare Nostrum (October 2013–October 2014) would support the claim, and what pattern would count against it. The solution says the reader has designed the test the next lesson runs on the UNHCR series — and that the honest result may be that the series cannot decide it, because Libya's collapse moves the numbers at the same time.

Register rule (binding on the lesson): The course judges arguments, not people: no condemning or approving labels for any author in its own voice; a label may appear only inside a quotation, with who applied it and when. It does pass verdicts on arguments: where the lesson stages a dispute it has the evidence to settle, and in every module's closing lesson, it says which side wins, how firmly (established / strong / on balance / open) and why, as recorded in research/verdicts/. "Open" is a finding, stated with what would settle it, never a way to avoid one. The same tests apply to the left and the right.

The lesson's test is symmetry: the same questions, at the same depth and in the same order (entries →
re-examination → reply → what survives), for the hereditarian tables and the institutional table — and
for the downstream uses on both sides of the dispute (a hereditarian-side meta-analysis and a
critic-side genetic bound). Its lesson verdicts are about what each table can carry. Its last section is
the module's verdict (REGISTER.md, module 13): on what causes the measured gaps, how firmly, and what would
settle what is open, in both directions. "Open" there is a finding with its reasons and its tests, not a
refuge.

    distinction (what the second table is for and the geographic objection to it); the module's four
    premises (for the close).
  - *Departures from the idealisation the lesson must name.* The ideal dataset is a random sample of each
    nation (or each colony's settlers), measured once with one invariant instrument at one date, with no
    missing entries, analysed by a method that can tell cause from association. The national-IQ tables
    depart: convenience samples (48 children; 22 skilled workers; immigrants tested abroad), a dozen tests
    over sixty years date-corrected by an assumed rate, a floor value for samples below a test's range,
    discretionary weights and corrections, invariance untested for African samples, and many countries
    filled from neighbours; their analysis is a correlation of country means with the direction of cause
    assumed. The 2019 revision publishes a sample rating for every entry — and its own analysis shows that

    soldiers, bishops and sailors rather than settlers, in barracks or on campaign, in different decades,
    with many colonies assigned another place's rate; its analysis is an instrument whose validity rests on
    an untestable assumption. The meta-analysis departs: 139 studies whose means differ far more than
    sampling error allows (I² = 98%), so its "mean" summarises a spread. The genetic bound departs: it
    takes a continental average from the national table as its input. All of these were made auditable by
    their authors, who published the derivations; every critique used that publication.

## VERDICT

### The module verdict (heredity-and-civilization)

- **Question judged:** are the measured gaps between peoples, within the US and between nations, caused by
  heredity, environment, geography or institutions, and in what proportion?
- **Verdict and grades:** **open** on the genetic share; **established** on the two limits; **strong** on
  what the national tables can carry; **strong** that regimes and freedom are not explained mainly by
  genes; **strong** that culture persists across generations and is not put on at will (round 2; see
  "Regimes and culture" below).
  - *The first three premises of the hereditarian argument largely hold.* The tests measure the same
    abilities in both US groups and predict outcomes without bias against the lower-scoring group
    (`what-intelligence-tests-measure`). Measured intelligence is substantially heritable within Western
    populations (`heritability-within-and-between-groups`). The US gap is real: about 0.75 SD in recent child
    norms, about 1 SD in adult norms (`us-gap-size-trend-and-environment`).
  - *The fourth step, from a real gap to a genetic one, has not been made.* No design has measured a genetic
    contribution free of confounding (`adoption-studies-across-groups`, `admixture-and-polygenic-scores`).
    Measured environment does not account for the whole gap either: parental education and income leave
    about 6 points of the child WISC gap.
  - *Two limits are established.* The gap as it stands is not wholly genetic: it narrowed by several points
    within decades, varies from near zero to 1.5 SD across school districts, and widened in maths after 2020.
    And no evidence excludes a genetic share.
  - *Between nations.* The association of measured skills with income survives better data. The lowest
    national-IQ entries do not survive audit: the sub-Saharan African levels are biased low by about ten
    points against the 2010 systematic review. Nothing in the national tables bears on whether the
    differences are genetic.
  - *Institutions.* Only institutions have a clean natural experiment behind them (the Koreas, Nogales;
    `geography-or-institutions`). It shows institutions can produce gaps as large as any between nations. It
    does not apportion the world's gaps.
- **What decides it:** research/heredity-evidence-review.md, "Overall answer" 1–7 and "Claims the lessons
  must NOT make" (both lists), with the lesson verdicts of the module.
- **Strongest objection:** "open" is evasion, because the indirect evidence (heritability, invariance, the
  Spearman pattern, admixture correlations, polygenic means, the stalled convergence) all points one way, and
  a majority of surveyed experts put the genetic share above zero. It does not overturn the verdict. Each
  line is logically uninformative about the between-group cause (heritability, invariance), confounded
  (admixture slopes, which sum genetic and environmental effects of ancestry), invalid across ancestries at
  present (polygenic means), or not evidence at all (opinion that correlates with the respondent's politics
  and sex). The opposite objection, that the narrowing proves the gap environmental, fails for a matching
  reason: it shows that part of the gap is environmental, not all of it.
- **What would settle it:** a well-powered sibling-difference admixture design with observed skin colour and
  local ancestry, and within-family GWAS of cognition in African-ancestry samples. For nations:
  representative samples, invariance tested across countries, and means tracked through development.

### Regimes and culture (round 2, author's review — part of the module verdict)

Two verdicts stand beside the module verdict. They answer a question the module's data raise and
the gap verdict does not: whether the kind of regime a people lives under, and how free it is, is
explained mainly by its genes. Sources are staged in `texts/round2-regimes/` (and
`texts/acemoglu-robinson/`, `texts/liberal-forecasts/integration/`); line numbers refer to the
staged files.

- **Verdict R1: regimes and freedom are not explained mainly by genes. Grade: strong.** Five
  natural experiments hold ancestry fixed and vary the regime within a lifetime:
  - *The Koreas.* Before division Korea had "an unprecedented homogeneity in terms of language,
    ethnicity, and culture" (Why Nations Fail, l.683); the differences "did not exist prior to
    the end of the Second World War" (l.769); a tenfold income gap by the late 1990s (l.779);
    one is a hereditary dictatorship, the other a democracy.
  - *Taiwan.* Martial law from 1949 "until 1987" (ROC portal, `taiwan-gov-portal-history.txt`
    l.148, l.182; exact days 19/20 May 1949 – 15 July 1987 [secondary]); the first direct
    presidential election in 1996 (l.198); Freedom House 2025: Free, 94/100
    (`fh-fiw2025-taiwan.txt`). The same population under both regimes: "nearly 95 percent" Han
    (`taiwan-gov-portal-people.txt` l.86), descended mostly from Fujian and Guangdong migrants of
    the past few centuries; in 10,801 Taiwan Biobank participants 79.9% have southern Han
    ancestry, 5% northern Han and 14.5% a third group "similar to other southern Han Chinese"
    (Chen et al., *Hum Mol Genet* 2016, `chen-2016-taiwan-biobank-han-structure.txt` l.249,
    l.284, l.603). The 1949 arrivals numbered about 1.2 million (portal l.146); a 2004 survey
    quoted by Chen gives post-1949 mainlanders 8%, Min-Nan 73.3%, Hakka 13.4%, indigenous 1.9%
    (l.255). The often-quoted "13% mainlanders" is **not bound** — use 8% (2004 survey) or
    1.2 million arrivals.
  - *The two Germanies.* One population, divided by military defeat and an Allied border; "ex
    ante uniform", with no significant income difference between the future East and West in
    1928–36 (Alesina & Giuliano 2015, `alesina-giuliano-2015-culture-and-institutions-iza.txt`
    l.1265–1278, n.58). By 1950 the East's output per head was about half the West's ($2,102
    against $4,281, 1990 $) and in 1989 still under half ($8,422 against $18,016)
    (`owid-two-germanies-gdp-per-capita-tcb2015.txt`, Conference Board via OWID): the gap opened
    under occupation and the East never closed it. Note for the writer: this is not a fresh
    divergence after 1950; say "opened between the 1930s and 1950, and never closed".
  - *Germany across 1919–1949.* One population lived under a parliamentary republic
    (1919–33), a dictatorship (1933–45) and two opposed regimes from 1949 [dates standard;
    Weimar counts bound elsewhere in the course, `CHANGELOG.md` "Weimar counts confirmed"].
  - *Spain and Portugal.* Dictatorships to the mid-1970s, democracies since: Portugal's
    constitution of 2 April 1976 "after the break with the previous authoritarian regime"
    (`assembleia-republica-revisoes-constitucionais.txt` l.11–17); Spain's constitution
    sanctioned 27 December 1978 (`cortes-1978-12-27-sancion-constitucion.txt`; Franco's death in
    1975 [secondary]). Output per head roughly quintupled in both, 1950–1989 (Spain $2,189 to
    $11,582; Portugal $2,086 to $10,372; same OWID file).
  What decides it: a gene pool cannot change in a generation, and in each case the regime did.
  Genes may still shape the range of what a population sustains easily; the natural experiments
  show that range is wide enough to hold a hereditary dictatorship and a free democracy within
  one ancestry. Would be overturned by: evidence that the populations diverged genetically on
  relevant traits within the periods (selective emigration on a scale that changes the means),
  which none of the sources shows.
- **Verdict R2: culture persists across generations and is not put on at will. Grade: strong.**
  The World Values Survey finds "both massive cultural change and the persistence of distinctive
  cultural traditions"; heritage "leaves an imprint on values that endures despite
  modernization" (Inglehart & Baker 2000, abstract, `inglehart-baker-2000-abstract.txt`). The
  trust of American descendants of immigrants is "significantly influenced by the country of
  origin", into the fourth generation (Algan & Cahuc, *AER* 2010,
  `algan-cahuc-2010-inherited-trust.txt` l.5–11, l.401–411). Values change "gradually, if ever,
  but rarely within two generations" (Alesina & Giuliano 2015, l.418–421). The course's own
  finding: 91% of those raised Muslim in France keep the religion, against 67% of those raised
  Catholic, and across fifteen countries second-generation Muslims keep first-generation
  religiosity while Christians converge (INSEE 2023; Güveli & Platt 2023;
  `murray-strange-death-of-europe`, strong). Regimes also shape culture: East Germans remain
  more pro-government after reunification (Alesina & Giuliano l.1272–1275). The two verdicts fit
  together: what persists is carried by upbringing and institutions, and it moves when
  institutions change, slowly.
- **Objection (author's review) (a), at full strength:** "Peoples live as they want. Some
  peoples — Muslims and the Chinese — do not understand or want freedom, and that is genetic:
  culture is made by people with certain genes." Its best form: gene–culture coevolution is a
  real field — "culture normally evolves more rapidly than genes, creating novel environments
  that expose genes to new selective pressures", with effects theory suggests "were profound"
  (Richerson, Boyd & Henrich, *PNAS* 2010, `richerson-boyd-henrich-2010-gene-culture-coevolution.txt`
  l.147; lactase persistence the canonical case, l.223); selection on alleles associated with
  cognition is detected in ancient DNA (Akbari et al. 2026, `texts/round2-west/`); and the
  surveys do record real value differences. The answer: what holds is the value difference, and
  it is cultural (R2) — Pew finds majorities for sharia as official law in South Asia (84%) and
  the Middle East and North Africa (74%) (`pew-2013-worlds-muslims-overview.txt` l.167); the
  fault line between Muslim and Western publics is gender equality and sexual liberalization,
  "Eros far more than Demos" (Norris & Inglehart 2002, l.23–36). What fails is "do not want
  freedom" as a description of preferences about government: the difference in support for democracy between Islamic and Western societies is "extremely
  modest" (Norris & Inglehart l.505–530); at least 73% in eight
  Arab countries hold that democracy, despite its problems, is the best system (Arab Barometer
  VIII, 2025, l.145); Senegal is rated Free (69/100). And "it is genetic" fails on the natural
  experiments: Taiwan is a Chinese population that lived under martial law and chose democracy,
  rated Free 94/100. Gene–culture coevolution works over millennia (lactase); the regimes in R1
  changed in years. Grade unchanged: R1 strong.
- **Objection (author's review) (b):** "The best Germans were killed in the war, so the
  post-war population was not the same." Full strength: German military dead were over 5.3
  million (Bundestag text archive 2011, `bundestag-2011-textarchiv-ueberfall-sowjetunion.txt`
  l.73, citing the standard figure [secondary for Overmans 1999; book not read]), concentrated
  in young men. The answer: the losses came from the whole Reich, and no source the course holds
  shows a difference between East and West per head [not bound — say so]; the Weimar republic
  and its fall happened before the war, in the undepleted population; the 1928–36 East–West
  parity and the 1950 gap opened under occupation, not by any selection the war could make
  between the zones; and the post-war recovery in the West was built by those who remained.
  Selection by war deaths would have to depress the East and spare the West, and nothing shows
  it did.
- **Objection (author's review) (c):** "East Germany was ruled by a foreign ressentiment
  culture imposed by the USSR." Conceded in part: the regime came from Moscow and was held by
  Soviet arms. The rest answered with numbers: the population was German — 18.4 million at the
  1950 census, 17.1 million in 1961, 16.4 million in 1989 (Statistisches Jahrbuch 1990, Anhang
  DDR, `statjb-brd-1990-anhang-ddr-bevoelkerung.txt` l.14–18); Soviet forces were a garrison,
  not settlers — 337,800 soldiers and 208,400 dependants and civilian staff under the 1991–94
  withdrawal plan (BT-Drs. 12/1064, l.200–203; about 340,000 soldiers, withdrawal completed
  31 August 1994, Bundesregierung Bulletin 1994, l.327–329); about 3 million fled the GDR
  between 1949 and summer 1961 (Bundesarchiv, `bundesarchiv-mfs-lexikon-republikflucht.txt`
  l.279); the regime fell in 1989. That is the point of the case: an imposed regime governed the
  same people for forty years, which is a regime explanation, not a genetic one. The objection
  concedes R1.
- **Objection (author's review) (d):** "The Taiwanese are not the same Chinese population."
  Answer from the population facts above: nearly 95% Han, mostly of Fujian and Guangdong
  descent, genetically clustering with southern Han Chinese (Chen et al. 2016); about 1.2
  million arrived in 1949 from across the mainland. Taiwan differs from the mainland in history
  (Japanese rule 1895–1945 [standard dates, not bound]) and institutions, not in a way the genetic data show. And the case
  does not need mainland comparison: the same Taiwanese population lived under martial law to
  1987 and under democracy after.

### The lesson verdicts (the tables)

- (a) "Sub-Saharan African IQ is about 70" as a measurement → **not supported.** Grade: **strong**. The 2010
  systematic review (Wicherts, Dolan & van der Maas) finds an N-weighted mean of 76.5 across 109 samples,
  and samples meeting its criteria at about 80–82; the reply's exclusions tracked each sample's own mean
  (biserial −.88). Strongest objection: the higher samples are urban and schooled, so representativeness
  favours the low entries. It does not overturn the verdict, because blind raters found the reply's
  inclusions tracked no objective feature of sampling, only the score; and even the critics' ~80 is a score
  on Western norms with untested invariance, which the verdict states. Would be overturned by:
  representative national samples, with invariance tested, averaging near 70.
- (b) "National IQ differences are artefacts" → **fails.** Grade: **strong**. National IQ correlates
  r = .88 with TIMSS mathematics, and harmonized learning outcomes for 164 countries show the same
  association with income. Strongest objection: school tests are themselves conversions with their own
  choices, and a 2022 critique holds comparable measures impossible in principle. It does not overturn the
  verdict: the abstract-level critique gives no evidence for "impossible", and independent instruments agree
  on the ordering. Would be overturned by: representative, invariance-tested data that dissolve the ordering.
- (c) The settler-mortality causal estimate (Acemoglu, Johnson & Robinson 2001) → **open.** The 2012
  recoding (Albouy) weakens the first stage on own-territory rates until the interval has no upper bound (verify, as in step 7); the reply holds
  the assigned rates carry information. What would settle it: independent mortality data for the colonies
  now assigned borrowed rates, and a test of the exclusion restriction (disease acting on income directly).
  The correlation and the institutional account's other evidence (Nogales, the Koreas) stand either way.

Opening (course.yaml, verbatim): "Both of the big explanations of why nations differ rest on one table. The
hereditarian account rests on a list of average IQs for eighty-one countries, since revised and extended; the
institutional account on a list of how many European soldiers and bishops died per year in each colony. Each
table has been re-examined by critics who went back to the studies behind the numbers, and the same can be
done for the tables that summarise a century of American testing. The lesson takes the tables, traces entries
to their sources, and asks what each can carry." Develop it in one paragraph before any answer: each account
is an argument; each argument's evidence is a table; a table is a list of claims, each with a source that can
be opened. The newcomer's two attempts both fail: to trust the table that supports the account one already
prefers, or to trust both because both were published. The lesson's question is narrower and answerable.

1. **What does one ask of a table?** The drill was built for a letter or a speech; what are its questions for a
   column of numbers? Obvious attempt: check the arithmetic. Fails: the arithmetic is usually right; what
   decides is what stands behind each cell. The answer (Figure 1, a drill card): for every entry — who
   measured, whom, when, how many, measured or filled in; for the table — what comparison it supports and
   what statistical step carries it to the conclusion. Metadata first, for every artefact, by the same rule:
   a British psychologist and a Finnish political scientist, Praeger's series on human evolution and
   intelligence, one author's thanks to a foundation that funds hereditarian research (l.466); the 2019
   revision from the Ulster Institute; three Amsterdam methodologists in *Intelligence*, the field's
   journal, with the reply and rejoinder in the same issue; three economists in the *American Economic
   Review*; a 2025 meta-analysis in *Mankind Quarterly*; a 2021 genetic analysis in the *American Journal of
   Physical Anthropology*. The prediction step as a Task: from metadata alone, what would you expect each
   to be good and weak at? Because: provenance tells the reader where to look first; it decides nothing
   about any entry ("use, not bias") — and the rule binds equally for the critics' venues and the
   defenders'.

2. **One national average, built in front of the reader — three times.** What is "Ghana: 71"? Recall the
   method in one clause (`<Ref>` hereditarian-case-iq-and-nations: each sample scored against British
   norms, date-corrected). FigureSequence (Figure 2): (a) 2002 — two studies: 225 Accra adults on the Culture
   Fair test (82 → 80 on British norms), 1,639 adolescents "a representative sample" on the Coloured
   Progressive Matrices (64 → 62); equal weight per study → 71 (l.8061–8064). The larger, representative
   sample counts no more than the smaller; the result describes neither sample. (b) 2019 — the same two
   studies recomputed (72.42 and 59.17), three more samples, weighted by sample rating, combined with a
   school-assessment IQ of 54.69 from TIMSS → 58.16 (l.3615–3651). (c) 2010 review — only the Accra sample
   meets its five criteria, rated 5 of 5 → 75.7 (73.3 with Raven's data; Table 5). Three published values for
   one country span 58 to 76. Because: an entry is a set of choices — which samples, which weights, which
   conversion — and each table records its choices; an average of disagreeing estimates is a convention, not
   a measurement.

3. **Tracing entries: what each edition records and omits.** Is Ghana typical? Three more countries, the same
   three tables (Figure 3, a small table-figure).
   - **Equatorial Guinea.** 2002: one study, 48 children aged 10–14, WISC-R, c. 1984, 63 → 59, the lowest
     value in the table (l.8037–8038); the appendix omits where they were tested, how chosen, their health.

     children, half diagnosed with brain organic disorders, and the chapter's lead author confirmed the sample
     was Spanish (footnote 5). 2019: Equatorial Guinea has no value (Table 16 row empty). The entry is the
     clearest case of what a traced source can change: not a re-weighting but a sample from another country.
   - **Sierra Leone.** 2002: 22 skilled workers c. 1965 and 60 adults c. 1964, both below the test's range,
     both "assigned" 64; no date correction, unlike the floor samples for Congo and Guinea (l.8188–8192 vs
     l.8013, l.8070). 2019: the 60 adults' study (Berry, 1966) recomputed — two Temne samples, one village and
     one town, ages "10 to above 40" averaged as 25, converted to an adult scale and date-corrected → 45.07,
     tied for second-lowest in the 2019 table (with Liberia; Nepal is lowest); the authors' own reservation, quoted (l.5699–5702):
     never confirmed by school assessments, "very old", "an ethnic minority". Liberia, with no samples, is given the same
     45.07 by imputation (l.7216). 2010 review: 84.5 from two samples, 91.3 from the one meeting its
     criteria. One old study yields 64 or 45 depending on the conversion; a different choice of studies yields

   - **Ethiopia.** 2002: 250 fifteen-year-old Ethiopian immigrants to Israel, c. 1989, 65 → 63

     move the number up.

     (l.1960–1968).
   Because: the precision of the tables is uneven, the weakest entries cluster where the hereditarian claim is
   strongest, and a traced entry can move by twenty points or by none.

4. **The 2019 revision's own instrument: coding representativeness.** Problem: the critics' complaint was
   unrepresentative samples; did the revision answer it? What it did (l.305–345, l.395–515): every sample
   coded by origin, SES, composition and character — representative, normative (a test-standardisation
   sample), random, or selected — and given a rating out of 5 used as a weight; every step published so
   others can "refollow" it. What its own data then show (l.7690–7745): samples of high SES score 16.78 above
   the total and low-SES samples 8.92 below; normative samples +8.17, representative +4.53, random −3.20,
   selected −4.98. Two readings, both the authors': convenience samples in poor countries are rural and poor,
   and standardisation samples may under-represent the worst-off; or test choice is confounded with country.
   Because: the revision measured the representativeness problem instead of removing it; a weight cannot
   restore a population that was never sampled, and the lowest final values went down, not up (Ghana 58,
   Sierra Leone 45). The genetics section ★ (one paragraph): Tables 41–42 correlate national IQ with country

   principle — not supported in its abstract (dossier §7). Because: an audit of entries attacks levels; an
   association can survive the loss of its worst entries; and "corroboration" from school tests is itself a
   conversion with its own choices.

6. **The institutional table: why it needed an instrument.** Seam (L10b): from the national-IQ table one
   expects the institutional table to be a second correlation, open to the same objection; it was built to
   escape that objection. Institutions and income are correlated; rich countries can also afford good
   institutions; how could anyone show which way the arrow runs? Obvious attempt: control for other
   variables. Fails: nothing controls for the reverse arrow. The answer — an instrument, defined in two
   sentences: a fact from the past that moved institutions and can reach today's income by no road except
   through them; compare incomes across places the instrument sorted. The chain (Figure 5, FigureSequence):
   where European soldiers and bishops died at high rates, Europeans did not settle → they set up institutions
   to extract (`<Ref>` geography-or-institutions, one clause) → early institutions persisted → today's
   protection against expropriation → income in 1995. Keeper magnitude only if verified (Nigeria–Chile).
   Because: an instrument turns a correlation into a causal estimate if, and only if, its one assumption (no
   other road) holds and its numbers are sound.

7. **Tracing the mortality entries, and the critic's recoding.** What is "Sierra Leone: so many deaths per
   thousand"? Three kinds of entry: an own-territory garrison series (British troops at Freetown in the early
   nineteenth century, from the returns behind Curtin's tables); a rate from soldiers on campaign (verify
   which entries); a colony with no figure of its own, assigned another place's rate; and Latin American
   values from bishops. The OBJECTION (the 2012 comment): more than half of the 64 carry a rate measured
   elsewhere (verify 36 of 64); campaign and garrison rates are mixed; keep own-territory rates and allow for
   campaigns, and the first stage weakens until the confidence interval has no upper bound — the data no
   longer identify the effect (verify). The REPLY (same issue): the assignments follow disease environments
   that cross borders; discarding them throws away information; capped values and alternative data leave the
   result standing. The second line of objection, to "no other road": disease may lower income directly;
   settlers brought skills as well as institutions; settler mortality is a fact of geography, the rival
   account's ultimate cause (`<Ref>` geography-or-institutions, one clause). Because: an instrument is only as
   good as its first link and its exclusion; the audit tests the first, argument tests the second. Sierra
   Leone appears in both tables — the symmetry in one country (Exercise 2).

   results built on such data? Two uses, one from each side of the dispute, the same drill.
   - **(a) A meta-analysis of 139 US studies (2025).** Metadata first (two authors, *Mankind Quarterly*,
     2025; inclusion by representativeness, l.15–19). What it reports: a random-effects Black mean of 85
     (I² = 98%: the studies differ far more than sampling error allows, so the mean summarises a spread);
     small-study effects, corrected to 81.5; the large national samples average 81.8 (l.151–193). What its own
     test shows about trend: cohort, age and subtests are not significant moderators; the cohort coefficient is
     +0.037 points a year (SE 0.024) — not significantly different from zero, and its interval also admits a
     rise of several points over the century (Table 1). What its abstract says: "strong evidence against
     convergence … in cohorts born after 1960" (l.22–23). Where that claim comes from: the discussion, from
     other sources — the Woodcock-Johnson cohorts, SAT and ACT gaps (two of them blog posts), ABCD's 80
     (l.311–331); its own by-cohort means (82.7, 83.8, 86.5, 85.9, Table A6) are descriptive. The drill's
     finding: the paper's evidence for its level is its own; its evidence for no convergence after 1960 is
     borrowed, and its own test does not show it — nor does it show convergence. The trend question was
     settled as far as it can be in `us-gap-size-trend-and-environment` (Ref, dossier §2 Status: narrowed
     between early-century and ~1970 cohorts, flat or slightly wider since on most series).
   - **(b) A genetic upper bound that takes national IQs as input (2021).** Recall the bound in one clause
     (`<Ref>` admixture-and-polygenic-scores: under neutrality, at most a small share of the
     Africa–Europe difference could be additive genetic). Trace its input: "the country-level IQ results used
     by" a 2015 analysis — Africa 68.4, Europe 99.2, a difference of 30.8 — i.e. the national table's African
     levels. Its output has two parts with different dependence (Figure 7): the genetic among-group variance
     (8.5–28.4 IQ points², from SNP F_ST, h² and the within-group variance) and the absolute bound "4.7–8.5 IQ

10. **The weighing: the gap is real and partly environmental, its genetic share is open, and the national
   tables cannot decide it.** (Heading as a claim; it replaces "What the module leaves settled and open".) The
   module's question set against everything the module has tested, against the strongest objection from each
   side (VERDICT: "open is evasion"; "the narrowing proves it environmental"), reaching the module verdict in
   the course's voice with its grades. First the symmetry
   (Figure 1 completed): all the tables rest on old, small, unrepresentative records or on filled gaps; all
   published their derivations, which is what made every audit possible; each drew a re-examination that
   changed entries and a reply that defended the conclusion. What survives differs with what was at stake: the
   national-IQ association with income survives the loss of its weakest entries because better data show the
   same association, but it never identified a cause, and nothing in it bears on whether the differences are
   genetic (`<Ref>` heritability-within-and-between-groups: the within/between gap is untouched by country
   means); the settler-mortality table was the causal identification, so a successful recoding removes the
   estimate while leaving the correlation and the institutional account's other evidence (Nogales, the
   Koreas). Then **the module's close**, as the `<Conclusion>` carries it (Figure 8, a three-column
   settled / open / what would close it):
   - *Settled* (dossier Overall answer 1–2, §2 Status): within groups, measured intelligence is substantially
     heritable in Western samples, with lower DNA-based and within-family estimates; the US Black–White gap is
     real on the tests, about 0.75 SD in recent child norms, about 1 SD in adult norms, 0.85 SD in 2010s
     achievement data; in the main US datasets the tests measure the same abilities in both groups; the gap
     narrowed between cohorts born early in the century and those born around 1970 and has not narrowed on most
     series since. National averages of measured skills differ widely and correlate strongly with income; the
     lowest entries are unreliable (§7 Status). So premises 1–3 largely hold.
   - *Open* (Overall answer 3, 5–7): whether any of the gap is genetic, and in what proportion. No design has
     measured a genetic contribution free of confounding; no measured set of environments accounts for all of
     it (parental education and income leave about 6 points in children). The indirect evidence on each side is
     weak about causes.
   - *Two limits that are established* (Overall answer 4) — said in so many words: the gap as it stands cannot
     be wholly genetic, because it narrowed by several points within decades, varies from near zero to 1.5 SD
     across school districts, and widened in maths after 2020 — changes no genetic cause can produce on those
     time-scales; and no evidence excludes a genetic share.
   - *Institutions* (Ref `geography-or-institutions`): only institutions have a clean natural experiment
     behind them (the Koreas, Nogales); it shows they can produce gaps as large as any between nations, and
     it does not apportion the world's gaps.
   - *Regimes are not mainly genetic; culture persists* (round 2, author's review; VERDICT "Regimes and
     culture"). A short paragraph pair, then the objections. R1 (**strong**): the Koreas, Taiwan under
     martial law 1949–87 and democratic since, Germany from Weimar to 1933 to the two states of 1949, Spain
     and Portugal to the mid-1970s — each the same ancestry under opposite regimes within a lifetime. R2
     (**strong**): beside it, the record that culture is inherited and slow — the World Values Survey's
     persistent cultural zones, origin-country trust into the fourth generation of American descendants,
     second-generation Muslim religiosity in Europe (`<Ref murray-strange-death-of-europe>`). Then the four
     **Objections (author's review)** in their best form, each answered in two or three sentences with the
     VERDICT's numbers: (a) peoples live as they want, Muslims and the Chinese do not want freedom, and
     culture is made by people with certain genes — gene–culture coevolution stated as a real field
     (lactase), the value differences conceded as cultural and persistent, "do not want freedom" and "it is
     genetic" answered by the surveys on democracy and by Taiwan; (b) the best Germans died in the war —
     over 5.3 million military dead conceded, answered by Weimar's fall before the war, the 1928–36
     East–West parity and the gap opening under occupation; (c) the GDR was a foreign culture imposed —
     the imposition conceded, the population German (18.4 million in 1950), the Soviet presence a garrison
     (about 340,000 soldiers), three million fled before 1961 and the regime fell in 1989; (d) the
     Taiwanese are not the same Chinese population — nearly 95% Han, southern Han in the genetic data, and
     the same population on both sides of 1987. The bridge sentence: whatever genes contribute to the gaps
     in measured ability (open, above), they do not decide whether a people is free; what it inherits and
     cannot put on at will is its culture, which is where the course's last modules take the question of
     what holds a people together (`<Ref hungarys-fundamental-law>`, forward).
   - *The strongest objection, answered* (VERDICT): "open" is evasion because the indirect evidence all
     points one way; the reply line by line (uninformative, confounded, invalid across ancestries, not
     evidence); and the opposite objection, that the narrowing proves the gap environmental, answered the
     same way.
   - *Expert opinion* (§8): the American Psychological Association's task force (1996) found "no adequate
     explanation" available, with less support for the genetic than for the cultural accounts; a 2012 review
     in the same journal found "no new direct evidence"; a 2020 survey of 86 experts put the genetic share at
     49% on average with a standard deviation of 31, 16% choosing wholly environmental and 6% wholly genetic,
     with belief correlated with the respondent's sex and politics — and the survey's authors list its limits
     (one response in five, self-selected). Opinion is reported as opinion, not as evidence of the answer.
   - *What would close it*: recalled, not re-derived (`<Ref>` admixture-and-polygenic-scores, one sentence —
     well-powered sibling-difference admixture designs with observed colour and local ancestry, and
     within-family GWAS of cognition in African-ancestry samples; the residue no design removes). For nations
     (§7): representative samples, invariance tested across countries, national means tracked through
     development.
   Because: the module's question is answered as far as the evidence goes and no further, in either direction;
   what remains open is open for stated reasons, with stated tests.
   `<Conclusion>` (the module verdict in plain words): the tests measure the same abilities in both groups and
   predict without bias; intelligence is substantially heritable within groups; the US gap is real. Whether
   any of it is genetic has not been shown: the course holds the genetic share **open**, and says what study
   would settle it. Two things are **established**: the gap as it stands is not wholly genetic, and no
   evidence rules out a genetic share. Between nations, measured skills go with income, the lowest national
   entries are too low by about ten points (**strong**), and nothing in the tables bears on genes; the rules
   a country lives under can open a gap as wide as any. Regimes and freedom are not explained mainly by
   genes (**strong**): the same people have lived under dictatorship and democracy within one lifetime, in
   Korea, Taiwan, Germany, Spain and Portugal. Culture, by contrast, is inherited and slow to change
   (**strong**). Then the hand-off: the settler-mortality table
   compares colonies with colonies, and the next module asks "compared to what?".

## Pitfalls / subtleties to flag

- **Symmetry is the lesson's test.** Same questions, same depth, same order for each table and each downstream
  use. The institutional table gets traced entries, a critic and a reply, as the national-IQ tables do; the
  critic-side genetic bound gets its input audited, as the hereditarian-side meta-analysis gets its abstract
  audited. Neither table is "the scientific one".
- **Use, not bias, on provenance — both directions.** The Pioneer Fund acknowledgment, the Ulster Institute,
  *Mankind Quarterly* and the 2012 review's authorship by parties to the dispute are metadata; so are the AER
  and *Intelligence*. None is evidence about any entry. The closing question for each source is what its
  provenance changes about what it can be USED to establish.
- **The reply was not read.** Lynn and Meisenberg 2010 is [not read]. The lesson states its position only as
  the rejoinder and the 2019 book report it, and says so; an audit that cites an unread paper as if read
  fails its own test.
- **"Validated by Lynn and Meisenberg (2010)"** (2019 book l.311) is the revision's claim about its pedigree;
  the rejoinder disputes the selection that paper used. State both.
- **Two different "69"s.** Rindermann's Sub-Saharan Africa 69 (2019 book l.264) combines school assessments
  and tests; the reply's 69 is from psychometric samples. Do not merge them.
- **Ghana's corroboration cuts the other way.** The review's own Table 1 shows achievement-based predictions
  for Ghana of 68.8 and 71.8 — agreeing with the low value — and the 2019 TIMSS-based 54.69 is lower still. Do
  not present "achievement data corroborate ~82" as true country by country; it is the review's overall
  estimate.
- **Bird's percentages vs his IQ-point bound.** Only the percentages depend on the national IQs; the 4.7–8.5
  point bound does not (Equation 3 uses SNP F_ST, h², σ²_P). Do not say the audit "undoes" the bound. And the
  bound is under neutrality (dossier §5): a bound, not a measured zero.
- **The meta-analysis is not a convergence test failed or passed.** Its cohort moderator is not significant —
  which is neither "no convergence" nor "convergence". The abstract's sentence is a claim the paper sources
  elsewhere.
- **Terms to anchor (L11), three per section at most:** imputation; floor (a score below the lowest point a
  test's norms can express); date correction (Ref the Flynn effect — never re-taught); Raven's Progressive
  Matrices (a non-verbal pattern-completion test; our schematic, never a real item); WISC-R; normative
  (standardisation) sample; systematic review and inclusion criteria; meta-analysis, heterogeneity (I²: the
  share of the spread among study results not due to chance), small-study effect; moderator; biserial
  correlation (one clause, or say "strongly predicted"); instrument and the exclusion restriction ("no other
  road"); first stage; mortality "per 1,000 mean strength"; expropriation risk; garrison vs campaign; F_ST (one
  clause, Ref). Places: Freetown; Equatorial Guinea (small former Spanish colony on the Gulf of Guinea,
  independent 1968).
- **Real misreading (L13), one slot:** "a published dataset is a measurement" — refute with Ghana three times
  and Equatorial Guinea. The other misreadings are Exercises 4–5.
- **Do not re-teach:** the hereditarian argument, the Flynn effect, within/between, PGS portability and the
  bound's genetics, inclusive/extractive institutions, the reversal of fortune, Diamond's causes, "what would
  settle it" — a `<Ref>` clause each. New matter: the audit, the propagation, the close.
- **Group labels:** the sources' categories inside their claims ("Black", "White", "Africans" as the review
  uses it for Black sub-Saharan test-takers); say once that they are the authors' categories.
- **Numbers (L12):** keepers — "58 to 76 for one country" (Ghana), "about 80 against about 68" (the review vs
  the compilations), "I² = 98%" (the meta-analysis's spread) and, if verified, "four colonies in ten".
  Everything else lives inside its traced entry or in a figure.
- **No re-run of regressions.** The two computations are Exercise 3 (the bound's percentage with a different
  input) and Exercise 2's consistency check; both inside a source inquiry, both ending in interpretation.
- **Verify before stating** every number from the settler-mortality papers (28/36 of 64; the Nigeria–Chile
  magnitude; specific mortality values; unbounded intervals) and Daley 2003. If a paper cannot be obtained, the
  lesson says so in References and states only what the dossier supports.
- **Copyright:** a sentence each at most; values tabulated; redraw any scatter in house style.

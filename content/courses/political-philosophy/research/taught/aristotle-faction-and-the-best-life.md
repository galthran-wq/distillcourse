# Taught: aristotle-faction-and-the-best-life

## Problem
Why are cities overthrown from within, what keeps a regime, and what is a city for? Answer reached: every regime rests on a partial justice (numerical or proportional equality), so it carries a slighted party; faction = state of mind + motive + occasion, the occasion often a trifle; the causes run backwards are the remedies (small lawbreaking first, education in the spirit of the constitution greatest); tyranny lasts only by playing the king, never over a century; survival is not the measure — the city is for virtuous activity in leisure (war for peace, business for leisure); the best city is small enough to be "taken in at a single view" and removes faction by admitting only equals and putting everyone who works outside citizenship. No verdict in this lesson (none in the register); the Roman objection is stated and split: Aristotle answers its second half (a city built for peace need not be weak), not its first (a quarrel given an office of its own makes liberty), which is weighed in machiavelli-discourses-republic.

## Explained
- Four opening cases (Pol. - "occasions may be trifling, but great interests are at stake"; Hestiaea (north Euboea) brothers' inheritance, poorer enlists the people, richer the wealthy: the quarrel finds the parties — ### The trifle and the stake
- *stasis* (faction: quarrel of parties inside one city) and *revolution* (change of regime) defined — same subsection
- V.1 quoted; III.9 partial justices only Ref'd (not re-taught); new step: a regime rules the other party by a measure that party rejects, so democracy contains the cheated rich, oligarchy the cheated poor; "Inferiors revolt in order that they may be equal, and equals that they may be superior" — ## Two equalities, two engines of civil war / ### Every regime is partly just
- Numerical (3−2 = 2−1, equal differences = greek-miracle's arithmetic) vs proportional (4:2 = 2:1, equal ratios = geometric); Example "one city, two fair shares" (1,000 citizens, 100 rich with 10× land: numerical → rich 1/10 of say; proportional → rich 1,000/1,900, just over half); remedy: use both; democracy safer than oligarchy (oligarchy's double danger); middle-class government "safest of the imperfect forms"; figure two-equalities.svg = the grievance each rule creates in the 1,000-citizen city; KeyFact: "both kinds of equality should be employed" is the recipe for polity (Ref polity-oligarchy-and-democracy-fused), each class finds its equality somewhere — ### The two kinds of equality, computed
- Anatomy V.2 quoted: state of mind (standing; "may and may not be just"), motives (gain/honour or fear of loss/dishonour), occasions (insolence, fear, excessive predominance, contempt, disproportionate increase, election intrigue, carelessness, neglect of trifles, dissimilarity); Rhodes notables (fear), Megara (contempt), ostracism (predominance; glossed as the yearly ten-year exile vote, Ref decline-of-regimes where-the-protector-was-real) — ## A state of mind, a motive, an occasion / ### Three questions
- Disproportionate growth: foot four cubits / body two spans (cubit ≈ forearm, span ≈ hand); Tarentum (Spartan colony, heel of Italy; Iapygians), Argos (Cleomenes c. 494; perioeci glossed: free but voteless people of surrounding towns), Athens (hoplite losses thinned propertied class; Ref Sicily) — ### Disproportionate growth
- Dissimilarity: Sybaris (Achaeans vs Troezenians), Piraeus more democratic, ditch breaks a regiment; greatest opposition virtue/vice, next wealth/poverty — ### Dissimilarity
- Example "placing the trifles": the four cases split into occasion/motive/state of mind; Aristotle's notes record occasion and motive, state of mind usually inferred; proxenus glossed (Mytilene) — ### The four quarrels read again
- Balanced classes with no middle → revolution (V.4); force and fraud; the Four Hundred (411) by fraud (Ref decline-of-regimes where-the-protector-was-real) — same section
- How each regime falls (V.5–7): democracy — demagogues drive the rich to combine (Cos, Megara); of old demagogue-generals became tyrants (Peisistratus); reversal seam: democracy falls most often to the rich, not the poor. Oligarchy — oppression, exclusion of rich (Massalia = Marseille), oligarch-demagogues, spendthrifts backing tyrants, oligarchy within oligarchy (Elis, 90 senators for life), mercenary generals, census overtaken by prosperity; most routes run through a slighted party = oligarchy's "double danger". Actual (so-called) aristocracies defined as mixtures: magistrates chosen by wealth and merit, virtue+wealth+number (Carthage) or virtue+number (Sparta); those leaning to oligarchy called aristocracies, to democracy polities (IV.7, V.7). Polity/aristocracy — ill-mingling, lean → change that way, aristocracies less safe (rich "insolent and avaricious"); small changes: Ambracia's qualification to nothing; Thurii (colony 440s) re-election law repealed → dynastic oligarchy, glossed: a few powerful families handing office father to son, ruling above the law (FigureSequence thurii + Task; frame-4 banner carries the gloss). External: Athens/Sparta imposing forms — ## How each regime falls
- Preservation: "opposites produce opposites" — ## What destroys, reversed, preserves
  - small lawbreaking (V.8 quoted; fallacy "if each part is little, the whole is little"); "No ordinary man can discern the beginning of evil, but only the true statesman"; IV-book deceptive devices "useless" — ### Guard against small lawbreaking
  - brotherhoods (hereditary family associations) and tribes (Ref greek-miracle Cleisthenes) glossed; bullet list: bring leading spirits of the excluded in; short tenures, moderate honours; night sentinels (invent terrors); census review; office unprofitable ("people do not … offence at being kept out … resent rulers stealing"), public accounts — ### Treat the excluded as equals, and make office unprofitable
  - the nose (V.9 quoted); neither regime survives without both rich and poor; oligarchic oath "I will be an enemy to the people" → "I will do no wrong to the people"; Aside: leaky cask, little farm, agrarian democracy best (Book VI) — ### Do not push the regime's principle to its extreme
  - education adapted to the constitution (V.9 quoted at length): not what oligarchs/democrats enjoy but what keeps the regime; oligarchs' sons in luxury vs poor hardened; "living as one likes" false freedom, living by the constitution = salvation; reversal seam: expected force, got small things + education = Plato's regime-in-the-soul as a rule; the "spirit of the constitution" sentence recalled as what survived decline-of-regimes' verdict (Ref decline-of-regimes the-verdict); remedies are judgments about particulars, not mechanical rules; KeyFact — ### The greatest preservative
- Tyranny (V.10–12): king from the better classes vs tyrant from the people against the notables; tyranny = compound of extreme oligarchy + extreme democracy — ### King and tyrant: two origins
- Monarchies fall by injustice (insult; confiscation of property), fear, contempt: insult (Harmodius and Aristogeiton, 514, Hipparchus), contempt (Sardanapalus carding wool; heirs lose tyrannies), hatred from exactions ("hatred of tyrants is inevitable"; Dionysius of Syracuse, whole property in five years), the two classes (notables plot "to rule or to escape subjection"; people do not defend him: Syracuse — Gelo tyrant from 485, succeeded by his brother Hiero, then a third brother Thrasybulus (explicitly NOT Thrasybulus of Miletus) ruling through Gelo's son; Gelo's family raised a party against him, the people drove them all out); "potter hates potter" (Ref greek-miracle); kingship preserved by limitation: Theopompus (Spartan king c. 700, credited with the ephors), "more lasting" — ### Insult, hatred, fear, contempt
- Two recipes: traditional (Periander, Persians: kill men of spirit, no common meals/clubs/education, spies, divide, impoverish — Pyramids, war; "humble, distrust, disable") vs playing the king; worked Example: each cause from the previous subsection reversed (hatred → steward with public accounts; insult → no outrage, punish through courts; contempt → temperate, soldier, pious; notables' plots/rich vs poor → honour merit, raise two not one, protect rich and poor from each other); "half virtuous, half wicked" quoted — ### Two recipes for a tyrant
- Durations: Orthagorids at Sicyon 100 yrs (moderation, laws), Cypselids 73½ (no bodyguard), Peisistratids 35 (17 + 18), Gelo and his brother Hiero 18 (7 + 10 + Thrasybulus under a year); a tyranny lasts only by becoming less of one; forward Ref machiavelli-effectual-truth: a later reader drew the opposite lesson from advice of this kind (political science = art of keeping power whatever the regime) — no causal claim that Book V shaped that work — ### The record: a century at most
- Survival not the measure; VII.1: best regime presupposes best life; three classes of goods; external goods are instruments with a limit (Ref chrematistics "the end is the limit"); lyre; best life = virtue with enough external goods; virtue not from fortune — ## What the city is for: not conquest / ### The best life
- War-cities (Sparta, Crete, Carthage armlets, Scythian cup, Iberian obelisks); VII.2 "might where there is no right" quoted; physician/pilot (Ref Thrasymachus craft); answer to Melos = inconsistency, not misdescription; exception "born to serve" (Ref natural slavery's difficulty); a city "happy in isolation" — ### Might without right
- Political vs philosophic life (VII.2–3): the withdrawn (rule = mastery or a burden) vs the statesmen ("he who does nothing cannot do well"); withdrawn right that mastery is ignoble, wrong that all rule is mastery and that inactivity beats action; statesmen right that happiness is activity, wrong that more power = more good action (robbers; among equals justice is "sharing alike", the taker has already lost virtue); contemplation "complete in themselves" is activity; an isolated city acts "by sections"; "the same life is best for each individual, and for states" → the isolated city is no lesser city — ### The political life and the philosophic
- *common good* (Remark): the good life of the whole citizen body; "not happy in regard to a portion … but … all" — same section
- VII.14 quoted: war for peace, business for leisure, necessary for honourable; *leisure* defined (time free of necessity, for what is worth doing for itself) — ## War for the sake of peace, business for the sake of leisure
- Spartan critique quoted ("lost the better part of life"; "like unused iron they lose their temper in time of peace"); Sparta's empire 404–371 (Leuctra, Thebes); VII.15: the virtues of peace (justice, temperance, philosophy) needed most by those with most leisure and good fortune — Islands of the Blest; FigureSequence means-ends + Task — ### The Spartan critique
- Objection (forward, not taught): a later republican — Rome's quarrel of nobles and people made it free; a republic built only for peace cannot defend itself. Split in two halves as a fact about the argument: second half ANSWERED (iron = war-city that won; best city brave, "no leisure for slaves"; quarrels teach laws, but quarrel not the end); first half NOT answered (Book V treats every faction as a state of mind to be prevented, never a quarrel given an office of its own, "a magistrate whose work is to object"); weighed on the Roman record in machiavelli-discourses-republic (single Ref, end of subsection). Conclusion repeats the split in one sentence — ### The objection: faction as the engine of liberty
- Size: great by function not headcount; ship a span / quarter mile; Stentor (Homer's herald, voice of fifty); citizens must know each other's characters to elect and judge (VII.4 quoted) = the feast's unstated condition (Ref); territory surveyable; harbour outside the city; fleet rowed by non-citizens — ## A city you can take in at one view / ### Size: a herald's voice
- Climate theory recalled one clause (Ref); Plato's guardians as watch-dog (Ref kallipolis-and-the-philosopher-king spirited-and-gentle-at-once); spirit stirred more against friends who slight us; "Cruel is the strife of brethren" = why faction is the bitterest conflict — ### Citizens: spirit, and the strife of brothers
- Parts vs conditions (house/builder): no craftsmen, traders or farmers as citizens (leisure); warriors young, councillors old, priests oldest = same men by age; land owned by citizens; farmers slaves or barbarian perioeci (recalled, Ref); rule by age as the one unresented inequality; dry seam: best city solves Book V by drawing its boundary round the equals — the household of Book I on a city's scale — ### Parts and conditions
- VIII.1–3: public, uniform education; "they all belong to the state"; leisure not amusement; "free and exalted souls" — ### An education for leisure

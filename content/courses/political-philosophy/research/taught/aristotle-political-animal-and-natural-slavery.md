# Taught: aristotle-political-animal-and-natural-slavery

## Problem
Can the ranks of ruler and ruled, master and slave included, be read off nature, against conventionalism (city made, justice agreed, force underneath: Plato's city of need + Glaucon's bargain + Melos V.105)? Answer reached: Aristotle makes the city natural (grows from natural pairings; is their end; man alone has speech about the just) and rule a feature of every composite, down to the natural slave who "apprehends reason without having it"; he accepts the objection that slavery by capture is force; then his own text removes every mark (body, unseen soul, capture, birth) by which a natural slave could be found. The difficulty is internal. Aristotle does hold some peoples more slavish by nature (III.14, VII.7); taught as two readings (a mark by peoples vs a tendency): both grant the group claim, I.6 means no people's label settles an individual case, and they divide over whether a sorting right on the whole is enough to enslave by. Chrematistics: acquisition bounded by living well; money as end has no bound; usury most unnatural.

## Explained
- Conventionalism named and defined in the intro (city made, justice agreed, force under the agreement) — intro paragraphs (no heading)
- Aristotle re-anchored: Stagira (north Aegean), 20 yrs in Plato's school, tutor of Alexander of Macedon (conqueror of Persian empire), Lyceum at Athens from 335, a metic there; Politics = lecture course; Bekker numbers explained (1253a7 = page, column, line of 1831 edition) — intro
- Plato's Statesman view (statesman/king/householder/master differ only in number) rejected: "governments differ in kind"; method: resolve compound into least parts, watch "first growth" — ## Begin with what grows / ### Take the compound apart
- Two natural pairings: male/female (to leave an image, not by plan); natural ruler/subject (foresight vs body, "same interest"); Delphian knife (nature makes each thing for one use; wife ≠ slave); *barbarians* (non-Greek speakers) "a community of slaves"; Euripides "meet that Hellenes should rule over barbarians" (reported as the poets' line, no preview of I.6); *Hellenes* — ### Two pairings nature makes
- Household (everyday wants; ox = poor man's slave) → village (colony of one family, eldest as king) → city (self-sufficing; originates in bare needs, continues for a good life); *telos*/end: "the nature of a thing is its end"; natural in two senses (grows unplanned; completed form); acorn = modern gloss, Aristotle's examples man/horse/family; reversal: "natural" = latest stage not earliest; need explains beginning not nature (vs Republic); assumption "nature makes nothing in vain" — ### Household, village, city
- Voice (pleasure/pain) vs speech (*logos*, speech and reason): expedient → just; shared sense of the just makes household and city; "political" = of the polis; polis of greek-miracle turned from historical outcome into human end — ## Speech about the just / ### Voice and speech (Task: what a hive lacks)
- City prior by nature (not time): stone hand; beast or god; "tribeless, lawless, hearthless"; Pericles's "useless" said of the species; best/worst animal "armed"; "justice is the bond of men in states"; founder "greatest of benefactors" ⇒ natural and founded compatible; KeyFact (reworked): natural = what it is for, so the slavery argument must find the natural slave in a soul, never in how he came to be owned (capture, birth, sale) — ### The whole before the part
- Ruling/ruled in every composite; *despotic* (soul over body; despotes) vs *constitutional* (over a part with a voice; intellect over appetite "constitutional and royal"); *royal* glossed right after the I.5 quote (king over kin, affection and age); rule of evidence: nature's intention read in uncorrupted specimens, corrupted cases set aside (does not say how to recognise them); tame animals preserved; male superior "extends to all mankind" — ## A ladder of rule / ### Every composite has a ruling part
- Household's three rules: master/slave despotic; father/child royal (love, age); husband/wife constitutional with permanent inequality (citizens take turns); deliberative faculty: slave none, woman without authority, child immature (I.13); against Socrates's same virtue (Ref first wave); "courage of a man in commanding, of a woman in obeying"; citizenship left to next lesson — ### Three rules in one household
- Slave as living possession/instrument; pilot's rudder and look-out; Daedalus statues/Hephaestus tripods ("if the shuttle would weave … masters [would not want] slaves"); production vs action; "the slave is the minister of action" — ## The natural slave / ### A living instrument
- Definition I.5 1254b16–24 in three parts: capacity (use of body), reason heard not had (above tame animal, deliberation absent not weak), benefit (better off ruled); "can be, and therefore is, another's" unpacked — ### The definition
- Natural relation = friends with common interest; law-and-force relation = "the reverse"; assumption: a human soul can lack its ruling part — ### Friends with a common interest
- Objection I.3 (contrary to nature, by law only, unjust); I.6 convention of capture; jurists "impeach"; dispute: justice as goodwill vs "mere rule of the stronger" (Ref Thrasymachus, Melos); worked Example: the middle position refuted in 3 steps (unjust war; "unworthy to be a slave"; men of highest rank captured); Aristotle accepts; Melos as the plain case of slavery by law and force — ## Slavery by law / ### What the objectors say
- Answer: Greeks won't call Greeks slaves ⇒ "they really mean the natural slave"; "some are slaves everywhere, others nowhere"; legal slavery just only where it coincides with natural — ### Aristotle's answer
- REVERSAL SEAM: four marks fail in his own words — body ("the opposite often happens"), soul ("the beauty of the soul is not seen"), capture, birth (Theodectes' Helen; "nature, though she may intend it, cannot always accomplish"); FigureSequence seam-1..6 + Task — ## Where the theory fails on its own terms / ### Four places a mark could be
- Aristotle's conclusion "all are not either slaves by nature or freemen by nature"; three consequences marked as not drawn by him: no procedure; every actual slave a legal slave; master may be the slavish one ("reverse" of friendship); KeyFact: the difficulty is internal; Aside: Aristotle's will (DL V.11–16: none sold, freed at proper age if deserving, Ambracis freed, Tycho/Philo/Olympius on daughter's marriage, handmaids given to Herpyllis; the Aside draws no argument from it) — ### What follows from the concessions
- III.14 1285a19–24 quoted (Aristotle's own voice: barbarians "more servile in character than Hellenes, and Asiatics than Europeans", don't rebel against despotic rule "because the people are by nature slaves"); VII.7 climate theory quoted whole (Europe spirit/no intelligence; Asia intelligent/no spirit, "always in subjection and slavery"; Hellas both, "rule the world" if one state); *spirit* glossed; plus I.2 Euripides line and I.8 "naturally just" war; conceded: Aristotle does treat barbarians as more slavish by nature — ## Greek and barbarian
- Two readings set out as an argument (NOT as misconception/refutation): (1) mark by peoples — III/VII supply Book I's missing mark, III.14 uses "by nature slaves" of a people, I.6 exceptions = errors at the edge; (2) tendency — comparative grammar, VII.7 "similar differences in the different tribes of Hellas", I.6 birth doesn't fix soul; Statesman 262d–e quoted (one name ≠ one species). Common ground: Aristotle held barbarians more slavish; no label settles an individual. Division: is a sorting right on the whole enough, given I.6 calls each mismatch "law and force". KeyFact: group tendency granted, individual case unsettled, enslaving by people enslaves the misses by force — ### Two readings: a mark by peoples, or a tendency
- VII.10 best city's farmers: "not all of the same race and not spirited … no danger of their making a revolution"; next best "perioeci of foreign race, and of a like inferior nature" (read as the first reading's language); *perioeci* (free voteless subject towns, as at Sparta); the reasons given are a planner's and hold either way; VII.10 "liberty should be always held out to them as the reward of their services": first reading's two replies (reward need only be wanted; liberty for those able to use it — the latter concedes man-by-man sorting); forward clause (citizenship of best city) — ### What the best city does with it
- *Chrematistics* glossed at first use (heading + first paragraph, from *chremata*); shoe: proper use vs exchange; barter natural (stops at enough; wine for corn); money: iron/silver by weight, then stamped; retail trade aims at coin — ## Getting without limit / ### Two uses of a shoe
- Midas; household wealth bounded ("not unlimited"); medicine: end unlimited, means limited ("the end is always the limit"); money-making makes a means the end ⇒ unlimited, every art (courage, generalship, medicine) turned to money; origin: "intent upon living only, and not upon living well"; *chrematistics* narrowed (this course: wealth-getting without limit; Aristotle's word looser) — ### Where the limit goes
- Usury: "birth of money from money", *tokos* = offspring; "most unnatural"; "against nature" = coin used for an end it was not made for, more coin, so no limit (no slavery analogy); Example: Thales's olive presses (Miletus, Chios; monopoly "of universal application"; cities use it), the Sicilian who cornered iron, banished by Dionysius (tyrant of Syracuse c. 400) — ### Money born of money

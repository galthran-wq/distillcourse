# Taught: aristotle-regimes-and-the-middle

## Problem
Among free men nature names no ruler; five claimants (the many, the rich, the well-born/good, the one best man, the law) each claim the city. Which should be sovereign? Answer reached (Conclusion: the three-part answer, the one gap, and the stability claim named as testable on Sparta; no verdict in this lesson): first define what is claimed (the constitution = arrangement of offices, above all the supreme one) and who may claim (citizen = shares in judging and deciding); classify by number AND end (six regimes; number is an accident, the real line is rich/poor); each party's justice is partial; the city's end (noble actions, living well) judges claims; every claimant alone yields injustice (III.10). Three-part answer: the many judge well together (feast) → assembly, courts, election and audit, not high offices; law rules where it can (reason unaffected by desire), the best man only if beyond all comparison (then king, else ostracism); best regime for most cities = polity, a fusion of oligarchy and democracy held up by a large middle class. Objection (mixture = no regime; who has the last word?) carried: Aristotle's reply meets the charge that a mixture has no structure (three elements assigned), not the charge that it has no last word; argued in bodin-sovereignty, decided in english-revolution-as-test (English civil war of the 1640s).

# Taught: decline-of-regimes

## Problem
How does the best city decay, regime by regime, into tyranny, and is the chain a law or a warning? Answer reached: change starts inside the ruling class (mistimed births, mixed metals); each regime honours one good, its excess breeds the son who ends it (honour → wealth → freedom → one lawless desire); the tyrant is the most enslaved man (Thrasymachus answered by a portrait, not a proof). The Laws' second telling: two mother forms (Persia/despotism, Athens/freedom), cure = mixture (Sparta). VERDICT (graded, last section): as a law every city follows, the chain fails — strong (Pol. V.12; Athens fell to oligarchs 411, 404, and to Macedon 322); the demagogue's road to tyranny holds on balance for archaic Greece (Pol. V.5) and fails for Athens after ostracism; overturned only by a body of classical democracies ending in tyrannies raised from the people. The psychology survives (Aristotle keeps it, V.9).

## Explained
- Change originates in division of the governing power; a united ruling class "cannot be moved"; external shock rejected — ## Change begins inside the ruling class / ### A united ruling class cannot be moved
- Muses' speech, nuptial number as a mock-solemn joke (not computed); rulers cannot know season of births → neglect of music, gymnastics → lost power of testing metal → iron with silver, brass with gold → "causes of hatred and war" (no KeyFact) — ### The Muses' joke
- Method: "States are as the men are"; regime (politeia) = who holds office + what the city honours + what citizen it rears; five pairs; city first, man second; "aristocracy" = Plato's name for Kallipolis, not landed nobility (no forward Ref); timocracy (Plato's coinage, "government of honour") — ### States are as the men are
- Timocracy: factions split land/houses, enslave producers ("friends and maintainers" glossed = farmers and craftsmen of the third class); keeps common meals/no trade/gymnastics; drops philosophers, prefers spirited men, everlasting wars; secret hoarded gold; "spirit of contention and ambition"; = Crete and Sparta — ## Timocracy / ### The compromise between gold and iron
- Four-step pattern (how it arises / what it honours / defect / the man formed by father, mother, servants, street); worked Example: timocratic son (quiet good father declining office; mother "half a man"; servants; busybodies honoured; spirit takes the throne; loses "philosophy tempered with music"); a regime is an education whether it means to be or not — ### The timocratic son (figure template.svg)
- Oligarchy: hidden gold → rivalry → riches/virtue scale → property qualification by force; definition "valuation of property…"; opens on the problem (rulers who despise money: how does money come to rule?); five defects (pilot chosen by property, two cities, can't fight/won't pay taxes, many callings against one man, one art — Ref'd with reminder, free sale of property); *drones* (stingless paupers, stinged criminals) — ## Oligarchy / ### Wealth becomes the qualification / ### Five defects, and the drone
- Oligarchic man: timocrat father wrecked "upon a sunken reef" by informers; money-loving part as "great king… tiara and chain and scimitar"; reason and spirit at its feet; honest by fear (orphan's estate); "two men, and not one" — ### The miser on the throne
- *Necessary* vs *unnecessary* desires DEFINED here (puzzle: appetite rules yet he denies himself): necessary = cannot be got rid of, satisfaction benefits (simple food); unnecessary = removable by training from youth, do no good (delicacies); first earns, second spends; oligarch satisfies only necessary; drone = "slave of the unnecessary desires" — ### The miser on the throne
- To democracy (opens on the problem: a regime built to protect wealth, where does it get the poor who end it?): oligarchs profit from spendthrifts' ruin; armed ruined men; poor sunburnt man beside puffing rich man (quoted); poor win, kill/banish, lot — ## Democracy / ### The poor man beside the rich one
- Democracy's character quoted at full strength: freedom/frankness, embroidered robe, bazaar of constitutions, no compulsion to rule or be ruled, "supremely delightful … for the moment", condemned man walks about; tramples the founders' principles, honours "the people's friend"; "equality to equals and unequals alike" = arithmetic equality extended to the unequal; KeyFact — ### The embroidered robe
- Pericles (II.37) vs Book VIII: same features, Plato removes the awe of law Pericles relied on; Republic ~50 years after 431 — ### Two portraits of one city
- Democratic man: oligarch's son, unnecessary desires recalled by a clause (held down by father's fear of poverty); son never saw a ruin; tastes drones' honey; civil war in soul; empty citadel taken; renamed vices quoted (insolence breeding, anarchy liberty, waste magnificence, impudence courage) — ## The democratic man / ### The citadel taken

# Taught: platos-laws-second-best-city (type: extra — no core lesson may depend on it)

## Problem
What can a city do when nobody in it knows the good (no philosopher kings)? Answer reached: the *Laws*' second-best city — law as "the distribution of mind" rules; rulers are "servants of the law", chosen for obedience; laws carry preambles that persuade before they compel; 5040 inalienable lots with a floor (the lot) and a ceiling (four times the lot) bound wealth that can no longer be abolished; Book X answers the materialist's "the highest right is might" by putting soul before body, then punishes those it cannot persuade. Read as Plato's second thoughts that keep the Republic's city as the pattern; each device concedes something and keeps something (table in the Conclusion).

## Explained
- Frame: Laws = last/longest dialogue, 350s (Ref decline-of-regimes); Cleinias's commission (most of Crete sends a colony to an empty site, Cnossus in charge, Cleinias one of ten lawgivers, "whether taken from the Cretan model or from any other"); walk from Cnossus (= Knossos of the Bronze Age palace, north coast) to the cave of Zeus on Mount Ida; only dialogue without Socrates — intro
- Problem development: Republic's institutions (metals, medicinal tale, no property/families, compelled reluctant rulers) all assume a knower; obvious fix (same institutions, best available men) fails at each — intro (no heading)
- Three cities: draughts move ("withdrawal of the stone from the holy line"); first = "friends have all things in common" (Republic's second wave, even eyes/ears/hands common), lived in by "Gods or sons of Gods", kept as "the pattern"; second = this city (private land/houses; community of goods "goes beyond their proposed origin, and nurture, and education"); third promised, never described — ## Second best: the pattern kept, the knower removed
- Legislator's prayer for a young temperate tyrant who meets a great legislator: about SPEED of reform, not legitimacy; ranking tyranny > monarchy > democracy > oligarchy (many potentates); "When the supreme power in man coincides with the greatest wisdom and temperance … but in no other way" = third wave kept as a prayer, "rarely surmounted in the course of ages" — ### The prayer for a young tyrant
- Age of Cronos: no human nature with supreme power avoids "insolence and wrong"; demigods ruled men as men rule flocks; imitation available = obey law, "the distribution of mind" (nomos / dianomē / nous pun); reason shifts from ignorance (Republic) to corruption (Laws), linked to Book III's "too large a sail" — ### Why law: the age of Cronos
- Thrasymachus's thesis restated by the Athenian in three steps and rejected as a "false principle" (principle of might among the seven titles); winners monopolise office → "not polities but parties" (Ref thrasymachus, not requoted in full); no office for wealth, strength, stature, birth — ## Rulers as servants of the law / ### The interest of the stronger, rejected again
- Palm for obedience; rulers "servants or ministers of the law"; "the law is above the rulers … has salvation"; reversal: Republic's most RELUCTANT vs Laws' most OBEDIENT; why: knowledge uncheckable by the ruled, obedience checkable; wisdom moves into the legislator, who stands outside the city he founds — ### The palm for obedience
- Megillus + Cleinias cannot name their regimes; mixed constitution recalled by Ref + reminder clause (decline-of-regimes ### Sparta saved by mixing: monarchic/aristocratic/popular elements check each other, none rules alone); Athenian's reply (IV 712e–713a): they "really have polities"; others are "named after the dominant power" = a part ruling the rest (Thrasymachus fits); why naming matters: the code does not keep itself — Book VI's 37 guardians of the law (elected by horse/foot soldiers) fill up the "outline" with the magistrates, change only with magistrates + whole people + oracles; whoever amends/administers holds a hand on what the law is ⇒ only with no nameable ruling part can the law stand above everyone; true state named after "the God who rules over wise men" = law as distribution of mind (Cronos); KeyFact (condition): the palm stands or falls with the mixture — if the votes choosing the guardians lean to one part, obedience = Thrasymachus's justice under a better name — ### A city named after a god
- Two doctors (slave doctor "like a tyrant", habits not reasons; free doctor converses, convinces first; free man owed an account); two instruments, persuasion and force; Republic's medicinal lie as the problem it answers — ## The free doctor: a law that gives its reasons / ### Two doctors
- Marriage law simple vs double form (worked Example, 3 steps: love of continuance / immortality through children → refusing is impiety → command + yearly fine + loss of honours); Megillus: short form Spartan, he prefers long; preludes in music, none ever for law — ### Simple law and double law
- *Preamble* (prelude) DEFINED in a Remark: persuasive address before the command, so the citizen receives it "by reason of this good-will" and "more intelligently"; law = preamble + "the law pure and simple" (command with penalty) — same subsection
- What the preamble keeps from the lie: Book V land preamble ("the earth is their parent … a goddess and their queen") = first half of the metals tale, spoken openly; second half (ranks of metal) dropped — ### What the preamble keeps from the lie
- Property: Republic's guardians without property vs Laws' lots for all, worked by slaves paying part of the produce (Book VII; citizens do not farm); danger = Book VIII's "two States" (Ref decline); empty site, no redistribution/debt cancellation ("superhuman folly"), "freedom from avarice … upon this rock" — ## 5040 lots and a ceiling of four / ### A clean start, and a fixed number
- 5040 = 1·2·3·4·5·6·7; fifty-nine divisors, all of 1–10 and 12; twelve tribes of 420; point = commensurability, no remainder that belongs nowhere (stated once, before the quote) — same subsection
- Keeping the number: one heir ("best beloved"), extra sons adopted by childless, daughters married, highest magistracy judges, "refrain", "rewards and stigmas", colony as last resort; lots unsellable (priests' curses on cypress tablets); no private gold/silver, local coin, no dowries, no interest — ### Keeping the number
- Why the very rich cannot be good (worked Example, 5 steps: double-getting, half-spending, balances, who ends very rich, lawsuits vs friendship); a claim about tendencies (heir/finder escape step 1) — ### Why the very rich cannot be good
- Four property classes (Ref greek-miracle Solon's four classes, geometric equality); floor = lot, ceiling = 4× lot; excess to state and gods; informer gets half; "not faction, but rather distraction": *stasis* = quarrel of parties inside one city, *diastasis* = the city split into two cities of rich and poor (Ref decline Book VIII "two States"), hence both extremes blamed; bound is a ratio not a sum; no argument given for four — ### Floor and ceiling
- Aristotle, Politics II.6: rounds 5040 to 5,000 citizens who do not work their lots → Babylon-sized territory; population objection quoted ("even more necessary to limit population than property … poverty is the parent of revolution and crime"); indivisible lot ⇒ extra sons get nothing; section opener says "most of his complaints" come back to the arithmetic; II.7 Phaleas of Chalcedon, train desires (Laws V agrees: poverty = increase of desires) — ## Aristotle's charge / ### The objection at full strength
- Regime charge (Politics II.6 1266a1–7 quoted): the Laws claims to mix the mother forms (Aristotle: "democracy and tyranny", his word, cf. the Book IV young-tyrant prayer) but its institutions have "no element of monarchy at all" (no king; elected magistrates, lot among the elected) — only oligarchy and democracy, leaning to oligarchy (rich compelled to attend assembly and vote, rest may stay home); Aristotle's *polity* glossed (narrower sense: one regime, mean of democracy/oligarchy, held by the heavy-armed); more elements = better mixture (Sparta's three: kings, elders, ephors); bearing: the mixture is narrower than claimed and tilted, so the KeyFact's condition holds only in part — ### A mixture narrower than it claims (placed after "What the Laws can answer", which answers only the births objection)
- Reply: remedies are named (letter of objection fails) but not shown to suffice (substance stands); "city of wax" and the artist's reply (pattern first, then concede the nearest feasible); Aristotle's charge = Laws did not concede enough — ### What the Laws can answer
- (Section order inside ## Aristotle's charge: The objection at full strength (size, births, Phaleas) → What the Laws can answer → A mixture narrower than it claims.)
- Book X: three errors about gods; preambles lean on gods so the atheist is the one they cannot reach; persuasion first ("O my son"; written laws "always at rest" can be reread) — ## Might is right, answered by the priority of soul (section intro)
- Materialist doctrine (nature/chance/art; elements first; gods and justice by art and convention; "the highest right is might"; "real dominion over others") quoted; linked to Glaucon, Thrasymachus, Melos V.105; what's new = a physics; envoys put gods with the stronger, materialist with convention — ### The materialist's doctrine
- Priority of soul: 4-step argument (moved movers; chain cannot begin; "if all at rest" only self-motion; soul defined as "the motion which can move itself"); Task asks which premise the materialist refuses (no answer in the Task); the prose after the steps answers it: load-bearing step = step 4 (definition); materialist can accept a self-mover and call it matter; stated: Book X sets a rival order of causes against him, not a refutation; heavens moved by best soul, "all things are full of Gods" — ### Soul before body
- Payoff: law and art "exist by nature" if creations of mind (Cleinias); Laws answers Thrasymachus by cosmology (Republic by portrait); "God ought to be to us the measure of all things, and not man" (IV) — ### What the argument buys the law
- Law on impiety: duty to inform, House of Reformation ≥5 years, nocturnal council (one clause, "later books"), death on second conviction, hypocrite atheist "death many times over", no private shrines; Laws still legislates belief — ### The law on impiety
- Closing table: Republic device → Laws device → concedes → keeps (5 rows), plus mixture as what holds the Laws column together, Aristotle's two weak points (lots vs sons; mixture with no monarchic part, leaning to the rich), and Book X: a rival order of causes, not a refutation (proof needs the premise the materialist denies; where persuasion ends the impiety law begins) — inside <Conclusion> (the only summary; no separate section)

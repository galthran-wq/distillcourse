# Taught: sparta-as-test-case

## Problem
Run the module's three theories as five tests (mixed constitution; natural rule twice: natural slavery and household hierarchy; decline twice: Plato's and Aristotle's) on the actual Spartan regime, known only from foreign witnesses. Answer reached: mixed constitution's stability FITS, but for Aristotle's reason (every part corrupt; each holds a share it would lose: II.9 "all the parts must wish it"), not the Laws' balance of virtues; Aristotle's single-virtue decline FITS, mechanism = land law (gift/bequest, heiresses/dowries, two-fifths to women) + loss of citizenship for unpaid mess dues → oliganthropia; Plato's timocracy PARTLY (symptom: gold; misses mechanism); natural slavery DOES NOT (Aristotle never applies it to the helots; by I.6's test their subjection is law and force); household hierarchy UNTESTED (persistence test fails it, but Aristotle's reply, constitutional rule can fail + unruled part's 'licence' expected + I.5 corrupted cases don't count, absorbs it; the only sign of corruption is the conduct explained, so no household could count against it; 'fits' on Aristotle's terms is the defensible alternative). MODULE VERDICT (grade ON BALANCE): of the three Greek answers to who should rule, the mixed regime of the middle survives (lasted while every part had a share; ended when the land law shrank the citizen body: give every part a share AND keep the citizen body broad; Sparta kept the first, lost the second); rule by nature fails by Aristotle's own I.6 test; rule by knowledge untested (no city ruled by philosophers). Objections: (1) *Spartan mirage* conceded, reply: theories tested on the same reports their authors used; limits the verdict's reach to one city seen from outside; (2) rival cause, fear of the helots (Thuc. IV.80, VIII.40; II.9 'lying in wait'), replies: fear explains unity against the helots, not who among the citizens commands; Aristotle names the helot danger and the shrinking roll as causes of the regime's end, so fear sits inside his account of the fall, not of the stability. Together: 'explains' → 'fits best', hence on balance. Overturned by: a closed group of families holding the offices throughout the stable centuries. Opening (rewritten in course.yaml): how to test a theory against a city known only from admirers and critics.

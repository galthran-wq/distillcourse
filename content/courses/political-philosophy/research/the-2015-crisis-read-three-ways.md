# Research brief: the-2015-crisis-read-three-ways (The 2015 migration crisis read against Carens, Miller and Murray)

status: planned
module: nation-and-immigration
type: core
goal: Apply the three theories of membership to the recorded facts of the 2015 European migration crisis and judge what each would have prescribed, what each predicted, and where the event escapes all three

## ARC (L10)

- **Inherits:** The module's three theories of membership, each at full strength: Carens's `open-borders` with the `global-original-position` and the public-order restriction as the only brake (`carens-open-borders`); Miller's `self-determination-over-membership`, `refugee-vs-economic-migrant`, the coconut and the Postscript's admission of strain (`miller-strangers-in-our-midst`); Murray's `two-concatenations`, the pull-factor claim, the Dublin inversion, the deportation gap and the `bockenforde-dilemma` (`murray-strange-death-of-europe`). From `powell-raspail-and-camus` the `prophecy-of-displacement` checked against the record — the method this lesson applies to a single year. From `the-nation-and-its-defenders` Scruton's remark that the EU has no first-person plural. From earlier modules: `friend-enemy-distinction` and `protego ergo obligo` (`schmitt-concept-of-the-political`) — reinforced here per course.yaml; `sovereignty` and `state-of-exception` (`bodin-sovereignty`, `schmitt-political-theology`) for a rule suspended by executive decision; the sourcing drill, practised on Gilley's citations (`auditing-the-case-for-colonialism`) and on the UCL table (`murray-strange-death-of-europe`).
- **Contributes:** The module's application (L16): one real event — the arrival of about a million people by sea in 2015 and the decisions Europe's governments took — read against the three theories. For each: what it would have prescribed, what it predicted, what it omits. Then the departures: a continental membership decision made in a week by one government's executive; a legal order (Geneva, Dublin, Schengen) whose enforcement did not follow its text; a crisis ended not by any of the three theories' instruments but by a treaty with a non-member state. Numbers are the lesson's material and every one is bound in its sentence.
- **Hands off:** This is the module's last lesson. The event leaves open the question the module has circled since Scruton's "we" and that Murray named — the `bockenforde-dilemma`: whether a free secular state can produce the loyalty its border depends on. The next module opens on it (`deneen-post-liberalism`): Deneen's answer is that liberalism failed by succeeding, drawing down a preliberal inheritance it cannot replenish — and his account of who decides (the state and the individual, with the local community dissolved between them) is the frame for this lesson's first departure, a membership decision made in a week by one government. Hazony's case for an order of national states against a union that decides for its members (`hazony-virtue-of-nationalism`) and Hungary's constitution (`hungarys-fundamental-law`) take the Visegrád refusal further; the synthesis (`the-argument-assembled`) places 2015 on the spine as the third question in its latest form. Nothing new is introduced here.
- **Spine step:** "The live question: what a nation is and who may join it — Scruton and Miller on the pre-political loyalty, America as England's heir from the Federalist to Who Are We?, the prophecies of displacement from Powell to Camus, Carens and Miller on borders, Murray on Europe — and 2015 as the test"
- **APPLICATION spec (L16):**
  - *Artefact:* the recorded 2015–16 European migration crisis — the Eurostat asylum series, the UNHCR Mediterranean arrival series, five dated decisions (25 Aug and 31 Aug 2015; Hungary's fence, 15 Sept; the EU relocation decision, 22 Sept; the EU–Turkey statement, 18 Mar 2016) and three aftermath records (Cologne New Year 2015/16; the deportation gap; the Bundestag election of 24 Sept 2017).
  - *Module results it uses:* `open-borders`, `global-original-position`, the public-order restriction (carens); `self-determination-over-membership`, `refugee-vs-economic-migrant`, `integration-civic-social-cultural`, the coconut and the "fair share" (miller); `two-concatenations`, the Dublin inversion, the pull-factor claim, the deportation gap, `bockenforde-dilemma` (murray); `friend-enemy-distinction` and `protection-and-obedience` (schmitt, by Ref); `state-of-exception` (by Ref).
  - *Departures from the idealisation the lesson must name:* (1) all three theories assume a *state* that decides for a *people*; the decision of 25–31 August was taken by one member state's executive (a ministry's tweet; a chancellor's press conference) and bound twenty-seven others through Schengen — no theory has a slot for a membership decision made by one member of a union for the rest. (2) All three assume law that is enforced: Dublin's "first country" rule was not applied (Hungary registered arrivals who did not stay; Greece and Italy waved through); refused claimants were not returned (the deportation gap); the refugee/economic line Miller and Murray both need was not drawn at the border but years later, by courts. (3) None contemplates the instrument that ended the crisis: a cash-and-visas agreement with Turkey that moved the border outside the Union. (4) Carens's public-order restriction and Miller's "morally excruciating" overload both describe the situation but neither tells a government *when* it has arrived there; Murray says it had arrived in 2010 and gives no number either.

## EXPLANATION PLAN (L0)

Start from the opening verbatim: in 2015 about 1.3 million people applied for asylum in the EU, most arriving by sea through Greece and Italy; Germany suspended the first-country rule, Hungary built a fence, a deal with Turkey closed the route the next spring; three theories say what should have been done. The newcomer meets it as a test: a theory of membership is a theory about what a people may decide; here is a year in which several peoples decided several things at once, and the lesson asks each theory to speak.

1. **What actually happened — the record before any theory.** Attempt: the reader thinks they know (a million refugees; Merkel opened the border). The record is more exact and less simple. Two series: UNHCR's sea arrivals (about 1.0 million in 2015 against about 216,000 in 2014 — five times; about 85 per cent through Greece from Turkey, the rest Libya to Italy; about 3,800 dead or missing) and Eurostat's first-time asylum applications (about 1.26 million in 2015 against 563,000 in 2014; Germany about 442,000 filed in 2015 and 722,000 in 2016; Syrians 29 per cent, Afghans 14, Iraqis 10 — and a tail of Western Balkan applicants with recognition rates near zero). Then the three German numbers that are not the same number (890,000 registered arrivals; 442,000 applications filed; "up to 1.5m" leaked). Because: the first result of the module's method is that the event has several sizes depending on what is counted, and every theory's prescription depends on which.
2. **The decisions, dated to the day.** The seam: the reader expects a flood that forced a response; the record shows a sequence of decisions each of which changed the flow. July: the Rostock girl ("politics is hard"). 25 August: a ministry's tweet suspends Dublin for Syrians. 31 August: "Wir schaffen das". 2 September: the photograph of Aylan Kurdi. 4–5 September: Germany and Austria admit the thousands stranded at Budapest Keleti. 13 September: Germany reintroduces border controls. 15 September: Hungary's Serbian fence closes and unauthorised crossing becomes a crime; 22 September: the Council adopts the 120,000 relocation scheme over four states' votes; October: the monthly peak of about 211,000 arrivals in Greece; 13 November: Paris; 18 March 2016: the EU–Turkey statement; April 2016: Aegean arrivals fall to a few thousand a month. Because: exact days here because contingency is the point (L12d) — the theories are about what *may* be decided, and the record shows what *was*, by whom, when.
3. **Carens applied.** What open borders prescribes: admit; the only brake is public order, on a reasonable expectation of damage to liberty itself, based on evidence acceptable to all, and never the hostility of residents; in nonideal theory, size of demand may justify "some restriction" with priority to those fleeing persecution. What it predicts: that most people do not love to move, so flows track how bad life is (Syria 2015: yes); that restriction protects privilege (the Balkan applicants with near-zero recognition are the test — were they claiming liberty or life chances?). What it omits: who decides. Carens's argument is addressed to a *state*; the decision of August 2015 was made by one state for twenty-seven, and his theory has no slot for the Union — nor for the fact that Germany's open door was Hungary's crisis (the first-country rule) before it was Germany's. Because: a theory of justice between a state and the person at its border says nothing about justice between states that share a border regime.
4. **Miller applied.** What the coconut prescribes: a strong enforced border, rapid status determination, refugees admitted or transferred to a safe place up to a fair share, economic migrants on fair terms or refused and returned, full inclusion for those who stay; burden-sharing through the EU "as an insurance mechanism". What it predicts: a mixed flow in which the refugee/economic line is "under strain" (his own Postscript) — borne out by the recognition rates (Syrians near 97 per cent; Western Balkans near zero; Afghans in between, the hardest case); a fight over fair shares (the September relocation decision and its refusal by the Visegrád four — Miller's "free-riding" problem exactly); the rescue paradox (rescue without admission, Australia — the Turkey statement is this). What it omits: the capacity assumption. "Rapid status determination" took Germany years; "returned" became the deportation gap (about 220,000 orders, 11,300 removals by May 2016; EU-wide about 533,000 orders and 227,000 returns in 2015); "enforced border" was a fence. Miller's framework describes the policy a competent state would run; the record is of states that could not. Because: an interest in self-determination presupposes a self that can determine.
5. **Murray applied.** What restriction prescribes (ch. 18): regional resettlement near home, offshore processing, Europe-wide deportation of failed claimants, temporary asylum that ends — and, before all, a people allowed to say who it is for. What it predicts: that rescue pulls (test it on the UNHCR monthly series around Mare Nostrum's end in October 2014 — Italy's arrivals in 2015 stayed near the 2014 level and deaths rose in the spring, so the series alone does not settle it); that Dublin would invert (Hungary's registrations against its asylum claims — CHECK Murray's "fewer than twenty" against Eurostat's 177,000: the discrepancy itself is a finding about what "claim" means); that the flow followed the German signal (arrivals rose through September and October after 31 August — consistent, not proof: the Syrian war and Turkey's policy are the rival causes); that integration would fail at the civic level (Cologne as the case he gives). What it omits: 2016. Flows fell when borders went back up and Turkey was paid — which is Murray's own point — but they fell *within* the liberal order he says cannot act; and the public that he says was never asked voted in September 2017: AfD 12.6 per cent, CDU/CSU at its lowest since 1949 — a shift, not a repudiation. Because: the second concatenation predicts paralysis, and the record shows a slow, late, partial correction.
6. **The two testimonies — the sourcing drill.** Murray's Afghan official (tortured, forced to plot a school water-poisoning, saved the children by a phone call) and the Hazara brothers (20 and 18; "We are Afghans. We have seen everything."; the complaint that Syrians are favoured). Metadata first: told to a British journalist on Lesbos in summer 2016, through an interpreter, by men whose claims were pending. Prediction, then records/argues/omits, then use: what each can establish (that Afghans in 2016 saw the Syrian preference and understood the categories — Miller's refugee/economic line was known to those it sorted) and cannot (whether the account is true; what share of arrivals it represents). Then the corroboration step: set the Afghan recognition rate (about two-thirds positive at first instance in 2015, falling in 2016) beside the testimony. Because: the method of the course applied to the most persuasive kind of evidence the module contains.
7. **Where the event escapes all three.** (a) A decision in a week by one government: no theory of membership has a people deciding for another people — Carens and Miller both address a state; Murray addresses a civilization; the Union, which made the decision binding, is in none of them (Scruton's "no first-person plural" by `<Ref the-nation-and-its-defenders>` is the nearest thing). (b) Law that was not enforced: Dublin suspended by announcement; refused claimants not removed; the border rule applied by Hungary and suspended by Germany on the same day — a norm's force decided by whoever decided (`<Ref schmitt-political-theology>`, with the care noted in Pitfalls). (c) A crisis ended by a treaty: the instrument that worked moved the border to Turkey and paid for it; none of the three theories contains it (Miller's "transfer to a third country" is the closest, and he would ask whether Turkey met the rights standard). (d) The missing threshold: Carens's "size of demand", Miller's "overload", Murray's "too many, too fast" all name a point and none gives it a number; the lesson says so and does not supply one. Because: an application that only confirms is not an application (L16 requires the departures).

Headings fall out as claims: "A year that has three sizes"; "Ten dated decisions"; "Carens: admit, and only public order may stop you — but who is 'you'?"; "Miller: the coconut needs a state that can do these things"; "Murray: the prediction, the series and 2016"; "Two men on Lesbos: what testimony can establish"; "Where 2015 escapes all three".

## Set pieces and worked examples to recreate

1. **One year, three sizes** (Eurostat, UNHCR, BAMF). The worked example in binding a number: 1.0 million sea arrivals (UNHCR: people who landed), 1.26 million first-time applications (Eurostat: forms filed in the year, including people who arrived earlier or by land), 890,000 German registrations (BAMF: people recorded on arrival in one country, revised down from 1.09m), 442,000 German applications filed in 2015 (Eurostat) and "up to 1.5m" (a leak reported by Murray). Show the reader why none is wrong and why the opening's "about 1.3 million" is the Eurostat figure. End with the uncertainty statement (playbook-history rule 4).
2. **The week of 25–31 August 2015** (Murray ch. 5, l. 2366–2378; the bundesregierung.de transcript). The tweet's text, Merkel's three sentences, "Wir schaffen das" — then the two readings: a humanitarian instinct (Murray's own concession: "the only thing a civilised people can do") and a continental decision taken without the Union (his charge). The reader is given both before either theory speaks.
3. **Keleti and Röszke, 4–16 September** (Murray ch. 11; press record). The station (thousands camped under the departures board; "Germany, Germany, Merkel"); the night of 4 September; Röszke on the 15th–16th (the fence closed, the crossing a crime, tear gas). Two scenes, dated; then Orbán's and Soros's two sentences as the two poles ("protection of refugees the objective, borders the obstacle" vs the reverse) — `<Ref schmitt-concept-of-the-political>`: the grouping made visible.
4. **The relocation vote of 22 September and the Visegrád refusal** (Council Decision 2015/1601). 120,000 to be relocated by quota; four states outvoted; by the scheme's end in 2017 fewer than 30,000 moved (Commission figures — obtain). Miller's "free-riding" problem exactly, and his own answer (a conscientious estimate; precommitment to a UNHCR-based mechanism) against the record.
5. **Hungary's two numbers** (Murray ch. 5 vs Eurostat). "400,000 crossed Hungary and fewer than twenty claimed asylum there" against Eurostat's 177,000 applications lodged in Hungary in 2015. Resolve: Hungary registered applications that were then abandoned as the applicants moved on (the Dublin inversion in a single table); Murray's figure, if it means anything, means claims *pursued*. The set piece is the method: a discrepancy between a source and a dataset is a finding, not an error to hide.
6. **Testing the pull factor on the series** (Murray ch. 4; UNHCR monthly). Mare Nostrum (Oct 2013–Oct 2014) → Triton (Nov 2014). Italy arrivals 2013 about 43,000; 2014 about 170,000; 2015 about 154,000; deaths in the central Mediterranean rose in spring 2015 (about 1,200 in one April week). Lay the series out and show that it is consistent with pull (arrivals rose during Mare Nostrum) and with its absence (they did not fall when it ended); the honest conclusion is that the series underdetermines the claim and that Libya's collapse is the confounder. End in prose and an uncertainty statement.
7. **The two testimonies** (Murray ch. 5 l. ~2652–2730) — the sourcing drill in full (see EXPLANATION PLAN 6). Metadata visible before the quotation; one Task — predict from the metadata what the men will stress and omit; then the three questions; then use.
8. **Cologne, 31 December 2015** (Murray ch. 12 l. 5852–5853; the prosecutor's July 2016 figures). The square between the station and the cathedral; about 1,200 complaints by mid-2016, roughly 500 of them sexual; about 2,000 men; about 120 suspects identified, most North African, few of them 2015 asylum applicants — the last fact matters for which theory the event supports (Murray's integration charge vs the tabloid reading that it was "the refugees"). The slow leak and the police press release of 1 January ("a relaxed atmosphere"). Bound figures; no scene invented.
9. **The deportation funnel** (Murray ch. 12 l. 6044–6070; Eurostat migr_eiord/migr_eirtn). Timmermans's 60 per cent (Jan 2016, citing Frontex) → Germany's 220,000 orders → 11,300 removals (May 2016); the EU-wide ratio of returns to orders (about 40 per cent in 2015). Then the three theories' response: Carens (removal needs justification to the removed); Miller (enforcement against evaders may be coercive and must be justified; but a border that does not return is no border); Murray (the pretence of repatriation).
10. **24 September 2017** (Bundeswahlleiter). AfD 12.6 per cent; CDU/CSU 32.9, lowest since 1949; the AfD first in Saxony. Then the two readings the lesson owes: the public spoke (Murray's "never asked" answered — and the answer was one voter in eight); a shift inside the liberal order, not its collapse (the liberal reply; Fawcett in References).
11. **18 March 2016** (the EU–Turkey Statement, points 1–2). Returns to Turkey; one-for-one; €6bn; visas; the Aegean series falling from about 57,000 in February to about 3,600 in April. Then the question the three theories cannot answer: is Turkey "a place where rights are secure" (Miller's standard)? — and the one Murray can: the border went back up and the flow stopped.

## Pitfalls / subtleties to flag

- **Every number bound** (L12b; playbook-history rule 2): unit (people / applications / registrations), base (EU-28 / Germany / Greece), timeframe (calendar 2015 / Jan–Aug / by May 2016), source (Eurostat table id / UNHCR portal / BAMF / Murray's citation). A number without its sentence-frame is a required fix. Keeper numbers (two or three only): about 1.0 million sea arrivals in 2015 (five times 2014); about 1.26 million first-time asylum applications (Germany about a third); the deportation ratio (220,000 orders, 11,300 removals). The rest lives in the figures and the aside.
- **Three German numbers are three quantities** (set piece 1); never let "a million refugees came to Germany" stand.
- **Murray's "fewer than twenty" for Hungary** conflicts with Eurostat; resolve as in set piece 5 or omit. **Murray's "129 dead" in Paris** is the early toll; 130 is final. **"Up to 1.5m"** is a leak; say so.
- **Terms to anchor at first mention** (L11): *Dublin*, *Schengen*, *Frontex*, *BAMF*, *Eurostat*, *UNHCR*, *EASY* (Germany's first-registration system — one clause), *Keleti* (Budapest's eastern railway terminus), *Röszke* (the Serbian-border crossing), *Visegrád four*, *relocation* (the EU scheme moving recognised applicants between member states), *recognition rate* (the share of first-instance decisions granting protection), *first-time applicant* (Eurostat's category excluding repeat applications), *Lesbos / Moria / Kara Tepe*, *Mare Nostrum / Triton*, *the EU–Turkey Statement*, *AfD* (Alternative für Deutschland, founded 2013 as an anti-euro party), *Bundestag*. Most were anchored in the Murray lesson; re-anchor with a clause and a Ref (L11d).
- **Do not re-teach the three theories.** Each "applied" section opens with one sentence and a `<Ref>` recalling the theory's prescription, then goes straight to the record. The lesson's new content is the application and the departures.
- **The state of exception**: the Dublin suspension was an administrative announcement, not a declared emergency; the lesson may say it has the *form* Schmitt described — a norm's application decided by whoever decided — but must not say Germany invoked emergency powers. `<Ref schmitt-political-theology>` with that care.
- **Causation on a timeline**: adjacency is not causation (playbook-history anchor-timeline rule). Arrivals rose after 31 August; the Syrian war, Turkey's policy and the season are rival causes; say so each time the pull or the signal is asserted.
- **Cologne**: use the prosecutor's figures with their definitions (complaints; suspects identified; nationality of suspects); note that few identified suspects were 2015 asylum applicants; do not generalise from the event to the flow, and do not minimise it. The point for the lesson is which theory it bears on (Miller's civic integration; Murray's cover-up charge — the police press release of 1 Jan is the document).
- **2017**: the AfD result is one voter in eight; it is evidence of a shift and of the limits of Murray's "never asked"; it is not evidence that the public endorsed or repudiated the decision as such (migration was one issue; the single-factor explanations are weak — the course's own statement, Fawcett in References).
- **Testimony** (playbook-history §Testimony): the two Lesbos accounts are permitted and preferred over any invented scene; they are reported through Murray and an interpreter; the sourcing drill makes that visible.
- **Data provenance in figures**: every chart cites its table in the caption; the Eurostat and UNHCR series are reusable with attribution; a chart drawn from the data is the course's own (not a sourced artefact).
- **Register.** The course passes no verdict of its own on any author or idea: no condemning or approving labels in its own voice. Judgement comes only through argument and evidence; a label may appear only inside a quotation, with who applied it and when. The lesson neither calls the decision of August 2015 a crime nor a duty, nor the Hungarian fence either; it records what each theory says and where the record leaves all three. Orbán's and Soros's sentences, and the press labels of the time, appear only as quotations with speaker and date.
- **Thin spots in the dossier**: everything after Murray's text (autumn 2016) — the 2017 relocation totals, the court judgment, the 2017 election, the Landtag inquiry — is outside it; the writer obtains the public records named above. Miller's Postscript is dated (800,000 "anticipated"); the lesson says so.

## EXERCISE PLAN (E6/E7 + playbook-history mix)

1. *Retrieval and chronology (E3)*: Place these in order and date each to the month: the Aylan Kurdi photograph; the EU–Turkey statement; the BAMF Dublin tweet; Hungary's fence closing; the Council relocation decision; the Cologne New Year; the Bundestag election. Then name, for three of them, which theory in the module treats that kind of act as the decisive one.
2. *Document reasoning — sourcing drill (the lesson's method, repeated)*: The BAMF tweet of 25 August 2015 ("we are at present largely no longer enforcing Dublin procedures for Syrian citizens"). Metadata (a federal agency's social-media account; Tuesday afternoon; addressed to nobody in particular). Predict; then read: what does it record (an administrative practice already in place — "at present", "largely"), argue (nothing), omit (any legal basis; any limit; any consultation with the other Dublin states)? What can it be USED to establish — about German policy; about how the message "went round the world" (Murray)? What can it not establish (that it caused the autumn's arrivals)?
3. *Research-shaped (E6d)*: Reproduce one count. From Eurostat migr_asyappctza, find first-time applicants in Hungary in 2015 and in Germany in 2015 and 2016; from migr_asydcfsta, find Hungary's first-instance decisions in 2015. Compute the ratio of decisions to applications for Hungary and interpret it (most applicants moved on — the Dublin inversion in one table). Then explain how Murray's "fewer than twenty claimed asylum" could be true of *some* quantity, and which. End with an uncertainty statement about what the table cannot show.
4. *Claim to refute*: "The monthly arrival series proves Murray's pull-factor thesis: arrivals rose during Mare Nostrum and the border crisis followed Merkel's announcement." Name the step that fails (post hoc: arrivals did not fall when Mare Nostrum ended; the Syrian war and Libya's collapse are rival causes; the autumn rise is consistent with the signal thesis but not proof of it) and state what evidence would distinguish the two readings (e.g. departures from Turkey by week against the dates the news reached the camps — the kind of data the lesson does not have).

Run the four conditions. The solution argues one defensible ranking and names the evidence each step would need.

Then corroborate against the Afghan first-instance recognition rate for 2015–16 (Eurostat): what does the brothers' complaint that "Syrians are favoured" establish about how Miller's refugee/economic line was perceived by those it sorted — and what does it not establish about whether they were refugees?

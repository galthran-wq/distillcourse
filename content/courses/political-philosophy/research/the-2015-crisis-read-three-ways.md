- **Hands off:** This is the module's last lesson, and it decides the module's question: Miller over Carens on principle (**on balance**); the 2015 admission decided by the executive alone (**established**), bypassing consent on a question of membership (**strong**), its lawfulness **open**; the security and removal failures systematic (**strong**); integration split by sex (**strong**); the correction made late, through the liberal order's own instruments, after electoral punishment. What passes on is the question the module has circled since Scruton's "we" and that Murray named, the `bockenforde-dilemma`: whether a free secular state can produce the loyalty its border and its burdens depend on. The record here shows a state that did not ask its people; it cannot show whether a liberal state could have produced the consent it skipped. The next module opens on that question (`deneen-post-liberalism`): Deneen's answer is that liberalism failed by succeeding, drawing down a preliberal inheritance it cannot replenish, and his account of who decides (the state and the individual, with the local community dissolved between them) fits this lesson's first finding, a membership decision made by one government's executive. Hazony's case for an order of national states against a union that decides for its members (`hazony-virtue-of-nationalism`) takes the relocation vote further, and `hungarys-fundamental-law` judges the premise (module 16's verdict); the synthesis (`the-argument-assembled`) places 2015 on the spine as the third question in its latest form.

- **APPLICATION spec (L16):**
  - *Artefact:* the recorded 2015–16 European migration crisis and its sequel to 2025 — the Eurostat asylum series, the UNHCR Mediterranean arrival series, the dated decisions and who took them (25 Aug, 31 Aug, 4–5 Sept and 13 Sept 2015; Hungary's fence, 15 Sept; the EU relocation decision, 22 Sept; the EU–Turkey statement, 18 Mar 2016), and the aftermath records (Cologne New Year 2015/16; the deportation gap; the BfV list of Islamist attacks 2016–2025; IAB-Kurzbericht 17/2025 on employment in 2024; the BKA *Bundeslagebild* 2024; the Bundestag elections of 2017 and 2025; the controls of 16 Sept 2024, the refusals of 7 May 2025 and the Berlin court's ruling of 2 June 2025).
  - *Module results it uses:* `open-borders`, `global-original-position`, the public-order restriction (carens); `self-determination-over-membership`, `refugee-vs-economic-migrant`, `integration-civic-social-cultural`, the coconut and the "fair share" (miller); `two-concatenations`, the Dublin inversion, the pull-factor claim, the deportation gap, `bockenforde-dilemma` (murray); `friend-enemy-distinction` and `protection-and-obedience` (schmitt, by Ref); `state-of-exception` (by Ref).
  - *Departures from the idealisation the lesson must name* (they enter the weighing, step 8, as evidence, not as a closing list): (1) all three theories assume a *state* that decides for a *people*; the decisions of 25 August–13 September were taken by one member state's executive (a federal office's tweet; a telephone agreement with Austria; a police order), with no vote of its own parliament, and bound twenty-seven others through Schengen — no theory has a slot for a membership decision made by one member of a union for the rest, nor for the relocation vote of 22 September, which decided membership for four peoples over their governments' votes. (2) All three assume law that is enforced: Dublin's "first country" rule was not applied (Hungary registered arrivals who did not stay; Greece and Italy waved through); refused claimants were not returned (the deportation gap); the refugee/economic line Miller and Murray both need was not drawn at the border but years later, by courts. (3) None contemplates the instrument that ended the crisis: a cash-and-visas agreement with Turkey that moved the border outside the Union. (4) Carens's public-order restriction and Miller's "morally excruciating" overload both describe the situation but neither tells a government *when* it has arrived there; Murray says it had arrived in 2010 and gives no number either.

## VERDICT (module verdict, from research/verdicts/REGISTER.md; full record in research/verdicts/2015-and-verification.md Part 1)

**Claim judged.** The module's question: who is "we", may a people decide who joins it, and what did 2015 show about the three answers (Carens, Miller, Murray)? Judged in four parts against the record of 2015–16 and its sequel to 2025.

**The module's answer — on balance.** A people may control its membership, within duties of rescue, fair terms for migrants and full inclusion of those it admits. 2015 was a failure of capacity *and* of principle, though not of Miller's principle: Miller's principle was not applied. The question of who joins was decided by an executive declining to use powers it said it had.

**(a) Procedure and consent.**
- *Record.* 25 August 2015: the BAMF announced on Twitter that Dublin procedures for Syrians were "weitestgehend faktisch nicht weiter verfolgt" (in practice largely no longer pursued; WD 3-3000-299/15, p. 4). No minister signed it; no cabinet or parliament decided it. 4–5 September: the Chancellor agreed by telephone with Austria to let the people stranded in Hungary travel on, as "eine Ausnahme" (an exception) because of the emergency (bundesregierung.de, 5 Sept 2015, quoted ibid.). 13 September: border controls returned, and the Federal Police order told officers to admit undocumented arrivals who asked for asylum ("die Einreise zu gestatten ist"). The government told parliament that refusal at the border "ist im Rechtsrahmen der Dublin-III Verordnung und des § 18 AsylG zulässig" (BT-Drs. 18/7311, answers 1 and 3). Its interior minister: "Politisch haben wir uns bisher jedenfalls dagegen entschieden" (politically, at any rate, we have so far decided against it; *Welt am Sonntag*, 13 Dec 2015, quoted in WD 3-3000-014/16). The Bundestag never voted on the admission; the Asylum Procedure Acceleration Act of October 2015 tightened procedure and did not authorise it. Asked in 2018 for the legal basis, the government named no order (BT-Drs. 19/753).
- *Verdict.* That the decisions were taken by the executive alone: **established**. That this bypassed rule by consent on a question of membership: **strong**. The emergency defence covers the weekend of 4–5 September, not the six months after controls existed, during which admission continued by decision. That the decision was unlawful: **open**. The Bundestag research service judged the statutory-basis argument weighty only on a premise of fact (discretion under § 18(4) no. 2, not Dublin responsibility) the government never clarified (WD 3-3000-014/16, p. 6), and found Di Fabio's concrete breach of federal duty unsubstantiated (WD 3-3000-237/18, p. 3). The Constitutional Court dismissed the AfD group's Organstreit as inadmissible (2 BvE 1/18, 11 Dec 2018); no court has ruled on the merits; Bavaria prepared a suit and did not file it.
- *EU level.* The relocation decision of 22 September 2015 (Council Decision 2015/1601, adopted by qualified majority over the Czech Republic, Hungary, Romania and Slovakia) was lawful: **established** (CJEU, Joined Cases C-643/15 and C-647/15, 6 Sept 2017). It was also a decision on who joins four peoples, taken over their governments' votes, by an act the Court held needed no involvement of national parliaments.

**(b) Security.**
- The BfV lists 17 Islamist attacks in Germany since February 2016, with 23 dead. Twelve were committed by men who came as asylum seekers, and those twelve account for 22 of the 23 deaths. That the lethal Islamist attacks were overwhelmingly by asylum-route arrivals: **strong** (not established, because several statuses rest on secondary sources and must be bound to judgments before the lesson states them; the Holocaust-memorial case [UNVERIFIED] has no deaths and does not affect the count).
- That the removal failure was systematic: **strong**. Amri (Berlin, December 2016) and the Hamburg attacker (July 2017) were obliged to leave and not removed for want of papers; Tunisia confirmed Amri's identity two days after the attack (BMI/BMJV chronology). The Ansbach attacker's Dublin removal was suspended. The Solingen attacker's transfer to Bulgaria was tried once, on 5 June 2023, and the deadline lapsed. The non-Islamist Aschaffenburg case (January 2025) has the same structure. This is Murray's deportation gap, observed directly.
- That screening failed in 2015–16: **strong**. A man registered under nine identities and assessed as a Gefährder had his claim pending for months; a minor was never fingerprinted; a Paris bomber was registered on Leros on a false passport [secondary].
- Foreseeability. As a class of risk the danger was foreseeable, and admission without refusal continued four months after 13 November 2015: **strong**. As individuals most attackers were not foreseeable — only three of twelve were known Islamists beforehand, and several radicalised years after arrival: **strong**.
- Scale, stated beside the share: about 3.0 million asylum applications in 2015–24, so twelve attackers is about four per million applications. Small relative to the flow, and almost the whole of Islamist terrorism's toll in Germany. Both statements belong in the verdict; a verdict that gives only one is shaded.
- Magdeburg (December 2024, not Islamist) and Aschaffenburg (no political or religious motive found) are excluded from the Islamist count.

**(c) Principle.** Miller over Carens, **on balance** (decided in `miller-strangers-in-our-midst`: equal moral worth grounds a right to *some* adequate place, not to *this* one). 2015 adds that Carens's own brake, the public-order restriction, is the case (a) and (b) document: admission without identification and removal that cannot be enforced. On his own terms his theory licensed more control than was exercised.

**(d) Integration.** One measure throughout: the employment rate (*Beschäftigungsquote*), the share of 18–64-year-olds in paid dependent employment, from the social-insurance register linked to the IAB-BAMF-SOEP survey; it excludes the self-employed (about 5% of the cohort) and civil servants. Source: IAB-Kurzbericht 17/2025 (Brücker, Jaschke & Kosyakova), for 2024, nine years after arrival.

| 2015 arrivals, 2024 | Employment rate | Population average, same measure |
|---|---|---|
| Women | 35% | 69% (all women) |
| Men | 76% | 72% (all men) |
| All | 64% | 70% |

- Counting self-employment, the arrivals' rate is about 70%; the report gives no population figure on that basis. 90% of the employed arrivals are in jobs subject to social insurance, against 92% in the population. Women are 29% of the cohort's working-age members; refugee women without children have an employment rate 19 points higher than those with a child under six. Over-50s: 45%.
- Pay: in 2023 the full-time median was €2,675 a month, 70% of the median of all full-time employees, just above the low-wage line of 66%. Benefits: in 2023, 34% of the cohort's working-age members lived in households drawing SGB II; asylum-seeker benefits are not in the register. 84% of the employed lived without supplementary SGB II.
- *Verdict, employment:* split by sex, **strong**. Refugee men exceed the male average on the same measure (76% against 72%); refugee women stand at about half the female average (35% against 69%); the overall gap is 6 points. The pay gap is large, and a third live in benefit households, against a population figure the report does not give.
- *Crime:* asylum-status immigrants were 8.8% of all suspects in 2024 (offences against residence law excluded), against 3.66% of the population (3,061,540 of 83,577,140, Destatis, 31 Dec 2024), about 2.4 times; in offences against life 12.2%, about 3.3 times; in sexual offences 7.9%, about 2.2 times (BKA *Bundeslagebild Kriminalität im Kontext von Zuwanderung 2024*). These are police statistics of suspects, unadjusted; the BKA says so. 82.9% of the suspects were male and 57.1% under 30, against 55.0% and 46.0% among resident refugees; 31.8% were repeat suspects, involved in 77.0% of the group's offences. Overrepresentation among recorded suspects: **strong**. How much survives adjustment for age and sex: **open** — settled by suspect rates by residence status standardised by age and sex. That refugee arrivals did not raise local crime rates in 2018–23: **on balance** (one district panel with district and year fixed effects, ifo Schnelldienst digital 3/2025, whose authors state that individual data are unavailable).

**What 2015 showed about the three answers.**
- *Carens.* Open admission held for months and was reversed by every government that tried it, Germany included.
- *Miller.* His principle stands, and 2015 is evidence for it in the negative: the state lacked the capacity to sort, screen and return that his framework presupposes. Ten years of correction followed along his lines: controls at all nine land borders from 16 September 2024, notified to 15 March 2027; refusal at the border resumed by instruction on 7 May 2025, vulnerable persons excepted, and held unlawful under Dublin in three cases by the Berlin administrative court (VG 6 L 191/25, 2 June 2025), so this correction is contested in law; the Pact on Migration and Asylum, applying from 12 June 2026.
- *Murray.* His "never asked" holds for 2015 in the strongest sense: not even the Bundestag was asked. His deportation gap is confirmed, with dead. His paralysis forecast held for about six months and then failed: the liberal order corrected itself through its own instruments, late, and after the electorate punished the governing parties (AfD 12.6% in 2017; 20.8% and second place in 2025). His integration forecast fails for men on employment, holds for women, and is partly borne out on crime pending adjustment.

**Forecast checks (autumn 2015).** (a) Zetsche's "next Wirtschaftswunder" (14 Sept 2015) → **failed**, **strong**: nine years on the cohort's employment rate is 64% against 70%, full-time pay 70% of the median, women at half the female rate. (b) Nahles to the Bundestag, 10 Sept 2015, "Der syrische Arzt ist nicht der Normalfall … Nicht einmal jeder Zehnte kann direkt in Arbeit oder Ausbildung kommen" → **held**, **strong**; it came from the governing coalition. (c) "Wir schaffen das" (31 Aug 2015), read as a forecast that Germany would absorb the inflow as it had absorbed earlier ones → **fails, on balance**: the state did not break down and refugee men exceed the male rate; women's employment, pay, benefit dependence, a 2.4-fold overrepresentation among recorded suspects (unadjusted) and the absence of prior consent were not "managed". Its author withdrew the phrase within thirteen months (19 Sept 2016, "beinahe … eine Leerformel"), not the decision. The IAB itself says that, for want of clear criteria of success ("klarer Erfolgskriterien"), the question cannot be answered unambiguously. Would be overturned by: female employment converging on the female average, crime overrepresentation disappearing after adjustment, or a fiscal study of the cohort showing a lifetime net gain.

**Strongest objections, from both sides, and why they do not overturn it.**
- *From one side:* on 4 September thousands were stranded at Keleti and on the motorway, and a state may act without parliament in an emergency; the Bundestag acquiesced and the 2017 election returned the same Chancellor, so consent was given after the fact; and naming the attackers' status blames three million people for twelve. *Answer:* the emergency covers days, not the months after controls existed. Acquiescence is not authorisation — that is the point of the *Wesentlichkeit* doctrine, by which the legislature must itself make the essential decisions — and an election in which migration was one issue among several ratifies no specific decision. The verdict states the per-capita rarity beside the share of the toll, and it locates the failure in institutions (screening, removal), not in a group.
- *From the other side:* the admission was not merely unauthorised but unlawful (Di Fabio). *Answer:* no court has ruled, the research service found the concrete breach unsubstantiated, and Bavaria did not sue.

**What would overturn it.** For (a): a published ministerial order under § 18(4) no. 2 with stated limits, proof that the admissions rested on Dublin responsibility, or a merits ruling. For (b): evidence that the asylum-route share of Islamist attackers is no higher than other groups' share, or that the removal failures were isolated. For (d): a fall in refugee men's employment, or age-and-sex-standardised suspect rates showing no overrepresentation. For (c): see `miller-strangers-in-our-midst`.

## EXPLANATION PLAN (L0)

Start from the opening verbatim (course.yaml, as revised): in 2015 about 1.3 million people applied for asylum in the EU, most arriving by sea through Greece and Italy; Germany suspended the rule that sent them back to the first country they entered, Hungary built a fence, a deal with Turkey closed the main route the next spring; ten years on, the record of what followed is in. The newcomer meets it as a test: a theory of membership is a theory about what a people may decide and what follows if it decides badly; here is a year in which several peoples decided several things at once, and a decade in which the results came in. The lesson asks each theory to speak, then weighs them.

**Length budget (est_minutes 45, about 5,500 words of body).** Steps 1–2 about 1,100 words together; steps 3–5 about 450 each (they recall the theory in one sentence and a `<Ref>`, then go to the record); step 6 about 1,000; step 7 300–500; step 8 about 1,000; Conclusion about 200. What was cut to make room is listed at the end of this plan.

1. **What actually happened — the record before any theory.** Attempt: the reader thinks they know (a million refugees; Merkel opened the border). The record is more exact and less simple. Two series: UNHCR's sea arrivals (about 1.0 million in 2015 against about 216,000 in 2014 — five times; about 85 per cent through Greece from Turkey, the rest Libya to Italy; about 3,800 dead or missing) and Eurostat's first-time asylum applications (about 1.26 million in 2015 against 563,000 in 2014; Germany about 442,000 filed in 2015 and 722,000 in 2016; Syrians 29 per cent, Afghans 14, Iraqis 10 — and a tail of Western Balkan applicants with recognition rates near zero). Then the three German numbers that are not the same number (890,000 registered arrivals; 442,000 applications filed; "up to 1.5m" leaked). One sentence on who came, from BAMF's own count: of 441,899 first-time applicants in Germany in 2015, 69.2 per cent were male and 71.1 per cent under 30. Because: the first result of the module's method is that the event has several sizes depending on what is counted, and every theory's prescription depends on which.
2. **The decisions, dated to the day, and who took each.** The seam: the reader expects a flood that forced a response; the record shows a sequence of decisions, each taken by an identifiable office, each of which changed the flow. 25 August: a federal office's tweet — "Dublin-Verfahren syrischer Staatsangehöriger werden zum gegenwärtigen Zeitpunkt von uns weitestgehend faktisch nicht weiter verfolgt" — signed by no minister. 31 August: the summer press conference, "Wir schaffen das". 2 September: the photograph of Aylan Kurdi. 4–5 September: the Chancellor agrees by telephone with Austria to let the people stranded at Budapest Keleti travel on, as "eine Ausnahme" (an exception). 13 September: Germany reintroduces border controls, and the Federal Police order of that day tells officers to admit undocumented arrivals who ask for asylum — while the government later tells parliament that refusal at the border was lawful (BT-Drs. 18/7311) and its interior minister says "Politisch haben wir uns bisher jedenfalls dagegen entschieden". 15 September: Hungary's Serbian fence closes and unauthorised crossing becomes a crime. 22 September: the Council adopts the 120,000 relocation scheme over four states' votes (upheld by the Court of Justice in 2017, C-643/15 and C-647/15). October: the monthly peak of about 211,000 arrivals in Greece. 13 November: Paris. 18 March 2016: the EU–Turkey statement; April 2016: Aegean arrivals fall to a few thousand a month. And the absence that matters most: the Bundestag never voted on the admission. Because: exact days here because contingency is the point (L12d) — the theories are about what *may* be decided and by whom, and the record shows what *was*, by whom, when. (Keleti and Röszke enter here as two dated scenes, set piece 3, not as a section of their own.)
3. **Carens applied.** What open borders prescribes: admit; the only brake is public order, on a reasonable expectation of damage to liberty itself, based on evidence acceptable to all, and never the hostility of residents; in nonideal theory, size of demand may justify "some restriction" with priority to those fleeing persecution. What it predicts: that most people do not love to move, so flows track how bad life is (Syria 2015: yes); that restriction protects privilege (the Balkan applicants with near-zero recognition are the test — were they claiming liberty or life chances?). What it omits: who decides. Carens's argument is addressed to a *state*; the decision of August 2015 was made by one state's executive for twenty-seven, and his theory has no slot for the Union — nor for the fact that Germany's open door was Hungary's crisis (the first-country rule) before it was Germany's. Close on the forward pointer step 8 cashes: admission without identification, and removal that cannot be enforced, are what his own public-order brake describes. Because: a theory of justice between a state and the person at its border says nothing about justice between states that share a border regime.
4. **Miller applied.** What the coconut prescribes: a strong enforced border, rapid status determination, refugees admitted or transferred to a safe place up to a fair share, economic migrants on fair terms or refused and returned, full inclusion for those who stay; burden-sharing through the EU "as an insurance mechanism". What it predicts: a mixed flow in which the refugee/economic line is "under strain" (his own Postscript) — borne out by the recognition rates (Syrians near 97 per cent; Western Balkans near zero; Afghans in between, the hardest case); a fight over fair shares (the September relocation decision and its refusal by the Visegrád four — Miller's "free-riding" problem exactly); the rescue paradox (rescue without admission, Australia — the Turkey statement is this). What it omits: the capacity assumption. "Rapid status determination" took Germany years; "returned" became the deportation gap (about 220,000 orders, 11,300 removals by May 2016; EU-wide about 533,000 orders and 227,000 returns in 2015); "enforced border" was a fence. And one thing his theory assigns that the record shows nobody exercising: the people's decision through its legislature. Because: an interest in self-determination presupposes a self that determines, and a state that can.
5. **Murray applied.** What restriction prescribes (ch. 18): regional resettlement near home, offshore processing, Europe-wide deportation of failed claimants, temporary asylum that ends — and, before all, a people allowed to say who it is for. What it predicts: that rescue pulls (the series underdetermines it — exercise 4); that Dublin would invert (Hungary's registrations against its asylum claims — set piece 5); that the flow followed the German signal (arrivals rose through September and October after 31 August — consistent, not proof: the Syrian war and Turkey's policy are the rival causes); that integration would fail at the civic level (Cologne as the case he gives); that the liberal order, tired and guilty, could not correct itself (the second concatenation). What it omits: 2016 — flows fell when borders went back up and Turkey was paid, which is Murray's own point, but they fell *within* the liberal order he says cannot act. Step 6 tests the rest. Because: the second concatenation predicts paralysis, and the record has to be read to its end before that forecast is scored.
6. **Ten years on: the record after 2016.** The seam: the reader who stops in March 2016 sees a crisis ended by a treaty; the record runs on, and three of its lines decide the verdict. Present each line as a bound table or count, with its source and its limit:
   - *Attacks and removals.* The BfV's list: 17 Islamist attacks in Germany since February 2016, 23 dead; twelve by men who came as asylum seekers, accounting for 22 of the deaths. The removal cases in one run: Amri (obliged to leave, not removed for want of Tunisian papers; identity confirmed two days after the Breitscheidplatz attack — the BMI/BMJV chronology), the Hamburg attacker (same mechanism), Ansbach (Dublin removal suspended), Solingen 2024 (transfer tried once, deadline lapsed). Aschaffenburg 2025 (no political or religious motive; same removal structure) and Magdeburg 2024 (not Islamist) stated and excluded from the count. Then the scale: about 3.0 million applications in 2015–24, about four attackers per million. Both numbers, side by side.
   - *Employment, nine years on.* IAB-Kurzbericht 17/2025, its definition stated first (paid dependent employment among 18–64-year-olds, register-linked; self-employed and civil servants excluded). The table: men 76 against 72; women 35 against 69; all 64 against 70. Then pay (full-time median €2,675 in 2023, 70 per cent of the median, just above the low-wage line) and benefits (34 per cent in SGB II households in 2023). One sentence on why the women's figure moves with young children (19 points).
   - *Recorded crime.* BKA 2024: 8.8 per cent of suspects against 3.66 per cent of the population; 12.2 per cent in offences against life. Then, in the same paragraph, what the figure is: suspects, not convictions; unadjusted for a group that is 82.9 per cent male and 57.1 per cent under 30 among suspects; the BKA's own warning; the ifo district panel that finds no effect of arrivals on local crime rates in 2018–23, and its stated limit.
   - *The correction.* Two elections (AfD 12.6 per cent in 2017; 20.8 per cent and second place in 2025); controls at all nine land borders from 16 September 2024, notified to 15 March 2027; refusal at the border from 7 May 2025, vulnerable persons excepted, and the Berlin administrative court's ruling of 2 June 2025 that three refusals broke Dublin; the EU Pact applying from 12 June 2026. The pattern to name: the liberal order did correct, through its own instruments, late, and after the electorate punished the governing parties.
   Because: the theories made predictions about consequences, and the consequences took a decade to arrive; a verdict on 2015 drawn from 2016 alone would score Murray's paralysis forecast as a hit and his integration forecast as untested.
7. **The autumn forecasts: the minister was right, the industrialist was not.** Three forecasts, each dated and quoted: Merkel, 31 August 2015, "Wir schaffen das"; Zetsche, 14 September, the arrivals as possibly "eine Grundlage für das nächste deutsche Wirtschaftswunder … so wie die Millionen von Gastarbeitern"; Nahles to the Bundestag, 10 September, "Der syrische Arzt ist nicht der Normalfall … Nicht einmal jeder Zehnte kann direkt in Arbeit oder Ausbildung kommen". Then the record from step 6 against each. Zetsche's "Wirtschaftswunder" failed (**strong**): 64 against 70, pay at 70 per cent of the median, women at half the female rate. Nahles's sober forecast held (**strong**), and it came from inside the governing coalition — the forecast that proved right was an official one, not the critics'. "Wir schaffen das", read as a forecast that Germany would absorb the inflow as it had absorbed earlier ones, fails **on balance**: the state did not break down and refugee men exceed the male rate, but women's employment, pay, benefit dependence, a 2.4-fold overrepresentation among recorded suspects (unadjusted) and the absence of prior consent were not "managed"; its author called it "beinahe … eine Leerformel" thirteen months later and did not withdraw the decision; and the IAB itself says the question has no unambiguous answer for want of clear criteria of success. State what would overturn (c): women's employment converging on the female average, the crime overrepresentation disappearing after adjustment, or a fiscal study of the cohort showing a lifetime net gain. 300–500 words. Because: the liberal forecasts of 2015 are forecasts too, and the course holds them to the same test it put to Powell and Raspail.
8. **2015 failed on consent and on capacity, and Miller's principle survives it** (the weighing). Set the module's three answers against the record and reach the verdict, part by part, each with its grade and its deciding evidence:
   - *(a) Procedure and consent.* The decisions were the executive's alone (**established**): a tweet, a telephone agreement, a police order, no vote. That this bypassed rule by consent on a question of membership is **strong** — the government's own answer to parliament says refusal was lawful and its minister called the choice political, and a political choice about who belongs is what the consent tradition the course builds (Locke; Miller's self-determination) gives to the people's representatives. The emergency covers the weekend of 4–5 September, not the six months after controls existed. Whether it was unlawful is **open**: the research service's weighty argument rests on a fact the government never clarified, Di Fabio's concrete breach was judged unsubstantiated, the Organstreit was inadmissible, and no court has ruled on the merits. The relocation vote was lawful (**established**, CJEU) and was still a decision on who joins four peoples over their governments' votes, made by an act the Court held needed no national parliament. The departures named in the APPLICATION spec enter here: no theory has a slot for one member of a union deciding for the rest.
   - *(b) Security.* The lethal Islamist attacks were overwhelmingly by asylum-route arrivals (**strong**); removal failed systematically (**strong**); screening failed in 2015–16 (**strong**); the danger was foreseeable as a class of risk, and admission without refusal continued four months after Paris (**strong**); most attackers were not foreseeable as individuals (**strong**). Then both scales: about four per million applications, and almost the whole of the Islamist toll. The failure is located in institutions — screening and removal — not in a group.
   - *(c) Principle.* Miller over Carens, **on balance** (by `<Ref miller-strangers-in-our-midst>`). Carens's own brake is the case (a) and (b) document; on his own terms his theory licensed more control than was exercised. And the point that reverses the draft's "capacity, not principle": the failure was of capacity *and* of principle — not of Miller's principle, which was not applied, but of the principle that a people decides through its institutions who joins it.
   - *(d) Integration.* Split by sex (**strong**); overrepresentation among recorded suspects (**strong**); how much survives adjustment for age and sex **open**, with what would settle it; no detectable effect on local crime rates (**on balance**).
   - *What 2015 showed about the three answers.* Carens: open admission held for months and was reversed by every government that tried it. Miller: his principle stands, as evidence in the negative, and the decade's correction ran along his lines. Murray: "never asked" holds in the strongest sense; the deportation gap confirmed, with dead; the paralysis forecast held about six months and then failed; the integration forecast fails for men on employment, holds for women, partly borne out on crime pending adjustment.
   - *The strongest objections, both sides, answered* (from `## VERDICT`): the emergency, the Bundestag's acquiescence, the 2017 re-election and the charge of collective blame; and from the other side, Di Fabio's unlawfulness. Give each its best form before answering it. Close with the missing threshold: Carens's "size of demand", Miller's "overload" and Murray's "too many, too fast" all name a point and none gives it a number; the record decides who should have decided, not where the line lies, and the lesson says so and does not supply one.
   Because: an application that only confirms is not an application (L16), and a module that stages three answers owes the reader which one the record supports and how firmly.

**Conclusion.** State the verdict in plain words with its grades: a people may control its membership, within duties of rescue, fair terms and full inclusion of those it admits — Miller over Carens, on balance. In 2015 that principle was not applied: who joined was decided by an executive declining to use powers it said it had, with no vote of parliament (established that the executive alone decided; strong that consent was bypassed; open whether it was unlawful). The security and removal failures were systematic (strong); nine years on, refugee men work at above the male rate and refugee women at half the female rate (strong); recorded crime is overrepresented, and how much survives adjustment is open. Of the autumn forecasts, Nahles's held and Zetsche's failed (strong), and "Wir schaffen das" fails on balance. Murray was right that no one was asked and wrong that the liberal order could not correct itself. Then the hand-off: the record shows a state that did not ask its people; it cannot show whether a liberal state could have produced the consent and the solidarity it skipped. That is the Böckenförde question, and module 16 opens on it with Deneen.

## Pitfalls / subtleties to flag

- **Every number bound** (L12b; playbook-history rule 2): unit (people / applications / registrations), base (EU-28 / Germany / Greece), timeframe (calendar 2015 / Jan–Aug / by May 2016), source (Eurostat table id / UNHCR portal / BAMF / Murray's citation). A number without its sentence-frame is a required fix. Keeper numbers (four only): about 1.26 million first-time asylum applications in 2015 (Germany about a third); 22 of 23 Islamist-attack deaths since 2016, beside about four attackers per million applications; employment in 2024, men 76 against 72 and women 35 against 69; suspects 8.8 per cent against 3.66 per cent of the population, unadjusted. The rest lives in the figures and the aside.
- **Three German numbers are three quantities** (set piece 1); never let "a million refugees came to Germany" stand.
- **One integration measure.** Every employment figure comes from IAB-Kurzbericht 17/2025, with its definition stated once (paid dependent employment, 18–64, register-linked; self-employed and civil servants excluded) and its population comparison on the same measure. The 2022 survey figures of IAB-Kurzbericht 10/2024 (64% against Destatis's 77%; men 75, women 31) measure something else; they are not quoted beside 17/2025, and the lesson does not mix benchmarks.
- **Crime figures are suspects, unadjusted.** Every BKA figure is introduced as a share of recorded suspects, with the BKA's own warning and the age-and-sex profile in the same paragraph; never "crime rate", never "refugees commit". The open question (adjustment) and the ifo district finding are stated beside it.
- **Attackers' statuses.** State a status only once it is bound to a judgment, a Federal Prosecutor release or a primary chronology (Amri's is). The others stay as "came as an asylum seeker" with the BfV list as source until bound. Magdeburg and Aschaffenburg are named and excluded from the Islamist count, with the reason.
- **Two scales, always together.** The share of the Islamist toll (22 of 23) and the rate per application (about four per million) appear in the same sentence or adjacent ones, everywhere the security record is stated.
- **"Unlawful" is open.** The lesson says the executive decided alone (established) and that consent was bypassed (strong); it never says the admission was illegal, and it gives the reason (no merits ruling; the research service's conditional; Di Fabio's breach unsubstantiated).
- **Murray's "fewer than twenty" for Hungary** conflicts with Eurostat; resolve as in set piece 5 or omit. **Murray's "129 dead" in Paris** is the early toll; 130 is final. **"Up to 1.5m"** is a leak; say so.
- **Terms to anchor at first mention** (L11): *Dublin*, *Schengen*, *Frontex*, *BAMF*, *Eurostat*, *UNHCR*, *EASY* (Germany's first-registration system — one clause), *Keleti* (Budapest's eastern railway terminus), *Röszke* (the Serbian-border crossing), *Visegrád four*, *relocation* (the EU scheme moving recognised applicants between member states), *recognition rate* (the share of first-instance decisions granting protection), *first-time applicant* (Eurostat's category excluding repeat applications), *Lesbos / Moria / Kara Tepe*, *Mare Nostrum / Triton*, *the EU–Turkey Statement*, *AfD* (Alternative für Deutschland, founded 2013 as an anti-euro party), *Bundestag*. Most were anchored in the Murray lesson; re-anchor with a clause and a Ref (L11d).
- **Do not re-teach the three theories.** Each "applied" section opens with one sentence and a `<Ref>` recalling the theory's prescription, then goes straight to the record. The lesson's new content is the application and the departures.
- **The state of exception**: the Dublin suspension was an administrative announcement, not a declared emergency; the lesson may say it has the *form* Schmitt described — a norm's application decided by whoever decided — but must not say Germany invoked emergency powers. `<Ref schmitt-political-theology>` with that care.
- **Causation on a timeline**: adjacency is not causation (playbook-history anchor-timeline rule). Arrivals rose after 31 August; the Syrian war, Turkey's policy and the season are rival causes; say so each time the pull or the signal is asserted.
- **Cologne**: use the prosecutor's figures with their definitions (complaints; suspects identified; nationality of suspects); note that few identified suspects were 2015 asylum applicants; do not generalise from the event to the flow, and do not minimise it. The point for the lesson is which theory it bears on (Miller's civic integration; Murray's cover-up charge — the police press release of 1 Jan is the document).
- **2017 and 2025**: the AfD results (one voter in eight in 2017; one in five and second place in 2025) are evidence of electoral punishment and of the pressure under which the correction came; they are not a referendum on the 2015 decision (migration was one issue among several; the single-factor explanations are weak — the course's own statement, Fawcett in References). The same holds for the 2017 re-election offered as after-the-fact consent: an election ratifies no specific decision.
- **Testimony** (playbook-history §Testimony): the two Lesbos accounts now appear only in exercise 7; they are reported through Murray and an interpreter; the sourcing drill makes that visible.
- **Data provenance in figures**: every chart cites its table in the caption; the Eurostat and UNHCR series are reusable with attribution; a chart drawn from the data is the course's own (not a sourced artefact).
- **Register.** The course judges arguments, not people: no condemning or approving labels for any author in its own voice; a label may appear only inside a quotation, with who applied it and when. It does pass verdicts on arguments: where the lesson stages a dispute it has the evidence to settle, and in every module's closing lesson, it says which side wins, how firmly (established / strong / on balance / open) and why, as recorded in research/verdicts/. "Open" is a finding, stated with what would settle it, never a way to avoid one. The same tests apply to the left and the right. The lesson judges the decisions, not the people who took them: it states that the 2015 admission was decided by the executive alone (established) and bypassed consent on a question of membership (strong), that its lawfulness is open, that the security and removal failures were systematic (strong), and that integration split by sex (strong) — each with its evidence and grade, as in `## VERDICT`. It calls neither the decision nor the Hungarian fence a crime or a duty, and no official, migrant group or voter is labelled. Failure is located in institutions (screening, removal, the absent parliamentary decision), never in a group; the per-capita rarity of the attackers is stated beside their share of the toll. Orbán's and Soros's sentences, and the press labels of the time, appear only as quotations with speaker and date.
- **Thin spots in the dossier**: the record after Murray's text (autumn 2016) is now staged for procedure, security, integration, forecasts and the 2024–25 correction (see Sources, "The verdict's record"). Still to obtain: the BKA official copy, the Bundeswahlleiter results, the 2017 relocation totals, the Pact's EUR-Lex text, the V13 judgment, BT-Drs. 19/30800, the NRW Solingen report and the Cologne Landtag inquiry.

## VERDICT

This lesson carries the COURSE verdict (research/verdicts/REGISTER.md, "Course verdict (`the-argument-assembled`)"). It is binding: state it no more and no less firmly.

- **Claim judged:** whether a free state can survive on liberal principles alone — the question the course's history leads to, answered by assembling the sixteen module verdicts.
- **Verdict, in the exact words of the last paragraph of course.yaml's `intro` (the thesis; quote it once, verbatim, at the head of the thesis section):** "Its answer, on balance, is that it cannot. Liberal freedom is real and worth keeping, but it does not found itself: it lives on a nation, an elite held under law, an inherited culture that it did not create and cannot produce on demand, and an economy of property and markets that every planned alternative failed to match. The radical right's alternatives (the decider, the racial nation, the conservative revolution) failed by their own tests; the constitution written by one majority has not shown the cohesion it was written to secure." Overall grade: **on balance**. In the lesson the opening words "Its answer" become "The answer the module verdicts add up to"; the lesson never says "this course".
- **What decides it, clause by clause (REGISTER "The module verdicts behind each clause"):**
  1. *"It cannot [survive on liberal principles alone]" — on balance.* Module 16: the premise holds on balance; a free state draws on a solidarity, a readiness to bear burdens for strangers who count as "us", which its procedures do not supply, and Habermas grants the motivational half (`hungarys-fundamental-law`). Module 2: the commonwealth was held by a public worship, and both sides of 384 acted on that (**strong**, `altar-of-victory`). Module 4: consent did not found the governments Hume surveys (**strong**, `hume-against-the-original-contract`). Module 15: membership was decided in 2015 outside the people's institutions, and the correction came only after electoral pressure (**strong** on procedure, `the-2015-crisis-read-three-ways`). Forecast checks: integration "by itself" failed for the non-EU second generation, and the expected secularization did not come (**strong**, `murray-strange-death-of-europe`) — liberal institutions did not reproduce a shared culture automatically. Firmness is limited because the falsifier (trust and willingness to fund transfers to strangers do not fall where shared identity or religion weakens) is not yet bound, and the Third Republic case is open.
  2. *"Liberal freedom is real and worth keeping" — strong.* Module 5: the wide franchise did not end liberty or stability in Britain (`british-franchise`). Module 7: parliamentary government lasted through the same years in Britain, Scandinavia and the Low Countries (`article-48-weimar`). Module 9: the civil core of rights became justiciable (`udhr-read-against-the-right`). Module 11: the republican peace holds (`testing-the-clash`). Module 9: Condorcet's material forecasts held (`voegelin-gnosticism`). Module 16: a constitutional order was voted out in 2026 (`hungarys-fundamental-law`).
  3. *"It lives on a nation, an elite held under law and an inherited culture that it did not create and cannot produce on demand."* A nation: **on balance** — membership is received rather than chosen (**strong**, `barres-and-maurras-integral-nationalism`); Miller over Carens (**on balance**, `miller-strangers-in-our-midst`); there is no neutral civic state (**strong**, `hazony-virtue-of-nationalism` (b)). An elite held under law: **strong** — a minority always rules, and regimes differ in juridical defence and in whether the many can remove the minority (`mosca-ruling-class`, `michels-and-pareto-oligarchy-and-elites`); Weimar shows what happens when the law does not hold the elite (`article-48-weimar`). An inherited culture that it did not create: **on balance**, from modules 2 and 16 and the forecast checks. "Cannot produce on demand": the register found the earlier wording "cannot replace" beyond the evidence (**open**: the Third Republic made a nation through schools and conscription and stayed free; Habermas says the state can justify itself from its own resources) and proposed "cannot produce on demand" in its place. The lesson states the phrase at the strength the evidence gives: the deliberate attempt in the course's record — Hungary's written presuppositions — has not shown the cohesion it promised (**open** on cohesion), and integration "by itself" failed (**strong**); whether a free state can build a nation over generations is **open**. "On demand" is the whole of what the clause claims, and the lesson says so.
  4. *"The radical right's alternatives failed by their own tests."* The decider: **on balance** — the commissarial remedy as practised had no criterion that kept it commissarial (`article-48-weimar`), and the doctrine's "who decided is law" cancelled its own 1932 criterion against a legal revolution (`the-right-in-1933`; **strong** on Schmitt's own texts); Schmitt's diagnosis held (**strong**), and that half goes to him. The racial nation: **established** — Gobineau and Chamberlain/Grant fail on their own tests (`gobineau-inequality-of-races`, `chamberlain-and-grant`), and ancient DNA refutes pure races (`reich-ancient-dna-and-the-indo-europeans`). The conservative revolution: **strong** for Moeller (`moeller-van-den-bruck-third-reich`); **on balance** for the module (`the-right-in-1933`). The constitution written by one majority is *not* listed among the failures by their own test: the Fourth Amendment's majority overriding its court is **strong**, cohesion — its own test — is **open**, and the liberal charge that it could not be voted out failed in 2026 (**strong**). The intro's second half of clause 4 says exactly that much; the lesson states the Fourth Amendment finding beside it, since the module verdict carries it.
- *Clause added by the author (2026-11-01): "and an economy of property and markets that every planned alternative failed to match."* **Established** that market economies outperform planned ones, necessary not sufficient (`burnham-managers-and-the-suicide-of-the-west`); Marx's distinctive forecasts failed, **strong** (`marx-manifesto`). The lesson states it with the other clauses; `the-doctrine` derives it as P14/T2.
- **Strongest objection:** four, staged at full strength in the thesis-tested section (plan step 7): Rawls (a just basic structure generates its own support), Carens (the bounded "we" is privilege), MacIntyre (a nation and inherited culture cannot supply what the lost tradition supplied), Habermas (the constitutional state can justify itself; its dependence on solidarity is only motivational). The strongest single one is Habermas's, because it is the one the thesis's most exposed phrase ("cannot produce on demand", formerly "cannot replace") must meet.
- **Why it does not overturn the verdict:** Habermas grants the half of the thesis that clause 1 needs — "liberal societal structures are dependent on the solidarity of their citizens", and if secularization goes "off the rails" its sources "may dry up" — and contests only the justificatory half, which the thesis does not claim. Clause 1 stands **on balance** against him; how far a state can produce the solidarity it depends on stays **open**, which is why the thesis says "on demand" and not "ever".
- **What would overturn it:** for clause 1, a bound cross-national series showing that trust and willingness to fund transfers to strangers do not fall where shared identity or religion weakens; for "cannot produce on demand", a cohesion series (trust, fertility, marriage, civic participation) that moves with a deliberate constitutional or educational programme and not with the economic cycle, or a bound account of the Third Republic showing a nation built by a free state within a generation; for clause 2, a liberal parliamentary order that fell to its own franchise where it was defended, or a war between two established democracies; for clause 4, an argument from the decider, the race or the revolution that meets its own stated test.

## EXPLANATION PLAN (L0)

Start from the opening verbatim (the new `opening`, `research/verdicts/yaml-final.md`): sixteen disputes have been put to the record, from Sparta's helots to Hungary's election of April 2026, each with a verdict and a grade; set side by side, they answer four older questions and the one the history leads to — whether a free state can survive on liberal principles alone — and each answer must hold under the same tests whichever side gave it. The reader, no longer a newcomer, meets the lesson as the closing of an argument whose parts they have: the problem is not to learn anything new but to see what the parts add up to and whether the sum survives its strongest objections. The first section states the method once and works one case; then one section per question, each reaching its verdict with its grade; then the thesis, argued clause by clause; then the thesis tested; then what remains open. Length: about 8,000 words of body (55–60 minutes); the four question sections carry most of it. Each `<Ref>` gets one clause of recall; the grades are the lesson's load, not the evidence behind them, which lives in the lessons Ref'd.

1. **The method: an answer is weighed three ways, and graded (heading: "An answer is judged by its objection, its forecast and its evidence — and the same tests run in both directions").** Question: what entitles anyone to say that one answer to "who should rule" beat another, when the authors disagree about the premises? Obvious attempt: count who still holds it. Fails: the mixed constitution was declared impossible by the most rigorous theory of sovereignty ever written and is the constitution of most free states now — popularity and survival are not the same as having won the argument. The three tests the verdicts used: (i) *the strongest objection* — did the answer meet the best objection made to it, from whichever side, on its own terms; (ii) *the forecast against the record* — where the answer predicted something datable, did it happen; (iii) *consistency with the evidence* — does it fit what is now known, and can it be tested at all. **Worked case — the divided sovereign** (set piece 1): Bodin and Hobbes held that sovereignty cannot be divided, and Hobbes forecast (XXIX) that a mixed or limited monarchy cannot stand (`<Ref bodin-sovereignty>`, `<Ref hobbes-sovereign-and-liberty>`: `indivisible-sovereignty`). Test (ii): 1642 bore out the first half — king and Parliament each claimed marks of sovereignty, and the result was war, regicide and an army's rule; 1660–61 and 1689 refuted the second — a limited crown lasted for centuries (`<Ref english-revolution-as-test>`). Test (i): the strongest reply for Hobbes — king-in-parliament is a single sovereign assembly, which his theory allows — narrows the verdict and does not reverse it, because the post-1689 constitution kept the divisions XXIX calls fatal. Test (iii): the record supports the narrower claim: there must be a settled last word, but a composite body can hold it and limits can bind it. Verdict: **strong**. Then the grade scale, defined by this case and three others the reader holds: *established* — the failure is internal to the text or the evidence admits no reasonable rival (natural slavery fails by Aristotle's own concessions, `<Ref aristotle-political-animal-and-natural-slavery>`); *strong* — the record decides it and the best objection narrows without reversing (the divided sovereign); *on balance* — the evidence leans one way and a serious rival reading remains (the mixed regime at Sparta, `<Ref sparta-as-test-case>`); *open* — the evidence does not decide, and the verdict says what would (the genetic share of group gaps, `<Ref national-data-audited>`). "Open" is a finding with its settling evidence named, never a refuge. Two rules follow from the tests. **The same tests run in both directions** (set piece 2): Burke's forecasts of 1790 and Condorcet's of 1795 were checked the same way — Burke's paper money and general were hits and his departments and France "expunged out of the system of Europe" were misses (`<Ref assignats>`); Condorcet's longer lives, literacy and abolition held (**strong**), equality between nations failed for a century and a half before turning his way (**on balance**) (`<Ref voegelin-gnosticism>`). **A clause that no outcome could refute earns no credit on either side**: Carlyle's Valet-World (`<Ref carlyle-heroes>`), Spengler's fellah device (`<Ref spengler-decline-of-the-west>`), Toynbee's golden mean (`<Ref toynbee-challenge-and-response>`), Pareto's success-defined elite (`<Ref michels-and-pareto-oligarchy-and-elites>`) — and Condorcet's "indefinite" perfectibility (`<Ref voegelin-gnosticism>`) and Fukuyama's "in the long run" (`<Ref huntington-clash-of-civilizations>`). The verdicts judge arguments, not the people who made them. Bearing: the four sections that follow apply exactly these tests, and the grade of each answer is the grade its weakest load-bearing verdict carries.

2. **Who should rule (heading: "Who should rule: an accountable minority under law, with a settled last word — not the one who knows, and not the one who decides").** Seam: the divided sovereign was the first verdict on this question; the others assemble around it. The course's answer, with its grade (**strong** on each part that rests on a strong verdict; the parts below on balance are marked): rule needs a settled last word, which a composite body can hold and law can bind; a minority always rules, and what separates free regimes from the rest is whether law holds that minority and the many can remove it; a wide franchise under such law did not destroy liberty, property or stability where it was tested; the rival answers — rule by knowledge, rule by the decider, rule by the designed constitution in Maistre's sense of impossibility — failed by their own tests or had no case to test. The assembly, one clause and one Ref per verdict, grouped by test:
   - *Rule by knowledge and the cycle.* No city was ever ruled by philosophers: the answer has no case to test (`<Ref sparta-as-test-case>`; reconstruction `<Ref kallipolis-and-the-philosopher-king>`). Plato's chain from democracy to tyranny fails as a law (**strong**; Athens's democracy fell to oligarchs and Macedon, never to a tyrant raised by the demos) and holds as a mechanism for archaic Greece (**on balance**) (`<Ref decline-of-regimes>`). Polybius's cycle as a fixed order fails; his forecast that Rome's mixture would decay through wealth and rivalry for office was borne out in outline (**strong** on both, `<Ref polybius-anacyclosis-and-rome>`). The mixed regime of the middle fits Sparta best (**on balance**, `<Ref sparta-as-test-case>`). Conflict channelled through institutions produced Roman liberty at little cost (**on balance**, `<Ref machiavelli-discourses-republic>`).
   - *The sovereign and consent.* The divided sovereign (above, **strong**). Filmer's descent from Adam fails at the heir (**established**), and his objection that no one consents to being born into a polity stands (`<Ref filmer-patriarcha>`). The original contract fails as history and tacit consent by residence fails (**strong**, `<Ref hume-against-the-original-contract>`) — a liberal claim failing — and what survives is consent as a hypothetical test, which passes to Rawls. The state lowers lethal violence (Hobbes, **strong**) and the solitary peaceable man fails (**established**) (`<Ref rousseau-inequality-and-general-will>`).
   - *Designed order.* Maistre's general thesis that nothing deliberated lasts fails on the American constitution, the departments and the metric system (**strong**); Burke's general argument that a whole society cannot be redesigned at one stroke without a heavy price holds **on balance**, with war finance as the rival cause (`<Ref assignats>`).
   - *The franchise.* The fragility forecast failed for Britain (**strong**), the taxation forecast arrived (**strong**), power passed to Cabinet and party machine, pressure from below forced the question and party competition answered it (**on balance**), and whether the welfare state is soft despotism is **open**, with what would settle it: whether associational life and electoral turnover shrank as the state's functions grew (`<Ref british-franchise>`). Mill's harm principle cannot apply itself (**on balance**); Stephen does not refute Mill's case for free discussion (**strong**) (`<Ref stephen-liberty-equality-fraternity>`). Taine's and Le Bon's crowd fails as psychology and as sociology (**strong**), and a club network captured institutions without making the crowds (**on balance**) (`<Ref taine-and-le-bon-revolution-and-crowd>`). Documents, courts and a parliamentary republic decided Dreyfus, not the louder crowd (**strong**, `<Ref dreyfus-affair-as-test>`).
   - *The minority and the decider.* Oligarchy is a strong tendency inside organisations, not an iron law, and does not decide the contest between parties (**strong**, `<Ref michels-and-pareto-oligarchy-and-elites>`; `<Ref mosca-ruling-class>`: `political-formula`). Weimar: the diagnosis of a parliament that would not govern held (**strong**); the commissarial remedy as practised had no criterion that kept it commissarial (**on balance**); whether the theory helped produce the collapse is **on balance** yes for 1930–32 and **open** after July 1932 (`<Ref article-48-weimar>`). In 1933 the doctrine that makes the successful decision law could not refuse a revolution once it had succeeded (**on balance** for the module; **strong** for Schmitt on his own texts), and Spengler's parliaments-as-pageantry forecast failed by the century's end (**strong**) while his Caesarism, dated 2000–2200, is untested (**open**) (`<Ref the-right-in-1933>`, `<Ref spengler-decline-of-the-west>`).
   - *After 1945.* The state's extent grew enormously across the democratising era (**established**) and fails as a law of near-uninterrupted growth after 1990 (**on balance**); whether Power's appetite or voters' demand drove it is **open** (`<Ref jouvenel-on-power>`). The rights critiques hit the missing foundation (**strong**) and miss the civil core, which became justiciable (**strong**) (`<Ref udhr-read-against-the-right>`). Vermeule's common-good constitutionalism supplies no check of its own against a bad-faith determination (**on balance**, `<Ref vermeule-common-good-constitutionalism>`); Hungary's majority overrode its court (**strong**) (`<Ref hungarys-fundamental-law>`).
   - **The liberal and left forecasts on this question, checked the same way** (300–500 words; set piece 3, the table): Marx's proletarian revolution in an advanced country failed (**strong**) — power came by the ballot and his graduated income tax by statute — while wage dependence became general (**strong**) (`<Ref marx-manifesto>`); Fukuyama's spread of liberal democracy held sixteen years and failed from 2006 (**strong**), and China did not liberalize (**strong**) (`<Ref huntington-clash-of-civilizations>`); Kant's peace between republics and the Schuman Declaration's "materially impossible" war held (**strong**), with the mechanism unproven (`<Ref testing-the-clash>`); the charge that Hungary's order could not be voted out failed on 12 April 2026 (**strong**, `<Ref hungarys-fundamental-law>`). Right-wing forecasts on the same question: Maine's and Lecky's fragility failed for Britain, Spengler's parliaments failed, Burke split, Schmitt's 1932 warning came true. Bearing: on "who should rule" the liberal forecasts that held were the broad ones (peace between democracies, the vote never taken back) and the ones that failed were the confident universal ones; the right's that held were diagnoses (the minority, the paralysed parliament, the growth of administration), and the ones that failed were its forecasts of collapse.
   Close with the bearing: the answer that survives is neither Plato's nor Schmitt's but a narrowed Aristotle and a narrowed Mosca — the mixed, law-bound rule of a removable minority — and it is graded **strong** because each of its parts rests on a strong verdict.

3. **Is inequality natural (heading: "Is inequality natural: people differ in abilities that matter, and no argument from that difference to a right of rule has survived").** Seam: Aristotle's natural slavery was the course's first established verdict and the same step fails at every later attempt; the question is what does survive. 5. **Why the West (heading: "Why the West: a lead older than 1800 and younger than the Greeks, built on borrowings — its cause still open").** Seam: the oldest fact in the course — the Greeks credited Egypt and Babylon with much of what they built on, and their own advance came from an arrangement, power placed "in the middle", not from a special people (**on balance**, `<Ref greek-miracle>`). ## Pitfalls / subtleties to flag

- **The register is binding, in both directions.** Every grade in the lesson is the register's grade for that claim, no firmer and no softer. Do not upgrade a component because the question verdict reads better that way, and do not soften a verdict because it lands on one side. Where a question's answer rests on verdicts of different grades, give each part its own grade (as in step 2) rather than one blended grade.
- **The thesis is quoted exactly as the `intro` now words it** (checked 2026-11-01: "Its answer, on balance, is that it cannot. … cannot produce on demand. The radical right's alternatives (the decider, the racial nation, the conservative revolution) failed by their own tests; the constitution written by one majority has not shown the cohesion it was written to secure."). If the human changes the `intro` before the lesson is written, re-read it and quote the new wording. **Changed since this check:** the `intro` now ends the third clause "… cannot produce on demand, and an economy of property and markets that every planned alternative failed to match" (the round-2 forecast check in `burnham-managers-and-the-suicide-of-the-west`, **established**, with its limit: necessary, not sufficient). Quote that wording and argue the added supplement at its grade in step 6; the VERDICT block above predates it. "Cannot produce on demand" is argued at exactly its strength (VERDICT, clause 3); the lesson must not slide back to "cannot replace" or "cannot produce", which the register finds open. The constitution written by one majority is not listed among the alternatives that "failed by their own tests".
- **No new material; no re-teaching.** Every sentence asserting something about a thinker or a finding carries a `<Ref>` to the lesson that established it, with one clause of recall. The evidence behind a verdict is in its lesson: the synthesis gives the verdict, its grade, and at most the one fact that decides it. The one permitted exception is Habermas's two sentences if `hungarys-fundamental-law` does not teach them (Sources). If the ledger shows a lesson did not state its verdict, Ref it only for what it did teach and report the gap.
- **Length.** About 8,000 words of body, 55–60 minutes. The question sections carry most of it; if they run long, cut assembly items that carry no load in the question's verdict (a verdict stays only if it supports, limits or opposes the answer), never the left/liberal checks — those are required (the same-tests rule), as are the strongest objection and its answer in each section where the register gives one. The forecast ledger figure carries the full list; the body names the decisive rows.
- **The same tests apply to left and right, visibly.** Each question section includes the liberal and left forecast checks the register places on it, and states the pattern without spin. Check the balance by reading each section as a partisan of either side would: the failures of the anti-hereditarian case appear as plainly as the failures of race theory; the failed migration forecasts as plainly as Powell's failed bloodshed; Marx's failed revolution as plainly as Spengler's failed parliaments. The thesis itself is stated as neither side's: liberal freedom kept, liberal self-sufficiency denied.
- **Register.** The course judges arguments, not people: no condemning or approving labels for any author in its own voice; a label may appear only inside a quotation, with who applied it and when. It does pass verdicts on arguments, and this lesson passes the course's: it says which answer wins, how firmly (established / strong / on balance / open) and why, as recorded in research/verdicts/. "Open" is a finding, stated with what would settle it, never a way to avoid one. The same tests apply to the left and the right.
- **2015 is stated as the module verdict states it:** the failure located in institutions, not in a group; the per-capita rarity of attackers stated beside their share of the toll; crime figures as unadjusted police suspect statistics, adjustment open; the integration figures on the one measure (IAB 17/2025 employment rate, 2024).
- **Contested empirical claims are weighed, not sealed** (outcome `co-contested`): the genetic share, the cause of the West's lead, the African colonial ledger and adjusted crime are open with their settling evidence; the hereditarian premises that hold are stated as holding, and the inferences that fail on either side as failing.
- **The lesson never talks about itself or the course** (L10a): not "this course has shown", not "the sixteen modules". The verdicts are stated as findings about the arguments and the record ("the record of 1660 refutes the forecast"), each with its Ref. The thesis is introduced as the answer the verdicts add up to. The spine is never quoted.
- **Dropped slugs.** Never Ref `cicero-mixed-constitution`, `cicero-natural-law` (→ `cicero-res-publica-and-natural-law`; the cycle → `polybius-anacyclosis-and-rome`), `war-is-divine` (→ `maistre-and-bonald`), `nietzsche-on-the-right` (→ `the-right-after-1945`, `ressentiment-and-envy`). The Federalist is an in-course lesson.
- **Nietzsche is a Ref into the other course**, never re-exposed.

1. *Method, run on a new case (E3)*: Weigh Michels's iron law of oligarchy by the three tests — its strongest objection, its forecast against the record, its consistency with the evidence — using only `<Ref michels-and-pareto-oligarchy-and-elites>` and `<Ref article-48-weimar>`. The solution reaches the register's verdict (a strong tendency inside organisations, not an iron law; does not decide the contest between parties; **strong**), names the counter-case (two organised internal parties alternating for decades) and the falsifier (a large competitive organisation whose leadership nonetheless proves irremovable).

For each, give the verdict the course reached on it (or the lesson where the question it raises was decided) with its grade, in one clause and a Ref.
3. *Claim to refute*: "Natural slavery was refuted by Vitoria in 1539 and never returned." Name the step that fails (Vitoria's own just titles worked as a licence, **on balance**; Laelius's "rule over those better off ruled" had already restated it; Locke's "waste" and Mill's exclusion of "backward states of society" follow from conditions written into their own principles, **strong**, `locke-and-mill-on-empire`).

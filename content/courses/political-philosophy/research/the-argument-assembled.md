## EXPLANATION PLAN (L0)

Start from the opening verbatim: twenty-four centuries of answers to four questions have gone past; the lesson lays them out as the chain of moves it was — each thinker answering the one before — and asks which were refuted, which abandoned without refutation, and which still stand in the arguments of the 2020s; it ends where the course's own problems remain open. The reader, no longer a newcomer, meets it as a sorting task. The first section gives the three bins and works one case through them; then one section per question, each a chain narrated as replies ("Plato answered Thrasymachus with a city; Aristotle objected that…"), ending in its sorting and a "because" — what forced the chain to move; then what stays open.

1. **The method: three bins, worked on one answer.** Question: how does one tell an answer that lost the argument from one that was set aside without an answer? Obvious attempt: an answer is dead when nobody holds it any more. Fails: the mixed constitution shows why. Polybius explained Rome's rise by a mixture of consuls, senate and people that let each part block the others (`<Ref polybius-anacyclosis-and-rome>`: `checks-and-balances`); Bodin and Hobbes answered that sovereignty cannot be divided — a mixed state is "a man with three bodies" (`<Ref bodin-sovereignty>`, `<Ref hobbes-sovereign-and-liberty>`: `indivisible-sovereignty`); the English Revolution seemed to confirm them, and then the restored monarchy of 1660 accepted limits the theory said it could not (`<Ref english-revolution-as-test>`); the Federalist redesigned the mixture without estates — ambition counteracting ambition (`<Ref federalist-one-people-and-the-extended-republic>`). Answer: the mixed constitution is refuted as a theory of *sovereignty* (no one has shown where final authority sits in it) and standing as a *practice* (every constitution with separated powers). Because: refutation is relative to the question asked, and the questions moved. That is the sorting rule the rest of the lesson applies: refuted = an objection it could not meet on its own terms; abandoned = the question moved and no one answered the old answer; standing = argued in the last two modules.

2. **Who should rule — the chain, and the four times the question moved.** Opens from the first lesson's fact: the question became askable only where kingship broke and power stood "in the middle" of a community of peers (`<Ref greek-miracle>`: `es-meson`). Links, one clause each: the Funeral Oration's rule by the many and the Athenians' answer at Melos that justice holds only between equals in power (`<Ref thucydides-power-and-justice>`: `athenian-thesis`) → Thrasymachus restates it inside the city (`<Ref thrasymachus-and-the-ring>`) → Plato: the one who knows (`<Ref kallipolis-and-the-philosopher-king>`: `philosopher-king`), and freedom left to itself slides into tyranny (`<Ref decline-of-regimes>`); his own second thought, law with preambles (`<Ref platos-laws-second-best-city>`, extra — cite only, C2) → Aristotle: law and the middle, the wisdom of the multitude (`<Ref aristotle-regimes-and-the-middle>`) → Polybius and Cicero: the mixed people under a natural law above every statute (`<Ref polybius-anacyclosis-and-rome>`, `<Ref cicero-res-publica-and-natural-law>`) → Augustine: no earthly city is just (`<Ref augustine-two-cities>`) → Aquinas: a law above kings, no private tyrannicide (`<Ref aquinas-law-and-kingship>`) → Machiavelli: judge by what works (`<Ref machiavelli-effectual-truth>`, `<Ref machiavelli-discourses-republic>`). **First displacement** (the wars of religion): from *who rules* to *what is a state* — Bodin's sovereignty, Hobbes's derivation from fear, Filmer's from fatherhood (`<Ref bodin-sovereignty>`, `<Ref hobbes-state-of-nature-and-covenant>`, `<Ref filmer-patriarcha>`) → Locke: consent and a right of resistance (`<Ref locke-property-and-consent>`) → Hume: no government rests on consent; all rest on opinion (`<Ref hume-against-the-original-contract>`) → Rousseau: the general will (`<Ref rousseau-inequality-and-general-will>`) → Burke, Maistre, Donoso Cortés: no generation knows enough; when law fails, the sabre or the dagger (`<Ref burke-reflections>`, `<Ref maistre-and-bonald>`, `<Ref donoso-cortes-on-dictatorship>`) → Hegel: the rational state (`<Ref hegel-ethical-life-and-the-state>`). **Second displacement** (equality of conditions arrives as a fact): from *what is a state* to *what will democracy do to men* — Tocqueville, Carlyle, Mill, Stephen, Marx, Maine and Lecky (`<Ref tocqueville-tyranny-of-majority-and-soft-despotism>`, `<Ref carlyle-heroes>`, `<Ref mill-liberty-and-its-limits>`, `<Ref stephen-liberty-equality-fraternity>`, `<Ref marx-manifesto>`, `<Ref maine-and-lecky-on-popular-government>`), tested on the franchise (`<Ref british-franchise>`) and on the crowd (`<Ref taine-and-le-bon-revolution-and-crowd>`). **Third displacement** (the elite theorists): from *one, few or many* to *which minority, under what formula, checked how* (`<Ref mosca-ruling-class>`, `<Ref michels-and-pareto-oligarchy-and-elites>` — the key result's wording). **Fourth displacement** (Weimar): *who decides the exception* (`<Ref schmitt-political-theology>`, `<Ref schmitt-concept-of-the-political>`, `<Ref article-48-weimar>`); the radical right's answer — the myth, the front, the Worker, Tradition (`<Ref sorel-reflections-on-violence>`, `<Ref junger-storm-of-steel-and-the-worker>`, `<Ref moeller-van-den-bruck-third-reich>`, `<Ref evola-revolt-against-the-modern-world>`) — and 1933, where three of its thinkers chose (`<Ref the-right-in-1933>`). After 1945: Jouvenel's Power, Oakeshott's umpire, Strauss's best regime, Burnham's managers (`<Ref jouvenel-on-power>`, `<Ref oakeshott-rationalism-in-politics>`, `<Ref strauss-natural-right-and-history>`, `<Ref burnham-managers-and-the-suicide-of-the-west>`) → Lasch: the credentialed elite as the new danger (`<Ref lasch-revolt-of-the-elites>`) → Deneen: the umpire state is already the liberal state, and it breeds a new aristocracy (`<Ref deneen-post-liberalism>`) → Vermeule: rulers ordered to the common good (`<Ref vermeule-common-good-constitutionalism>`) → Hungary as the case (`<Ref hungarys-fundamental-law>`). **Sorting.** The Athenian thesis was answered many times — by construction (Plato's city, Hobbes's covenant) — and refuted by none; it returns as Carneades, Machiavelli, Hobbes's "covenants without the sword", Schmitt (recurrence table, set piece 2). The philosopher king was abandoned by Plato himself in the Laws and returns as the aristocracy of talent and as meritocracy — which Lasch and the cognitive-elite data then put to the test (`<Ref the-cognitive-elite>`). Consent: Filmer's and Hume's objection (no one consented to being born into a polity) was never met on its own terms, and consent stands as the formula every Western government invokes — which is what Mosca's `political-formula` predicts (a formula is needed whether or not it is true). Elite theory was not refuted; liberals absorbed it (Mosca's own late defence of the representative system; competition among elites). Schmitt's decider stands as a description of the exception and was tested, ambiguously, on Weimar; whether it disposed its author to 1933 is the right's hardest question about itself (key result; `<Ref the-right-in-1933>`). Because: each displacement was forced by an event the thinkers did not choose (a religious war, a revolution, a franchise, a parliament that abdicated by procedure), and an answer to the old question was left standing because no one asked it any more.

3. **Is inequality natural — the chain, and what "natural" meant.** *Aristotle*: hierarchy runs through all nature; some are slaves by nature — and nature "often fails" to mark them (`<Ref aristotle-political-animal-and-natural-slavery>`: `natural-slavery`, the difficulty internal); Sparta's helots as the standing case the theory cannot classify (`<Ref sparta-as-test-case>`) → *Cicero*: empire as rule over those better off ruled — natural slavery in Roman dress (`<Ref cicero-res-publica-and-natural-law>`) → *Augustine*: slavery by sin, not nature (`<Ref augustine-two-cities>`) → *Vitoria*: the Indians have dominion; not natural slaves — and the title left open "for the sake of argument" (`<Ref vitoria-right-to-the-americas>`) → *Hobbes*: equality of ability, the weakest can kill the strongest (`<Ref hobbes-state-of-nature-and-covenant>`) → *Filmer*: natural subjection to fathers (`<Ref filmer-patriarcha>`) → *Locke*: equality of jurisdiction, not of age or virtue (`<Ref locke-property-and-consent>`) → *Rousseau*: inequality is artificial, born of `amour-propre` and "this is mine" (`<Ref rousseau-inequality-and-general-will>`) → *Burke*: the natural aristocracy (`<Ref burke-reflections>`) → *Maistre*: there is no such thing as man (`<Ref maistre-and-bonald>`) → *Herder and Fichte*: peoples equal as cultures, and the step to rank (`<Ref herder-and-fichte-volk-and-nation>`) → *Tocqueville*: equality of conditions as a providential fact (`<Ref tocqueville-equality-and-the-township>`) → *Carlyle, Stephen*: the hero; inequalities of capacity no law removes (`<Ref carlyle-heroes>`, `<Ref stephen-liberty-equality-fraternity>`) → *Mill*: individuality, and the civilized-community clause (`<Ref mill-liberty-and-its-limits>`, `<Ref locke-and-mill-on-empire>`: `strategies-of-exclusion`) → *Marx*: class is the inequality that matters (`<Ref marx-manifesto>`) → *Gobineau, Chamberlain, Grant, Renan's masters and workers*: inequality of races as the cause of history (`<Ref gobineau-inequality-of-races>`, `<Ref chamberlain-and-grant>`, `<Ref renan-what-is-a-nation>`) → *Maurras*: protective inequality (`<Ref barres-and-maurras-integral-nationalism>`) → *Ortega*: nobility is obligation; the mass-man is a kind of man, not a class (`<Ref ortega-revolt-of-the-masses>`) → *Evola*: the castes as degrees of being (`<Ref evola-revolt-against-the-modern-world>`) → *Nietzsche's order of rank*, as the right kept and gave it up after 1945 (`<Ref the-right-after-1945>`: `aristocratic-radicalism`; cross-course `nietzsche/order-of-rank`) → *Strauss*: nature as the standard against historicism (`<Ref strauss-natural-right-and-history>`) → *Rawls*: talents are natural and undeserved — the natural lottery (`<Ref rawls-theory-of-justice>`) → *Scheler and Schoeck*: the demand for equality as ressentiment and envy; Rawls's reply, no excusable envy in a just structure (`<Ref ressentiment-and-envy>`) → *Scruton*: the stratified society (`<Ref scruton-meaning-of-conservatism>`) → *the cognitive elite* (`<Ref the-cognitive-elite>`) → *the heredity module*, premise by premise: what the tests measure (`<Ref what-intelligence-tests-measure>`), what heritability within groups can and cannot imply (`<Ref heritability-within-and-between-groups>`: `default-hypothesis`), the hereditarian case at full strength (`<Ref hereditarian-case-iq-and-nations>`), the US gap, adoption, admixture and polygenic scores (`<Ref us-gap-size-trend-and-environment>`, `<Ref adoption-studies-across-groups>`, `<Ref admixture-and-polygenic-scores>`), what follows politically — Harden's genetic lottery against Murray's reading of the same facts (`<Ref gould-and-harden-on-heredity>`), ancient DNA (`<Ref reich-ancient-dna-and-the-indo-europeans>`), geography and institutions (`<Ref geography-or-institutions>`), the datasets audited (`<Ref national-data-audited>`) → *between peoples*: the colonial balance sheet and inherited guilt (`<Ref colonial-balance-sheet>`, `<Ref inherited-guilt-and-reparations>`) → *Carens*: birthright citizenship as feudal privilege (`<Ref carens-open-borders>`) → *Deneen*: a regime of equality breeds a liberalocracy (`<Ref deneen-post-liberalism>`). **The step the chain hides:** "natural" changed meaning at least four times — Aristotle's *telos* (what a thing is for), Hobbes's *ability* (what any man can do to any other), Tocqueville's *providential fact* (what history is doing regardless), and the twentieth century's *heritable* (what genes contribute) — and Rawls added that what is natural need not be deserved. So the hereditarian and Rawls can agree that some inequality of talent is natural in the fourth sense and disagree entirely about what follows; Harden's genetic lottery is Rawls's natural lottery with a genome (set piece 3). **Sorting.** Natural slavery refuted internally (Aristotle's own concession) and by Vitoria; whether it returned in Mill's tutelage under another name is the contested `strategies-of-exclusion` reading against the "contradiction" reading (both from `locke-and-mill-on-empire` — state both). The nineteenth-century typological race theory: its classifications did not survive the 1911 measurement of immigrants' children and the ancient-DNA finding that every present population is a mixture (`<Ref chamberlain-and-grant>`, `<Ref reich-ancient-dna-and-the-indo-europeans>`: `population-mixture`) — refuted on its own evidence. The `hereditarian-hypothesis` in its modern form is neither: standing and contested, with its status stated as the module stated it (`research/heredity-evidence-review.md`, "Overall answer") — of its four premises, three largely hold in the main US data (the tests measure a general ability that predicts outcomes; it is heritable within groups; the gap is real on invariant measures), and the step to a genetic cause is open: heritability within a group does not by itself imply a genetic cause of differences between groups (key result, `<Ref heritability-within-and-between-groups>`), the gap as it stands is not wholly genetic because it narrowed within decades and varies with place, and no study has measured or excluded a genetic share (key result, `<Ref national-data-audited>`; what would settle it, `<Ref admixture-and-polygenic-scores>`); the national datasets on both sides failed parts of the same audit (`<Ref national-data-audited>`). Neither "shown to be partly genetic" nor "shown to be environmental" may be written in this bin. Hobbes's equality of ability never refuted — every later contract theory assumes it. Rousseau's "artificial" and Burke's "natural aristocracy" standing side by side, neither having met the other's objection. The envy charge and Rawls's reply: open — the course found no evidence that tells motive from principle (`<Ref ressentiment-and-envy>`). Because: the question was re-posed, not settled, each time "natural" took a new sense.

Because: every theory of membership needs a "we" before it begins, and none of them derives it.

5. **Why the West — the two answers at full strength, then the third family.** The section opens from the oldest fact in the course: the Greeks themselves credited Egypt and Babylon with much of what they built on (`<Ref greek-miracle>`, key result) — so the question was posed with borrowing in it from the start. **The exceptionalist case, at full strength, as its defenders make it:** the polis put power in the middle (`<Ref greek-miracle>`); Weber's "only in the West" — rational science, law, bureaucracy and capitalism, and a religious ethic that made acquisition a duty (`<Ref weber-only-in-the-west>`: `protestant-ethic`, `rationalization`); Landes's divided Europe, where no ruler could stop an invention and exit forced rulers to bargain — set against Zheng He's recalled fleets and the Ottoman print ban (`<Ref landes-european-exceptionalism>`: `european-fragmentation`, `invention-of-invention`); Charles Murray's count of significant figures, concentrated in Europe (`<Ref murray-human-accomplishment>`: `accomplishment-inventory`); Henrich's Church that broke the clans and left individuals free to form voluntary associations — towns, guilds, universities (`<Ref henrich-church-and-kinship>`: `marriage-and-family-program`); and the reply that ends the borrowing argument on its own terms: everyone borrowed, but the West turned what it borrowed into self-sustaining systems — numerals into the calculus and proof from measurement, powder into the military revolution, print into the Reformation and the scientific journal (`<Ref the-borrowing-ledger>`: `borrowing-into-system`). **The chain-of-exchange thesis, at full strength, as its authors make it:** in 1750 Jiangnan and England resembled each other in markets, labour and consumption; the gap opened after 1800 and owed much to coal located near industry and to the New World's land (`<Ref pomeranz-and-goody-the-rival-thesis>`: `great-divergence`); the institutions Europe claimed as its own — the city, the market, even love — had parallels across Eurasia, and leadership alternated rather than stayed put (`theft-of-history`); the West is one link in a long chain of exchange. Neither is phrased as a reproach of the other or of the West; each is stated as its authors would state it. **The evidence both sides accept — graded, never flat** (`<Ref the-borrowing-ledger>`: `graded-transmission`): paper reached Europe through the Arab world by a documented chain (secure); gunpowder most likely through Mongol-era contact, with no document of the transfer, and Europeans plausibly unaware of its origin (probable, unproven); Gutenberg's press most probably an independent invention; numerals from India through Baghdad to Fibonacci (secure, slow to spread). **The third family** — why some peoples rather than others — taught in the heredity module as argument and evidence: heredity (`<Ref hereditarian-case-iq-and-nations>`), geography (Diamond's axes and domesticable species) and institutions (Nogales; the two Koreas) (`<Ref geography-or-institutions>`), each with the datasets audited to the same standard (`<Ref national-data-audited>`). **Sorting.** Neither of the two answers is refuted, and the ledger shows why they talk past each other: they explain different things. The exceptionalist explains what was *done with* techniques (systems, institutions, accomplishment; a lead the defenders date long before 1800); the rival thesis explains *when the gap in wealth* opened and what fuelled it (after 1800; coal and colonies). Each concedes something the other needs: the rival thesis concedes the West's science and fragmentation as real; the exceptionalist concedes the borrowings and the late timing of the income gap. What would discriminate them is stated, not decided (exercise 5). The heredity question: standing and contested (as in section 3). Because: "why the West" is two questions — why the West produced philosophy, self-government and science, and why it became richer — and the two answers are strongest on different ones.

6. **What stays open.** The Böckenförde question (`<Ref murray-strange-death-of-europe>`: `bockenforde-dilemma`) — does the free secular state live on presuppositions it cannot itself produce? — with the module's answers side by side: Deneen's no (`<Ref deneen-post-liberalism>`); Hazony's "the nation supplies them" (`<Ref hazony-virtue-of-nationalism>`); Vermeule's "law ordered to the common good can" (`<Ref vermeule-common-good-constitutionalism>`); de Benoist's "only a people's own culture can, and liberalism cannot see peoples" (`<Ref de-benoist-european-new-right>`); Scruton's and Oakeshott's "not alone, but with" (`<Ref the-nation-and-its-defenders>`, `<Ref oakeshott-rationalism-in-politics>`); and the liberal reply — yes, if the rules hold and the right accepts them (from `<Ref deneen-post-liberalism>`'s objections). Hungary is the case none of them settled (`<Ref hungarys-fundamental-law>`: a constitution that writes the presuppositions into its preamble, adopted by one parliament's two-thirds — which each side reads as its proof). Then the course's own open findings, one sentence each: the Athenian thesis answered many times and refuted never; "natural" has meant four things, and the inequality question is re-posed, not settled; the "we" every theory of membership needs is the one thing none of them derives; "why the West" is two questions, and the two answers are strongest on different ones. **Strongest objection to the synthesis itself, stated at full strength:** that sorting answers into bins is the historicist move Strauss warned against — treating every answer as a product of its moment (`<Ref strauss-natural-right-and-history>`: `historicism`). Reply the lesson can give: the bins record what objections were made and met, not whether the answer is true; "standing" is not "true", and "abandoned" is not "false" — which is why the last exercise asks the reader to judge. The `<Conclusion>` says what the reader can now do (reconstruct any link of the four chains, state its strongest objection, place a live dispute on the course's map) and that the remaining judgement is theirs.

Headings fall out as claims: "An answer can be refuted, set aside or still standing — often two at once"; "Who should rule: the question moved four times"; "Is inequality natural: 'natural' changed meaning"; "What holds a people together: every theory needs a 'we' it cannot derive"; "Why the West: two answers to two questions"; "What stays open".

## Pitfalls / subtleties to flag

- **No new material.** Every sentence asserting something about a thinker carries a `<Ref>` to the lesson that established it; a claim with no lesson behind it is a required fix. If the ledger shows a lesson did not establish a link, drop the link and report it. Links most at risk (check the ledgers first): Schmitt's 1963 Foreword as a self-limitation (`schmitt-concept-of-the-political`); Spengler's "race does not migrate, languages do" (`spengler-decline-of-the-west`, `what-measures-greatness`); Carneades in Cicero (merged lesson); Hume as Madison's source (`federalist-one-people-and-the-extended-republic`); the 1911 head-form study (`chamberlain-and-grant`); the Islam–West partial exception (`testing-the-clash`).
- **No re-teaching.** Each Ref gets one clause of recall. The lesson's length comes from the number of links, not from their exposition: four chains at roughly 30–40 links each, a clause per link, is about 3,500–4,000 words — the 40-minute target. If the chains run longer, cut links that add no turn in the argument (a link stays only if it answers, objects or displaces); the full list lives in the figures.
- **Dropped slugs.** Never Ref `cicero-mixed-constitution`, `cicero-natural-law` (→ `cicero-res-publica-and-natural-law`; the mixed constitution and the cycle → `polybius-anacyclosis-and-rome`), `war-is-divine` (→ `maistre-and-bonald`), `nietzsche-on-the-right` (→ `the-right-after-1945` for the reception, `ressentiment-and-envy` for the slave revolt). The Federalist is an in-course lesson now: no cross-course Ref.
- **Register.** The course passes no verdict of its own on any author or idea: no condemning or approving labels in its own voice. Judgement comes only through argument and evidence; a label may appear only inside a quotation, with who applied it and when. The bins are argued from the objections the course itself carried; where the course left a question open (Rousseau vs Burke; the envy charge vs Rawls; the hereditarian gap; exceptionalism vs exchange; the Böckenförde question), the synthesis says "open" and does not close it. "Refuted" is used only in the defined sense of plan step 1 and always with the objection that did it; no word in the course's voice prejudges a bin before the argument has put the answer there.
- **"Refuted" must be earned and symmetrical.** The temptation in a synthesis is to refute the right's answers and leave the left's "standing", or the reverse. Check the balance: the contract and consent carry their unmet objection (Filmer, Hume, "which people?") as plainly as natural slavery carries Aristotle's concession; Rousseau's artificial inequality is left standing *beside* Burke, not over him; the hereditarian hypothesis and its critics are both "standing and contested"; Marx's prediction and Spengler's forecast are treated by the same standard (a forecast tested against what followed).
- **Why the West: both theses at full strength, neither as a reproach.** Do not phrase the rival thesis as an accusation ("the West stole") nor the exceptionalist case as a boast; the concept names (`theft-of-history`) are the author's title, introduced as such in their lesson. State every borrowing with its grade — never "Europe took China's inventions" as one line. The exceptionalist reply ("everyone borrowed; the West built systems") is given in full, as is the rival thesis's answer to it (late timing; coal and colonies).
- **Contested empirical claims stay contested** (course tone, outcome `co-contested`): the hereditarian hypothesis, the sources of Western divergence, replacement, the colonial balance sheet. Each is placed in its bin as "standing and contested" with the lesson that holds its evidence and the evidence against.
- **The spine is never quoted** (L10a), and the lesson never talks about the course ("this course has shown…"). The chains are narrated as the argument's own history.
- **Nietzsche is a Ref into the other course**, never re-exposed. The course's own reception of him is in `the-right-after-1945` (`aristocratic-radicalism`) and `ressentiment-and-envy` (the slave revolt by Ref).
- **Extra lessons** (`platos-laws-second-best-city` is the only one): cite as "see also", never as the ground of a core claim (C2).
- **Terms**: none new. Every term is a concept id already introduced; the definition in concepts.yaml is the recall clause. The three bins (refuted / set aside / standing) are the lesson's only vocabulary of its own; define them once in section 1.
- **Dossier**: nothing to obtain. The one check is that every `<Ref>` points at the lesson that actually established the claim — the validator catches a bad slug, not a wrong lesson. Several lessons were briefed concurrently with this one (`hazony-virtue-of-nationalism`, `vermeule-common-good-constitutionalism`, `de-benoist-european-new-right`, `hungarys-fundamental-law`, `federalist-one-people-and-the-extended-republic`): read their briefs and ledgers for the exact claims before writing sections 2, 4 and 6.

## EXPLANATION PLAN (L0)

Start from the opening verbatim: twenty-four centuries of answers to three questions have gone past; the lesson lays them out as the chain of moves it was and asks which answers were refuted, which abandoned without refutation, and which still stand in 2015. The newcomer — who by now is not one — meets it as a sorting task with three bins and a method: an answer is *refuted* when the next thinker's objection is one it cannot meet on its own terms; *abandoned* when the question itself moved (a war of religion, a revolution, a census) and nobody answered the old answer; *standing* when it appears, under a new name, in the arguments of 2015. The lesson runs the three questions in the order the course's intro gives them, each as a chain, and then the three halts, and then what stays open.

1. **Who should rule — the chain.** Opens from the first lesson's fact: the question became askable only where kingship broke and power stood "in the middle" (`<Ref greek-miracle>`: `es-meson`). Then each link as answer → objection → next answer. *Thrasymachus*: whoever is stronger, and "justice" is his interest (`<Ref thrasymachus-and-the-ring>`) → the objection is Plato's whole construction → *Plato*: the one who knows (`<Ref kallipolis-and-the-philosopher-king>`: `philosopher-king`); his own second thought, law with preambles (`<Ref platos-laws-second-best-city>`) → *Aristotle's* objection "all say mine" and the happiness of the guardians, and his answer: law and the middle (`<Ref aristotle-regimes-and-the-middle>`: `rule-of-law`, `polity`, `wisdom-of-the-multitude`) → Plato's standing objection that a mixture is no regime (Laws IV, Ref there) → *Cicero*: the mixed people bound by agreed right (`<Ref cicero-mixed-constitution>`: `res-publica`, `mixed-constitution`) → *Carneades'* objection (justice is convention) and Laelius's natural law (`<Ref cicero-natural-law>`) → *Augustine's* objection to all of them: no earthly city is just; rulers serve (`<Ref augustine-two-cities>`) → *Aquinas*: a law above kings, and no private tyrannicide (`<Ref aquinas-law-and-kingship>`) → *Machiavelli's* objection: measure by what works; the armed prophet; conflict as the source of liberty (`<Ref machiavelli-effectual-truth>`, `<Ref machiavelli-discourses-republic>`) → the wars of religion move the question from "who" to "what is a state" → *Bodin, Hobbes*: one sovereign bound by no law, derived from fear, authorised by all (`<Ref bodin-sovereignty>`, `<Ref hobbes-state-of-nature-and-covenant>`, `<Ref hobbes-sovereign-and-liberty>`); tested on 1642–60 (`<Ref english-revolution-as-test>`) → *Locke's* objection: a sovereign judge in his own cause is still in the state of nature; consent and the right of resistance (`<Ref locke-property-and-consent>`) → *Hume's and Burke's* objection to consent (no one consented; `<Ref burke-reflections>`) → *Rousseau*: the general will (`<Ref rousseau-inequality-and-general-will>`) → *Constant's, Hegel's and Maistre's* objections (ancient vs modern liberty; the general will as caprice; no nation gives itself a constitution — `<Ref maistre-and-bonald>`) → *Burke*: inheritance and the natural aristocracy → *Paine's and Strauss's* objections (`<Ref strauss-natural-right-and-history>`: prescription as the road to Hegel) → *Hegel*: the rational state (`<Ref hegel-ethical-life-and-the-state>`) → *Marx's* inversion (`<Ref marx-manifesto>`) → democracy arrives as a fact and the question changes again to "what will it do to men" (`<Ref tocqueville-tyranny-of-majority-and-soft-despotism>`, `<Ref carlyle-heroes>`, `<Ref mill-liberty-and-its-limits>`), tested on the franchise (`<Ref british-franchise>`) → *Schmitt*: whoever decides the exception; friend and enemy (`<Ref schmitt-political-theology>`, `<Ref schmitt-concept-of-the-political>`), tested on Weimar (`<Ref article-48-weimar>`) → the liberal and Ortega's objection (rule is opinion, not force; `<Ref ortega-revolt-of-the-masses>`) → *Oakeshott*: the umpire, a disposition not a doctrine (`<Ref oakeshott-rationalism-in-politics>`) → *Deneen's* objection: the umpire state is already liberal (`<Ref deneen-post-liberalism>`) → *Scruton*: authority before freedom (`<Ref scruton-meaning-of-conservatism>`). Sorting: Thrasymachus never refuted, only answered (he returns as Carneades, Machiavelli, Schmitt); the philosopher king abandoned by Plato himself; the mixed constitution refuted by Bodin on indivisibility and still standing in every constitution with separated powers (`<Ref udhr-read-against-the-right>` and the us-history Federalist by Ref if the course allows cross-course Refs — otherwise `<Ref cicero-mixed-constitution>`); sovereignty standing, now in the hands of whoever decides (25 August 2015 — `<Ref the-2015-crisis-read-three-ways>`); consent standing and unmet by its objection (no one consented; everyone acts as if they did). Because: the chain shows the question's three displacements — from *who* (Greece) to *what is a state* (1576) to *what democracy does to men* (1835) to *who decides the exception* (1922) — each forced by an event the thinkers did not choose.
2. **Is equality natural — the chain.** *Aristotle*: hierarchy runs through all nature; some are slaves by nature — and nature "often fails" to mark them (`<Ref aristotle-political-animal-and-natural-slavery>`: `natural-slavery`, the internal difficulty) → *Cicero's* Laws I: the likeness of all men, one commonwealth of gods and men — beside the imperial justification of rule over the servile (`<Ref cicero-natural-law>`) → *Augustine*: slavery by sin, not nature (`<Ref augustine-two-cities>`) → *Vitoria*: the Indians have dominion; not natural slaves — and the title left open "for the sake of argument" (`<Ref vitoria-right-to-the-americas>`) → *Hobbes*: equality of ability (the weakest can kill the strongest), hence war, hence the sovereign (`<Ref hobbes-state-of-nature-and-covenant>`) → *Locke*: equality of jurisdiction, not of age, virtue or merit (`<Ref locke-property-and-consent>`) → *Rousseau*: inequality is artificial, born of amour-propre and "this is mine" (`<Ref rousseau-inequality-and-general-will>`) → *Burke's* objection: the natural aristocracy; a legislature of lawyers and curates is a departure from nature (`<Ref burke-reflections>`) → *Maistre*: no such thing as man (`<Ref maistre-and-bonald>`) → *Tocqueville*: equality of conditions is a providential fact to be guided, not resisted (`<Ref tocqueville-equality-and-the-township>`); its danger is the flock (`<Ref tocqueville-tyranny-of-majority-and-soft-despotism>`) → *Carlyle's* refusal: the hero, the aristocracy of talent (`<Ref carlyle-heroes>`) → *Mill*: individuality against assimilation, and the civilized-community clause that excludes barbarians (`<Ref mill-liberty-and-its-limits>`, `<Ref locke-and-mill-on-empire>`: `strategies-of-exclusion`) → *Marx*: class is the inequality that matters and history abolishes it (`<Ref marx-manifesto>`) → *Nietzsche* (Ref the other course): order of rank, the herd (`nietzsche/order-of-rank`, `nietzsche/herd-morality-in-modern-dress`; the course's own `<Ref nietzsche-on-the-right>`) → *Ortega*: nobility is obligation; the mass-man is a kind of man, not a class (`<Ref ortega-revolt-of-the-masses>`) → *Spengler, Toynbee* on race: "race does not migrate, languages do"; race refuted as a cause of civilization (`<Ref what-measures-greatness>`: the hereditarian account as an argument with history) → *Strauss*: return to nature as the standard, against historicism (`<Ref strauss-natural-right-and-history>`: `classic-natural-right`) → *Gilley and the balance sheet* (`<Ref colonial-balance-sheet>`) and *Waldron* (`<Ref inherited-guilt-and-reparations>`): whether inequality between peoples is owed rectification → *Carens*: birthright citizenship as feudal privilege (`<Ref carens-open-borders>`) → *Deneen*: a regime of equality breeds a liberalocracy (`<Ref deneen-post-liberalism>`). Sorting: natural slavery refuted *internally* (Aristotle's own concession) and by Vitoria, yet standing in Mill's tutelage under another name (the course's `strategies-of-exclusion` thesis — one clause); Hobbes's equality of ability never refuted — every later theory assumes it; Rousseau's "artificial" standing and Burke's "natural aristocracy" standing side by side, neither having met the other's objection (the course's honest finding: the question is not settled but *re-posed* each century); the hereditarian account of civilization refuted on the module's cases and standing as a claim with a history (`co-contested`). Because: the chain shows that "natural" changed meaning — from Aristotle's *telos* to Hobbes's *ability* to Tocqueville's *providential fact* — and that each change moved the argument without refuting the last.
3. **What holds a people together, and why it fails — the chain.** *Cicero*: agreement about right and shared advantage (`<Ref cicero-mixed-constitution>`: `populus`) → *Augustine's* objection: by that definition Rome was never a republic; a people is defined by the objects of its love; the earthly city's engine is the lust of ruling (`<Ref augustine-two-cities>`: `two-cities`, `libido-dominandi`) → *Machiavelli*: civil religion and the return to beginnings (`<Ref machiavelli-discourses-republic>`) → *Bodin, Hobbes*: the sovereign's protection in exchange for obedience (`<Ref hobbes-sovereign-and-liberty>`: `protection-and-obedience`) → *Locke*: a contract (`<Ref locke-property-and-consent>`) → *Rousseau*: the general will and a civil religion (`<Ref rousseau-inequality-and-general-will>`) → *Burke*: a partnership of the dead, the living and the unborn; prejudice as collected reason (`<Ref burke-reflections>`: `partnership-of-generations`, `prejudice`) → *Maistre*: religion and the executioner (`<Ref maistre-and-bonald>`: `executioner`) → *Hegel*: ethical life — the institutions one grows into (`<Ref hegel-ethical-life-and-the-state>`: `ethical-life`) → *Tocqueville*: mores and the art of association; failure as soft despotism (`<Ref tocqueville-equality-and-the-township>`, `<Ref tocqueville-tyranny-of-majority-and-soft-despotism>`) → *Carlyle*: reverence; failure as the cash-nexus (`<Ref carlyle-heroes>`) → *Mann*: Kultur (`<Ref mann-reflections>`) → *Ortega*: a project of common future, a daily plebiscite; failure as the mass-man's "rights without obligations" (`<Ref ortega-revolt-of-the-masses>`: `nation-as-project`) → *Schmitt*: a shared enemy; failure as depoliticisation (`<Ref schmitt-concept-of-the-political>`: `friend-enemy-distinction`) → *Spengler, Toynbee, Huntington*: failure as organism, as failed response, as fault line (`<Ref spengler-decline-of-the-west>`, `<Ref toynbee-challenge-and-response>`: `challenge-and-response`, `<Ref huntington-clash-of-civilizations>`; tested in `<Ref testing-the-clash>`) → *Voegelin*: a society exists by being represented; failure as Gnosticism (`<Ref constantine-and-representation>`, `<Ref voegelin-gnosticism>`) → *Scruton*: oikophilia, the pre-political "we" (`<Ref scruton-meaning-of-conservatism>`, `<Ref the-nation-and-its-defenders>`: `oikophilia`) → *Miller*: national identity as one strand with cooperation and citizenship (`<Ref the-nation-and-its-defenders>`, `<Ref miller-strangers-in-our-midst>`) → *Carens*: a contract anyone may sign (`<Ref carens-open-borders>`) → *Murray*: two concatenations; failure as tiredness (`<Ref murray-strange-death-of-europe>`: `two-concatenations`) → *Deneen*: anticulture; failure because of success (`<Ref deneen-post-liberalism>`: `post-liberalism`). Sorting: Cicero's definition refuted by Augustine and standing in Miller's "public culture"; the contract standing in Carens and never having met Burke's and Scruton's "which people?"; a shared god abandoned (not refuted) by the wars of religion and reasserted by Maistre, Voegelin and Murray as the thing whose loss the Böckenförde question names; the shared enemy standing in every fence of 2015 and refuted, by its own author's 1963 Foreword, as a theory of politics; decline as organism refuted by Toynbee and standing as a mood (Murray's "tiredness"). Because: the course's third question has had five kinds of answer — a god, a way of life, an enemy, a future, a contract — and 2015 was argued in all five at once.
4. **The three halts revisited.** Each in one section of ~200 words, as the place where a chain crossed into history and changed direction. (i) *Christianity's Greek remaking* (`<Ref hellenization-of-christianity>`, `<Ref constantine-and-representation>`, `<Ref altar-of-victory>`): the bond of a shared god was itself a Greek and Roman construction (Harnack's kernel and husk; Nietzsche's slave revolt by Ref; Spengler's pseudomorphosis) — so the "shared god" answer to question 3 was never simply given; it was made, and Symmachus and Ambrose fought over who represented it. (ii) *The West's judgment of its conquests* (`<Ref vitoria-right-to-the-americas>`, `<Ref locke-and-mill-on-empire>`, `<Ref burke-against-hastings>`, `<Ref colonial-balance-sheet>`, `<Ref inherited-guilt-and-reparations>`, `<Ref auditing-the-case-for-colonialism>`): question 2 applied between peoples — natural slavery rejected and reinstated as tutelage; a conservative prosecuting empire; the balance sheet and supersession as the two methods, neither able to observe its counterfactual. (iii) *Civilizations one or many* (`<Ref spengler-decline-of-the-west>` → `<Ref testing-the-clash>`): question 3 widened from the state to the civilization, three measures of greatness, and the hereditarian account refuted on the cases. Because: the halts are where the reader learned the course's method — a contested claim as an argument with history, evidence and objections — and the synthesis names them as such without re-teaching.
5. **What stays open.** The Böckenförde question (`<Ref murray-strange-death-of-europe>`: `bockenforde-dilemma`), stated once more in the course's words — does the free secular state live on presuppositions it cannot produce? — and the three answers the module gave side by side (`<Ref deneen-post-liberalism>`: no; Scruton's and Oakeshott's "not alone"; the liberal "yes, if the rules hold"), with 2015 as the case none of them settled (`<Ref the-2015-crisis-read-three-ways>`). Then the course's own three open findings: that Thrasymachus was answered many times and refuted never; that "natural" has meant three things and the equality question is re-posed, not settled; that the "we" every theory of membership needs is the one thing none of them can derive. No verdict. The `<Conclusion>` says what the reader can now do — reconstruct any of the chain's arguments, state its strongest objection, and place a live dispute on the spine — and that the last exercise is theirs.

## Set pieces and worked examples to recreate

1. **The three bins, worked on one case** (the method). Take the `mixed-constitution` and run it: proposed by Plato's Laws and Aristotle's polity, systematised by Polybius and Cicero, *refuted* by Bodin and Hobbes on indivisibility (`<Ref bodin-sovereignty>`, `<Ref hobbes-sovereign-and-liberty>`), tested in 1642–60 where the restored monarchy accepted limits the theory said it could not (`<Ref english-revolution-as-test>`), and *standing* in every separation of powers since. One paragraph showing that an answer can be in two bins at once — refuted as a theory of sovereignty, standing as a practice — which is the sorting the lesson asks for throughout.
2. **Thrasymachus's returns.** A short recurrence table, each row one clause and a Ref: Thrasymachus (justice is the stronger's interest) → Carneades (utility against justice; the plank) → Machiavelli (the effectual truth) → Hobbes (justice is keeping covenants, which need a sword) → Schmitt (the political is friend and enemy) → the 2015 fence. The point: the realist challenge was answered by construction each time and refuted by none. No quotation beyond the one the first lesson gave.
3. **"Natural" three ways.** Aristotle's *telos* (a thing's nature is what it is for), Hobbes's *ability* (what any man can do to any other), Tocqueville's *providential fact* (what history is doing regardless) — three short definitions, each with its lesson's Ref, then the observation that Rousseau and Burke argue past each other because one means the first and the other the third. The exercise material for question 2.
4. **Augustine's test of Cicero, replayed on Miller.** Cicero: a people is a multitude bound by agreement about right (`<Ref cicero-mixed-constitution>`); Augustine: by that test Rome was never a people; a people is defined by what it loves (`<Ref augustine-two-cities>`). Replay on Miller's "public culture" (`<Ref miller-strangers-in-our-midst>`): is a people that agrees on democracy and the rule of law, but loves nothing in common, a people on Cicero's test? on Augustine's? One paragraph; the question is left with the reader.
5. **Five answers to one week.** The week of 25–31 August 2015 (`<Ref the-2015-crisis-read-three-ways>`) read in the five vocabularies of question 3 in turn — a contract anyone may sign (Carens); a way of life with a right to decide who joins (Scruton, Miller); an enemy at the fence (Schmitt via Orbán); a shared future (Ortega — "we can do this" as a plebiscite on a programme); a god whose loss leaves the state without presuppositions (Murray, Böckenförde). One sentence each; the point is that all five were spoken that week by someone.
6. **The three halts as three turns of one method.** A single paragraph for each halt stating the contested claim (Christianity's kernel; the colonial balance; the unity of civilization), the evidence the course brought (Harnack's lectures; the three re-checked citations; the dyadic conflict counts), and the objection that stood — then the method's name (an argument with history, evidence and objections) as the thing the halts taught.

## Pitfalls / subtleties to flag

- **No new material.** Every sentence that asserts something about a thinker carries a `<Ref>` to the lesson that established it; a claim with no lesson behind it is a required fix. If the ledger (`research/taught/`) shows a lesson did not establish a link the plan assumes, drop the link and report it.
- **No re-teaching.** Each Ref gets one clause of recall (L11d) — "Hobbes's equality of ability (the weakest can kill the strongest)" — and no more. The lesson's length comes from the number of links, not from their exposition; a chain of forty links at a clause each is ~2,500 words, which is the target.
- **No verdict** (course tone). The sorting into refuted / abandoned / standing is argued from the objections the course itself carried; where the course left a question open (Rousseau vs Burke on the artificiality of inequality; Deneen vs the liberal reply; the Böckenförde question), the synthesis says "open" and does not close it. The final exercise asks the reader for their position and its strongest objection; the solution offers "possible answers" (voita: for open-ended exercises the label is possible answers, not the answer) in the form of three model paragraphs from three positions, none endorsed.
- **The spine is never quoted** (L10a) and the lesson never talks about the course ("this course has shown…"). The chains are narrated as the argument's own history: "Plato answered Thrasymachus with a city; Aristotle objected that…".
- **Nietzsche is a Ref into the other course**, never re-exposed; the course's own `nietzsche-on-the-right` is `type: extra`, so no non-extra claim may depend on it (C2) — cite it as "for the reception, see…" only.
- **Cross-course Refs**: the Federalist is in the us-history course; if the component permits a cross-course Ref use `us-history/federalist-papers`; otherwise point the separated-powers claim at `cicero-mixed-constitution` and the UDHR lesson.
- **The three halts are revisited, not re-run**: ~200 words each; the temptation is to re-argue the hellenization thesis or the balance sheet. Resist; name the claim, the evidence, the objection, the method.
- **Terms**: none new. Every term is a concept id already introduced; the lesson may use concepts.yaml's one-line definitions as the recall clause.
- **Figures must be large and few** (4–6), each carrying one of the three chains or the grid; this is the one lesson where a figure may hold twenty labels, provided each label is a concept id and the colour binding is literal (amber = the answer, blue = the objection, a third hue only for "standing in 2015").
- **Where the plan may exceed what earlier lessons established**: the "strategies of exclusion" reading of Mill's tutelage as natural slavery under another name is the course's thesis (`locke-and-mill-on-empire`, from Mehta — References there); Schmitt's 1963 Foreword as self-refutation (`schmitt-concept-of-the-political` notes); Spengler's "race does not migrate" (`what-measures-greatness`). Check the ledgers for each before asserting.
- **Dossier**: nothing to obtain. The one check: that every `<Ref>` slug exists in course.yaml (the validator will catch a bad slug, but a wrong *lesson* for a claim will not be caught — read the ledgers).

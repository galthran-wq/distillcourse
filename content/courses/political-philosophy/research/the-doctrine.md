## Register against plan (where the register governs)

The plan's postulate grades were written before round 2 closed. The register as it now stands differs in these places; the lesson uses the register's grade and says nothing of the difference (the lesson never talks about its own making). Report to the author:

1. **P6 "Power grows unless it is checked" (plan: established for growth; on balance as a law).** The register (`jouvenel-on-power`) grades growth across the democratising era **established**, but the law of near-uninterrupted growth **fails, on balance** after 1990 (Canada, New Zealand, the Netherlands cut spending shares for a decade without war), and appetite against voters' demand is **open**. So P6 may be stated only as a *tendency* with the checks named in advance (elections, budgets, courts, removability) — graded **on balance** — never as a law. "Unless it is checked" must name what counts as a check before the record is read, or it becomes the kind of clause no outcome can refute, which earns no credit (the Carlyle/Spengler rule).
2. **P10 "Religion persists as a bond" (plan: strong).** The register supports two narrower findings: a commonwealth was held by a public worship and both sides acted on that (**strong**, `altar-of-victory`), and Muslim-origin Europeans did not secularize across the second generation while Christian-origin Europeans converged (**strong**, `murray-strange-death-of-europe`). The second finding cuts both ways: the *majority* religion of Western Europe weakened (Christians converge; chapel attendance collapsed in Britain [secondary]). P10 must be stated as "religion is a bond that has held commonwealths and that persists across generations where it is held; where it has weakened, nothing has been shown to take its place" — **strong** for that wording, and the weakening of the majority's religion is stated as part of the evidence, since it is the evidence Habermas's "may dry up" points to.
3. **P15(a) "Without a common authority, power decides between peoples" (plan: strong).** The register's grade for the Melos verdict is **on balance** (`thucydides-power-and-justice`): the thesis holds as a description of cities at war, and its second half — that fairness protects no one — fails on the record of 404. `vitoria-right-to-the-americas` upholds the description and fails "the right of the stronger" as a theory of right (**strong**). No register grade states the description half at strong on its own. P15(a) is graded **on balance**, and stated without the "fairness is worthless" half. P15(b), the republican peace, stays **strong** (`testing-the-clash`).
4. **P17 "science and self-government from c. 1400" (plan).** The register dates the lead in recorded *science* from c. 1400 (**strong**, `murray-human-accomplishment`), the lead in *wealth* from c. 1300 in Italy (**on balance**), and the lead in *political thought and philosophy* to the Greeks (**on balance**, `greek-miracle`, which judged why it arose there; that it arose there is not disputed). It grades no claim dating *self-government* to c. 1400. The lesson dates self-government by the verdicts that hold it — the limited crown after 1689 (`english-revolution-as-test`, strong), the wide franchise that did not end liberty (`british-franchise`, strong) — and does not date it to 1400. The round-2 verdicts on module 12 enter P17: no continuous Western lead from the Greeks (**strong** against the author's "almost always ahead"), Europe's trailing c. 1000 a shock after Rome, not a deficiency (**on balance**), and Clark's genetic candidate among the five open causes, its England-specific form failing **on balance**.

### Opening (verbatim from course.yaml `opening`)

### 0. How the doctrine is built (heading: "A doctrine is a set of premises that can each be overturned, and of consequences no firmer than their weakest premise") — ~300 words

Problem: a position on who may join a country, who rules it and what it owes is usually stated as a set of conclusions; nobody can tell which premise each conclusion needs, or what would make its holder give it up. The obvious attempt — list the conclusions with their reasons — fails because the same reason supports one conclusion strongly and another weakly, and a list hides which. The answer: state the premises separately, grade each, and derive each conclusion from named premises, so that a conclusion is visibly no firmer than the weakest premise it uses, and overturning a premise shows exactly which conclusions fall.

State, in plain words:
- **What counts as a ground:** a verdict reached against the record (a forecast checked against what followed, a claim checked against the evidence), or an argument that survived its strongest objection. Popularity and survival are not grounds (`<Ref the-argument-assembled>`: the method, one clause).
- **The grades** (one sentence each, by the scale `the-argument-assembled` defined): established, strong, on balance, open; and `argued` — a premise about what is good, which evidence can inform and cannot decide. Two postulates are of this kind, and the lesson says so where they stand.
- **Every postulate can be overturned**, and each says by what.
- **The background aims no side disputes**: peace is better than war; offices should go to the capable (Aristotle, Plato, Mosca and the meritocratic liberals agree — "common ground, not a dispute", `<Ref aristotle-political-animal-and-natural-slavery>`). They are stated once so the theses that use them do not hide them.

### I. Postulates

Each postulate is a `<Postulate grade grounds overturnedBy>`; under it, in prose: the plain-word explanation (one or two sentences, for the reader who has not read the course), the grounds as Refs with one clause each, the strongest objection in one or two sentences at full strength, and the reply. Where a postulate has two halves of different grades, the `grade` prop carries the weaker and the prose states each.

Four `##` sections, each opening with a seam tying it to the running question (what a free state can take as given):

**I.1 Human nature and rule (heading: "People are unequal in ability and equal in standing, social by nature, and always ruled by a few")** — P1–P6.

- **P1. People are unequal in ability, partly by birth, and equal in standing under law.** Grade **strong**. Plain words: people differ in the abilities that matter for work and for leadership, and part of the difference is inherited; nothing in that difference makes one person the owner or the natural master of another. Grounds: capacity to lead differs, partly by birth (**strong**) and measured ability predicts adult outcomes within families (**strong**) (`<Ref aristotle-political-animal-and-natural-slavery>`, `<Ref the-cognitive-elite>`); every argument from unequal ability to ownership failed — natural slavery by Aristotle's own concessions (**established**), "freedom among equals, slavery for outsiders" (**strong**, the author's review objection, acknowledged here in one sentence: the Greeks enslaved Greeks and the principle runs both ways), slavery as a "positive good" (**strong**, `<Ref locke-and-mill-on-empire>`). Objection: equal standing is itself a value, not a finding. Reply: the half that is a finding is negative — no criterion that picks out the natural subordinate has survived — and that is all the postulate claims; the positive value is P16's. Overturned by: a criterion, stated in advance, that separates natural subordinates from free men and does not reduce to "whoever was captured" or "whoever was born where".
- **P2. People are social by nature, and the state lowers violence; there was never a peaceful solitary stage.** Grade **strong** (the solitary stage fails, **established**; the state lowers lethal violence, **strong**) (`<Ref rousseau-inequality-and-general-will>`; `<Ref hobbes-state-of-nature-and-covenant>` for the claim). Objection: chiefdoms and early states were also violent, and states make wars. Reply: the comparison is in rates of violent death per head, and states are lower. Overturned by: a consistent finding that states have higher rates of violent death than non-state societies.
- **P3. Envy is a real political motive, though it decides no question of justice.** Grade **on balance** (the motive: the weakest of three behind support for redistribution, about a third to a half of compassion's weight; that it decides nothing: **strong**) (`<Ref ressentiment-and-envy>`). The author's review objection — the demand for redistribution is ressentiment, so refuse it — is acknowledged: the same transfers were proposed without egalitarian motive (Bismarck's insurance; Charles Murray's grant), and a motive neither refutes nor proves a cause (**strong**). Overturned by: replication failures of the envy effect in representative samples (for the motive half); none for the logical half.
- **P4. A few always rule; what matters is how they are selected and checked.** Grade **on balance** (that an organised minority holds office: **strong** as a tendency, not an iron law; that selection by competition and removability keeps it capable: **on balance**) (`<Ref mosca-ruling-class>`: `political-formula`; `<Ref michels-and-pareto-oligarchy-and-elites>`, both verdicts). Objection: two parties alternating inside one organisation for decades show the few can be many. Reply: the alternation is between organised minorities, which is P4's second half at work. Overturned by: closed, hereditary or co-opting rulers who kept their capacity across generations.
- **P5. Every order needs a settled last word; undecided sovereignty means war; the last word can be held by a composite body bound by law.** Grade **strong** (`<Ref english-revolution-as-test>`: 1642 and 1689; `<Ref bodin-sovereignty>`, `<Ref hobbes-sovereign-and-liberty>` for the claim). Objection (Hobbes's best): king-in-parliament is one sovereign, so the theory stands. Reply: the post-1689 constitution kept the divisions his chapter XXIX calls fatal. Overturned by: showing the limits of 1660 and 1689 were nominal.
- **P6. Power tends to grow unless it is checked by bodies it does not control.** Grade **on balance** (growth across the democratising era **established**; as a law **fails, on balance**; appetite or demand **open**) (`<Ref jouvenel-on-power>`). The checks are named before the record is read — elections that can remove, courts that can annul, a budget voted by others — and the evidence for the tendency is where a check was removed: Weimar's decree regime with no end set (`<Ref article-48-weimar>`, on balance), a majority overriding its court (`<Ref hungarys-fundamental-law>`, Fourth Amendment, strong), a theory whose only check is the good faith of the checked (`<Ref vermeule-common-good-constitutionalism>`, on balance). Objection: the post-1990 cuts show power can shrink. Reply: they came through the named checks (elected governments, voted budgets), which is the postulate's condition, not its exception. Overturned by: power that stayed limited where the named checks were absent.

Bearing of I.1: rule is by a few, needs a last word, and grows unless checked from outside; what is left open is how a people that must be ruled stays a people — I.2.

**I.2 Belonging (heading: "Belonging is received before it is chosen, and a free state lives on it without producing it")** — P7–P10.

- **P7. Belonging is inherited before it is chosen; the contract fails as an account of origins.** Grade **strong** (`<Ref barres-and-maurras-integral-nationalism>` (a): membership received; `<Ref hume-against-the-original-contract>`: origin and tacit consent fail; `<Ref filmer-patriarcha>`: no one consents to being born into a polity). Objection: inherited belonging licenses inherited rank or exclusion. Reply: those steps do not follow (**strong**, Barrès (b)); P7 says how belonging begins, not who may join later — that is T3/T4. Overturned by: a body of states founded and kept by consent individuals actually gave.
- **P8. A free state lives on a solidarity its procedures do not produce.** Grade **on balance**. Plain words: freedom needs citizens willing to bear burdens — taxes, service, losing elections — for strangers they count as "us", and no voting rule or court produces that willingness. Böckenförde's sentence quoted once (Sources). Grounds: `<Ref hungarys-fundamental-law>` (premise, on balance; Habermas grants the motivational half); `<Ref altar-of-victory>` (Rome held by a public worship, strong); `<Ref murray-strange-death-of-europe>` (integration "by itself" failed for the non-EU second generation, strong). Objection (Habermas): the state can justify itself from its own resources. Reply: P8 claims the motivational dependence, which he grants. Overturned by: a bound cross-national series showing trust and willingness to fund transfers to strangers do not fall where shared identity or religion weakens (not yet bound — the reason for "on balance").
- **P9. Culture persists across generations; it is not carried by blood and not acquired on demand.** Grade **strong** (`<Ref national-data-audited>`: culture persists, strong; regimes and freedom not explained mainly by genes — the same people free and unfree within a lifetime, Taiwan, the two Germanies, the Koreas — strong; `<Ref gobineau-inequality-of-races>`, `<Ref chamberlain-and-grant>`, `<Ref reich-ancient-dna-and-the-indo-europeans>`: the racial account, established failure; `<Ref murray-strange-death-of-europe>`: integration by itself failed, strong). The author's review objection — peoples live as they want, and culture is made by genes — is acknowledged: the value differences are conceded as cultural and persistent; "genetic" fails on Taiwan and on the time-scale (coevolution over millennia, regimes changed in years) (`<Ref national-data-audited>`). Overturned by: genetic divergence on relevant traits within the periods in which regimes changed; or values that change within a generation on demand.
- **P10. Religion is a bond that has held commonwealths and persists across generations where it is held.** Grade **strong** for that wording (Register against plan, 2). Grounds: `<Ref altar-of-victory>` (both sides of 384 acted as if the commonwealth turned on its worship, strong); `<Ref murray-strange-death-of-europe>` (Muslim-origin Europeans kept their religion into the second generation, Christian-origin Europeans converged on secular practice, strong). Objection: Western Europe's majority secularized, so religion is not a lasting bond. Reply: conceded as fact — and it is the case P8 is about: the majority's bond weakened and nothing has been shown to take its place (Habermas's "may dry up"). Overturned by: the third generation of Muslim-origin Europeans converging as Christians did (the register says nothing of the third generation yet).

Bearing of I.2: a free state draws on a received, slow-moving culture and faith it cannot manufacture; what is left open is whether such orders decline by fate — I.3.

**I.3 Change, economy and power between peoples (heading: "Societies decline through causes that can be named, grow rich under property and law, and meet each other as powers")** — P11–P15.

- **P11. Redesigning a whole society at one stroke is costly; designed institutions can last.** Grade **on balance** (Burke's general argument on balance, with war finance as rival cause; Maistre's "nothing deliberated lasts" fails, **strong** — the American constitution, the departments, the metric system) (`<Ref assignats>`; `<Ref what-conservatism-is>` for the narrower pointer: caution scales with the size of a change). Overturned by: a whole-society redesign at one stroke that paid no heavy price where war is absent.
- **P12. Civilizations decline through mechanisms, not by fate, and decline can be reversed.** Grade **strong**. Every law of decline tested failed — Plato's chain (`<Ref decline-of-regimes>`), the cycle (`<Ref polybius-anacyclosis-and-rome>`), Spengler's organism (`<Ref spengler-decline-of-the-west>`), Toynbee's challenge (`<Ref toynbee-challenge-and-response>`), Gobineau's mixture (established) — while named mechanisms held (rivalry for office in Rome, strong; a parliament that would not govern, strong). Objection: decline is merely slow. Reply: a decline that may take any time and any form forecasts nothing (the fellah device). Overturned by: a law of decline stated in advance that predicts a dated fall.
- **P13. Material progress is real but not a law of history.** Grade **strong** for the first half (Condorcet's longer lives, literacy, abolition held, `<Ref voegelin-gnosticism>`); the second half is not a claim the record can test — "indefinite" perfectibility and "in the long run" earn no credit on either side (`<Ref huntington-clash-of-civilizations>`: Fukuyama's spread of democracy failed from 2006, strong). Overturned by: a sustained world fall in life expectancy and literacy.
- **P14. Property and markets under law produce wealth; planning failed.** Grade **established** for the comparison (the two Koreas, the two Germanies, China after 1978, India after 1991; `<Ref burnham-managers-and-the-suicide-of-the-west>`, forecast check). The author's review objection — every country that adopts capitalism becomes uniformly richer — is answered by the limit P14 carries: necessary, not sufficient (Argentina, level with Canada in 1913, at a third of US output per head in 2022), with secure property and an unbiased law deciding (`<Ref geography-or-institutions>`: institutions alone can produce gaps as large as any between nations, established). Overturned by: a planned economy at parity with a matched market economy over decades.
- **P15. (a) Without a common authority, power decides between peoples; (b) among free republics, peace has held.** Grade **on balance** (a: the Melos thesis as description of cities at war, `<Ref thucydides-power-and-justice>`, on balance — the claim that fairness protects no one failed in 404; b: no war between established democracies or EU members since 1950, `<Ref testing-the-clash>`, strong). Objection to (a) (the author's "the right of the stronger"): if power decides, power gives right. Reply: as description upheld, as a theory of right it gives every successful rebel and every unopposed invader the same title (strong, `<Ref vitoria-right-to-the-americas>`). Overturned by: (a) a period of stable restraint among unequal powers with no common authority and no balance; (b) a war between two established democracies.

Bearing of I.3: these are the facts a doctrine of a free state must work within; what they cannot supply is why freedom should be kept — I.4.

**I.4 Values (heading: "Two premises are argued, not measured: freedom among citizens, and the worth of the West's achievement")** — P16–P17. ~550 words. The section says openly, in its first paragraph, why these two are graded `argued`: evidence can show what freedom and an inheritance cost and yield; it cannot show that the yield should be preferred to what others would choose instead. A premise of value is defended by showing that its rivals need something they cannot supply.

- **P16. Freedom among citizens is a good worth keeping.** Grade `argued`. The argument, in three steps, each grounded:
  1. Every alternative to freedom among citizens needs someone entitled to decide for others what their good is.
  2. No criterion for picking that someone, stated in advance, survived its test: rule by knowledge had no case in the record (`<Ref sparta-as-test-case>`); the hero cannot be told from the quack before the outcome (`<Ref carlyle-heroes>`, strong); "the wise minority" is the premise in dispute (`<Ref stephen-liberty-equality-fraternity>`, strong); the decider had no criterion that kept him commissarial (`<Ref article-48-weimar>`); a common-good authority has no check but its own good faith (`<Ref vermeule-common-good-constitutionalism>`).
  3. So freedom among citizens is the arrangement that does not rest on an unestablished premise; and where it was kept it did not cost what its critics forecast — the wide franchise did not end liberty or stability (`<Ref british-franchise>`, strong), the civil core became justiciable (`<Ref udhr-read-against-the-right>`, strong), a constitutional order was voted out in 2026 (`<Ref hungarys-fundamental-law>`, strong).
  Strongest objection (the post-liberal, `<Ref deneen-post-liberalism>`): freedom is not the highest good, and unbounded it dissolves the goods it lives on. Reply: the doctrine concedes the second half as P8 and T2 — freedom does not found itself — and refuses the first because step 2 holds against every named alternative. One clause across: `nietzsche/death-of-god` names the loss beneath the objection. What would overturn it: not evidence but an argument — a criterion, fixed in advance, for who may decide others' good that survives its own test. The Jouvenel question whether the "protected subject" is less free is the open value question inside P16 (`<Ref jouvenel-on-power>` (d)).
- **P17. The West's achievement is real and worth conserving.** Grade `argued`; the factual half graded by parts: philosophy and political theory arose with the Greeks (`<Ref greek-miracle>`); the lead in recorded science after c. 1400 is overwhelmingly European and not an artefact of sources (**strong**, `<Ref murray-human-accomplishment>`); the lead in wealth opened in late-medieval Italy and became decisive in north-western Europe (**on balance**, `<Ref the-borrowing-ledger>`); self-government as the limited crown and the wide franchise (strong, `<Ref english-revolution-as-test>`, `<Ref british-franchise>`). The cause is **open** (fragmentation, kinship, a religious ethic, the high-medieval inheritance, genetic selection — the last's England-specific form failing on balance). The author's review objection — the West was almost always ahead by a large margin — is acknowledged: the shock after Rome and the fast recovery are conceded; "almost always ahead" fails (**strong**) on the Near East before the Greeks, on Han China beside Rome, and on the Abbasids and the Song c. 1000. The value argument: an achievement whose cause is open cannot be reproduced on purpose (P11, P9), so it is kept by conserving the institutions and culture it arose with — and what Europe borrowed (paper, numerals) it built into lasting systems, which is the part worth keeping. Objection (the rival thesis, `<Ref pomeranz-and-goody-the-rival-thesis>`): the lead was late luck in a shared exchange. Reply: the broad late-luck view fails on timing (**strong**); but the borrowings were real, and P17 claims no ancient or racial priority. What would overturn the factual half: a documented list of omitted non-European firsts after 1400 at a scale that changes the shares.

Bearing of I.4: with the value premises stated, the theses can be derived; each "should" in them now has a named source.

### II. Theses (heading: "Nine theses follow, each no firmer than the weakest premise it uses") — ~1,300 words

One `##`. Each thesis is a `<Thesis from="…" grade="…">`; under it, in prose: the plain-word statement, then **the step** — two to four sentences showing how the premises yield it (premise → premise → conclusion, with the value premise named where a "may" or "should" appears), then the grade and its weakest premise. The `from` prop is plain text (no Ref): postulates by number, verdicts by short name with module number, e.g. `from="P7, P8, P9, P16; Verdicts: Miller over Carens (m15), 2015 procedure (m15)"`. Refs go in the prose.

- **T1. A mixed, limited regime: a settled last word held under law, and an elite that can be removed.** `from="P1, P4, P5, P6, P16; common ground: office to the capable; Verdicts: 1642 and 1689 (m3), capacity with removability (m1, m7), Weimar (m7), the Fourth Amendment (m16)"`. Step: a last word is needed (P5); a few will hold it (P4); they stay capable only where office is contested and removable (P4, P1, the common-ground aim); left unchecked, their power grows (P6); and no one is entitled to rule others without their consent (P16) — so: a last word held by a composite body bound by law, with the few removable by the many. Grade **on balance**; the "settled last word under law" part **strong**.
- **T2. Liberal freedom does not found itself: it lives on a nation, an elite held under law, an inherited culture it cannot produce on demand, and an economy of property and markets.** `from="P4, P7, P8, P9, P14, P16; Verdict: the course thesis (the-argument-assembled)"`. Step: freedom is worth keeping (P16); it depends on a solidarity it does not produce (P8), which is received (P7) and carried by a culture that cannot be acquired on demand (P9); its rulers must be held by law (P4); its wealth rests on property and markets under law (P14) — so keeping freedom means keeping these. "On demand" is the whole of what is claimed: whether a free state can build a nation over generations is hard case 3 (open). Grade **on balance** (P8). Ref `<Ref the-argument-assembled>` for the clause-by-clause argument; do not repeat it.
- **T3. A people may decide who joins and how many, and select by capacity to integrate — within duties of rescue, fair terms, and full inclusion of those it admits.** `from="P7, P8, P9, P16; Verdicts: Miller over Carens (m15), 2015 procedure and integration (m15), Carens's movers (m15), the fiscal checks (m15)"`. Step: the bond a free state lives on is received and slow (P7, P9) and cannot be produced on demand (P8); a people that governs itself (P16) may therefore govern its own membership; equal moral worth grounds a right to *some* adequate place, not to *this* one (Miller over Carens, on balance); integration does not follow by itself (strong), selected labour migration is a net gain and asylum and family migration in high-welfare states are not (strong) — so selection by capacity to integrate. The limits are the verdict's own. Grade **on balance**.
- **T4. Membership is cultural, not racial: open in principle, slow, demanded rather than assumed.** `from="P1, P9; Verdicts: the racial account fails (m6, m13), Barrès's step to private rights (m6), regimes not by genes (m13)"`. Step: what holds a people is culture (P9), not blood (the racial nation fails, established; regimes not mainly by genes, strong); equal standing (P1) forbids status by descent; culture is acquired, but slowly and not on demand (P9) — so membership is open to anyone who takes on the culture, takes time, and must be required rather than presumed. The step is unaffected by the open genetic share of group gaps: group means do not determine an individual (`<Ref heritability-within-and-between-groups>`: `within-between-fallacy`; `<Ref gould-and-harden-on-heredity>` (a): facts do not settle policy, strong). Grade **strong**.
- **T5. A market economy with a guaranteed minimum, not redistribution aimed at equal outcomes.** `from="P1, P3, P6, P14; Verdicts: the floor as common ground (m10), the merit case (m10), maximin (m10)"`. Step: markets under law produce wealth (P14); abilities differ (P1), so equal outcomes require continuous correction by an authority whose power grows (P6), and strict end-state patterns fall to the merit case (strong); the floor is what every side accepts, impartial choosers and the merit side included (on balance); envy is real but decides nothing (P3) — so P3 can be cited neither for nor against redistribution, and the thesis does not rest on it (say so: P3 is idle in the derivation; exercise 2 makes the reader find this). Between the floor and Rawls's difference principle the question is **open**. Grade **on balance**.
- **T6. A free state may favour in public life the religion and culture it historically lives on, while protecting religious liberty.** `from="P8, P9, P10, P16; Verdicts: no neutral civic state (m16), the civil core (m9), Rome's worship (m2)"`. Step: every state chooses a language, holidays, a public history and naturalisation terms (strong, `<Ref hazony-virtue-of-nationalism>` (b)); the solidarity it lives on is carried by a culture and faith that persist (P8, P9, P10); so the choice it must make anyway is made for what it lives on; freedom (P16) and the justiciable civil core (strong) bound it — favour, never compulsion. Whether writing the culture into the supreme law produces cohesion is **open** (Hungary). Grade **on balance**.
- **T7. No inherited guilt; liability only through institutions that persist; repair owed to people now alive.** `from="P7; Verdicts: inherited guilt (m14 (a)–(d))"`. Step: guilt attaches to a will, so it does not pass (strong); belonging and institutions are inherited (P7), so liabilities pass through estates, corporations and states that persist (strong); old wrongs cannot be rectified by counterfactual (strong); wrongs to people now alive are owed repair (strong) — e.g. the federal exclusion of Black neighbourhoods from mortgage insurance from the 1930s. The thesis takes no side on the general colonial ledger (open, `<Ref auditing-the-case-for-colonialism>`) and on the author's "every colony did better under colonial rule" (fails as uniform, strong): neither is needed. Grade **strong**.
- **T8. Between peoples, interest and power where no common authority exists; cooperation among free states.** `from="P5, P15; Verdicts: Melos (m1), republican peace (m11), right of the stronger (m14), the EU charge (m16)"`. Step: without a settled last word, power decides (P5 applied between peoples; P15a); among free republics peace has held (P15b) — so a free state arms and bargains where there is no common authority, and binds itself by treaty to free states where it can, keeping exit (the EU as rule without consent or exit fails, strong; as compulsory jurisdiction holds, on balance). Power gives no right (Vitoria, strong). Grade **on balance**.
- **T9. The radical alternatives are excluded: the decider, the racial nation, the conservative revolution, conquest as a right, ownership of people.** `from="P1, P4, P6, P11, P15; Verdicts: the decider (m7, m8), the racial nation (m6, m13), Moeller (m8), the unjust titles (m14), natural slavery and the positive-good defence (m1, m14)"`. Step, one clause per item: the decider has no check but himself (P4, P6; on balance); the racial nation contradicts P1 and P9 (established); the conservative revolution is redesign at one stroke without a programme (P11; strong for Moeller, on balance for the module); conquest as a right turns power into right (P15; strong); ownership of people contradicts P1 (established; positive-good defence fails, strong). The constitution written by one majority is not in the list (its cohesion is open); its majority overriding its court is excluded by T1. Grade **on balance** overall.

Bearing: the theses are the doctrine; what follows is what they let a state do.

### Conclusion (`<Conclusion>`, ~150 words)

In plain words: the doctrine is seventeen premises, two of them value premises argued, the rest graded on the record; nine theses each derived from named premises; the position they compose — a free state, limited, ruled by a removable few under law, living on a nation and an inherited culture it cannot produce on demand, controlling its membership within duties of rescue, open to anyone who takes on its culture and closed to status by descent, with a market and a floor, and no inherited guilt. Two theses are strong and seven on balance, each of the seven resting on at least one on-balance premise; P8 is the one whose deciding evidence is named and not yet gathered. What the reader can now do: take any proposal, find the thesis it falls under, trace it to its postulates, and say what would overturn it. The optional continuation: the Nietzsche course (`nietzsche/nietzsche-and-politics`). Nothing in this course comes next.

## Pitfalls / subtleties to flag

- **No new material, no re-teaching, no re-assembly.** Every factual claim is a `<Ref>` with one clause. The per-question assembly and the thesis argued clause by clause belong to `the-argument-assembled`; Ref it, never re-run it.
- **Grades are the register's** (see the VERDICT table and "Register against plan"). A postulate with two halves takes the weaker in its `grade` prop. A thesis is graded by its weakest premise, and the prose never puts it more firmly (L17).
- **Every "may" and "should" has a named value premise** (P16, P17, or a section-0 background aim). A thesis that turns a fact into a permission without one is a derivation gap.
- **The limits the verdicts carry stay attached**: T3's duties of rescue, fair terms and inclusion; T7's repair owed to the living; P6 as tendency with named checks; P14 necessary, not sufficient; P15(a) without "fairness protects no one"; P10 with the majority's secularization stated.
- **2015 as the module verdict states it**: per-capita rarity beside the share of the toll; failure located in institutions, not a group; suspect statistics unadjusted, adjustment open; the integration figures on the one measure (IAB 17/2025).
- **The racial right and the post-liberals are judged as arguments**, at full strength in their best form, with no labels in the lesson's voice (course tone: judge arguments, not people).
- **No talk about the course** (L10a): not "the course has shown"; verdicts are findings about the record ("the record of 1689 refutes…"). The plan's phrase "readable by someone who has not read the course" is a design constraint, never a sentence in the lesson.
- **Register:** dry, exact, non-pompous. No rhetoric, no rallying, no "we must". Every sentence states, grounds or qualifies. The doctrine is persuasive only as an inference.
- **Components** (PR #195, not yet on this branch): `<Postulate grade="strong" grounds="…" overturnedBy="…">`, `<Thesis from="P3, P4; Verdicts …" grade="on balance">`, `<Verdict grade decidedBy overturnedBy>`. Props are plain text — no math, links or `<Ref>`; Refs go in the prose beneath. Postulates and theses are numbered through the lesson (P1–P17, T1–T9) and must match the plan's numbering. `grade` on a Postulate is one of the four grades or `argued`; on a Verdict, the four only.
- **Author's review objections that bear on the doctrine** — each acknowledged in one sentence where it bears, answered by Ref, never re-staged:
  - "Freedom among equals, slavery for outsiders" → P1, T9 (`aristotle-political-animal-and-natural-slavery`: historical premise fails, established; principle fails, strong).
  - Marx's doctrine is ressentiment / the demand for redistribution is ressentiment → P3, T5 (`marx-manifesto`, `ressentiment-and-envy`: a motive decides nothing, strong).
  - Every country that adopts capitalism becomes uniformly richer → P14, T5 (`burnham-managers-and-the-suicide-of-the-west`: necessary, not sufficient).

## EXERCISE PLAN (E1–E7; 7 exercises)

1. *Derive a thesis (parallel to set piece 1, L7)*: Given P5 and P15 only, derive T8 step by step; state where a value premise enters (peace over war, section 0) and grade the thesis by its weakest premise. Solution: P5 applied between peoples gives "where no common authority exists, power decides"; P15(b) gives the exception among free republics; the grade is on balance, from P15(a) (`<Ref thucydides-power-and-justice>`); "power gives no right" needs the Vitoria verdict, which is not among the given premises — so the derived thesis is descriptive, and the permission half needs the extra ground.
2. *Find the weakest premise — and the idle one*: For T5, list its premises with grades, name the weakest and the one that does no work. Solution: the floor as common ground is the weakest load-bearing ground (on balance); P3 is idle — envy decides nothing, so it can be cited neither for nor against redistribution; removing it leaves T5's derivation intact. Then the same for T6 (weakest: P8).
3. *Apply the allows/forbids — a naturalisation applicant*: A woman born in Morocco, resident ten years, fluent in the language, employed, Muslim, no record, wears a headscarf, applies for citizenship. Which theses decide, and what may the state require and refuse? Solution: T4 (cultural, not racial; strong) and T3 (conditions: language, law, loyalty); religion as such is not a ground for refusal (hard case 1, open; T6 protects religious liberty); her record meets the integration criterion on its own measure; refusal on origin or faith is forbidden (T4, P1).
4. *Apply — an ideological ban*: An association preaches that the constitution is void and law must be replaced by religious law; another is a congregation of the same faith that preaches no such thing. Which may be banned, under which thesis? Solution: the first falls under T1 and T3 (hostility to the constitutional order; on balance), with the 1932 criterion that a constitution may deny the equal chance to those who would use legality to abolish it (`<Ref the-right-in-1933>`) as the precedent the doctrine shares; the second may not (T6, religious liberty; hard case 1 open on religion as such). State the grade and what would move it.
5. *Apply — a reparations claim*: (a) Descendants of people enslaved before 1865 claim reparation from the state; (b) people now living who were refused mortgage insurance in the 1960s because of their neighbourhood claim against the same state. Solution: (a) as a claim of guilt, T7 refuses it — guilt does not pass (strong); as a claim of liability against a state that has persisted since 1865, T7 does not exclude it in principle (liability passes through persisting institutions, strong), but a remedy measured by what the claimants' position would have been without the wrong cannot be computed (indeterminacy, non-identity, choice of baseline: strong), and supersession is possible, not automatic — so the claim fails in its counterfactual form and the doctrine says what form could succeed (a wrong whose effects run, through a persisting institution, to people now alive); (b) owed repair (strong) — T7's third clause; the doctrine forbids refusing it.
6. *Spaced retrieval across the course (E3)*: For P5, P7 and P12, name the event or text that grounds each (1642/1689; Barrès and Hume; Spengler's parliaments and Polybius's forecast), the lesson that judged it, and its grade — one clause and a Ref each.
7. *Attack the doctrine at P8 (claim–evidence–reasoning; last; E7)*: P8 is named in the task as the on-balance postulate whose deciding evidence is named and not yet gathered (as written, the lesson's attack from the other side is MacIntyre's alone, since the post-liberals target P16 and T1); attack it at full strength from whichever side you think strongest, say which theses fall if the attack succeeds, and name the evidence that would overturn it. Solution (labelled "possible answers"): P8 (on balance; under T2, T3, T6). Liberal attack: Habermas plus the EU-second-generation result plus the Third Republic — a free state can produce its solidarity over generations; succeeds if a bound diversity-and-trust series shows no fall and a bound Third Republic account shows a nation built under free institutions; then T2's "on demand" stands but T3's case for selection weakens and T6 loses its ground. Post-liberal attack: MacIntyre plus Deneen — the loyalty cannot outlast the lost account of the good, so P8 understates the problem; succeeds if a cohesion series shows liberal institutions eroding the supplement faster than any free state restores it; then P16 is under pressure and T2's "keep" with it. Each answered at the register's grade; neither preferred over the evidence. The solution ends with the forward clause: the reader has reached `nietzsche/nietzsche-and-politics`, where the most radical answer to the question beneath P16 is read (optional continuation).

## Module 9 — postwar-conservatism: After fascism, do the rebuilt right's critiques of abstract rights hold against the postwar order's founding text, the 1948 Declaration?

**Module verdict** (in `udhr-read-against-the-right`). Grade: on balance.
The critiques hit two things and miss one. They hit the missing foundation: the drafters chose to
leave it out, so Strauss's "list without a ground" describes the text accurately. They hit the
social claims of Arts. 22–27: Kirk's test asks who bears the correlative duty, and for these
articles nobody is named. Art. 22 itself says they depend on "the organization and resources of
each State". The critiques miss the civil and procedural core, Arts. 3–21. Those articles are
Burke's "real rights" and Oakeshott's "availability of a procedure", written down with the
tribunals that must enforce them (Arts. 8, 10), and they answer Burke, Oakeshott and Jouvenel in
the critics' own terms. The decisive evidence is what happened after 1948. The Declaration split
along the critics' line. The civil core became justiciable: the European Convention of 1950 has a
court, and the 1966 ICCPR has an individual-complaint procedure. The social claims became the
ICESCR's obligation of "progressive realization" (Art. 2(1)), an aim rather than a claim on a named
party. Kirk's test is also wrong on one of its own examples: the right to marry is a liberty, not
a claim on a woman (brief, link 6). So the conservative critique is right about the kind of thing
the social rights are, and wrong that abstract rights as such cannot be made real.
- Strongest objection to this verdict: social rights have since been made justiciable in several
  constitutions (e.g. South Africa 1996), so the split may be a political choice rather than a
  difference of kind. This does not overturn the verdict, because those courts enforce them only
  as far as "available resources" allow, which is the point the critics made. It would be
  overturned by evidence that social claims are routinely enforced against a named duty-bearer
  regardless of resources.

### Lesson verdicts
- `the-right-after-1945`: the charge of continuity, that fascism descends from the conservative
  tradition (Maistre, Burke) → **fails at the level of argument, holds at the level of words and
  persons**. Grade: on balance. Decided by: prescription and epistemic modesty (Burke) are the
  opposite of will, myth and the remade man, so fascism contradicts the tradition's core
  arguments. But the Conservative Revolution's vocabulary ("Third Reich", Volk, the Worker) and
  its men (Schmitt and Heidegger joined the party in 1933) cross over directly, and the renaming
  of 1950 was a relabelling. The Burke side of the charge has no text in the dossier at all
  (brief, Pitfalls). Would be overturned by: documentary evidence that fascist ideologues took
  their arguments, not just their words, from Maistre or Burke. For Maistre the evidence is real
  but disputed (his violence and sacrifice passages); for Burke there is none in the course.
- `jouvenel-on-power`: Power has grown in extent almost without interruption, fastest under
  regimes that claim to give power to the people → **holds as description; the objection ("it was
  demanded") confirms the mechanism rather than refuting it, and leaves the normative charge
  open**. Grade: strong (description); open (whether the growth is a loss of freedom). Decided by:
  conscription, income tax and public spending as a share of output all rose across the
  democratizing era (spending in industrial countries from roughly a tenth of output in 1870 to
  over 40 per cent by the 1990s [UNVERIFIED figures; Tanzi & Schuknecht 2000]). Jouvenel's own
  mechanism, Power allied with the common man against the intermediate authorities, predicts that
  the growth is demanded. So "the people asked for it" is his thesis, not a reply to it. What stays
  open is whether a "protected subject" is less free than the subject of the old intermediate
  powers. That depends on whose freedom the aristocracies and corporations protected, a value
  question the lesson should leave to argument. Would be overturned by: a sustained democratic
  period in which the state's extent shrank without external shock.
- `strauss-natural-right-and-history`: the two modern grounds for rejecting natural right
  (radical historicism; Weber's fact–value distinction as a proof that values are beyond reason)
  refute themselves → **holds against radical historicism; does not establish natural right**.
  Grade: strong. Decided by: "all thought is the product of its time" is itself offered as a
  trans-historical truth, and Strauss concedes the rest himself ("a wish is not a fact"; the
  teleology dilemma unsolved). The same self-exemption test settles the question carried from
  `oakeshott-rationalism-in-politics`. "The only standard is the tradition" is not a standard any
  tradition supplies, so Oakeshott's disposition needs a standard above tradition, or it exempts
  itself (brief, exercise 4). Would be overturned by: a formulation of historicism that does not
  claim trans-historical validity for itself and still rules out natural right. Moderate
  historicism (thought is conditioned, not determined) escapes the argument but no longer rules
  natural right out.
- `voegelin-gnosticism`: modern mass movements are one phenomenon, Gnostic re-divinization
  descending in one line from Joachim → **fails as a genealogy, holds as an observation that
  totalitarian movements borrowed eschatological symbols**. Grade: on balance. Decided by: (a)
  the vagueness test: a category that takes in Hegel, the Puritans, Comte, Marx, Hitler and
  progressives has no discriminating use unless something fails it; (b) the author's own 1974
  recantation of "the monomaniacal desire to force the operations of the spirit in history on the
  one line that will unequivocally lead into the speculator's present". The borrowing of symbols
  (the Third Reich's three-age schema; a classless end of history) is documented and survives.
  Would be overturned by: a showing that the transmission from Joachim to the modern movements is
  textual and causal, not analogical.
- `burnham-managers-and-the-suicide-of-the-west`: (1941) the managers will replace owners as the
  ruling class, and the managerial powers will win → **the war forecasts failed; the description
  of the managed corporation and the administrative state held**. Grade: established. (1964)
  Liberalism is the ideology of Western suicide → **fails on the test Burnham himself set**.
  Grade: on balance. Decided by: Burnham said that if the contraction stopped and reversed,
  liberalism "will fade away". Between 1989 and 1991 the Soviet bloc collapsed, much of eastern
  Europe joined Western institutions, and liberal states led the coalition that won; liberalism did
  not fade. The former imperial states also grew richer after empire than under it, so the
  contraction was real only on Burnham's chosen measure, territory ruled. Would be overturned by:
  the heirs' reading carried through. That would need evidence that the Cold War outcome came
  against the liberal governing class rather than through it, or a renewed Western contraction
  that this class's ideology demonstrably caused (the case `murray-strange-death-of-europe`
  argues later).

### No verdict
- `what-conservatism-is`: a concept lesson that maps four definitions; its one decidable point
  (Broad's missing premise that unforeseen effects are "more probably bad than good") is better
  carried as a pointer: the epistemic argument supports caution scaled to the size of a change,
  not a general presumption against change.
- `oakeshott-rationalism-in-politics`: reconstruction; its self-application problem is decided
  in `strauss-natural-right-and-history`.
- `kirk-and-nisbet-american-conservatism`: founding reconstruction; whether the market dissolves
  the communities it is meant to protect is carried to `deneen-post-liberalism`.

## Module 10 — equality-after-rawls: Does unequal natural talent make unequal outcomes unjust, and is the demand for equality principle or envy?

**Module verdict** (in `the-cognitive-elite`). Grade: on balance.
The factual premise everyone in the module needs is true. In the NLSY data, a test score taken
in youth predicts adult outcomes more steeply than the parental-SES index does, and sibling
comparisons, which hold the whole family constant, leave the estimates nearly unchanged
(Korenman & Winship). So the natural lottery is a real force in the distribution of life
chances. It is neither a hypothesis (Rawls) nor a legitimating story (Lasch's "merit"). The data
choose no normative conclusion; each of the three readings adds a premise the data do not supply
(brief, link 6). On the normative question, the module's arguments decide this much. The
difference principle is not what the original position yields (see `rawls-theory-of-justice`).
What impartial choosers endorse is a guaranteed floor. The envy charge cannot defeat a floor or
any other principle (see `ressentiment-and-envy`). So the defensible conclusion is a social
minimum secured against the natural lottery; strict maximin over the worst-off is not
established. The book's own authors arrive at income supplements and "valued places", so the
floor is common ground between them and the Rawlsian reading.
- Strongest objection to this verdict: the AFQT absorbs schooling and the SES index is crude, so
  "talent" is partly background. This does not overturn it: the sibling fixed-effects results
  hold family background constant and the AFQT effect survives. Korenman and Winship also argue
  that background matters at least as much as IQ, which the lesson should state. The verdict would
  be overturned by within-family designs in which the score's effect disappeared.

### Lesson verdicts
- `rawls-theory-of-justice`: rational parties behind the veil would choose the difference
  principle (maximin) → **fails as a derivation**. Grade: on balance. Decided by: the decision
  theorist's objection the brief already stages with Rawls's own gain-and-loss table: maximin is
  rational only on Rawls's special assumptions. The experimental evidence points the same way. In
  Frohlich, Oppenheimer & Eavey's veil-of-ignorance experiments every group reached consensus,
  and no group chose maximizing the floor. The usual choice was maximizing the average subject to a
  floor. The equal-liberties principle and the case for some guaranteed minimum survive; the
  specific difference principle does not. Would be overturned by: an argument that the original
  position's conditions (no probabilities at all, little concern for gains above the minimum)
  are the right model of impartiality, not just one possible model. That is Rawls's own reply, and
  it makes the conclusion depend on a stipulation rather than on rational choice.
- `ressentiment-and-envy`: egalitarian principles are envy institutionalised (Scheler, Schoeck)
  → **fails as an argument against any principle; holds as a claim that envy is one real motive
  behind support for redistribution**. Grade: strong (the logical half); on balance (the empirical
  half). Decided by: both sides concede the logical point. Schoeck says a motive "neither refutes
  nor proves" a cause, and Rawls's parties are stipulated non-envious, so envy supplies no
  premise of the theory. On the empirical side, a four-country study (US, UK, India, Israel) finds
  dispositional envy predicts support for redistribution, alongside compassion and self-interest;
  measured fairness did not predict it (Sznycer et al. 2017). That supports Schoeck's empirical
  claim about motives. It does nothing to show that a principle chosen behind the veil is unjust.
  Would be overturned by: (empirical half) replications that find no envy effect once compassion
  and self-interest are controlled. A later study reports that a fairness motive also contributes,
  which qualifies the finding but does not remove envy from it [UNVERIFIED].

### No verdict
- `scruton-meaning-of-conservatism`: reconstruction of a reply; the internal quarrel (authority
  vs disposition) is carried to `the-argument-assembled`.
- `macintyre-after-virtue`: a historical diagnosis whose decisive question (can a tradition
  defeat its rivals without neutral standards) the brief rightly leaves open; carried to
  `deneen-post-liberalism`.
- `lasch-revolt-of-the-elites`: its empirical claim is tested in `the-cognitive-elite`.

## Module 11 — civilizations: Are civilizations the units whose borders organise conflict — and does Huntington's clash, the one testable theory of the three, survive the record since 1993?

**Module verdict** (in `testing-the-clash`). Grade: strong.
The clash hypothesis fails as a prediction about the frequency of conflict and about alignment.
It survives only as a narrower claim about the Islam–West pairing and about the first phase of
kin-country rallying. The published dyadic tests find conflict within civilizations at least as
common as conflict across them, and they find no post-1989 shift toward the cross-civilizational
kind. Bosnia, Huntington's first case, ran against his own criterion that alignments would follow
civilizational lines "increasingly … as the conflict continued". From the 1994 Washington
Agreement onward, Western power was used for a Muslim-led government against Orthodox Serbs. The
corollary he stated as a test fails on his own example: violence between "two Slavic, primarily
Orthodox peoples" should be low, and Russia–Ukraine ended in annexation (2014) and the largest
European interstate war since 1945 (2022). Civilizations remain real as units of identity; as
the battle lines of the future, the record refutes them.
- Strongest objection to this verdict: the tests measure interstate dispute frequency, while
  Huntington claimed that cross-line conflicts would be the most dangerous and the most likely to
  escalate. This only partly answers the verdict. It retreats to a claim the tests did not measure
  and that he did not specify, and it does not touch the two failures on his own cases. It would be
  overturned by evidence that cross-civilizational conflicts since 1993 have been systematically
  more intense and more prone to spread than intra-civilizational ones.

### Lesson verdicts
- `toynbee-challenge-and-response`: (a) civilizations arise and grow by a creative minority's
  response to a challenge of the right severity, a law drawn from 21 cases → **fails as a law**.
  Grade: strong. Decided by: the golden mean makes the theory unfalsifiable. Any failure can be
  recoded as a challenge too hard or too easy, and the cases are selected by the theory they
  illustrate; Toynbee concedes the attempt is "at our own peril". The scheme survives as a
  vocabulary, not a mechanism. (b) Toynbee's refutation of the racial theory of civilization in
  Gobineau's form (all civilizations from "the impulse of the white races") → **holds**. Grade:
  established. Decided by: his own list, with civilizations arising independently among peoples
  Gobineau's scheme cannot credit (Sinic, Andean, Mayan), which meets Gobineau's own pledge that
  one counter-instance falsifies "the whole theory". This verdict reaches Gobineau's claim only.
  The modern statistical form of the question belongs to module 13 and is not decided here. Would
  be overturned by: (a) a statement of the law that names in advance which challenges are too
  severe; (b) nothing in the present record.

### No verdict
- `huntington-clash-of-civilizations`: reconstruction; the test is the module's closing lesson.
- `what-measures-greatness`: a synthesis of three measures. Which measure is right is a choice
  of what to count, not a dispute the module can settle; its testable part is handed to
  `testing-the-clash` and `murray-human-accomplishment`.

### Sources the briefs must add
- `testing-the-clash`: the brief already names Russett, Oneal & Cox (2000), Chiozza (2002) and

## Module 12 — why-the-west: Why did the West pull ahead — an old, unique inheritance, or late luck inside a shared Eurasian exchange?

**Module verdict** (in `the-borrowing-ledger`). Grade: strong that the scientific lead is
early-modern; on balance that the economic lead is too; open on cause.
Neither pure answer survives. The "late luck" thesis fails on timing for China as a whole:
re-measured national accounts put north-western Europe ahead before coal and colonies, and China's
output per head was falling through the Qing. Whether the Yangzi delta, its richest region, was
still level in 1750 is contested (by the 1820s Dutch output per head was nearly twice the delta's). Europe's lead in recorded
science opens from about 1400 (Murray's count). The "ancient, unique inheritance" thesis fails
too. Europe around 1000–1400 was poorer and technically behind Song and Ming China, by Landes's
own account. It borrowed heavily: the ledger grades paper and numerals as secure transfers and
gunpowder as probable. The divergence is early-modern, and what Europe did with borrowings (the
mechanised paper mill, corned powder, print that no one could suppress) is where the difference
lies. Which cause produced that difference is open. Fragmentation (Landes), kinship
(Henrich) and a religious ethic (Weber) are each partly supported and none is decisive.
Evidence that would settle it: within-Europe designs that separate the timing of kin-dissolution,
of state competition and of literacy, and show which one predicts where innovation was kept.
- Strongest objection to this verdict: Pomeranz's reply that wage series compare unlike workers
  and miss women's textile earnings. This narrows the gap; it does not overturn the verdict,
  because the national-accounts estimates do not rest on wage series. It would be overturned by
  output-per-head estimates showing the Yangzi delta level with England or Holland in 1750 on a
  like-for-like basis.

### Lesson verdicts
- `weber-only-in-the-west`: ascetic Protestantism produced the spirit of modern capitalism →
  **fails as the main cause; the description of Western rationalization stands**. Grade: on
  balance. Decided by: capital-accounting enterprise predates the Reformation (Italian banking,
  Pacioli's double entry, 1494). In 272 German cities, 1300–1900, Protestantism had no measurable
  effect on city growth (Cantoni 2015). The Protestant advantage found in Prussian data runs
  through literacy, not a work ethic (Becker & Woessmann 2009). Protestantism mattered, but
  through Bible-reading schooling rather than predestination anxiety. Would be overturned by:
  evidence that Calvinist conduct, not literacy, explains Protestant economic performance where
  both can be separated.
- `murray-human-accomplishment`: accomplishment in the sciences after 1400 is overwhelmingly
  European, and the finding is not an artefact of Eurocentric sources → **holds for the
  sciences; weaker for the arts; says nothing about causes**. Grade: strong (sciences); on balance
  (arts). Decided by: the sciences rest on checkable "firsts". The most carefully corrected source,
  edited with specialists in non-Western science, gives the same proportions. Murray's challenge
  (any even rule that admits a dozen non-Europeans admits hundreds of Europeans) has not been met
  in the dossier. For the arts there is no test outside expert consensus, as Murray concedes. His
  causal account (Christianity, transcendental goods) has no mechanism. Would be overturned by: a
  documented list of non-European firsts the inventories omit, at a scale that changes the shares.
- `pomeranz-and-goody-the-rival-thesis`: (Pomeranz) the most advanced regions of China and Europe
  were alike c. 1750, and the gap opened after 1800 from coal and the New World → **fails for
  China as a whole; contested for the richest regions**. Grade: on balance. Decided by: Broadberry,
  Guan & Li (2018, restated 2021) date the divergence between China as a whole and north-western
  Europe to the Qing, before 1800. They allow that parts of China may still have matched the
  richest parts of Europe at 1750, which is Pomeranz's own regional claim, and Li & van Zanden's
  near-twofold Dutch lead over the Yangzi delta is measured in the 1820s, after his date, so it
  does not by itself refute parity in 1750. (Reviewer correction: the agent graded this "strong";
  the evidence it cites does not carry that grade.) Agricultural productivity there was close to Dutch and English levels,
  while the gap lay in industry and services. Pomeranz's windfall argument survives as an account of
  why the industrial take-off was British and so fast. It fails as an account of why the lead
  existed at all: Spain had the New World and China had coal. (Goody) Parallels across Eurasia show
  no categorical Western difference → **open**: it turns on comparing institutions by function
  rather than name, which the lesson cannot do for all fifteen parallels. Would be overturned by:
  (Pomeranz) like-for-like output estimates showing parity in 1750.

### No verdict
- `landes-european-exceptionalism`: fragmentation is tested within the lesson and is
  necessary-not-sufficient (fragmented India and Italy did not diverge); the cause is carried to
  the module verdict, where it stays open.
- `henrich-church-and-kinship`: the brief grades its own evidence: strongest for kinship ↔
  psychology, weakest for the links to science and industry. As a cause of the divergence it is
  carried to the module verdict (open).

## Balance check

These are the 13 verdicts (4 module, 9 lesson), each scored by which side it favours.

**Against right-leaning positions (6):**
- Voegelin's genealogy.
- Burnham's 1941 war forecasts and his 1964 suicide thesis.
- The envy charge as a refutation of egalitarian principles.
- Gobineau's racial theory of civilization (via Toynbee).
- Huntington's clash.
- Weber's Protestant-ethic causation, which is exceptionalist-coded rather than right-coded.

**For right-leaning positions (6):**
- The continuity charge fails at the level of argument.
- Jouvenel's growth of Power.
- Strauss's self-refutation of historicism.
- Envy as a measured motive.
- The reality of cognitive stratification, with sibling-robust IQ effects.
- Murray's count, for the sciences.

**Against left-leaning positions (4):**
- Rawls's derivation of maximin.
- Pomeranz's late dating.
- The "late luck" half of the module 12 verdict.
- The social-rights half of the UDHR verdict, where the critics' line was borne out by the 1966
  split.

**Mixed (3):** the right-after-1945 verdict (persons and words), the UDHR module verdict, and the
module 12 verdict, which rejects both pure answers.

The tally is roughly even, and no verdict was chosen to balance it. The two verdicts I am least
sure of:
- **The Rawls verdict.** It leans on lab experiments with real people, while Rawls argues about
  idealized parties. I graded it "on balance" for that reason, not "strong". A defender can fairly
  say that the experiments test a different question.
- **The module 10 verdict.** Its normative conclusion (a floor, not the difference principle) is an
  argued position, not a finding. It should be read as the best-supported synthesis of the
  module's arguments.

I deliberately did not upgrade the cognitive-elite verdict into anything about group differences;
that is module 13's question. Nothing here was shaded to soften a verdict against Murray,
Huntington or Burnham, or to soften one against Rawls or Pomeranz.

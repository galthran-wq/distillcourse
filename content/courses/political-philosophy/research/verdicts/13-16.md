## Module 13 — heredity-and-civilization: Are the measured gaps between peoples, within the US and between nations, caused by heredity, environment, geography or institutions, and in what proportion?

**Module verdict** (in `national-data-audited`). Grade: **open** on the genetic share; **established** on the two limits; **strong** on what the national tables can carry.
The first three premises of the hereditarian argument largely hold. The tests measure the same abilities in both US groups and predict outcomes without bias against the lower-scoring group. Measured intelligence is substantially heritable within Western populations. The US gap is real: about 0.75 SD in recent child norms, about 1 SD in adult norms. The fourth step, from a real gap to a genetic one, has not been made. No design has measured a genetic contribution free of confounding. Measured environment does not account for the whole gap either: parental education and income leave about 6 points of the child WISC gap. Two limits are established. The gap as it stands is not wholly genetic: it narrowed by several points within decades, varies from near zero to 1.5 SD across school districts, and widened in maths after 2020. And no evidence excludes a genetic share. Between nations, the association of measured skills with income survives better data. The lowest national-IQ entries do not survive audit: the sub-Saharan African levels are biased low by about ten points against the 2010 systematic review. Nothing in the national tables bears on whether the differences are genetic. Of the four explanations the module weighs, only institutions have a clean natural experiment behind them, the Koreas and Nogales (see `geography-or-institutions`). It shows that institutions can produce gaps as large as any between nations. It does not apportion the world's gaps.
- Strongest objection to this verdict: "open" is evasion, because the indirect evidence (heritability, invariance, Spearman's pattern, admixture correlations, PGS means, a 2020 expert mean of 49% genetic) all points one way. It does not overturn the verdict. The dossier shows that each line is logically uninformative about the between-group cause (heritability, invariance), confounded (admixture slopes, PGS), or not evidence at all (opinion that correlates with the respondent's politics and sex). The opposite objection, that the narrowing proves the gap environmental, fails for a matching reason: it shows that part of the gap is environmental, not all of it. What would settle it: a well-powered sibling-difference admixture design with observed skin colour and local ancestry, and within-family GWAS of cognition in African-ancestry samples, as stated in `admixture-and-polygenic-scores`. For nations: representative samples, invariance tested across countries, and national means tracked through development.

### Lesson verdicts
- `what-intelligence-tests-measure`: the claims that IQ tests are biased against Black Americans, or measure nothing outside the test room. Verdict: **both fail in the main US data**. The claim that g is a single capacity in the head is **open**. Grade: **strong**. Decided by: predictive validity of about .5, with no under-prediction for the lower-scoring group, and measurement invariance tenable (strict in the Philadelphia cohort and NLSY97) in the main US datasets. On reification: rotation hides g, but oblique factors bring a general factor back one level up, and Gould concedes that a "second-order general factor must therefore exist". Whether g is one ability, or abilities that reinforce one another, the correlations cannot decide. Would be overturned by: a representative US sample in which invariance fails, or in which the tests under-predict the lower-scoring group. The dossier notes partial non-invariance only in a referred special-education sample.
- `heritability-within-and-between-groups`: the inference "intelligence is highly heritable within groups, so the gap between them is probably genetic", including "equal heritabilities across groups show it". Verdict: **invalid**. Grade: **established**. Decided by: the 2024 proof (Schraiber & Edge) that within-group data cannot partition a between-group difference; the simulations in which an environment lowers one group's mean and leaves its heritability unchanged; and the seed-corn case, which both sides grant. Jensen's default hypothesis survives as a hypothesis, not as a default: it is the premise to be tested, and the argument cannot assume it. Would be overturned by: nothing within-group. The verdict is a logical result.
- `us-gap-size-trend-and-environment`: the hereditarian statements that the gap is fixed and has never narrowed, against the environmental statements that SES explains it or that it is closing now. Verdict: **both sets fail**. The gap narrowed by about 0.3–0.5 SD between cohorts born early in the century and those born around 1970, then stopped on most series. Measured SES accounts for roughly half to two-thirds of the child gap and leaves about 6 points. The gap as it stands is not wholly genetic. Grade: **established** for the limit; **strong** for the trend. Decided by: the cohort series (Dickens & Flynn, Murray 2007), the district variation, the movement after 2020, and Weiss & Saklofske 2020. The Flynn effect proves that environments can move scores by this much. It differs in structure from the gap, so it does not show that the gap is environmental. Would be overturned by: cohort series showing no narrowing at any time (none in the dossier do), or a measured environment set that removes the residual.
- `adoption-studies-across-groups`: the claims that the Minnesota study shows the gap surviving a good environment, and that Eyferth and Tizard show no ancestry effect. Verdict: **neither study decides the question**. Grade: **strong**. Decided by: placement history, age at placement and attrition travel with ancestry in Minnesota, whose own authors deny the hereditarian reading. The Eyferth and Tizard samples are small, their children were tested young, and the studies are confounded. The decisive design (adoption at birth, placement independent of ancestry, testing in adulthood) has never been run. Would be overturned by: a study with that design.
- `admixture-and-polygenic-scores`: the claims that direct genetic tests "show genes cause the gap", or "show no genetic difference". Verdict: **neither is shown**. Grade: **strong**. Decided by: admixture slopes are small and positive (under 1% of variance). They sum the genetic and the environmental effects of ancestry, colourism included. The one within-family estimate (Fuerst et al. 2023) is positive and not significant. Polygenic scores lose most of their accuracy outside European ancestry and carry family and population-structure effects. Bird's 2021 bound holds only under neutrality and the assumptions it states. Would be overturned by: the sibling-difference design named under the module verdict.
- `gould-and-harden-on-heredity`: (a) the claim that the factual answer settles the policy question, made on either side; (b) Gould's charge that Morton's skull measurements were bent by bias; (c) the 1920s use of the army tests to rank European nationalities. Verdicts: (a) **fails**. Gould, Harden and Murray accept overlapping facts and reach opposite policies, so the disagreement is in the value premises. One conclusion follows on any factual outcome: since the tests predict equally for both groups and the groups differ on them, treating individuals the same will not produce equal group outcomes, so an unequal outcome is not by itself proof of discrimination. Grade: **strong**. (b) **Gould's charge fails.** The 2011 re-measurement found Morton's measurements largely accurate. Whether Morton's ranking followed from them is a separate question, argued again in 2014. Grade: **strong**. (c) **Failed on its author's own retraction** (Brigham 1930). Grade: **established**. Would be overturned by: (a) a value premise that both sides share and that makes the policy depend on the group cause; (b) a third measurement that contradicts the 2011 one.
- `reich-ancient-dna-and-the-indo-europeans`: two older doctrines, old pure races (Gobineau, Chamberlain, Grant) and "pots, not people" (the postwar archaeological consensus), against the ancient-DNA record. Verdict: **both refuted**. Mass migrations replaced most of the earlier ancestry of whole regions, and every migrating people was itself a recent mixture. Grade: **established**. Reich's further inference, that average genetic differences between populations in cognitive traits will be found, is **open**: no current design can find them (`admixture-and-polygenic-scores`). Would be overturned by: nothing now in prospect for the first verdict. The second needs the designs named under the module verdict.
- `geography-or-institutions`: whether geography, institutions, culture or genes explain the gaps between nations. Verdict: **institutions can, by themselves, produce gaps as large as any between nations**. Grade: **established**. Decided by: the comparisons within one population, the two Koreas and Nogales, which hold geography, ancestry and most culture fixed. At world scale, how much each cause explains is **open**. Diamond's account explains Eurasia's lead in 1500 at the proximate level (**strong**). The reversal of fortune is claimed by both the institutional and the "people, not places" readings. The hereditarian account needs an institutional term on its own data: China and Japan fall far below their IQ prediction in 1820 and 1900, which Lynn and Vanhanen attribute to autocracy (**strong**). Would be overturned by: same-population comparisons in which different institutions leave incomes unchanged.
- `national-data-audited` (lesson verdicts beside the module verdict): (a) "sub-Saharan African IQ is about 70" stated as a measurement → **not supported**. Grade: **strong**. Decided by: Wicherts et al.'s inclusion criteria were fixed in advance; the N-weighted mean is 76.5 and the samples meeting the criteria average about 80–82. The reply's exclusions tracked each sample's own mean (biserial −.88). Even ~80 is a score on Western tests, with invariance untested. (b) "National IQ differences are artefacts" → **fails**. Grade: **strong**. Decided by: r = .88 with TIMSS mathematics, and harmonized learning outcomes for 164 countries. (c) The settler-mortality causal estimate → **open**. Albouy's recoding weakens the first stage and the authors' reply keeps the result; the institutional account's other evidence (Koreas, Nogales) does not depend on it. Would be overturned by: (a) representative African samples with invariance tested that come in near 70; (c) a re-estimate on own-territory rates only, published with the authors' agreement on coding.

## Module 14 — empire-and-guilt: By what right did Europeans rule other peoples, did their rule leave those peoples better or worse off, and does anything pass to the descendants of either side?

**Module verdict** (in `auditing-the-case-for-colonialism`). Grade: **strong** on title; **on balance** on the ledger; **strong** on inheritance.
(1) *Title.* Conquest of a people that already owned its land and governed itself had no title under any standard the course holds. Vitoria's natural law gives none, Locke's ch. XVI makes the unjust conqueror a robber, and Burke holds that arbitrary power "neither any man can hold nor any man can give". The behavioural "legitimacy" offered in its place does not show consent: compliance under the threat of punitive force is the requerimiento's void consent again. (2) *Ledger.* The Case for Colonialism's general thesis, that most episodes were a net benefit compared with a feasible counterfactual, is **not established**. Its counterfactual rests on never-colonised comparators (Ethiopia, Liberia, Haiti, Thailand) that are not comparable and are selected by survival. The study its own author concedes meets scientific standards (Heldring & Robinson) finds a negative effect on African development. The critics' picture of uniform harm is not established either. Real urban wages rose in every British colony studied, though East Africa stayed near subsistence for decades. Heights in Kenya did not break trend at colonization. The slave trades left a real, modest, lasting effect on trust, mostly before the Case's 1824 boundary. The measured effects differ with the kind of colony (settlement or extraction, West or East Africa), and the one cross-colony causal estimate rests on contested data (`national-data-audited`). (3) *Inheritance*: see `inherited-guilt-and-reparations`. Liability can pass and guilt cannot.
- Strongest objection to this verdict: "on balance" on the ledger lets the Case off, since the burden lies on whoever claims that alien rule was beneficial. The objection is right about the burden, and the verdict meets it: it says the claim of benefit is not established. It does not say the opposite claim is. It would be overturned by a design that compares colonised with uncolonised places under a credible counterfactual, for example a border or timing discontinuity, with results in one direction across many colonies.

### Lesson verdicts
- `vitoria-right-to-the-americas`: Spain's claimed titles (discovery, papal donation, the Emperor, the Indians' unbelief, their sins against nature, their supposed natural slavery). Verdict: **every unjust title fails**, on Vitoria's own premises and on the consent premise the course later builds. The just titles he leaves, travel, trade and preaching enforceable by war, are **a licence rather than a restraint**, because the party that judged a refusal was the party that went to war over it. Grade: **strong** on the first, **on balance** on the second. Decided by: dominium: the Indians had cities, laws and marriages, so they owned. On the second: Vitoria's own sequence of persuasion, defence, redress and full war, and the eighth title left open "for the sake of argument". Would be overturned by: a mechanism in his scheme that lets a neutral party judge a refusal (there is none).
- `locke-and-mill-on-empire`: whether Locke's "waste" and Mill's tutelage are lapses from their principles or consequences of them. Verdict: **consequences**. Both follow from conditions written into the principles: Locke's labour criterion, applied to land, and Mill's exclusion of "backward states of society", stated in *On Liberty* itself. Grade: **strong**. Decided by: the exclusion appears in the text that states the principle, not in a later aside. The gate is a universal capacity with thick conditions of exercise, a condition that can in principle be met later, so the exclusion can be indefinite and still consistent. Would be overturned by: a reading of *On Liberty* ch. 1 and Locke's chs V and XVI on which the exclusions do not follow (the "contradiction" reading has to treat the clause as a slip).
- `burke-against-hastings`: Hastings's defence that power in Asia must be judged by Asian standards, "geographical morality", and the charge that Burke's impeachment contradicts the *Reflections*. Verdict: **the defence fails, and there is no contradiction**. Grade: **strong**. Decided by: the defence proves too much, since it would license any act by citing the worst local precedent. Burke's principle that all power over others is a trust under law is the *Reflections*' inherited order turned on the Company. Would be overturned by: a passage in which Burke grants a conqueror arbitrary power that he denies to the Jacobins.
- `colonial-balance-sheet`: the Case's three charges against the anti-colonial critique. Verdict: **split**. (a) "The critics never ran a cost–benefit against a counterfactual" → **holds** (strong). A catalogue of harms is not a balance any more than a catalogue of railways is. (b) "The never-colonised comparators show colonialism was no handicap" → **fails** (strong). The comparators are not comparable, survivorship selects them, and the Case itself concedes the counterfactual is unobservable for both sides. (c) "Behaviour reveals the colonised treated colonial rule as legitimate" → **fails as proof of consent** (strong). It shows that legitimacy was graded rather than nil. Would be overturned by: for (b), comparators matched on the conditions that kept them uncolonised.
- `inherited-guilt-and-reparations`: whether historic wrongs bind the living, and how. Verdict: (a) **guilt does not pass**: it attaches to a will, and a descendant can regret and repair but cannot confess. Grade: **strong**. (b) **Liability can pass**, through estates, corporations and states that persist. Burke's partnership of generations implies it, and the same people inherit the estate's assets. Grade: **strong**. (c) Counterfactual rectification of wrongs centuries old **cannot be carried out** (indeterminacy, non-identity, contagion, choice of baseline). Grade: **strong**. (d) Wrongs done to people now alive, such as redlining and contract selling, **are owed repair**, on Waldron's continuing-injustice model, with no counterfactual needed. Grade: **strong**. Supersession is **possible, not automatic**, and applies only where just arrangements for the future are being attempted. Would be overturned by: for (a), an account of guilt that does not rest on agency; for (d), evidence that the living claimants were not themselves wronged.

## Module 15 — nation-and-immigration: Who is "we", may a people decide who joins it, and what did 2015 show about the three answers?

**Module verdict** (in `the-2015-crisis-read-three-ways`). Grade: **on balance**.
A people may control its membership, within duties it cannot set aside: rescue for refugees, fair terms for migrants, and full inclusion for those it admits. Miller's qualified right of self-determination survives Carens's objections, and 2015 did not refute it. The year showed that the decisive failure was one of capacity, not principle. Governments could not register arrivals, sort refugees from economic migrants, or return the refused. Each theory's prediction partly failed against the record now available. *Carens*: open admission held for weeks. Then every government reversed it, Germany included, by re-imposing border checks on 13 September 2015 and extending them to all land borders from 16 September 2024. No democratic electorate sustained it. *Murray*: his paralysis prediction failed. The liberal order corrected itself, slowly and late, through the Turkey statement (2016), the land-border controls (2024) and the Pact on Migration and Asylum (adopted 2024, applying from 12 June 2026). His "never asked" was answered at the ballot box: AfD 12.6% in 2017 and 20.8% in 2025, second place. His integration forecast failed on employment and is partly borne out on crime. *Miller*: the framework held as a description of what a competent state does. Its capacity assumption failed in 2015, and governments have spent ten years rebuilding that capacity along his lines: a harder outside border, shared burdens, faster status decisions. On integration: by 2022, 64% of the 2015 cohort were employed (men 75%, women 31%), against 77% of the whole population. After eight or more years, refugee men's employment rate (86%) exceeded the male average (81%). Benefit receipt in the 2015 cohort fell from 89% in 2016 to 39% in 2022 (IAB). Immigrants of asylum status were 8.8% of all crime suspects in 2024 (offences against residence law excluded), while refugees resident in Germany were about 3.7% of the population, an overrepresentation of a little over two to one. The share is 12.2% in offences against life. A third of suspects are repeat offenders, and they account for 77% of the offences. The group differs sharply in age and sex: 82.9% of suspects were male and 57.1% under 30 (BKA). One district-level study finds no link between a district's foreign share and its crime rate. It attributes most of the overrepresentation to where migrants live (ifo 2025), but how much age and sex explain is disputed.
- Strongest objection to this verdict: the AfD's rise and the reversal of open admission show that Murray was right where it matters, because the liberal state needed a populist revolt before it acted. That is the strongest reading, and the record half-supports it. The reversal followed electoral pressure. But it came through the liberal order's own instruments (elections, legislation, treaties), and Murray predicted that those instruments could not act. The verdict would be overturned if the 2024–26 instruments fail: controls lifted without effect on flows, the Pact's solidarity mechanism unused, or return rates unchanged. On integration, a fall in the 2015 cohort's employment, or crime overrepresentation that survives full adjustment for age, sex and location, would move the integration part toward Murray.

### Lesson verdicts
- `albions-seed-and-who-are-we`: (a) Jay's claim in Federalist 2 that Americans of 1787 were one people with the "same ancestors … same language … same religion" → **false as stated**. There were four British regional cultures, plus Africans, Indians, Germans and Dutch. Grade: **strong**. (b) Huntington's 2004 forecast that Hispanic immigration would not assimilate → **failed on language and identity** (English near-universal by the third generation, attachment rising with generation, high intermarriage) and **partly supported on education**, where one long panel found the gap stalling across generations. Grade: **strong** on language and identity, **on balance** on education. Decided by: Fischer's own evidence, and the post-2004 tests (Citrin et al. 2007; Telles & Ortiz 2008). Would be overturned by: for (b), later-generation data showing language retention or falling attachment. Ethnic attrition, descendants who stop identifying as Hispanic, has to be measured before such data counts.
- `america-and-the-english-decline`: the assembled thesis that America is following England's decline because it inherited the causes. Verdict: the **broad forecast of contraction is contradicted so far**; the **narrow thesis is open**. The broad forecast: share of world output roughly constant, military reach unmatched, Burnham's lost atlas largely reversed after 1989. The narrow thesis is an elite that cannot say why its inheritance deserves defence. Grade: **strong** on the broad forecast; open on the narrow thesis. Decided by: the power indicators, and the fact that decline has been forecast in every generation since 1918 without a reversal counting against it. What would settle the narrow thesis: measured series on religious practice, the gap between elite and public opinion on national identity, and immigrant assimilation, with a horizon fixed in advance.
- `powell-raspail-and-camus`: the three prophecies checked against the record. Verdict, claim by claim: Powell's **numbers were right in direction and roughly in size, with a lag**: about 4.6 million non-White in the UK in 2001, against his 5–7 million for 2000; England and Wales 18.3% not White in 2021. His forecast of **bloodshed and domination failed** in the form he gave it. Raspail's **central premise, that the West would not refuse, failed in its strong form**: Australia 2013, the Hungarian fence 2015, the EU–Turkey statement 2016. The 1985 Figaro projection **understated migration and still got the religious outcome wrong**, so its mechanism was mis-specified. Camus's **descriptive claim is partly true** (the population's composition is changing, by an amount that depends on how mixed descent is counted). His **intentional claim, that the change is wanted and organised, is unsupported**: his own text rejects the plot, and the UN's 2000 "replacement migration" report is a projection exercise. Grade: **strong** throughout. Would be overturned by: for the intentional claim, documentary evidence of a policy aimed at changing the population's composition. Murray's "never chosen" (next lesson) is evidence of drift, which is the opposite.
- `murray-strange-death-of-europe`: the first concatenation's claim that post-war mass immigration to Western Europe was never chosen: guest workers who stayed, forecasts wrong every decade, targets missed, persistent majority opposition. Verdict: **holds**. Grade: **strong**. Decided by: the forecasts (13,000 a year predicted for 2004; Eastern Europeans in Britain at 1.24 million by 2013), the missed "tens of thousands" target, and polls from Gallup 1968 to YouGov 2011. The second concatenation, "tiredness", is **not testable as stated** and gets no verdict. The Böckenförde question it raises goes to module 16. Would be overturned by: evidence that the inflows were planned and endorsed in advance by the legislatures that received them.
- `miller-strangers-in-our-midst` (decides the dispute `carens-open-borders` stages): whether the equal moral worth of persons entails a right to cross borders freely. Verdict: **it does not**. It entails duties of rescue, fair terms and inclusion of residents, and leaves a people a qualified right to control membership. Grade: **on balance**. Decided by: Carens's cantilever argues that free movement within a state is a basic liberty, so movement between states must be too. But the basic interests that internal movement protects can be met in any adequately free state, so they ground a right to *some* adequate place, not to *this* one. Carens's extension of the original position to the globe also bypasses Rawls's own placement of self-determination at the level of peoples (§58). Abizadeh's coercion argument would equally give every person affected by a state's law a vote in it, which no one defends. Would be overturned by: an argument that the interests served by free movement within a state can only be served by access to particular states, or a resolution of Abizadeh's "boundary problem" that does not prove too much.

## Module 16 — after-liberalism: Can a free state survive on liberal principles alone, and is the post-liberal remedy better?

**Module verdict** (placed in `hungarys-fundamental-law`). Grade: **on balance**.
No. A free state does not live on liberal principles alone. It draws on a loyalty and a readiness to bear burdens for strangers who count as "us", which it did not produce and cannot command without ceasing to be free (Böckenförde). The course's own lessons show the premise held by a conservative (Scruton), a liberal of the left (Miller) and Jay in 1787 alike. Carens, its strongest denier, concedes it in his nonideal theory, and no lesson refutes it. The post-liberal remedy fails on the course's own test case. Writing the presuppositions into the supreme law by one majority turned them into that majority's instrument. In Hungary the constitution was written in four months by the alliance that had won two-thirds of the seats on 53% of the list vote. The alliance held two-thirds again on about 45% in 2014. The Fourth Amendment reinstated provisions the Constitutional Court had annulled. That is Deneen's own warning and the failure Madison predicted, not a cohesion produced by law. The same case shows that the liberal mechanism the post-liberals discount still works: in April 2026, under that constitution, voters removed the governing alliance and gave Tisza a two-thirds majority (53.2% of the list vote, 141 of 199 seats). The answer the course's evidence supports is that liberal principles are necessary and not sufficient. The supplement they need has to come from below: a people's own institutions and loyalties, which the state can protect and cannot write. This is Deneen's prescription, set against what he and his allies have endorsed in practice. It is also why the liberal states of 2015 corrected themselves only once their publics spoke.
- Strongest objection to this verdict: the 2026 alternation shows the opposite of what it is used for here. The post-liberal constitution did not entrench a party, so the lock-in charge fails, and Hungary is evidence that a national-Christian constitution and free elections coexist. Part of this is right: the charge that the system could not be voted out failed in 2026, and the verdict accepts that. It leaves untouched the finding that the text did not produce the cohesion the theory promised: the national-Christian programme lost a free election to a party whose own platform is not post-liberal [UNVERIFIED as to Tisza's programme]. It also leaves the lock-in charge standing on its own terms: cardinal acts outlive the majority that passed them, and a new two-thirds majority holds the same lever. What would overturn the verdict: a free state that kept its cohesion over generations on procedure alone, with no shared language, history or faith, or a state that wrote its presuppositions into law and kept both cohesion and alternation of power. Where it belongs: here, as the module's application. `the-argument-assembled` then takes the Böckenförde question to the course's close, by the human's decision.

### Lesson verdicts
- `hazony-virtue-of-nationalism`: (a) the claim that a federation of nations, the EU above all, is an empire, "in effect a universal state". (b) The claim that there is no neutral, purely civic state. Verdicts: (a) **fails**. Members of the EU joined by treaty, may leave (Article 50, used by the United Kingdom in 2020), and, in Hungary's own text, authorise the Union's competences themselves (Article E). An empire in Hazony's sense rules nations that did not consent and cannot exit. Grade: **strong**. (b) **Holds**. Every state chooses an official language, a calendar of holidays, a public history and the terms of naturalisation, and so favours some culture. The liberal-nationalist alternative concedes this. Grade: **strong**. Would be overturned by: for (a), a case of a member state prevented from leaving, or of competences transferred without its consent.
- `vermeule-common-good-constitutionalism`: the claim that the safeguard against abuse of common-good governance can be internal to officials: reasoned ordinances, good faith, rationality review. Verdict: **fails as a safeguard**. Grade: **strong**. Decided by argument from the course's own premises: Madison's "enlightened statesmen will not always be at the helm", and Polybius's checks among separate powers. A check located in the good faith of the checked is not a check. It is borne out in the application: a two-thirds majority amended the constitution to reinstate what the Court had annulled, and the theory has no test external to the authority's own reasons. Would be overturned by: a mechanism in the theory, not in the officials' character, that can block a governing majority's "determination".
- `de-benoist-european-new-right`: whether the right to difference ("ethnopluralism"), a Europe of peoples each entitled to remain itself, holds together. Verdict: **on balance, it does not**. Grade: **on balance**. Decided by: it assigns the right to peoples, not persons, so the individual who marries out, assimilates or leaves has no standing in it. And its premise of distinct, durable peoples to be preserved conflicts with the established ancient-DNA finding that every European people is a recent mixture (`reich-ancient-dna-and-the-indo-europeans`). The requirement of generality ("I may claim my difference only if I defend yours") is consistent, and it is what separates the doctrine from a ranking of races. Would be overturned by: an account of the "people" as a cultural unit that is persistent and can be specified without ancestry, and that gives its members standing to leave it.

## Balance check

I counted the decided items in the four modules, sub-verdicts separately, by the side whose claim each one goes against or for. The count leaves out verdicts that are open or cut both ways equally.

- **Against a right-leaning claim (17):** within-group heritability as evidence for a genetic gap; the gap fixed and never narrowed; the 1920s army-test rankings (Brigham); pure races (ancient DNA); African national IQ ≈ 70 as a measurement; Spain's titles; colonial comparators as proof that colonialism was no handicap; behavioural legitimacy as consent; Huntington on Hispanic language and identity; the broad Anglosphere-decline forecast; Powell's bloodshed forecast; Raspail's "the West will not refuse"; Camus's intentional replacement; Murray's paralysis prediction; the restrictionist integration forecast on employment; the EU as empire; Vermeule's internal safeguard. The tally leans against the right. In module 16, three of the four post-liberal theories' central claims fail. I checked each of these for shading. The verdict on Hazony's "empire" rests on a dated exit (2020).

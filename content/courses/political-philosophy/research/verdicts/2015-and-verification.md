# Verdict register: the 2015 verdict rewritten, and the register's open checks

This file does two things. Part 1 is a replacement for the module 15 verdict in `13-16.md`, the one placed in `the-2015-crisis-read-three-ways`. The draft said the failure was one of capacity, not principle. It left out how the decisions were taken and the security record, and it led with the favourable employment figures. Part 1 grades four questions separately: procedure and consent, security, principle, and integration. It then answers the module's question from all four. Part 2 checks every item the four register files mark `[UNVERIFIED]`, "from memory" or "not checked".

Every source I read is saved under `research/texts/2015-procedure/`, `research/texts/2015-security/`, `research/texts/2015-integration/` and `research/texts/verification-2026-11/`, each with a `.meta.yaml` sidecar. Facts that rest only on secondary sources (encyclopaedia articles, news reports) are marked `[secondary]`. Facts I could not check at all are marked `[UNVERIFIED]`.

## Module 15 — nation-and-immigration: Who is "we", may a people decide who joins it, and what did 2015 show about the three answers?

**Module verdict** (in `the-2015-crisis-read-three-ways`). Graded in four parts plus integration, below. Summary grades: (a) consent bypassed, **strong**; unlawful, **open**. (b) Removal and screening failures systematic, **strong**; the lethal Islamist attacks since 2016 mostly by men who came as asylum seekers, **strong**; danger foreseeable as a class of risk, **strong**, and as individuals, mostly not. (c) Miller over Carens, **on balance**. (d) Integration split by sex, **strong**; crime overrepresentation real, **strong**, and how much survives adjustment for age and sex, **open**.

### (a) Procedure and consent: how the decisions of August and September 2015 were taken

**The record.**
- *25 August 2015.* The Federal Office for Migration and Refugees (BAMF) announced on its Twitter account: "#Dublin-Verfahren syrischer Staatsangehöriger werden zum gegenwärtigen Zeitpunkt von uns weitestgehend faktisch nicht weiter verfolgt." (Dublin procedures for Syrian nationals are, for now, in practice largely no longer being pursued by us.) No minister signed it, and no parliament or cabinet decided it. Quoted in Bundestag research service WD 3-3000-299/15, p. 4.
- *4–5 September.* The federal government announced: "Deutschland und Österreich haben am Freitagabend der Ausreise von Flüchtlingen aus Ungarn zugestimmt." (On Friday evening Germany and Austria agreed to let the refugees leave Hungary.) The same statement says that Merkel and Orbán agreed the next day that Dublin still applied and that the onward travel was "eine Ausnahme" (an exception) because of the emergency at the Hungarian border (bundesregierung.de, 5 Sept 2015, quoted in WD 3-3000-299/15, p. 4). The decision was the Chancellor's, taken by telephone with Austria. Whether the cabinet or the coalition partner CSU was consulted before it is `[secondary]` (press accounts and R. Alexander, *Die Getriebenen*, 2017, not read).
- *13 September.* Border controls were reintroduced. The Federal Police order of that day told officers to refuse entry to undocumented third-country nationals who did not ask for asylum, and to admit undocumented ones who did ("die Einreise zu gestatten ist"; government answer, BT-Drs. 18/7311, 20 Jan 2016, answer 1). The same answer states that "Eine Zurückweisung ist im Rechtsrahmen der Dublin-III Verordnung und des § 18 AsylG zulässig" (refusal at the border is permitted within Dublin III and § 18 of the Asylum Act; answer 3). The interior minister put it plainly: "Politisch haben wir uns bisher jedenfalls dagegen entschieden" (politically, at any rate, we have so far decided against it; *Welt am Sonntag*, 13 Dec 2015, quoted in WD 3-3000-014/16, n. 20). Admission through the controlled border continued until the Balkan route closed and the EU–Turkey statement took effect in March 2016.
- *Legal basis.* § 18(2) of the Asylum Act requires refusing entry to asylum seekers coming from a safe third country, and every neighbour of Germany is one. § 18(4) allows two exceptions: Dublin responsibility, or an order of the interior ministry "aus völkerrechtlichen oder humanitären Gründen oder zur Wahrung politischer Interessen" (on grounds of international law or humanity, or to protect the Federal Republic's political interests). The Bundestag's research service could not establish whether such an order was ever given, or for whom and for how long (WD 3-3000-299/15, p. 4; WD 3-3000-014/16, p. 4). Asked in 2018 on what legal basis over a million people were admitted "ohne einen vorherigen Bundestagsbeschluss" (without a prior Bundestag resolution), the government referred back to its 2016 answer and named no order (BT-Drs. 19/753, answers 1–2). In 2025 the AfD group's question (BT-Drs. 21/625, 23 Jun 2025) said that the interior minister's instruction of 7 May 2025 withdrew "die mündliche Weisung vom 13. September 2015, den § 18 Absatz 2 Nummer 1 des Asylgesetzes (AsylG) nicht anzuwenden" (the oral instruction of 13 September 2015 not to apply the refusal rule). On that account the policy rested for almost ten years on an unpublished oral instruction. The statement is the questioners' preamble, citing the reported wording of the 2025 instruction, and the government's answer neither confirms nor disputes it `[secondary]`.
- *The Bundestag* never voted on the admission decision. It legislated afterwards: the Asylum Procedure Acceleration Act passed on 15 October 2015 and was promulgated in BGBl. I 2015, p. 1722 `[secondary: Bundestag text archive and promulgation record via search; the vote record not read]`. That act tightened procedure. It did not authorise the admission.
- *Legal opinions.* The research service found the question of whether the Bundestag had to decide hard to call. Under the Constitutional Court's *Wesentlichkeit* doctrine, by which the legislature must itself make the essential decisions, the outcome "lässt sich … kaum vorhersagen" (can hardly be predicted; WD 3-3000-299/15, p. 9). Its supplementary opinion went further: if the entries rested on executive discretion under § 18(4) no. 2 rather than on Dublin responsibility, "sprechen gewichtige Argumente für die Erforderlichkeit einer gesetzlichen Grundlage" (weighty arguments favour the need for a statutory basis). It gave its reasons: lasting burdens, a changed social structure, and considerable integration problems (WD 3-3000-014/16, p. 6). Udo Di Fabio's report for Bavaria (January 2016) held that an uncontrolled mass inflow reaching beyond "eine momentane, zeitlich und örtlich begrenzte Grenzöffnung" (a momentary border opening limited in time and place) "bedarf … einer gesetzlichen Grundlage" (needs a statutory basis; p. 97, quoted verbatim in WD 3-3000-014/16, p. 5, and WD 3-3000-109/17). His main thesis was a federal duty to the Länder to control the border. The research service called that line "nicht abwegig" (not far-fetched), but found that he "gerade eine solche konkrete Pflichtverletzung … nicht substantiieren konnte" (could not substantiate a concrete breach of that duty; WD 3-3000-237/18, p. 3, summarising WD 3-3000-010/16). Bavaria prepared a suit and never filed it, because the inflow fell after the Balkan route closed (Möstl, AöR 2017, quoted in WD 3-3000-237/18, p. 4). The report itself was not obtainable (both hosted copies return HTML); every quotation of it here comes through the research service.
- *Courts.* The Constitutional Court dismissed the AfD group's Organstreit (a dispute between constitutional organs) against the 2015 decision as inadmissible: the group had not shown that its own rights were violated, and the procedure allows no general review of legality (BVerfG, 2 BvE 1/18, order of 11 Dec 2018, press release 87/2018). No German court has ruled on the merits.
- *The EU vote, 22 September.* Council Decision (EU) 2015/1601 relocated 120,000 people from Italy and Greece. It was adopted by qualified majority under Art. 78(3) TFEU against the votes of the Czech Republic, Hungary, Romania and Slovakia, with Finland abstaining. The Court of Justice dismissed Slovakia's and Hungary's actions in full (Joined Cases C-643/15 and C-647/15, 6 Sept 2017). Three of its holdings matter here. The decision was a non-legislative act, so "its adoption was not subject to the requirements relating to the participation of national Parliaments". The European Council's June 2015 conclusions requiring "consensus" concerned an earlier scheme, and the European Council "cannot under any circumstances alter the voting rules laid down by the Treaties". And the small number of relocations was explained partly by "the lack of cooperation on the part of certain Member States" (CJEU press release 91/17).

**Verdict (a).** That the decisions were taken by the executive alone is **established**. The admission of more than a million people was decided in days by the Chancellor and her ministry, by a tweet, a telephone agreement and a police order, with no vote of the Bundestag. That this bypassed rule by consent is **strong**. The government's own answer to parliament says that refusal at the border was lawful, and its interior minister called the choice "political". A political choice with lasting effects on who belongs to the polity is the kind of decision the consent tradition the course builds (Locke; Miller's self-determination over membership) assigns to the people's representatives. The emergency defence covers the weekend of 4–5 September. It does not cover the following six months, when border controls existed and admission continued by decision. That the decision was unlawful is **open**. The research service judged the statutory-basis argument weighty only on a premise of fact (discretion, not Dublin responsibility) that the government never clarified. Di Fabio's federal-duty thesis was judged unsubstantiated, and no court has ruled on the merits. Gravity: a serious breach of procedure on a question of membership, not a breach of the constitutional order. The Bundestag could have legislated at any time and did not. The governing parties kept their majority. The breach was answered through elections (AfD 12.6% in 2017, 20.8% in 2025). At EU level the relocation vote was lawful (**established**, CJEU). It was also a decision on who joins four peoples, taken over their governments' votes, by an act the Court held needed no involvement of national parliaments. By Miller's standard that overrides self-determination over membership, and its implementation largely failed for that reason.

### (b) Security: Islamist attacks by people who came as asylum seekers

**The record.** The Federal Office for the Protection of the Constitution (BfV) lists 17 Islamist attacks in Germany since February 2016, with 23 dead (BfV, "Zahlen und Fakten", list "Anschläge in Deutschland", state of 2026). Twelve of them were committed by men who came to Germany as asylum seekers, and those twelve account for **22 of the 23 deaths**. **Scale.** About 3.0 million asylum applications were made in Germany in 2015–2024: 476,649; 745,545; 222,683; 185,853; 165,938; 122,170; 190,816; 244,132; 351,915; 250,945 (bpb from BAMF; first and follow-up applications together). Twelve attackers among them is about four per million applications. Twenty-two dead over ten years is a small toll beside the size of the flow. It is almost the whole toll of Islamist terrorism in Germany over the same years.

**Verdict (b).**
- *Who committed the attacks.* That the lethal Islamist attacks in Germany since 2016 were overwhelmingly committed by men who came as asylum seekers is **strong** (22 of 23 deaths). Eight of the twelve arrived in 2014–2016. The grade is "strong" rather than "established" because several statuses rest on secondary sources.
- *Removal failure.* That the removal failure was systematic is **strong**. The same mechanism recurs from 2016 to 2024. Amri and the Hamburg attacker were legally obliged to leave, and the origin state did not supply papers. The Ansbach attacker's ordered Dublin removal was suspended. The Solingen attacker's Dublin transfer was tried once, and the deadline was then allowed to lapse. (The non-Islamist Aschaffenburg case has the same structure.) This is Murray's deportation gap, observed directly.
- *Screening failure.* That screening failed in 2015–16 is **strong**: a man registered under at least nine identities and assessed as a Gefährder still had his asylum claim pending for months; a minor was never fingerprinted; a Paris bomber was registered on a false passport.
- *Foreseeability.* As a class of risk, the danger was foreseeable: admitting large numbers from war zones where the Islamic State recruited, with registration suspended in practice, carried that risk. It was shown on 13 November 2015, and admission without refusal continued for four more months. That is **strong**. As individuals, most attackers were not foreseeable. Only three of the twelve (Amri, the Hamburg attacker, the Dresden attacker) are recorded as known Islamists beforehand, and several radicalised years after arrival (Solingen, Mannheim, Munich). That is **strong**.
- *Size.* The danger is small relative to the whole flow, at a few per million, and large relative to Islamist terrorism in Germany, nearly all of it. Both statements are true. A verdict that gives only one of them is shaded.

### (c) Principle: may a people control its membership?

Kept from the draft (`miller-strangers-in-our-midst`). The equal moral worth of persons does not entail a right to cross borders freely. It entails duties of rescue, fair terms and full inclusion of those admitted, and leaves a people a qualified right to control membership. Grade: **on balance**. Decided by the cantilever reply: the basic interests that internal movement protects can be met in any adequately free state, so they ground a right to *some* adequate place, not to *this* one. Abizadeh's coercion argument proves too much. 2015 adds two things, neither of which overturns this.
- First, Carens's own brake, the public-order restriction, is exactly the case (a) and (b) document: admission without identification, and removal that cannot be enforced. On his own terms, his theory licensed far more control than was exercised.
- Second, the draft's "capacity, not principle" is withdrawn. 2015 was a failure of capacity *and* of principle, though not of Miller's principle. Miller's principle was not applied. The question of who joins was not decided by the people through its institutions; it was decided by an executive declining to use powers it said it had.

### (d) Integration: both sexes, and the population comparisons

**Employment** (IAB-Kurzbericht 10/2024, IAB-BAMF-SOEP survey of refugees who arrived 2013–mid-2019, aged 18–64, read in full):

| | Refugees | Comparison |
|---|---|---|
| 2015 cohort in 2022, all | 64% | 77% population average; 70% all immigrants (Destatis 2023). An age-adjusted gap would be about 2 points smaller (IAB, p. 3) |
| 2015 cohort in 2022, men | 75% | 81% all men |
| 2015 cohort in 2022, women | 31% | 72% all women |
| All cohorts after 8+ years, all | 68% | 77% |
| All cohorts after 8+ years, men | 86% | 81% all men |
| All cohorts after 8+ years, women | 33% | 72% all women |

The IAB states the comparison with all men and all women for the 8+-year group. Applying the same benchmarks to the 2015 cohort's men and women is my arithmetic, not the IAB's.

**Quality of employment and benefits.** 90% of employed refugees were in jobs subject to social insurance. 76% of the employed 2015 arrivals worked full-time, at a median full-time gross wage of €2,570 a month. Benefit receipt (asylum-seekers' benefits, unemployment benefit II or unemployment insurance) in the 2015 cohort fell from 89% (2016) to 39% (2022). The IAB counts every member of a benefit household, including members who support themselves from work (n. 2).

**Survey caveats.** The 2022 wave had 1,839 person observations, under preliminary weights. It covers only those still in Germany. Employment is self-reported paid work of any extent.

**Crime.** As in the draft, from the BKA's *Bundeslagebild Kriminalität im Kontext von Zuwanderung 2024* (federal situation report on crime in the context of immigration, read in full by the earlier drafter):
- Immigrants of asylum status were 8.8% of all suspects (offences against residence law excluded), against 3.66% of the population (3,061,540 resident refugees out of 83,577,140, Destatis, 31 Dec 2024; see Part 2). That is an overrepresentation of about 2.4 times.
- In offences against life the share is 12.2%, about 3.3 times; in sexual offences 7.9%, about 2.2 times.
- 82.9% of suspects were male and 57.1% under 30, against 55.0% male and 46.0% under 30 among resident refugees.
- 31.8% of suspects were repeat suspects, involved in 77.0% of the group's offences.
- The BKA states that the report is not an adjusted rate comparison.

**The adjusted-rate study I read in full.** Adema & Alipour (ifo Schnelldienst digital 3/2025). It is a district-level study, not a study of individuals.
- In 2023 there were 57 foreign suspects per 1,000 foreign residents, against 19 per 1,000 Germans. Excluding suspects with no German residence, the foreign rate is still "fast dreimal höher" (almost three times higher).
- Across districts, a higher foreign share goes with more crime (elasticity 0.41). After controls for the district's mean age, male share, German suspect rate and unemployment, the link disappears (−0.06, n.s.).
- In the 2018–2023 panel with district and year fixed effects, a change in the foreign share, or in the share of protection seekers, has no detectable effect on local crime. That includes homicide and sexual offences. Property damage and fare evasion rise slightly (+0.17 per 1% for property damage).
- The study's claim that demography plays only a small part rests on district averages, and the authors say individual suspect data "stehen in Deutschland jedoch nicht zur Verfügung" (are not available in Germany). So it shows that immigration has not raised *local* crime rates. It does not show how much of the *individual* overrepresentation is explained by age and sex.
- No German study adjusting individual suspect rates by status for age and sex was found or read.

**Verdict (d).**
- That the integration record is split by sex is **strong**. Refugee men reached the male employment rate after eight years. Refugee women stayed at under half the female rate, 33% against 72%.
- That refugees are overrepresented about 2.4 times among recorded suspects, and more in offences against life, is **strong** (police statistics of suspects, not convictions).
- How much of that overrepresentation survives adjustment for age and sex is **open**. What would settle it: suspect rates by residence status standardised by age and sex, which the BKA's data could produce.
- That the arrival of refugees did not raise local crime rates in 2018–2023 is **on balance** (one district panel, with the limits its authors state).
- Murray's integration forecast fails for men on employment, holds for women, and is partly borne out on crime, pending adjustment.

### Integration: what 2015 showed about the three answers

Grade for the module's answer (a people may control its membership, within duties of rescue, fair terms and inclusion): **on balance**, as before. What changes is the reading of 2015.
- **Carens.** Open admission held for months and was reversed by every government that tried it, Germany included. The record of 2015–16 (no identification, unenforceable removals, a lethal security cost concentrated in a few cases) is the case his own public-order restriction covers.
- **Miller.** His principle stands, and 2015 is evidence for it in the negative. The decision on who joins was taken neither by the people nor by their legislature. The state lacked the capacity to sort, screen and return that his framework presupposes. Ten years of correction followed along his lines:
  - controls at every land border from 16 September 2024, now notified to 15 March 2027;
  - refusal at the border resumed by instruction on 7 May 2025, with vulnerable persons excepted. The Berlin administrative court held three such refusals unlawful under Dublin on 2 June 2025 (VG 6 L 191/25), so even this correction is contested in law;
  - the Pact on Migration and Asylum, applying from 12 June 2026;
  - faster decisions.
- **Murray.**
  - His "never asked" holds for 2015 in the strongest sense: not even the Bundestag was asked.
  - His deportation gap is confirmed, with dead.
  - His paralysis forecast held for about six months and then failed: the liberal order corrected itself through its own instruments, though late, and after the electorate punished the governing parties.
  - His integration forecast is split, as in (d).

- **Strongest objection to this verdict.** From one side: on 4 September thousands were stranded at Keleti and on the motorway, and a state may act without parliament in an emergency. The Bundestag acquiesced, and the 2017 election returned the same Chancellor, so consent was given after the fact. And naming the attackers' status is collective blame on three million people for twelve. From the other side: the admission was not merely unauthorised but unlawful, as Di Fabio held. The verdict meets both. The emergency covers days, not the six months after controls were in place. Acquiescence is not authorisation: that is the point of the *Wesentlichkeit* doctrine, and an election in which migration was one issue among many does not ratify a specific decision. The verdict states the per-capita rarity next to the share of the toll, and it locates the failure in institutions (screening, removal), not in a group. Against the second objection: no court has ruled, the research service found Di Fabio's concrete breach unsubstantiated, and Bavaria did not sue. What would overturn it:
  - for (a), a published ministerial order under § 18(4) no. 2 with stated limits, or proof that the admissions rested on Dublin responsibility (either would make it lawful executive action under statute), or a merits ruling;
  - for (b), evidence that the asylum-route share of Islamist attackers is no higher than the share of other groups, or that the removal failures were isolated rather than recurrent;
  - for (d), age-and-sex-standardised suspect rates showing no overrepresentation, or a fall in refugee men's employment.

Against a left-leaning position:
- the decision bypassed consent (a);
- removal and screening failures were systematic (b);
- the lethal Islamist attacks were mostly by asylum-route arrivals (b);
- the risk was foreseeable as a class (b);

## Part 2 — checks of items flagged in the four register files

Unless another folder is named, every file below is in `research/texts/verification-2026-11/`. I spot-checked the Hungary, Destatis, Schengen and Spengler files against their saved text.

**Corrections that change what a verdict or brief may say:**
- **Spengler (05-08).** His own table puts Western Caesarism at 2000–2200, not within the twentieth century. The brief's phrase must change, and so must any verdict that treats "Caesarism in the twentieth century" as his dated forecast. What he does date to the twentieth century is the passage from "Napoleonism" to Caesarism, and parliaments becoming "solemn and empty pageantry".

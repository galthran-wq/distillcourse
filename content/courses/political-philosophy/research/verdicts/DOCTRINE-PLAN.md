# Plan: the course's final lesson, `the-doctrine`

Agreed with the course author on 2026-11-01. Binding for the brief and the lesson.

## What it is

A new last lesson after `the-argument-assembled`. `the-argument-assembled` looks back and judges the course's four questions; `the-doctrine` sets out the resulting position as a derivation — postulates, theses derived from them, what the doctrine allows and forbids, its strongest critics, what stays open. Shape of Spinoza's *Ethics* or a constitution: principles, then consequences. Readable by someone who has not read the course: every postulate and thesis is explained in plain words in place, and every ground links (`<Ref>`) to the lesson and verdict that established it.

Tone: dry and exact; no rhetoric, no rallying. Every sentence states, grounds or qualifies. Persuasive only as an inference from data and from philosophy.

Components: `<Postulate>` (P1…, grade, grounds, would be overturned by), `<Thesis>` (T1…, "from P…, Verdicts …"), `<Verdict>` where a verdict is restated; and a course-specific figure: the derivation map (postulates on top, theses below, verdicts and lessons at the base, lines for what rests on what; selecting a thesis highlights its grounds).

## Structure

0. How the doctrine is built: what counts as a ground (verdicts against the record; arguments that survived their strongest objection), the grades, that every postulate can be overturned and what would do it.
I. Postulates (below), each: statement, grounds (philosophical and empirical, with Refs), grade, strongest objection and reply.
II. Theses derived (below), each with what it follows from.
III. What the doctrine allows and forbids, each item tied to its thesis.
IV. The doctrine against its strongest critics: Rawls, Carens, MacIntyre, Habermas; the post-liberal; the racial right. Answered or conceded, graded.
V. What stays open and what would settle it: the genetic share of group gaps; the cause of the West's rise; whether a free state can build a nation over generations; the number at which immigration is too many; the line between screening by ideology and by religion.

## Postulates

Human nature
- P1. People are unequal in ability, partly by birth, and equal in standing under law. (strong)
- P2. People are social by nature, and the state lowers violence; there was never a peaceful solitary stage. (established / strong)
- P3. Envy is a real political motive, though it decides no question of justice. (on balance)

Rule
- P4. A few always rule; what matters is how they are selected and checked. (strong)
- P5. Every order needs a settled last word; undecided sovereignty means war (1642); the last word can be held by a composite body bound by law (1689). (strong)
- P6. Power grows unless it is checked. (established for growth; on balance as a law)

Belonging
- P7. Belonging is inherited before it is chosen; the contract fails as an account of origins. (strong)
- P8. A free state lives on a pre-political solidarity its procedures do not produce (Böckenförde). (on balance)
- P9. Culture persists across generations; it is not carried by blood and not acquired on demand. (strong)
- P10. Religion persists as a bond: from Rome's public worship to the failed secularization forecast. (strong)

Change and decline
- P11. Redesigning a whole society at one stroke is costly; designed institutions can last. (on balance / strong)
- P12. Civilizations decline through mechanisms, not by fate; decline can be reversed. (strong)
- P13. Material progress is real but not a law of history. (strong / untestable)

Economy
- P14. Property and markets produce wealth; planning failed. (established)

Between peoples
- P15. Without a common authority, power decides between peoples (Melos, as description); among free republics, peace has held (Kant, Schuman). (strong / strong)

Values — argued, not measured (the text says so)
- P16. Freedom among citizens is a good worth keeping. (argued)
- P17. The West's achievement — philosophy and political theory from the Greeks, science and self-government from c. 1400 — is real and worth conserving. (achievement strong; worth conserving argued; cause open)

## Theses (each shown as derived)

- T1. A mixed, limited regime with a settled last word held under law and an elite that can be removed. (P1, P4, P5, P6)
- T2. Liberal freedom does not found itself: it lives on a nation, an elite held under law, an inherited culture it cannot produce on demand, and an economy of property and markets. (P7, P8, P9, P14, P16)
- T3. A people may decide who joins and how many, and select by capacity to integrate. (P7, P8, P9; Miller over Carens; the 2015 verdicts)
- T4. Membership is cultural, not racial: open in principle, slow, demanded rather than assumed. (P9; Gobineau/Grant/Reich verdicts)
- T5. A market economy with a guaranteed minimum, not redistribution aimed at equal outcomes. (P1, P3, P6, P14)
- T6. A free state may favour in public life the religion and culture it historically lives on, while protecting religious liberty. (P8, P10, P16)
- T7. No inherited guilt; liability only through institutions that persist. (inherited-guilt verdict)
- T8. Between peoples, interest and power where no common authority exists; cooperation among free states. (P15)
- T9. The radical alternatives are excluded: the decider, the racial nation, the conservative revolution, conquest as a right, ownership of people. (their verdicts)

## Allows (each tied to a thesis)
- A people decides who joins and how many, selecting by capacity to integrate (T3).
- Language, law and loyalty required as conditions of membership (T3, T4).
- Refusal and removal of those hostile to the constitutional order, Islamists above all; refused applicants actually removed (T3; 2015 verdicts).
- Precedence for the national culture in public life: schools, calendar, civic symbols (T6).
- Inequality of outcome; an elite rising by ability under competition and removability (T1, T5).
- Markets and property with a guaranteed minimum (T5).
- No inherited guilt for colonialism or slavery (T7).

## Forbids
- Status by descent: a citizen of non-European origin who has taken on the culture is a full member (T4).
- Ownership of persons; conquest as a source of right (T9).
- Rule without consent or removability (T1, T9).
- One majority writing the constitution for all (Hungary verdict).
- Planned economies; open borders as a right (T5, T3).
- Collective guilt by origin, in either direction (T7).

## Hard cases weighed in the lesson, not resolved by slogan
1. Screening by ideology vs by religion.
2. How many is too many — what would determine it (integration outcomes by cohort).
3. Whether a free state can build a nation over generations (Third Republic, to be bound).

# Verdict register: political-philosophy (final)

This is the merged register. It replaces `01-04.md`, `05-08.md`, `09-12.md` and `13-16.md` as the list the briefs are rewritten from. It folds in two audits (`audit-left-shading.md`, `audit-right-shading.md`), the replacement 2015 verdict and the verification pass (`2015-and-verification.md`), and the liberal and left forecast checks (`liberal-forecasts.md`). `CHANGELOG.md` lists every verdict that changed and every audit proposal that was rejected.

How it was merged:
- An audit proposal was accepted when the source it cites holds what the audit says. The cited text was read in `research/texts/…` or in the brief. A proposal that rests only on a secondary or unread source could lower a grade but not reverse a verdict.
- Every correction in `2015-and-verification.md` Part 2 is applied.
- Liberal and left forecast checks sit under their host lesson as lesson verdicts marked **(forecast check)**.
- `[UNVERIFIED]` marks a fact nobody has checked against its source. `[secondary]` marks a fact checked only in an encyclopaedia, a press report, an abstract-level summary or a quotation by another author. Neither is used as decisive evidence for a grade of established or strong.
- Grades: **established / strong / on balance / open**. "Open" always states what would settle it.

The integration figures for 2015 now use one measure: IAB-Kurzbericht 17/2025, the newest report. That measure is the employment rate (*Beschäftigungsquote*): the share of 18–64-year-olds in paid dependent employment, taken from the Federal Employment Agency's social-insurance register linked to the IAB-BAMF-SOEP survey. Self-employed people and civil servants are not counted. The 2015 arrivals are compared with the population average on the same measure, in 2024, nine years after arrival. The earlier figures from IAB-Kurzbericht 10/2024 (survey-reported paid work set against Destatis's 77%) measure something else and are not used.

## Module 1 — greek-discovery: Who should rule, and which Greek answer (rule by knowledge, rule by nature, or the mixed regime of the middle) survives a test against a real city?

**Module verdict** (in `sparta-as-test-case`) — grade: **on balance**
Of the module's three theories, the mixed constitution holds up best against Sparta. Sparta lasted for centuries, and Aristotle gives the reason in II.9: "the people are contented when they have a share in the highest office". What held the city together was a balance of interest, not a balance of virtue. Aristotle's single-virtue account of decline fits the record better than Plato's sequence from honour to money. The city trained for war won its empire, ran short of citizens (*oliganthropia*), and lost Messenia after Leuctra (371).

Mastery by nature fails the test. The helots were Greeks enslaved by conquest, held down by the *krypteia* and "always lying in wait". Aristotle's own I.6 refuses to justify slavery of that kind, and II.9 treats the helots as a management problem, not as slaves by nature. (Round 2: of the four claims in "rule by nature" — see the lesson verdict — Sparta tests only the fourth, that some men are by nature fit to be owned. It does not test that people differ in the capacity to lead or that the more capable should hold office among equals; Sparta's yearly ephors chosen from the whole people are part of the mixture that held.) Rule by knowledge has no case in the record to test. The Greek answer that survives is Aristotle's: give every part a share and keep the citizen body broad.
- Strongest objection to this verdict: the record is one city, known only through admirers and enemies, and Sparta may have held together through fear of the helots rather than through its mixture. This weakens "proves" to "fits". It does not overturn the verdict, because Aristotle's own account already names the shrinking citizen body and the helot danger as the causes of the end. It would be overturned by evidence that a closed clique held Sparta's offices throughout its stable centuries.

### Lesson verdicts
- `greek-miracle` — Claim: political argument arose in Greece because sovereignty was broken up and power placed "in the middle", not because of a special people, human-like gods, rivalry between cities or the phalanx. → The arrangement is the only rival present wherever the result appears and absent wherever it is missing. "A special people" cannot be tested at all, because no trait can be named that existed before the change it is meant to explain. Grade: **on balance**. Decided by: the lesson's own test cases (Mesopotamia, Phoenicia, Etruria) and its timeline exercise. Would be overturned by: Phoenician or Etruscan inscriptions showing rotating offices and public law, which would make their silence an accident of survival.
- `thucydides-power-and-justice` — Claim: the Athenian thesis at Melos, that justice arises only between equals in power and fairness is worthless to the weak. → As a description of how cities at war behaved, the thesis is borne out on both sides: Sparta did not come for Melos, Sparta killed the Plataeans (III.68), Athens destroyed Scione and Melos. As a claim that fairness protects no one, it fails on the record of 404. Besieged, the Athenians feared they would "suffer what they had done to the Melians" (Xen. *Hell.* II.2.3). Lysander restored the Melians (II.2.9). Sparta refused the Corinthian and Theban demand to destroy Athens, saying it would not "enslave a Greek city which had done great service" (II.2.19–20). Sparta's motive can also be read as interest, a counterweight to Thebes. Grade: **on balance**. Kept after review: the lesson stages a dispute, and the deciding passages are now checked. Would be overturned by: a reading of Sparta's restraint in 404 as pure balance-of-power calculation, with no weight on the appeal to Athens's past service.
- `decline-of-regimes` — Claim: Plato's chain (timocracy → oligarchy → democracy → tyranny) as a law every city follows, ending in democratic freedom breeding tyranny. → As a fixed sequence it fails. Aristotle's objection (V.12) holds: regimes change into any other, and tyranny often passes into another tyranny. The demagogue-to-tyrant mechanism has support for archaic Greece (V.5: "most of the ancient tyrants were originally demagogues"). It fails for the democracy Plato knew best. Athens's democracy was overthrown by oligarchs in 411 and 404 and ended under Macedon, never in a tyranny raised by the demos. Grade: **strong** against the law; **on balance** for the mechanism in archaic cases. Would be overturned by: a body of classical-era democracies that ended in tyrannies raised from the demos.
- `aristotle-political-animal-and-natural-slavery` — Claim: some men are slaves by nature, so mastery over them is just. → **Round 2: four claims, graded separately, hinged on Aristotle's own distinction between political rule (over the free and equal, ruling and ruled in turn: I.7, I.12, III.4, III.6) and despotic rule (mastery).**
  - (1) People differ in their capacity to lead, partly by birth → **holds. Grade: strong.** Twin studies: genetic factors account for about 30% of the variance in leadership role occupancy among 213 adult male twin pairs (CI .14–.44; Arvey et al., *Leadership Quarterly* 2006) and 32% among 196 female pairs (Arvey et al., *JAP* 2007), shared upbringing negligible. Meta-analyses: extraversion ρ = .31, conscientiousness .28, the five traits together R = .48 (Judge, Bono, Ilies & Gerhardt, *JAP* 2002, 73 samples); intelligence .21, .27 corrected for range restriction, "considerably lower than previously thought" (Judge, Colbert & Ilies, *JAP* 2004 — abstract only). Limits: occupancy is not effectiveness; heritability is a population statistic; correlations moderate.
  - (2) The most capable should rule → **common ground, not a dispute** (Plato, *Rep.* V 473c–d; Aristotle's aristocracy and offices by merit; Mosca; meritocratic liberals). No grade.
  - (3) Whoever actually rules is the most capable → **fails as a general rule; holds where there is competition and removability. Grade: on balance.** Plato's own premise (cities "never have rest from their evils" until philosophers rule); Aristotle's deviant regimes (III.7); Thucydides on Pericles' successors (II.65) and Cleon, "the most violent man at Athens … by far the most powerful with the commons" (III.36); hereditary succession, Marcus to Commodus — "from a kingdom of gold to one of iron and rust" (Dio 72[71].36.4, LacusCurtius, unproofread); Pareto's label drifting from capacity and Michels's selection for loyalty (`michels-and-pareto-oligarchy-and-elites`). Holds where office is contested and removable — the module verdict's mixed regime, Pareto's open elite (§2179). Would be overturned by: closed, hereditary or co-opting rulers who kept their capacity across generations.
  - (4) Some men are by nature fit to be owned (despotic rule) → **fails by Aristotle's own concessions. Grade: established** (the failure is internal and textual). Nature "often fails" to give the bodies and souls the theory needs, so no mark picks out a natural slave. War captives may be the wrong men, and the master may have the slavish soul. A justification that cannot pick out its object justifies no actual slavery. Would be overturned by: a criterion, stated in advance, that separates natural slaves from free men and does not reduce to "whoever was captured" or "whoever was born where".
  - Unequal capacity (1) grounds at most a claim to office among equals, in turn and removably (2–3); it cannot ground ownership of a man (4).
  - **Objection (author's review), required in the lesson at full strength:** "freedom among equals, slavery for outsiders" — the Greek/barbarian line held consistently (I.2 quoting Euripides; I.6; III.14). Answered: the Greeks enslaved Greeks (the helots; Melos, Thuc. V.116; Plato must forbid it, *Rep.* V 469b–c), so the historical premise **fails — established**; Aristotle concedes the line does not track nature (I.6 "some are slaves everywhere, others nowhere"; VII.7 "similar differences in the different tribes of Hellas"); and the principle is symmetric — Caesar sold 53,000 Atuatuci at one town (*BG* II.33), *slave* from *Slav* on the traditional derivation [secondary], Viking slaving [UNVERIFIED], Barbary corsairs took on one estimate 1–1.25 million Europeans 1530–1780 (R. C. Davis 2003 [secondary: press release and press report; method a replacement-rate extrapolation; disputed as possibly exaggerated]) — so as a justification of mastery it reduces to "whoever was born where" and **fails — strong**. "Beneficial to them": one clause, the record in module 14 (`auditing-the-case-for-colonialism`).

## Module 2 — rome-and-christianity: What holds a commonwealth together (its constitution, its justice, or its gods), and what happened to that bond when Rome's gods were replaced?

**Module verdict** (in `altar-of-victory`) — grade: **strong**
The documents of 384 bear out the civil-theology account of what held Rome together, and both sides held it. Symmachus argues the ancestral cult is the condition of Rome's existence. Ambrose does not answer that the commonwealth can do without a public worship; he answers that it must have the right one. He reasons do-ut-des himself (the good harvest of 384). He backs "the emperor serves God" with the threat that the emperor will find no bishop in his church. The Christian victory replaced Rome's god; it did not separate the city from worship. Augustine's later "the heavenly city preserves the diverse laws and customs of the nations" (XIX.17) describes neither party's conduct in 384. Natural law decides nothing in the case, because both sides claim it in opposite senses.
- Strongest objection to this verdict: the record shows bargaining (a boy emperor, a divided court, a usurper in Gaul), so the outcome may reflect power, not either theory. The bargaining was over the cult itself and lasted a decade (391, 393, Prudentius c. 402). Both sides acted as if the public worship were what the commonwealth turned on, so this does not overturn the verdict. It would be overturned by evidence that the court treated the altar as a pure patronage question, indifferent to doctrine.

### Lesson verdicts
- `polybius-anacyclosis-and-rome` — (a) The cycle of regimes as a natural law with a fixed order → **fails**. It survives only with "stage" read loosely, and Athens, Sparta and Rome do not follow its order. (b) The forecast (VI.57) that Rome's mixture would decay through wealth, rivalry for office and a flattered people → **borne out in outline**. A tribune carried a land law over the senate in 133 and was killed by senators. A century of rivalry for office followed and ended in one man's rule. The route differs from the forecast: generals with armies loyal to them ended the republic, not mob-rule. Grade: **strong** on both. Would be overturned by: an account on which the late Republic's collapse owed nothing to rivalry for office.
- `augustine-two-cities` — (a) The pagan charge of 410, that Rome fell because it abandoned its gods → **fails**. Rome was sacked under its old gods (the Gauls, c. 390 BC, Augustine's own example), and the Christian East lasted to 1453. (b) Augustine's proof that, by Scipio's definition, Rome was never a republic → **fails as a refutation on Scipio's terms**. It changes *ius* from the Romans' agreed right to worship of the true God. Grade: **strong** on both. Would be overturned by: for (a), evidence that Christianization of the West, unlike the East, removed something its defence depended on. That is a different and better argument than the one made in 410.

### No verdict
- `cicero-res-publica-and-natural-law` — natural law against convention is the course's long arc. Two partial results the brief may state: diversity of customs does not prove that justice is convention, and Laelius's "rule over those better off ruled" inherits natural slavery's failure.
- `constantine-and-representation` — carried to `schmitt-political-theology`. Note for that lesson: Byzantium and Charlemagne tell against the strong form.
- `hellenization-of-christianity` — trimmed from the draft. The dispute the lesson stages is between Harnack's kernel and Nietzsche's, and which kernel should have survived is a question of value the course's evidence cannot settle. The draft's verdict judged a different question, what the historical Jesus preached. The lesson may state the scholarly consensus on that (eschatological preaching, the line since Weiss 1892 and Schweitzer 1906) as background, with its minority, but not as a verdict on the staged dispute.

### Sources the briefs must add

## Module 3 — christendom-to-sovereignty: Must sovereignty be one and undivided, or can a mixed or limited state stand?

**Module verdict** (in `english-revolution-as-test`) — grade: **strong**
Bodin and Hobbes were right that a kingdom with no settled answer to "who decides" goes to war. In 1642 king and Parliament each claimed marks of sovereignty, neither could say where sovereignty lay, and the result was war, regicide and an army's rule. Hobbes's further claim (XXIX), that a mixed or limited monarchy cannot stand at all, fails on the same record. The settlement of 1660–61 restored a crown limited by the reforms of 1641, and after 1689 a monarchy limited by Parliament lasted for centuries. The record supports a narrower claim than the theory: there must be a settled last word (king-in-parliament), but a composite body can hold it, and limits can bind it.
- Strongest objection to this verdict: Hobbes allows sovereignty to an assembly, so king-in-parliament is a single sovereign and the theory is vindicated. This narrows the verdict but does not overturn it. The restored and post-1689 constitutions kept real division (the Crown's executive, Parliament's purse, independent courts), which XXIX calls fatal. It would be overturned by showing that the limits of 1660 and 1689 were nominal.

### Lesson verdicts
- `machiavelli-discourses-republic` — Claim: the quarrel of nobles and people was the source of Roman liberty (I.4), against the classical view of faction and Filmer's "ocean of blood". → Conflict channelled through institutions (tribunes, public accusation) produced liberty laws at little cost: eight or ten exiles and very few deaths from the Tarquins to the Gracchi (I.4). Conflict outside institutions destroyed the Republic, as Machiavelli concedes (I.37). Filmer's reading fits only the century after 133. Grade: **on balance**. Would be overturned by: evidence of large-scale bloodshed in the conflict of the orders before 133.
- `filmer-patriarcha` — Claim: political power is fatherly power inherited from Adam. → Fails at the step it needs most: no living king can be shown to be Adam's heir, and the fallback ("however acquired") collapses right into possession. Filmer's strongest point, that no one consents to being born into a polity, stands and is carried on. Grade: **established** (for the inheritance step). Would be overturned by: no conceivable evidence, since the heir cannot be identified.

### No verdict
- `aquinas-law-and-kingship` — reconstruction; the enforcement question is carried to Bodin and 1642.
- `machiavelli-effectual-truth` — the dispute with Aquinas largely dissolves into Machiavelli's own rule; carried to the Discourses.
- `bodin-sovereignty` — definition; judged in the module verdict.
- `hobbes-state-of-nature-and-covenant` — derivation; its empirical premise is judged in `rousseau-inequality-and-general-will`.
- `hobbes-sovereign-and-liberty` — tested in `english-revolution-as-test`.

## Module 4 — contract-revolution-reaction: Can a people frame a government for itself from principles, or does designed order fail, as Burke and Maistre said it would?

**Module verdict** (in `assignats`) — grade: **strong** against Maistre's general thesis; **on balance** for Burke's.
*Burke.* His forecasts of 1790 were mixed. The paper money on confiscated land collapsed, and a general took power: hits, **strong**. Two forecasts failed. He said the departments would not obey Paris; they became the instruments of Europe's most centralised administration. He also said France was "expunged out of the system of Europe" as a power and would take "much time" to recover (Speech on the Army Estimates, 9 Feb 1790). Within four years French armies were beating the coalition, and they dominated the Continent until 1814. Both misses are **strong**. His general argument, that no generation can redesign a whole society at once without a heavy price, is supported by the record. The same record also holds a rival cause for the worst of that price: the war of 1792, war finance and price controls. On the general argument: **on balance**.

*Maistre.* His forecast on his own target, the written constitution, was borne out for revolutionary France: the constitutions of 1791, 1793, 1795 and 1799 all fell. His general thesis, that nothing durable is made by deliberation and designed law is "paper", fails. The United States constitution of 1787 was deliberated, written and has lasted, which falsifies his Washington prophecy. The departments (1790), the metric system (1795) and the Civil Code also outlasted every regime. Grade: **strong**.

The answer to the module question: a people cannot redesign a whole society at one stroke without paying a heavy price, but designed institutions and designed constitutions can last. Most of France's lasting designs were made under one ruler, on the old monarchy's centralisation, and were not its constitutions.
- Strongest objection to this verdict: the institutions that lasted were Napoleon's, made by a "legislator who says fiat", so Maistre can claim them as proof that only a single will founds. This narrows the verdict but does not overturn it. The departments and the metric system were assembly work, and the American constitution was made by a convention and ratified by state conventions. It would be overturned if the departments and the metric system turned out to continue designs of the old regime, and the American case turned out to be, as Maistre's "four points" claim, inherited English law with nothing designed in it.

### Lesson verdicts
- `hume-against-the-original-contract` — Claim: legitimate government rests on consent of the governed, given at the origin or tacitly by residence. → As history the original contract fails: the governments Hume surveys began in conquest and usurpation. Tacit consent by residence fails too (the sleeping passenger). Hume's further point holds: allegiance and promise-keeping rest on the same ground, the general interest, so deriving one from the other gains nothing. What survives is consent as a hypothetical test, carried to Rawls. Grade: **strong**. Would be overturned by: a body of states founded and kept by consent that individuals actually gave. Ratification in 1787–88, the nearest case, bound non-voters and later generations.
- `rousseau-inequality-and-general-will` — Claim: natural man is solitary and peaceable, and violence and domination are made by society. → Each author is right about a different stage. (a) The solitary, pre-social natural man fails; humans have always lived in groups. Grade: **established**. (b) The state lowers lethal violence. Prehistoric bands and tribes sit near the phylogenetic baseline of about 2% of deaths, chiefdoms are higher, and states are lower (Gómez et al. 2016). That paper also reports high lethal violence in present-day bands and tribes, and among mobile foragers much killing is personal rather than war (Fry & Söderberg 2013). Hobbes's point holds. Grade: **strong**. (c) Inequality rises with property, storage and settlement. Wealth transmission and inequality are "substantial" among pastoral and agricultural societies and "limited among horticultural and foraging peoples" (Borgerhoff Mulder et al. 2009). That is Rousseau's sequence, and Rousseau puts the "horrible state of war" in nascent society, after property (Second Discourse, Part II, staged l.468). Grade: **strong** for inequality; **on balance** for organised violence. Would be overturned by: for (b), a consistent finding that states have higher rates of violent death than non-state societies; for (c), foraging societies with heritable wealth inequality on the pastoral scale.
- `maistre-and-bonald` — Claim: Maistre's doctrine is a first draft of fascism. → The strong version, Maistre as a source of fascism, fails. His influence ran to ultramontanes and the Action française, not to Rome or Berlin. His system is centred on God and anti-nationalist, and the charge leaves "fascism" undefined. Two narrower points stand: the glorification of war as "mysterious and divine", and the decisionism that passes through Donoso to Schmitt. His argument against the contract (a promise presupposes a society in which promises bind) is correct, and the charge's own author concedes it. Grade: **strong** against the source version; **on balance** for the narrower affinity. Would be overturned by: documented reading of Maistre among the founders of Italian Fascism or National Socialism.
- `herder-and-fichte-volk-and-nation` — Claim: Fichte's step from Herder's plurality of peoples to rank: German is a living original language and the neo-Latin languages are "dead at the root". → The ranking fails. Fichte's reply answers the charges of racial nationalism and conquest, not the charge of rank. The "living/dead" criterion has no stated test, and his own example cuts against it. *Humanität*, his specimen of a dead foreign word, is the word Herder made the end of history. *Menschlichkeit*, his living alternative, was formed in the 16th century as a translation of Latin *hūmānitās* (Pfeifer via DWDS). Grade: **strong**. Would be overturned by: a measurable sense in which speakers of a continuously inherited language think more "livingly".

### No verdict
- `locke-property-and-consent` — reconstruction; consent is judged in Hume, property in Rousseau and module 14, resistance in Burke.
- `burke-reflections` — his forecasts are tested in `assignats`.
- `hegel-ethical-life-and-the-state` — reconstruction; carried to Strauss and Marx.
- `donoso-cortes-on-dictatorship` — his dilemma is tested on 1799 in `assignats` and fully in `article-48-weimar`.

## Module 5 — democracy-and-its-critics: Were the nineteenth-century critics right that a mass franchise would endanger liberty, property and stable government?

**Module verdict** (in `british-franchise`) — grade: **strong** that the fragility forecast failed for Britain; **strong** that the taxation forecast arrived; **on balance** on the mechanism of extension; **open** on tutelage.
On the British record the critics were right about how power would move inside a democracy and wrong about stability.
- *Direction and administration.* Tocqueville was right about the direction: the vote was never taken back once widened. He was also right about the growth of administration: schooling in 1870, pensions in 1908, national insurance in 1911. He was wrong about the tempo, which took ninety-six years and paused four times.
- *Mechanism.* Both accounts are partly borne out. Pressure from below moved the decision in 1832: the Swing riots shifted voters toward pro-reform candidates (Aidt & Franck 2015). In 1866–67 it made reform unavoidable. Party competition set the timing and the terms in 1867 and 1884, through aristocratic and Conservative ministries; that fits Lecky's mechanism and Tocqueville's Maryland aside. Pressure from below forced the question; party competition answered it.
- *Power and taxation.* Maine and Lecky were right that power would pass to the Cabinet and the party machine, and that graduated taxation would come. Lecky's danger arrived, and his remedy, the Lords' veto, was the institution removed in 1911 to let it through. Whether that was the "catastrophe" he feared is a value judgment. His admirers point to the long-run growth of taxation; his critics point to a democracy that taxed the rich and did not break. The lesson states both readings.
- *Fragility.* Their central forecast failed for Britain. Popular government did not break down after 1867, 1884, 1918 or 1928, and it survived two world wars. Carlyle's forecast was framed so that no outcome could refute it.
- *Tutelage.* Tocqueville's counterweights appeared without a legislator: unions, friendly societies, chapels, a free press, the courts. Several were later absorbed or displaced. Friendly societies lost their role as approved societies after the National Insurance Act 1946, and chapel attendance collapsed [secondary: standard history; bind before use]. Whether the welfare state is soft despotism is **open**, carried to `deneen-post-liberalism`. What would settle it: evidence on whether associational life and electoral turnover shrank as the state's functions grew.
- Strongest objection to this verdict: Britain was exceptional (old parliamentary habits, no defeat or revolution), so one survival does not refute a general claim. This limits the verdict to Britain before 1945 and does not overturn it. The critics made dated forecasts about Britain in particular, and those failed. The Continent is tested at Weimar.

### Lesson verdicts
- `carlyle-heroes` — Claim: history is the work of great men, a society rests on hero-worship, and a democracy is ruled by quacks. → **Fails as an argument**; the diagnosis of the Condition of England stands as description. ## Module 6 — crowd-race-nation: Does the crowd, the race or the will of its members explain what a nation is and how mass politics works?

**Module verdict** (in `dreyfus-affair-as-test`) — grade: **strong**
The Affair shows that the module's theories explain the passions of mass politics but not its outcome.
- *Race.* The racial theory fails outright. Barrès's inference, "capable by race", pointed to Dreyfus. The bordereau was Esterhazy's, a French officer of Christian family.
- *Crowd.* The crowd theory explains the noise (the one-word cry, the prestige of the uniform) but not the decision. The anti-Dreyfusard mass was larger and louder for years, and it lost. Documents decided the case, along with officers inside the closed institutions (Picquart, Cuignet), the Cour de cassation and a parliamentary republic. Le Bon's own better line is borne out: the caste, the General Staff, held its verdict for twelve years and yielded only to courts.
- *Integral nationalism* is refuted by its own criterion. Judged by consequences for the nation, Henry's forgery did the army more harm than any revision.
- *Renan.* The outcome fits Renan's civic nation: the Dreyfus family had opted for France, and France restored him by court and statute. No plebiscite was held, so the case supports his principle only, not his procedure.
- Strongest objection to this verdict: the nationalists' reply about the price. The *affaire des fiches* (1904) sorted the officer corps by religion and opinion, so justice for one man cost the army. The verdict is about what decided the case, not about whether the decision was costly, so the objection does not overturn it. The price question is **open**. What would settle it: the French command's performance in August–September 1914, traced to officers promoted or blocked under the *fiches*.

## Module 7 — crisis-of-liberal-europe: If a minority always rules and the political is friend against enemy, can a liberal parliamentary democracy govern and defend itself?

**Module verdict** (in `article-48-weimar`) — grade: **strong** on the diagnosis; **on balance** on the remedy as practised; **on balance** (1930–32) and **open** (after July 1932) on whether the theory helped produce the collapse.
Weimar does not show that liberal parliamentary government is impossible. It shows a parliament that would not govern, an exception with no criterion, and a constitution its enemies abolished by its own procedures.
- *The diagnosis held.* From March 1930 the Reichstag could block but could not form a majority will. The Reichstag sat and legislated less each year: 98 laws, 5 Article 48 decrees and 94 sitting days in 1930; 34, 44 and 41 in 1931; 5, 66 and 13 in 1932 [secondary: citing Bracher 1980 p. 129; bind]. Michels's party leaderships decided the SPD's toleration of Brüning. Schmitt's 1932 warning came true: the "equal chance" that the constitution gave its enemies was used to abolish it, through Article 48 and an Article 76 two-thirds vote. Grade: **strong**.
- *The commissarial remedy, as practised in 1930–32, had no criterion that kept it commissarial.* Decrees were drafted in ministries, a president signed, a circle chose the chancellor, and no end was ever set. The Reichstag could have demanded repeal under §3 in any month and did not. On 20 July 1932 the Prussian ministry yielded to a single police officer and went to court. In October the court split the decision and derived part of it from a norm, which Schmitt says cannot be done, and the Reich obeyed the half it lost. Grade: **on balance**. The remedy Schmitt urged at the end, an emergency regime that would keep the NSDAP out (the Schleicher circle), was never tried: Hindenburg appointed Hitler by the parliamentary route.
- *Did the theory help produce the collapse?* For 1930–32, **on balance** yes: a majority without both NSDAP and KPD was still arithmetically possible, and the decree regime taught parliament not to govern. After July 1932 the NSDAP and KPD held 319 of 608 seats [secondary: standard election figures], a negative majority, so no government could command a majority without one of them. For that period the question is **open**.
- Elsewhere the module's own measure holds. Mosca's juridical defence by competing organised forces, and the competitive account of democracy, survive. Parliamentary government lasted through the same years in Britain, Scandinavia and the Low Countries.
- Strongest objection to this verdict: the presidential regime was the only alternative to collapse, so the theory described a necessity. That is why the causal half is graded on balance for 1930–32 and open after July 1932. It does not touch the diagnosis. Would overturn the on-balance half: evidence that no majority for any government was politically available after September 1930.

- `michels-and-pareto-oligarchy-and-elites` — "Who says organisation says oligarchy" as an iron law, and Pareto's circulation of elites → **holds as a strong tendency inside mass organisations; fails as an iron law; does not decide outcomes between parties.** Michels's own continuity data show a tendency. ## Module 8 — conservative-revolution: Could the radical right's revolution against liberalism be kept conservative, and did its doctrines give its thinkers any ground to refuse National Socialism in 1933?

**Module verdict** (in `the-right-in-1933`) — grade: **on balance**
The doctrines gave reasons on both sides, and what decided each author's choice was a step the doctrine left open.
- *Schmitt.* His doctrine contained a criterion against a legal revolution. *Legalität und Legitimität* (July 1932) argued that a constitution may deny the "equal chance" to parties that would use legality to abolish it. It also contained the step that makes whoever has succeeded the source of law: *auctoritas non veritas facit legem*, read as accepting whoever holds authority. In 1933–34 the second step won. His article of 1 August 1934, declaring the June killings law, is that step applied to its hardest case. **Strong**, on his own texts.
- *Heidegger.* Historicity gave no criterion for telling a people's destiny from a movement that claims to be one. **On balance**.
- *Spengler* refused. His Table III dates Caesarism to 2000–2200, so his morphology did offer a ground for denying that the man of 1933 was the Caesar. The course has not shown that he used it. His stated grounds in *Jahre der Entscheidung* (contempt for a mass party, "ethos, not zoology") are flagged "verify" in the brief [UNVERIFIED].
- *Evola*'s doctrine itself predicted his distance: his law ranks every mass movement as rule by the last caste.
- The finding is narrower than "the right had no norm": a doctrine that makes the successful decision the source of law has no ground to refuse a revolution once it has succeeded. Schmitt's had that clause and Heidegger's had nothing in its place.
- Strongest objection to this verdict: the authors are not answerable for the use made of their words, and the liberal parties and the Centre also voted for the Enabling Act. The first does not overturn the verdict, which is about what the doctrines contained. The second is a fair demand of one standard: the same question, whether liberal doctrine gave its holders a ground to refuse on 23 March 1933, must be put to the liberal parties' vote, and the brief must stage it. Would overturn the verdict: evidence that the Conservative Revolution's main theorists, read as a whole, refused on grounds their doctrines supplied. ## Module 9 — postwar-conservatism: After fascism, do the rebuilt right's critiques of abstract rights hold against the postwar order's founding text, the 1948 Declaration?

**Module verdict** (in `udhr-read-against-the-right`) — grade: **on balance**
The critiques hit the missing foundation and partly hit the social articles. They miss the civil core.
- *Foundation.* The drafters chose to leave the foundation out, so Strauss's "list without a ground" describes the text accurately. **Strong**.
- *Civil core.* Arts. 3–21 are Burke's "real rights" and Oakeshott's "availability of a procedure", written down with the tribunals that must enforce them (Arts. 8, 10). They became justiciable: the European Convention of 1950 has a court, and the 1966 ICCPR has an individual-complaint procedure. **Strong**.
- *Social articles.* The critics are right that Arts. 22–27 depend on resources more than the civil core does. The 1966 split recorded that judgment: the ICESCR asks only for "progressive realization" (Art. 2(1)). **On balance**. They are wrong that the social articles name no duty-bearer and cannot be made claims. Art. 22 names "national effort and international co-operation", which puts the duty on the state. Since 5 May 2013 an Optional Protocol (GA Res. 63/117, 2008) gives individuals a complaint procedure under the ICESCR, within resource limits [secondary: ICJ statement and encyclopaedia; bind to the UN treaty record]. Kirk's test is also wrong on one of its own examples: the right to marry is a liberty, not a claim on anyone. The civil core is not costless either, since Arts. 8 and 10 need tax-funded courts. The difference is one of degree. **On balance**.
- Strongest objection to this verdict: courts that enforce social rights (South Africa 1996, s. 27) do so only "within available resources", which is the critics' point. That is why the resource-dependence half stands. Would be overturned by: social claims routinely enforced against a named duty-bearer regardless of resources.

But the Conservative Revolution's vocabulary and its men crossed over directly, and the renaming of 1950 was a relabelling. The Burke side has no text in the dossier. Grade: **on balance**. Would be overturned by: evidence that fascist ideologues took their arguments, not just their words, from Maistre or Burke.
- `jouvenel-on-power` — Power has grown in extent almost without interruption, fastest under regimes that claim to give power to the people.
  - (a) The state's extent grew enormously across the democratising era. Government spending averaged about 10.8% of output around 1870 and 41.9% in 1980 across fourteen industrial countries (Tanzi 2005, from Tanzi & Schuknecht 2000). Grade: **established**.
  - (b) As a law of near-uninterrupted growth the thesis fails after about 1990. Several democracies cut government spending as a share of GDP for a decade or more with no war: Canada 53.3% (1992) to 38.5% (2007), New Zealand 53.0% (1991) to 36.2% (2004), the Netherlands 56.4% (1995) to about 45% (2001–07) (IMF *Public Finances in Modern History*). Caveats: a share can fall because GDP grows, coverage changes in some series (Sweden's 1993 jump is a series break and is not used), and spending is one measure of extent. Grade: **on balance**.
  - (c) Whether Power's appetite or voters' demand drove the growth is **open**. The series cannot tell them apart, and the draft's "the objection confirms the mechanism" is withdrawn. What would settle it: cases where the franchise widened and spending did not follow, or the reverse.
  - (d) Whether the "protected subject" is less free than the subject of the old intermediate powers is a value question. **Open**, left to argument.
- `strauss-natural-right-and-history` — The two modern grounds for rejecting natural right (radical historicism; Weber's fact–value distinction as proof that values are beyond reason) refute themselves → **holds against radical historicism; does not establish natural right.** "All thought is the product of its time" is offered as a trans-historical truth, and Strauss concedes the rest ("a wish is not a fact"; the teleology dilemma unsolved). The same self-exemption test settles the question carried from `oakeshott-rationalism-in-politics`. Grade: **strong**. Would be overturned by: a historicism that claims no trans-historical validity for itself and still rules out natural right.
- `voegelin-gnosticism` — (i) Modern mass movements descend in one textual and causal line from Joachim → **not shown.** Grade: **on balance**. (ii) The vagueness objection, that "gnosis" takes in everything → **fails**. The typology discriminates: three faculties, three variants, and England and America expressly excluded. (iii) The movements borrowed eschatological symbols, the three-age schema of the Third Reich and a classless end of history → **holds**. Grade: **on balance**. The stronger claim, that the movements share the structure of an immanentized eschaton, is **open**. Its independent support (Cohn 1957, Löwith 1949) is not in the course. The 1974 sentence on "the one line" is reported as a revision of Voegelin's plan of history; whether it touches the Gnosticism diagnosis is disputed, and it is not decisive evidence either way.
- `voegelin-gnosticism` **(forecast check)** — Condorcet's *Esquisse* (1795), Tenth Epoch, the course's example of progressivism. Voegelin's objection to "progress as a law" stands as argument. The concrete forecasts can be checked.
  - (a) Longer life, universal instruction, the end of slavery and colonial rule, and legal equality of the sexes → **held**. World life expectancy rose from 28.5 (1800) to 73.2 (2023), and literacy from 12% (1820) to 87.7% (2024) (OWID). Grade: **strong**.

- `burnham-managers-and-the-suicide-of-the-west` — (1941) The managers will replace owners, and the managerial powers will win the war → **the war forecasts failed; the description of the managed corporation and the administrative state held.** Grade: **established**. (1964) Liberalism is the ideology of Western suicide → **not settled by 1989–91. Open.** Burnham's own test reads: "if the contraction of the past fifty years should cease and be reversed, then the ideology of liberalism, deprived of its primary function, will fade away" (`texts/burnham/suicide-of-the-west.txt` l.2573). His "liberalism" is a specific ideology (his 19 propositions, guilt, accommodation in place of rollback), not liberal democracy. The reversal of 1989–91 came under governments that had rejected détente in his sense. "Liberal states won" scores the test in the wrong sense of the word, and the growth of former imperial states is beside a thesis he defined by territory ruled. What would settle it: a series on elite opinion on Burnham's propositions (rollback against containment, decolonisation and guilt), 1964–2000, to test whether those positions weakened before or after the reversal.
- `burnham-managers-and-the-suicide-of-the-west` **(forecast check, round 2)** — (1941) "there is good reason to believe that under managerial economy there will be a greater total output of material goods in relation to the total population than under capitalism" (`texts/burnham/the-managerial-revolution.txt` l.3685–3692) → **failed. Market economies outperform planned ones. Grade: established.** Matched pairs (Maddison Project 2023, GDP per head, 2011 $): South Korea $13,874 vs North $2,456 (1990), $41,321 vs $1,569 (2022), no measured North series before 1990; East Germany about 56% of West in 1950 and "about 56 per cent" through 1989, against 103% per-capita national income in 1936 (Sleifer 2003/2006; other estimates differ in level); China $1,744 (1978) → $15,977 (2018), 5.7% a year on the Wu-revised series (8.4% official), against 2.8% a year 1950–78; India $2,062 (1991) → $7,294 (2019), 4.6% a year, against 1.4% a year 1950–80. ## Module 10 — equality-after-rawls: Does unequal natural talent make unequal outcomes unjust, and is the demand for equality principle or envy?

**Module verdict** (in `the-cognitive-elite`) — grade: **on balance**
- *The factual premise.* In the NLSY data a test score taken in youth predicts adult outcomes more steeply than the parental-SES index does. Sibling comparisons, which hold shared family background constant, leave the estimates nearly unchanged (Korenman & Winship). So a measured ability predicts outcomes within families. How much of that ability is native endowment is undecided: sibling designs do not remove non-shared environment or schooling's effect on the score. Korenman and Winship also find background matters at least as much as IQ. **Strong**.
- *The data choose no normative conclusion.* Each reading adds a premise the data do not supply.
- *On the normative question.* The decision-theoretic route to the difference principle fails (`rawls-theory-of-justice`). Rawls's other route, from reciprocity, stability and self-respect, is not tested in the course: **open**. The envy charge cannot defeat a principle (`ressentiment-and-envy`). What every side in the module accepts is a guaranteed social minimum. Impartial choosers in the experiments chose it, and *The Bell Curve*'s authors arrive at income supplements and "valued places". **On balance**.
- *(Round 2) Envy, strengthened.* Envy is a measured motive for redistribution: compassion β 0.28–0.39, self-interest 0.18–0.30, envy 0.10–0.14 in four countries; envy alone predicted preferring a tax that took more from the rich and gave the poor less (14–18% chose it); a taste for fairness "unreliable and of far smaller magnitude" (Sznycer et al. 2017); procedural fairness null again, envy held as malicious envy, in the British replication (Lin & Bates 2021). **On balance.** It decides nothing about what is owed (**strong**).
- *(Round 2) The merit case against heavy redistribution, at full strength and graded.* Nozick's Wilt Chamberlain ("Taxation of earnings from labor is on a par with forced labor", ASU p. 169): **strong** against strict end-state patterns; **open** against a floor or difference principle levied by general rules announced in advance. Hayek's mirage (social justice "meaningless" for a spontaneous order [secondary: via Lister 2011]): **open** against rewards set by authority; withdrawn by its author against a floor (the "assured minimum income" outside the market, LLL II: 87 [secondary]). Murray's *Losing Ground* (1984) account of 1965–80 [secondary: via IRP 1985]: **fails, on balance** — real AFDC benefits fell almost 30% in the median state 1970–80 while the share of children in female-headed families rose from 14% to nearly 20% and the share on AFDC held at 12% (Ellwood & Summers, NBER WP 1711); the narrower incentive claim is **open**. "Heavy redistribution makes a nation poor": **fails, on balance** — Denmark, Finland, Sweden and Norway take 40–45% of GDP in tax (OECD 2024; US 25.6%) and stand at 72–124% of US GDP per head (WDI 2025, PPP), but a cross-section is not a counterfactual, Norway has oil, and the US is richer still. Jouvenel by Ref. Overall: against strict equality and authority-set rewards **strong**; against a floor **fails** (Hayek's minimum, Murray's own $10,000 grant); against the difference principle's further claim **open**. The floor is common ground from the merit side too.
- Strongest objection to this verdict: the AFQT absorbs schooling and the SES index is crude, so "talent" is partly background. The verdict now states that limit. It would be overturned by within-family designs in which the score's effect disappeared.

### Lesson verdicts
- `rawls-theory-of-justice` — (a) Maximin alone yields the difference principle → **fails**, and Rawls concedes it: the maximin rule "alone cannot be used to justify the difference principle" (JF 43n, 94–95). He uses maximin mainly against average utility and for the first principle. Grade: **established**. (b) Rational parties would choose the difference principle over average utility with a guaranteed floor (a "mixed conception", which Rawls concedes is "much more difficult to argue against", TJ 316/278 rev.) → **open** on the course's evidence. Rawls rests this on reciprocity, publicity, stability and self-respect (TJ §29, §49; JF §§34–38, §42.3), and the brief must stage those at full strength. In the Frohlich–Oppenheimer experiments (76 groups, 380 subjects, four locations, unanimity always reached) the groups chose average income with a floor. That is evidence of what actual people prefer, not a test of Rawls's reciprocity argument. What would settle (b): whether the "discouraged and depressed underclass" Rawls predicts under restricted utility (JF §42.3) forms under floor-plus-average arrangements, an empirical question about welfare-state capitalism.
- `ressentiment-and-envy` — (a) Egalitarian principles are envy institutionalised, as an argument against any principle → **fails**. Both sides concede the logic: Schoeck says a motive "neither refutes nor proves" a cause, and Rawls's parties are stipulated non-envious. Grade: **strong**. (b) Envy is a real motive behind support for redistribution → **holds, as the weakest of three**. In four countries dispositional envy predicts support alongside compassion and self-interest, and a taste for fairness does not (Sznycer et al. 2017). Compassion is two to three times stronger (β 0.28–0.39 against envy's 0.10–0.14). Envy did not differ by US party, and compassion alone predicted personally aiding the poor. A UK replication confirms compassion, self-interest and malicious envy (β 0.13–0.15 against compassion's 0.40), with dispositional envy non-significant until split, and again finds no fairness effect (Lin & Bates 2021). Round 2 adds: in the same studies envy alone predicted preferring a tax that took more from the rich and gave the poor less (14–18% of respondents chose it), and a taste for fairness had effects "unreliable and of far smaller magnitude"; in the replication procedural fairness was null again. Envy is a measured, replicated motive, the smallest of three — about a third to a half of compassion's weight. This supports "envy is one motive". It does not support "egalitarianism is envy". Grade: **on balance** (dispositional envy failed the replication's first study and returned only as malicious envy; the scales measure a disposition; the choice item is hypothetical).
  - **Objection (author's review), required in the lesson at full strength:** "the demand for redistribution is ressentiment, so it should be refused" (Nietzsche, *Antichrist* 57; Scheler, "only he who is afraid of losing demands equality"; Schoeck's growth thesis). Answered: the motive is real (**on balance**) but does not decide justice (**strong**, verdict (a)); the same transfers were proposed without egalitarian motive — Bismarck's social insurance (1883, 1884, 1889), announced in 1881 beside "the repression of Social Democratic excesses" as "guarantees of internal peace" and defended in 1884 as "the complement to the Socialist Law"; Charles Murray's *In Our Hands* (2006), a $10,000 annual grant to every adult, for civil society and to "compensate the most unlucky". As an argument for refusal: **fails — strong**.

## Module 11 — civilizations: Are civilizations the units whose borders organise conflict, and does Huntington's clash survive the record since 1993?

**Module verdict** (in `testing-the-clash`) — grade: **on balance**
The theory was checked and gave a mixed answer. Civilizations are real units of identity. The clash describes the Islam-centred conflicts of the period well and the rest of world politics badly.
- *Frequency* (conflict across civilizational lines more common than within them) → **fails, on balance**. The dyadic tests find within-line conflict at least as common and no post-1989 shift (Russett, Oneal & Cox 2000, on disputes 1950–92). Tusicisny (2004), on UCDP conflict-years 1946–2001, also finds the trends "unaffected by the end of the Cold War". Most of the frequency data predate 1993, hence on balance.
- *Islam at the centre* of cross-line conflict after the Cold War → **holds**. "The majority of intercivilizational conflict-years during the post-Cold War period have involved Islamic groups", while Sinic–West conflict stayed marginal (Tusicisny 2004). Islam was involved in a disproportionate share of civil wars from 1940 to 2000 (Toft 2007, summary). The record after 2001 (Afghanistan, Iraq, the Islamic State wars, the Sahel, jihadist terrorism in Europe) runs the same way, but the course has not bound a post-2001 count. Grade: **strong** for the period the studies cover.
- *Escalation* → **open**. Huntington did specify it: intra-civilizational conflicts "are likely to be less intense and less likely to expand than conflicts between civilizations" (1993, staged l.195). Tusicisny finds within-line conflicts less likely to escalate after the Cold War, while between-line intensity stayed at Cold War levels; he also finds no significant link between civilizational difference and duration. What would settle it: a post-2001 UCDP comparison of intensity and spread across and within lines.
- *Russia and Ukraine.* The corollary he stated as a test ("the likelihood of violence between Ukrainians and Russians should be low") is **refuted**: annexation in 2014 and the largest European interstate war since 1945 in 2022. Grade: **strong**. The same article's fault line "separating the more Catholic western Ukraine from Orthodox eastern Ukraine" (l.170) fits the geography of 2014 (Crimea, the Donbas). It fails the alignment of 2022, when Orthodox and Catholic Ukrainians fought Russia together and the country turned West. **On balance** against.
- *Bosnia* as a test of alignment → fits the first phase (Iran, Saudi Arabia and volunteers rallying to the Bosnians, as he records) and fails after the 1994 Washington Agreement, when Western power was used for a Muslim-led government against Orthodox Serbs. **On balance**.
- Strongest objection to this verdict: from one side, the dyadic tests measure the wrong thing and the jihadist wars are the clash. From the other, the Islam-centred conflicts are explained by state weakness, oil and outbidding (Toft's own mechanism), not by civilization. The verdict meets the first by grading the Islam finding strong and frequency only on balance. It meets the second by leaving the cause outside the claim graded. Would be overturned by: a post-2001 count showing cross-line conflicts no more Islam-centred, and no more intense, than within-line ones.

### Lesson verdicts
- `toynbee-challenge-and-response` — (a) Civilizations arise by a creative minority's response to a challenge of the right severity, as a law → **fails**. The golden mean makes it unfalsifiable: any failure is recoded as a challenge too hard or too easy. Grade: **strong**. (b) Toynbee's refutation of Gobineau's racial theory of civilization → **holds**: civilizations arose independently among peoples Gobineau's scheme cannot credit, which meets Gobineau's own pledge that one counter-instance falsifies the whole. Grade: **established**. Reaches Gobineau's claim only.

Freedom House records twenty consecutive years of net decline, with Not Free countries rising from 45 to 59 (*FIW 2026*). ## Module 12 — why-the-west: Why did the West pull ahead: an old, unique inheritance, or late luck inside a shared Eurasian exchange?

**Module verdict** (in `the-borrowing-ledger`) — grade: **strong** that the broad late-luck view fails on timing; **on balance** on when the lead opened; **strong** that there was no continuous Western lead from the Greeks; **on balance** that Europe's trailing around 1000 was the collapse after Rome, a shock and not a deficiency; **open** on cause, with the genetic candidate (Clark) among the five.
Neither pure answer survives.
- *Late luck.* The broad late-luck view, that the gap opened after 1800, fails for China as a whole. China "led the world in living standards during the Northern Song dynasty, but had fallen behind Italy by 1300". Its output per head fell through the Qing, and "by 1750 the gap was too large to be bridged by regional variation within China" (Broadberry, Guan & Li). **Strong**. Pomeranz's own claim, regional parity between the Yangzi delta and England or Holland around 1750, is **open** (see below).
- *When.* The lead in output per head opened in the late Middle Ages, in Italy first, and became decisive in north-western Europe in the early modern period. **On balance**. The lead in recorded science opens from about 1400 on Murray's count, with the caveat that pre-1400 Islamic and Chinese work is where source bias would bite.
- *Ancient inheritance.* A continuous Western lead from the Greeks, "almost always ahead by a large margin", fails. Writing, cities, law codes and mathematics came first in Mesopotamia and Egypt: proto-cuneiform at Uruk c. 3350–3200 BC; Hammurabi r. c. 1792–1750 BC; Old Babylonian mathematics c. 1800–1600 BC [secondary, uncontested dates]. The Greek alphabet is the Phoenician one adapted. Rome was of one order with Han China: the Han census of 2 AD gives 57.7 or 59.6 million (Scheidel 2012), against 44–70 million Romans (Scheidel & Friesen 2009); Roman output per head was $610–700 against Maddison's conjectural $450 for China, a Roman edge inside the uncertainty (**on balance**). Around 1000 the Islamic world and Song China were ahead (Maddison, conjectural: Iraq and Iran $650, China $466, western Europe $427; Pamuk & Shatzmiller 2014 [abstract]; Broadberry, Guan & Li), and Europe borrowed heavily from them (paper and numerals secure transfers, gunpowder probable). **Strong**.
- *Why Europe trailed around 1000: a shock, not a deficiency.* The western Roman economy collapsed. Italy fell from $809 per head in year 1 to $450 in 1000, and western Europe from $576 to $427 (Maddison, conjectural). European lead emissions in Greenland ice peaked under the Empire and were "low for >500 years" from the Antonine plague (McConnell et al., *PNAS* 2018, abstract). Ward-Perkins (2005) and Wickham (2005) describe the material collapse [both read through reviews]. The recovery was fast: western Europe went from $427 in 1000 to $771 in 1500, Italy from $450 to $1,100. **On balance**: the per-head series are conjectural, the books were read through reviews, and the lead proxy falls from the second century.
- *What is true in the claim of an old lead.* In political thought and philosophy the lead is Greek (`greek-miracle`, on balance). In science it opens c. 1400 (strong). In wealth it opens c. 1300 in Italy and becomes decisive in the north-west in the early modern period (on balance). The margin has widened since. What Europe did with its borrowings (the mechanised paper mill, corned powder, print that no one could suppress) is where the difference lies.
- *Objection (author's review):* "The West was almost always ahead by a large margin — Greeks, Romans, a hit from Rome's fall, a fast recovery and a widening margin." The shock and the recovery are conceded and enter the verdict. "Almost always ahead by a large margin" fails on the pre-Greek Near East, Rome and Han, and the Abbasid world and the Song.
- *Cause.* There are five candidates. Fragmentation (Landes), kinship (Henrich), a religious ethic (Weber) and an inheritance of institutions from the high Middle Ages (the Church's kinship rules, corporations, universities, chartered towns) are each partly supported, and none is decisive. The fifth is genetic selection (Clark, *A Farewell to Alms* 2007; Cochran & Harpending 2009): the rich out-bred the poor in England 1200–1800 and spread bourgeois traits. Its parts:
  - (i) The rich had more surviving children: **strong**. But the advantage was not English. It is found in southern Germany, Austria, France, Sweden and Switzerland (Allen, *JEL* 2008), so it cannot explain why England came first.
  - (ii) Status persists at a rate that fits an additive genetic model: a correlation falling by a factor of only 0.79 a generation, 422,374 English people, 1600–2022 (Clark, *PNAS* 2023). The pattern is **strong**; the genetic mechanism is **not shown**. Clark: "there is no proof here that additive genetic transmission causes social status". Non-genetic transmission, wealth above all, may mimic the pattern (Benning et al. 2023–25, abstracts; Breen 2015). His surname data give the same persistence in China, Japan and Korea (Clark 2014).
  - (iii) Ancient DNA shows selection on alleles associated with test scores and schooling in West Eurasia, but "largely driven by selection before ~2000 BP, after which χ tends toward zero" (Akbari et al., *Nature* 2026).
  - (iv) East Asian school-test scores are at or above Europe's (PISA 2022 [secondary]), and China and Japan fell far below their IQ-predicted income in 1820 and 1900 (`geography-or-institutions`, strong). China did not industrialise first.
  - (v) Regimes and growth change within one population faster than selection can act (module 13, R1).
  The genetic contribution to the West's rise is **open**. Its England-specific form, that selection unique to England explains why England came first, **fails, on balance**. The whole cause is **open**. What would settle it: for the four cultural and institutional candidates, within-Europe designs that separate the timing of kin-dissolution, state competition and literacy, and show which predicts where innovation was kept. For the genetic candidate, polygenic scores from dated remains or documented lineages in England, the continent and China, 1000–1800, rising first in England; and within-family genomic designs on Clark's lineages that separate genes from inherited wealth.
- Strongest objection to this verdict: Pomeranz's reply that wage series compare unlike workers and miss women's textile earnings. It narrows the gap but does not overturn the timing finding, because the national-accounts estimates do not rest on wage series.

## Module 13 — heredity-and-civilization: Are the measured gaps between peoples, within the US and between nations, caused by heredity, environment, geography or institutions, and in what proportion?

Bound by `research/heredity-evidence-review.md`. Neither audit found shading here; the one change is a scope clause in `gould-and-harden-on-heredity` (a).

**Module verdict** (in `national-data-audited`) — grade: **open** on the genetic share; **established** on the two limits; **strong** on what the national tables can carry; **strong** that regimes and freedom are not explained mainly by genes; **strong** that culture persists across generations and is not put on at will.
- *The first three premises of the hereditarian argument largely hold.* The tests measure the same abilities in both US groups and predict outcomes without bias against the lower-scoring group. Measured intelligence is substantially heritable within Western populations. The US gap is real: about 0.75 SD in recent child norms, about 1 SD in adult norms.
- *The fourth step, from a real gap to a genetic one, has not been made.* No design has measured a genetic contribution free of confounding. Measured environment does not account for the whole gap either: parental education and income leave about 6 points of the child WISC gap.
- *Two limits are established.* The gap as it stands is not wholly genetic: it narrowed by several points within decades, varies from near zero to 1.5 SD across school districts, and widened in maths after 2020. And no evidence excludes a genetic share.
- *Between nations.* The association of measured skills with income survives better data. The lowest national-IQ entries do not survive audit: the sub-Saharan African levels are biased low by about ten points against the 2010 systematic review. Nothing in the national tables bears on whether the differences are genetic.
- *Institutions.* Only institutions have a clean natural experiment behind them (the Koreas, Nogales). It shows institutions can produce gaps as large as any between nations. It does not apportion the world's gaps.
- *Regimes (R1).* Regimes and freedom are not explained mainly by genes. **Strong**. In each of these cases the same ancestry lived under opposite regimes within a lifetime:
  - The Koreas: homogeneous before 1945 (Why Nations Fail l.683, l.769).
  - Taiwan: martial law 1949–87; first direct presidential election 1996; Free, 94/100 (FH 2025). Its population is "nearly 95 percent" Han (ROC portal). In the Taiwan Biobank, 79.9% have southern Han ancestry and 14.5% fall in a group "similar to other southern Han Chinese" (Chen et al. 2016). About 1.2 million arrived in 1949; a 2004 survey gives 8% mainlanders. The "13%" figure is not bound.
  - Germany: Weimar, 1933 and the two states of 1949. East and West were "ex ante uniform", with no income difference in 1928–36 (Alesina & Giuliano 2015). By 1950 the East was at about half the West's output per head, and it never closed the gap (Conference Board via OWID).
  - Spain and Portugal: dictatorships to the mid-1970s, constitutions of 1978 and 1976.
  Would be overturned by: genetic divergence on relevant traits within those periods.
- *Culture (R2).* Culture persists across generations and is not put on at will. **Strong**. The evidence: World Values Survey cultural zones (Inglehart & Baker 2000, abstract); origin-country trust into the fourth generation of American descendants (Algan & Cahuc 2010); values change "rarely within two generations" (Alesina & Giuliano 2015); second-generation Muslim religiosity (INSEE 2023; Güveli & Platt 2023). Regimes also shape culture: East Germans remain more pro-government after reunification.
- *Objections (author's review)*, each stated at full strength in the brief and answered:
  - (a) Peoples live as they want; Muslims and the Chinese do not want freedom; culture is made by genes. Gene–culture coevolution is a real field (Richerson, Boyd & Henrich 2010; lactase). The value differences are conceded as cultural: sharia 84% in South Asia and 74% in MENA (Pew 2013); "Eros far more than Demos" (Norris & Inglehart). "Do not want freedom" fails on the democracy surveys: the difference is "extremely modest"; at least 73% in eight Arab countries call democracy best (Arab Barometer 2025). "Genetic" fails on Taiwan.
  - (b) The best Germans died in the war. Over 5.3 million German military dead [secondary for Overmans]. But Weimar fell before the war; there was East–West parity in 1928–36; no source shows unequal losses between East and West [not bound]; the gap opened under occupation.
  - (c) The GDR was a foreign imposition. The imposition is conceded. But the population was German: 18.4 million in 1950, 16.4 million in 1989 (Statistisches Jahrbuch 1990). The Soviet forces were a garrison: 337,800 soldiers and 208,400 dependants and civilian staff (BT-Drs. 12/1064). About 3 million fled in 1949–61 (Bundesarchiv), and the regime fell in 1989. The objection concedes R1.
  - (d) The Taiwanese are not the same Chinese population. Answered by the population facts above, and by the same population living on both sides of 1987.
- Strongest objection to this verdict: "open" is evasion, because the indirect evidence all points one way. The dossier shows that each line is logically uninformative about the between-group cause, confounded, or not evidence (opinion that correlates with the respondent's politics and sex). The opposite objection, that the narrowing proves the gap environmental, fails for a matching reason: it shows that part of the gap is environmental, not all of it. What would settle it: a well-powered sibling-difference admixture design with observed skin colour and local ancestry, and within-family GWAS of cognition in African-ancestry samples. For nations: representative samples, invariance tested across countries, and means tracked through development.

### Lesson verdicts
- `what-intelligence-tests-measure` — Tests biased against Black Americans, or measuring nothing outside the test room → **both fail in the main US data.** Predictive validity is about .5, with no under-prediction for the lower-scoring group, and invariance is tenable (strict in the Philadelphia cohort and NLSY97). On reification, oblique factors bring g back one level up, as Gould concedes. Whether g is one ability or abilities that reinforce one another is **open**. Grade: **strong**. Would be overturned by: a representative US sample in which invariance fails or the tests under-predict.
- `heritability-within-and-between-groups` — "Highly heritable within groups, so the gap is probably genetic", including "equal heritabilities show it" → **invalid.** Within-group data cannot partition a between-group difference (Schraiber & Edge 2024). In simulations an environment lowers a group's mean and leaves its heritability unchanged, and the seed-corn case is granted by both sides. Jensen's default hypothesis survives as a hypothesis, not a default. Grade: **established**.
- `us-gap-size-trend-and-environment` — "Fixed and never narrowed" against "SES explains it" or "it is closing now" → **both fail.** The gap narrowed by about 0.3–0.5 SD between early-century cohorts and those born around 1970, then stopped on most series. - `adoption-studies-across-groups` — Minnesota shows the gap surviving a good environment; Eyferth and Tizard show no ancestry effect → **neither study decides.** Placement history, age at placement and attrition travel with ancestry in Minnesota, whose authors deny the hereditarian reading. Eyferth and Tizard are small, tested young and confounded. Grade: **strong**. Would be overturned by: adoption at birth, placement independent of ancestry, testing in adulthood.
- `admixture-and-polygenic-scores` — "Genes cause the gap" or "no genetic difference" → **neither is shown.** Admixture slopes are small and positive, and they sum genetic and environmental effects of ancestry, colourism included. The one within-family estimate (Fuerst et al. 2023) is positive and not significant. Polygenic scores lose accuracy outside European ancestry. Bird's 2021 bound holds only under neutrality. Grade: **strong**.
- `gould-and-harden-on-heredity` — (a) The factual answer settles the policy question, on either side → **fails.** Gould, Harden and Murray accept overlapping facts and reach opposite policies. One conclusion follows on any factual outcome. The tests predict equally for both groups and the groups differ on them, so treating individuals the same at the point of selection will not produce equal group outcomes, and an unequal outcome there is not by itself proof of discrimination. That says nothing about earlier causes of the difference in scores, which this module finds partly environmental. Grade: **strong**. (b) Gould's charge that Morton's measurements were bent by bias → **fails**: the 2011 re-measurement found them largely accurate, and whether the ranking followed from them is a separate question. Grade: **strong**. (c) The 1920s army-test ranking of European nationalities → **failed on its author's retraction** (Brigham 1930). Grade: **established**.
- `reich-ancient-dna-and-the-indo-europeans` — Old pure races, and "pots, not people" → **both refuted**: mass migrations replaced most earlier ancestry of whole regions, and every migrating people was itself a recent mixture. Grade: **established**. Reich's inference that average population differences in cognitive traits will be found → **open**.
- `geography-or-institutions` — Institutions can by themselves produce gaps as large as any between nations → **holds** (the Koreas, Nogales). Grade: **established**. World-scale apportionment is **open**. Diamond explains Eurasia's lead in 1500 at the proximate level (**strong**). The hereditarian account needs an institutional term on its own data: China and Japan fall far below their IQ prediction in 1820 and 1900 (**strong**).
- `national-data-audited` (beside the module verdict) — (a) "Sub-Saharan African IQ is about 70" as a measurement → **not supported** (Wicherts et al.: N-weighted mean 76.5, criterion samples about 80–82; the reply's exclusions tracked each sample's mean, biserial −.88). Grade: **strong**. (b) "National IQ differences are artefacts" → **fails** (r = .88 with TIMSS; harmonized outcomes for 164 countries). Grade: **strong**. (c) The settler-mortality causal estimate → **open**.

## Module 14 — empire-and-guilt: By what right did Europeans rule other peoples, did their rule leave those peoples better or worse off, and does anything pass to the descendants of either side?

**Module verdict** (in `auditing-the-case-for-colonialism`) — grade: **strong** on the failed titles, **on balance** on the limited title; **open** on a general ledger, **on balance** by kind of colony; **strong** on inheritance.
(1) *Title.*
- The titles from discovery, papal donation, the Emperor, unbelief and natural slavery fail, on Vitoria's premises and on the consent premise the course builds. **Strong**.
- On Vitoria's own premises there was a limited title to intervene: defence of the innocent against tyranny and human sacrifice "even if the victims consent", and the rights of travel and trade. Whether a lasting conquest could rest on it is answered **no, on balance**: the conqueror judged its own case. Locke (ch. XVI) and Burke ("arbitrary power … neither any man can hold nor any man can give") give no title to lasting rule over a people that governed itself.
- The behavioural "legitimacy" offered instead does not show consent. It shows that legitimacy was graded, not nil.

(2) *Ledger.* The burden lies equally on anyone who asserts a net effect in either direction.
- A general verdict of net benefit or net harm is **not established either way: open**.
- Where Europeans settled, and on islands, colonial tenure goes with higher income today. Islands with longer colonial tenure, instrumented by wind patterns, are richer and have lower infant mortality, and years after 1700 count for more (Feyrer & Sacerdote 2009). Colonial European settlement shows "a strong and uniformly positive relationship" with development, even at low settler shares (Easterly & Levine 2016). **On balance**: an instrumented design on islands and a cross-country association. Islands are not the African mainland.
- For sub-Saharan Africa the one Africa-specific assessment, Heldring & Robinson, concludes that colonialism "probably had a uniformly negative effect". It is a qualitative review against stated counterfactuals, which its authors call "very subjective and speculative", and it also holds that in the world context the effects were "very heterogeneous". Easterly & Levine's finding cuts against the extractive-institutions mechanism it relies on. **Open**, with the best assessment leaning negative. What would settle it: a border or timing discontinuity design on the mainland.
- Measured effects differ by kind of colony. Real urban wages rose in every British colony studied, though East Africa stayed near subsistence for decades. Heights in Kenya did not break trend at colonization.
- The slave trades, mostly before the Case's 1824 boundary, left a real and lasting effect. In the one causal estimate on income it is large: a one-standard-deviation smaller slave export goes with mean income of $1,864 instead of $1,249, a 50% increase (Nunn 2007, OLS; the IV estimates are larger). On trust there is an effect whose size the explained-variance share does not give.
- After independence several former colonies collapsed, and the verdict names them. GDP per head (Maddison Project Database 2023, 2011 dollars): Zimbabwe halved between 1998 ($2,369) and 2008 ($1,198), after the land seizures of 2000, and in 2022 ($1,704) was still below its 1974 level under the Rhodesian government ($2,275). The Congo stood at $1,192 at independence in 1960 and $414 in 2002, and in 2022 ($844) was still 29% below 1960. Uganda fell 38% (1970–86), Mozambique 47% (1970–92), Angola 51% (1970–90), Ghana 25% (1957–83), Sierra Leone about half (1990–2000), Guinea-Bissau 17% (1974–80).
- The objection that every colony did much better under colonial rule and much worse after independence (author's review) **fails as a uniform claim**. India's income per head rose 16% in 77 years of British rule (1870–1947, $850 to $985) and was lower in 1947 than in 1913; it rose 3.8-fold after 1991 ($2,062 to $7,766 in 2022). After independence Singapore rose twentyfold (1965–2022), Botswana nearly twenty-five-fold (1966–2022), South Korea from $1,548 (1960) to $41,321 — though Korea had also grown under Japanese rule ($1,108 in 1911, $1,975 in 1940). The Congo Free State cost between about one and about ten million lives; no census was taken before 1924, so the range is wide (Sanderson's modelling at the low end; Hochschild, following the 1919 commission's "reduced by half", at the high). South Korea ($41,321) and North Korea ($1,569) had the same colonial past. Grade: **strong**. The post-independence record, like the colonial one, varies by kind of colony and by what the government did. A before-and-after comparison is not a counterfactual, and the burden rule applies to it as to every other entry.

(3) *Inheritance.* See `inherited-guilt-and-reparations`: liability can pass, guilt cannot.
- Strongest objection to this verdict: from one side, the burden lies on whoever claims alien rule was beneficial, so "open" lets the Case off. From the other, the instrumented island design and the settlement association meet the register's own test of a credible counterfactual, so "open" lets the critics off. The verdict answers both with one standard: each claim of net effect carries its own burden, and neither has met it across colonies. It would be overturned by a credible counterfactual design with results in one direction across many colonies of different kinds.

### Lesson verdicts
- `vitoria-right-to-the-americas` — Spain's claimed titles → **every unjust title fails** on Vitoria's premises. The Indians had cities, laws and marriages, so they owned. The just titles he leaves (travel, trade, preaching, defence of the innocent), enforceable by war, are **a licence rather than a restraint**, because the party that judged a refusal went to war over it. Grade: **strong** on the first, **on balance** on the second. Would be overturned by: a mechanism in his scheme letting a neutral party judge a refusal. The author's-review objection, "they were stronger and more capable, and that is all the right you need" (the Athenians at Melos, Thrasymachus, Callicles in the *Gorgias* 483d–484c), is stated at full strength and answered. As a description it is upheld: see `thucydides-power-and-justice`. As a theory of right it collapses right into success. It gives every colony that won its independence (the United States, Haiti, Spanish America, Algeria), and anyone who crosses an undefended border, the same title, and leaves no ground to say anything ought to be otherwise. Socrates' reply (488d) turns it on Callicles: the many are stronger. Vitoria had already excluded it: "enlargement of empire cannot be a cause of just war". It is one more title that fails. Grade: **strong**, within the first part.
- `locke-and-mill-on-empire` — Locke's "waste" and Mill's tutelage: lapses from their principles or consequences of them? → **Consequences.** Both follow from conditions written into the principles: Locke's labour criterion applied to land, and Mill's exclusion of "backward states of society" in *On Liberty* itself. Grade: **strong**.
  - Second verdict (author's review): slavery as a "positive good" (Calhoun, Senate, 1837; Fitzhugh, *Sociology for the South* 1854 and *Cannibals All!* 1857). Its three claims: the master's interest protects the slave, as an owner would not wreck his car or lame his horse; the abuse is propaganda; to be ruled by a superior culture benefits the slave. → **Fails** against the record. Grade: **strong**.
  - Interest. The owner's interest protects the asset, not the person, and only where a replacement costs more than the loss. Fitzhugh concedes the condition: in Cuba, "the most efficient cause of cruelty and neglect, is the African slave trade, which makes it cheaper to buy than to rear slaves". The record follows it. In the British Caribbean few colonies ever reached natural increase. After the trade ended in 1807 the slave population fell from 775,000 to 665,000 by 1834; Jamaica's fell from 347,000 in 1810 to 311,070. In 1810 fewer than 800,000 slaves lived there, against some 1.7 million Africans imported. In the United States, where import was dear after 1808, the black population of 1807 was already over three times the number of Africans imported (Higman 1986, with Engerman's comment). The interest also never forbade what raised the asset's value: the whip that got more work, and the sale that parted a family.
  - Abuse. "Propaganda" fails on the defence's own best modern evidence. On Bennet Barrow's plantation Fogel and Engerman counted 0.7 whippings per hand a year. Their critics counted 1.19, and a whipping witnessed by the men about every six days [secondary via Vedder 2024]. Of 12.5 million Africans shipped, 10.7 million landed: about one in seven died at sea, though the shippers' interest was to land them alive (TAST [secondary]).
  - Benefit. The benefit claim fails on revealed behaviour: the Fugitive Slave Act of 1850, and Saint-Domingue 1791 / Haiti 1804. It also fails Mill's test, because a wardship made hereditary and permanent effects no improvement.
  - True part, stated as such: where buying was dear, slave populations grew (the United States after 1808).
  - Would be overturned by: natural increase where buying was cheaper than rearing, or enslaved people choosing to stay where flight or self-purchase was open.
- `burke-against-hastings` — "Geographical morality", and the charge that the impeachment contradicts the *Reflections* → **the defence fails, and there is no contradiction.** The defence would license any act by citing the worst local precedent. Burke's principle that power over others is a trust under law is the *Reflections*' inherited order turned on the Company. Grade: **strong**.
- `colonial-balance-sheet` — The Case's three charges. (a) "The critics never ran a cost–benefit against a counterfactual" → **holds against the polemical catalogues; fails as a description of the critical literature**, which has run counterfactual designs since 2001 (Acemoglu, Johnson & Robinson, cited in the Case's own note 4; Nunn; Heldring & Robinson). Grade: **strong** on each half. (b) "The never-colonised comparators show colonialism was no handicap" → **fails**: the comparators are not comparable, survivorship selects them, and the Case concedes the counterfactual is unobservable. Grade: **strong**. (c) "Behaviour reveals the colonised treated colonial rule as legitimate" → **fails as proof of consent**; it shows legitimacy was graded. Grade: **strong**.
- `inherited-guilt-and-reparations` — (a) **Guilt does not pass**: it attaches to a will. Grade: **strong**. (b) **Liability can pass**, through estates, corporations and states that persist; Burke's partnership of generations implies it. Grade: **strong**. (c) Counterfactual rectification of wrongs centuries old **cannot be carried out** (indeterminacy, non-identity, choice of baseline). Grade: **strong**. (d) Wrongs done to people now alive **are owed repair** on the continuing-injustice model. The example to use is the FHA's exclusion of Black neighbourhoods from mortgage insurance from the 1930s, not the HOLC maps, which had little effect (Fishback et al. 2021). Grade: **strong**. Supersession is **possible, not automatic**.

## Module 15 — nation-and-immigration: Who is "we", may a people decide who joins it, and what did 2015 show about the three answers?

**Module verdict** (in `the-2015-crisis-read-three-ways`) — grade for the module's answer: **on balance**. It is graded in four parts. The full record is in `2015-and-verification.md` Part 1, and the integration figures are realigned to IAB 17/2025.

The answer: a people may control its membership, within duties of rescue, fair terms for migrants and full inclusion of those it admits. 2015 was a failure of capacity *and* of principle, though not of Miller's principle. Miller's principle was not applied: the question of who joins was decided by an executive declining to use powers it said it had.

**(a) Procedure and consent.**
- *25 August 2015.* BAMF announced on Twitter that Dublin procedures for Syrians were "weitestgehend faktisch nicht weiter verfolgt" (in practice largely no longer pursued).
- *4–5 September.* The Chancellor agreed by telephone with Austria to let the people in Hungary travel on, as "eine Ausnahme" (an exception).
- *13 September.* Border controls returned, and the Federal Police order told officers to admit undocumented arrivals who asked for asylum. The government told parliament that refusal at the border was lawful under Dublin III and § 18 AsylG (BT-Drs. 18/7311). Its interior minister said: "Politisch haben wir uns bisher jedenfalls dagegen entschieden" (politically, at any rate, we have so far decided against it).
- *Parliament.* The Bundestag never voted on the admission.
- That the decisions were taken by the executive alone is **established**. That this bypassed rule by consent on a question of membership is **strong**. The emergency defence covers the weekend of 4–5 September, not the six months after controls existed.
- That the decision was unlawful is **open**. The Bundestag research service judged the statutory-basis argument weighty only on a premise of fact the government never clarified, and it found Di Fabio's concrete breach unsubstantiated. The Constitutional Court dismissed the AfD's Organstreit as inadmissible, and no court has ruled on the merits.
- At EU level the relocation decision of 22 September 2015 was lawful (**established**, CJEU C-643/15 and C-647/15). It was also a decision on who joins four peoples, taken over their governments' votes by an act the Court held needed no involvement of national parliaments.

**(b) Security.**
- The BfV lists 17 Islamist attacks in Germany since February 2016, with 23 dead. Twelve were committed by men who came as asylum seekers, and those twelve account for 22 of the 23 deaths. That the lethal Islamist attacks were overwhelmingly by asylum-route arrivals is **strong**. Several statuses rest on secondary sources and must be bound to judgments; the Holocaust-memorial case [UNVERIFIED] has no deaths and does not affect the count.
- That the removal failure was systematic is **strong**. Amri and the Hamburg attacker were obliged to leave and not removed for want of papers. The Ansbach Dublin removal was suspended. The Solingen transfer was tried once and the deadline lapsed. The non-Islamist Aschaffenburg case has the same structure.
- That screening failed in 2015–16 is **strong**. A man registered under nine identities and assessed as a Gefährder had his claim pending for months, a minor was never fingerprinted, and a Paris bomber was registered on a false passport [secondary].
- As a class of risk the danger was foreseeable, and admission without refusal continued four months after 13 November 2015: **strong**. As individuals most attackers were not foreseeable, since only three of twelve were known Islamists beforehand: **strong**.
- Scale: about 3.0 million asylum applications in 2015–24, so twelve attackers is about four per million. That is small relative to the flow and almost the whole of Islamist terrorism's toll in Germany. Both statements belong in the verdict.
- Magdeburg (not Islamist) and Aschaffenburg (no political or religious motive found) are excluded from the Islamist count.

**(c) Principle.** Miller over Carens, **on balance** (see `miller-strangers-in-our-midst`). Carens's own brake, the public-order restriction, is the case (a) and (b) document: admission without identification and removal that cannot be enforced. On his own terms his theory licensed more control than was exercised.

**(d) Integration.** One measure throughout: the employment rate (*Beschäftigungsquote*), the share of 18–64-year-olds in paid dependent employment, from the social-insurance register linked to the IAB-BAMF-SOEP survey. It excludes the self-employed (about 5% of the 2015 cohort) and civil servants. The source is IAB-Kurzbericht 17/2025 (Brücker, Jaschke & Kosyakova), for 2024, nine years after arrival:

| 2015 arrivals, 2024 | Employment rate | Population average, same measure |
|---|---|---|
| Women | 35% | 69% (all women) |
| Men | 76% | 72% (all men) |
| All | 64% | 70% |

- Counting self-employment, the 2015 arrivals' rate is about 70%. The report gives no population figure on that basis.
- 90% of the employed arrivals are in jobs subject to social insurance, against 92% in the population.
- Women are 29% of the cohort's working-age members. Refugee women without children have an employment rate 19 points higher than those with a child under six.
- Employment does not differ significantly by age except over 50, at 45%.
- Pay: in 2023 the full-time median was €2,675 a month, 70% of the median of all full-time employees and just above the low-wage line of 66%. Refugee women's full-time pay is 77% of refugee men's. The IAB notes the cohort is young and short of work experience in Germany.
- Benefits: in 2023, 34% of the cohort's working-age members lived in households drawing basic income support (SGB II). Asylum-seeker benefits are not in the register and are not counted. 84% of the employed lived without supplementary SGB II.
- Verdict (d), employment: integration is split by sex, **strong**. Refugee men exceed the male average on the same measure, 76% against 72%. Refugee women stand at about half the female average, 35% against 69%. The overall gap is 6 points (64% against 70%). The pay gap is large, and a third live in benefit households, against a population figure the report does not give.
- Crime: asylum-status immigrants were 8.8% of all suspects in 2024 (offences against residence law excluded), against 3.66% of the population, about 2.4 times. In offences against life the share is 12.2%, about 3.3 times; in sexual offences 7.9%, about 2.2 times. These are police statistics of suspects, unadjusted; the BKA says so. 82.9% of the suspects were male and 57.1% under 30, against 55.0% and 46.0% among resident refugees. 31.8% were repeat suspects, involved in 77.0% of the group's offences. Overrepresentation among recorded suspects: **strong**.
- How much survives adjustment for age and sex is **open**. What would settle it: suspect rates by residence status standardised by age and sex.
- That refugee arrivals did not raise local crime rates in 2018–23 is **on balance**: one district panel (ifo 2025), with no detectable effect after district and year fixed effects, and the authors' own statement that individual data are unavailable.

**What 2015 showed about the three answers.**
- *Carens.* Open admission held for months and was reversed by every government that tried it, Germany included.
- *Miller.* His principle stands, and 2015 is evidence for it in the negative. The state lacked the capacity to sort, screen and return that his framework presupposes. Ten years of correction followed along his lines:
  - controls at all nine land borders from 16 September 2024, notified to 15 March 2027;
  - refusal at the border resumed by instruction on 7 May 2025, with vulnerable persons excepted. The Berlin administrative court held three such refusals unlawful under Dublin (VG 6 L 191/25, 2 June 2025), so this correction is contested in law;
  - the Pact on Migration and Asylum, applying from 12 June 2026.
- *Murray.*
  - His "never asked" holds for 2015 in the strongest sense: not even the Bundestag was asked.
  - His deportation gap is confirmed, with dead.
  - His paralysis forecast held for about six months and then failed. The liberal order corrected itself through its own instruments, late, and after the electorate punished the governing parties (AfD 12.6% in 2017; 20.8% and second place in 2025).
  - His integration forecast fails for men on employment, holds for women, and is partly borne out on crime pending adjustment.
- Strongest objection to this verdict: from one side, the emergency, the Bundestag's acquiescence, the 2017 re-election, and the charge that naming the attackers' status blames three million people for twelve. From the other, the admission was not merely unauthorised but unlawful (Di Fabio). The emergency covers days, not months. Acquiescence is not authorisation, which is the point of the *Wesentlichkeit* doctrine, and an election in which migration was one issue among several ratifies no specific decision. The verdict states the per-capita rarity beside the share of the toll, and it locates the failure in institutions, not in a group. Against the second objection: no court has ruled, the research service found the concrete breach unsubstantiated, and Bavaria did not sue. Would overturn it:
  - for (a), a published ministerial order under § 18(4) no. 2 with stated limits, proof that the admissions rested on Dublin responsibility, or a merits ruling;
  - for (b), evidence that the asylum-route share of Islamist attackers is no higher than other groups' share, or that the removal failures were isolated;
  - for (d), a fall in refugee men's employment, or age-and-sex-standardised suspect rates showing no overrepresentation.

### Lesson verdicts
- `albions-seed-and-who-are-we` — (a) Jay's claim in Federalist 2 that Americans of 1787 were one people with the "same ancestors … same language … same religion" → **false as stated**: four British regional cultures, plus Africans, Indians, Germans and Dutch. Grade: **strong**. (b) Huntington's 2004 forecast that Hispanic immigration would not assimilate. This is a liberal assimilation forecast against a restrictionist one, and the record splits.
  - Language: the forecast **failed**. English proficiency is near-universal by the second generation. Grade: **strong**.
  - Attachment to the United States **rises with generation** (Citrin et al. 2007), against the "threat to American identity" claim. Grade: **on balance**.
  - A persistent ethnic identity, endogamy and residential concentration into the fourth generation are **partly borne out**: "the majority of fourth generation Mexican Americans continue to live in Hispanic neighborhoods, marry other Hispanics, and think of themselves as Mexican" (Telles & Ortiz 2008, publisher summary [secondary to the book]). Ethnic attrition may bias that sample toward those who kept the identity. Grade: **on balance**.
  - Education stalls after the second generation and declines in the third and fourth: **borne out**. Grade: **on balance**.
- `america-and-the-english-decline` — The broad forecast of contraction → **not borne out so far.** Grade: **on balance**. The measure decides the answer: the US share of world output has been roughly stable since 1990 at market exchange rates and has fallen at purchasing-power parity [UNVERIFIED: IMF WEO series to be bound]. The reach of US arms has to be weighed against the withdrawals from Afghanistan (2021) and Iraq. Decline has also been forecast in every generation since 1918 with no reversal counted against it. No grade above on balance before both output measures and a military series are bound. The narrow thesis, an elite that cannot say why its inheritance deserves defence, is **open**. What would settle it: series on religious practice, the elite–public gap on national identity, and assimilation, with a horizon fixed in advance.
- `powell-raspail-and-camus` — Claim by claim:
  - Powell's numbers: right in direction and roughly in size, later than forecast. About 4.6 million non-White in the UK in 2001, against his 5–7 million for 2000; England and Wales 18.3% not White in 2021. **Strong**.
  - Powell's bloodshed and domination: not borne out in the form he gave them. **Strong**. The record of ethnic disorder (1981, 1985, 2001) is stated beside this.
  - Powell's consent claim: **open**, carried to the Murray lesson.
  - Raspail: the scale arrived in 2015 by another route, and for months the core states did not refuse, the nearest the record came to his scenario. The strong form ("the West will not refuse") failed: Australia 2013, the Hungarian fence 2015, the EU–Turkey statement 2016. **Strong** on the strong form. The 2045 claim is **pending**. The 1985 Figaro projection understated migration and still got the religious outcome wrong.
  - Camus, descriptive claim: the population's composition is changing, by an amount that depends on how mixed descent is counted. **On balance**.
  - Camus, intentional claim, as a plot: **unsupported, strong**. His own text rejects a plot, and the UN's 2000 report is a projection exercise.
  - Camus, intentional claim, as "wanted by part of the governing class": **unsupported, on balance**. One contested insider account (Neather, *Evening Standard*, October 2009, later disowned) [secondary] sits against the documented forecasting errors, which point to drift.
- `carens-open-borders` **(forecast check)** — Carens (1987, p. 270): open borders threaten a community's character "only because we assume that so many people would move if they could"; "most human beings do not love to move". → The premise **holds as stated**: about 84–85% of adults do not wish to emigrate. The inference that the movers would be "few" **fails**. 15–16% of adults, about 900 million people, would like to leave their country permanently (Gallup 2023, 2025). With roughly 3–5% of them naming Germany, the would-be migrants to Germany number about 27–45 million. Where moves were cheap and legal, realised flows ran above official forecasts (the UK in 2004). Grade: **strong** (a stated wish is not a move). Would be overturned by: evidence that under free access realised migration runs an order of magnitude below stated desire. Carens's nonideal clause ("size of demand") concedes the point in advance.
- `murray-strange-death-of-europe` — "Never chosen". (a) The scale and permanence of post-war immigration were neither intended nor forecast, and majorities opposed it throughout the period Murray covers. The Home Office forecast 5,000–13,000 a year for the UK after 2004, while Eastern Europeans in Britain reached 1.24 million by 2013. The "tens of thousands" target was missed. Polls from Gallup 1968 to YouGov 2011. Grade: **strong**. (b) "Never chosen" in the sense that no one decided → **fails**. The instruments were chosen by governments and legislatures: the recruitment agreements (Germany–Turkey 1961, with a two-year rotation clause that was not kept), and in 2004 the decision not to use the transitional restrictions the accession treaty allowed, set out in the very report that made the failed forecast (Dustmann et al. 2003). That the UK, Ireland and Sweden alone opened at once [UNVERIFIED], and the British Nationality Act 1948 [UNVERIFIED], are to be bound. Grade: **strong**. Short form: chosen in its instruments, not in its scale. The "tiredness" concatenation is not testable as stated, and the Böckenförde question it raises goes to module 16.
- `murray-strange-death-of-europe` **(forecast check)** — Immigration as a net fiscal gain that helps carry an ageing population (European Commission, COM(2015) 240; the UCL "34 per cent more in taxes" headline).
  - (a) Immigration as such is a net fiscal gain → **fails** as a general claim. The lifetime, origin-split studies of high-welfare states find a negative balance overall, strongly negative for asylum and family migration and non-Western origins. In the Netherlands, per immigrant: labour +€125,000, asylum −€475,000; no second-generation group made a positive contribution (van de Beek et al. 2023). In Denmark, non-Western immigrants and descendants come to −27bn kr in 2019 (Finansministeriet 2023). The OECD finds "between ‑1% and +1% of GDP for most countries". The UK gives a positive lifetime figure for non-EEA migrants (MAC 2018). Grade: **strong** for the Dutch and Danish type of welfare state; "small either way" for the OECD.
  - (b) Selected labour migration, and migration from rich and EU countries, is a net gain → **holds**. Grade: **strong**.
  - (c) Immigration can carry the pension burden → **fails**. Holding the support ratio needs 674 million migrants into the EU by 2050, which the UN's own report calls "totally unrealistic". Grade: **established**.
- `murray-strange-death-of-europe` **(forecast check)** — "They will go home" and "integration will happen by itself".
  - (a) The guest workers would return → **failed**. Of about 870,000 Turkish recruits about half a million returned [secondary], and the policy's own authors said so in 1982 (BT-Drs. 9/1629) and 2010 (Merkel at Potsdam). Grade: **established**.
  - (b) Integration would follow by itself → **failed** for second-generation Europeans of non-EU parentage on education, employment and felt membership. Employment is 68% against about three-quarters, and reported discrimination rose for that group. It **held** for those of EU parentage, and the NEET gap is narrowing (OECD/EU 2023). Grade: **strong**.
  - (c) Multicultural policy caused the shortfall → **open** (Koopmans against Wright & Bloemraad and Heath & Demireva, abstracts only). What would settle it: a cross-national panel of policy indices against second-generation outcomes, with welfare generosity controlled.
- `murray-strange-death-of-europe` **(forecast check)** — Muslim-origin Europeans will secularize as Christian-origin Europeans did → **fails.** In France 91% of those raised Muslim keep the religion, against 67% of those raised Catholic (INSEE 2023). Across fifteen countries, second-generation Muslims keep first-generation religiosity while Christians converge (Güveli & Platt 2023). German and French studies agree; only Dutch data show some decline. Grade: **strong**. Says nothing about the third generation.
- `miller-strangers-in-our-midst` (decides the dispute `carens-open-borders` stages) — Does the equal moral worth of persons entail a right to cross borders freely? → **It does not.** It entails duties of rescue, fair terms and inclusion of residents, and leaves a people a qualified right to control membership. Grade: **on balance**. Decided by: the interests that internal movement protects can be met in any adequately free state, so they ground a right to *some* adequate place, not to *this* one. Miller himself extends refuge-type claims to those whose rights cannot be protected at home. Carens's global original position bypasses Rawls's own placement of self-determination at the level of peoples (§58). The point in dispute with Abizadeh is the coercion/prevention distinction. Abizadeh holds that "the demos of democratic theory is in principle unbounded", so the draft's "which no one defends" is struck. How far duties of admission, or of effective aid instead, reach people above Miller's floor but far below the options in rich states is **open**. It is a value question that the place-premium data sharpen but cannot settle: wage ratios for observably identical workers of about 4 at the median (Clemens, Montenegro & Pritchett 2009 [ratios secondary]).
- `the-2015-crisis-read-three-ways` **(forecast check)** — The autumn-2015 forecasts.
  - (a) Zetsche's "next Wirtschaftswunder" → **failed**. Nine years on the cohort's employment rate is 64% against 70%, its full-time pay is 70% of the median, and its women are at half the female rate. Grade: **strong**.
  - (b) Nahles's sober forecast to the Bundestag ("Der syrische Arzt ist nicht der Normalfall") → **held**. It came from the governing coalition. Grade: **strong**.
  - (c) "Wir schaffen das", read as a forecast that Germany would absorb the inflow as it had absorbed earlier ones → **fails, on balance**. The state did not break down, and refugee men exceed the male employment rate on the same measure. Women's employment, pay, benefit dependence, a 2.4-fold overrepresentation among recorded suspects (unadjusted), and the absence of prior consent were not "managed". Its author withdrew the phrase within thirteen months, though not the decision. The IAB itself says "unklare Erfolgskriterien" (unclear criteria of success) rule out an unambiguous answer. Would be overturned by: female employment converging on the female average, crime overrepresentation disappearing after adjustment, or a fiscal study of the cohort showing a lifetime net gain.

## Module 16 — after-liberalism: Can a free state survive on liberal principles alone, and is the post-liberal remedy better?

**Module verdict** (in `hungarys-fundamental-law`) — grade: **on balance** on the premise; **open** on whether written presuppositions produce cohesion; **strong** on the Fourth Amendment; **strong** that the lock-in forecast failed; **strong** that regimes and freedom are not explained mainly by genes, and **strong** that culture persists and is not put on at will (carried from module 13).
- *The premise.* A free state does not live on liberal principles alone. It draws on a solidarity, a readiness to bear burdens for strangers who count as "us", which its procedures do not supply (Böckenförde). The premise is held by a conservative (Scruton), a liberal of the left (Miller) and Jay in 1787 alike, and Carens concedes it in his nonideal theory. Its strongest liberal interlocutor grants the motivational half: Habermas, in the 2004 exchange, holds that "liberal societal structures are dependent on the solidarity of their citizens", and that if secularization goes "off the rails" the sources of that solidarity "may dry up". He contests the other half, that the state cannot give itself a secular justification, and offers to give Böckenförde's theorem "a harmless meaning". **On balance**. The draft's falsifier ("a free state with no shared language, history or faith") could never be met. Restated so it can be: trust and willingness to fund transfers to strangers do not fall where shared identity or religion weakens. The cross-national diversity-and-trust literature is the evidence to bind [not fetched].
- *"Cannot command without ceasing to be free."* This is **open**. The Third Republic made "peasants into Frenchmen" through schools, roads and conscription and stayed a parliamentary republic (E. Weber 1976) [secondary: publisher's description only]. Bind before the course states it either way.
- *The post-liberal remedy on Hungary.* The constitution was written in four months by an alliance that won two-thirds of the seats on 53% of the list vote, and it held two-thirds again on about 45% in 2014. The Fourth Amendment (2013) reinstated provisions the Constitutional Court had annulled: a majority overriding its court. **Strong**.
- Whether writing the presuppositions into the supreme law produced cohesion is **open**. The demographic indicators the policy aimed at rose and then fell back [secondary: OSW via search summary; bind from KSH], and no cohesion measure is in the course. One election loss does not measure cohesion.
- *The liberal forecast that failed.* The common liberal charge that the system could not be voted out failed on 12 April 2026. Tisza won 53.18% of the list vote and 141 of 199 seats; Fidesz–KDNP won 38.61% and 52 seats; turnout was 78.99% (National Election Office data). **Strong**. See the forecast check below. Cardinal acts still give any two-thirds majority an outsized lever, including the new one: **on balance**.
- *What the solidarity is made of (round 2).* It is not genes. Regimes and freedom are not explained mainly by genes, **strong**: the same peoples were free and unfree within a lifetime, Taiwan above all. It is an inherited culture that persists and is not put on at will, **strong** (see module 13, R1–R2). The strongest objection, the author's at full strength: peoples live as they want; Muslims and the Chinese do not want freedom; culture is made by genes, and gene–culture coevolution is a real field. The value differences are conceded as cultural and persistent (sharia, gender). "Do not want freedom" fails on the democracy surveys. "Genetic" fails on Taiwan, and on the time-scale: coevolution works over millennia, while these regimes changed in years. This strengthens the inherited, not legislated, half of Böckenförde's premise. Claim 1 keeps its grade, because R2 is not the trust-and-transfers series its falsifier asks for.
- *The answer the evidence supports:* liberal principles are necessary and, on balance, not sufficient. The supplement they need comes from a people's own institutions and loyalties, which the state can protect. Whether it can also write them is the open question above. Hungary is the case each side reads as its proof, and it settles neither the premise nor the remedy.
- Strongest objection to this verdict: the 2026 alternation shows that a national-Christian constitution and free elections coexist, so the remedy is vindicated. The verdict accepts the coexistence: the lock-in charge failed. It does not accept vindication, because the winner's programme is pro-EU on courts, media and prosecution [secondary: Reuters factbox]. That programme is also restrictive on migration (it rejects the Pact's quotas and relocation), which narrows the contrast. The cohesion the text promised is unmeasured. What would overturn the open finding: a cohesion series (trust, fertility, marriage, civic participation) that moves with the constitutional changes and not with the economic cycle.

### Lesson verdicts
- `hazony-virtue-of-nationalism` — (a) A federation of nations, the EU above all, is an empire. Hazony's test is his own definition, "any project to bring many nations under one authority and one doctrine", together with the "who decides?" cascade.
  - As rule without consent or exit, the charge **fails**: members joined by treaty and may leave (Article 50, used by the UK in 2020), and Hungary's own Article E authorises the Union's competences. Grade: **strong**.
  - As compulsory jurisdiction under one legal order over member nations, the charge **holds**: primacy of EU law, the Court of Justice's compulsory jurisdiction, infringement proceedings, and the conditionality mechanism under which the Council withheld funds from Hungary in December 2022 [secondary]. Grade: **on balance**.
  - Whether that makes the EU an "empire" depends on his stipulated definition. The brief leaves that open, and so does the register.
  - (b) There is no neutral, purely civic state → **holds**. Every state chooses an official language, holidays, a public history and naturalisation terms, and the liberal-nationalist alternative concedes this. Grade: **strong**.
- `vermeule-common-good-constitutionalism` — The safeguard against abuse can be internal to officials (reasoned ordinances, good faith, rationality review) → **the theory supplies no check of its own** against a common-good determination made in bad faith. It relies on the external checks of the order it works within (elections, separated powers), which it does not abolish. Grade: **on balance**. Decided by: Madison's "enlightened statesmen will not always be at the helm" and Polybius's checks. A check located in the good faith of the checked is not a check. Vermeule's replies (no neutral baseline; power is conserved; every polity inculcates; positive law is disputed too) show the liberal state is not neutral. They do not say who corrects a bad-faith authority. Hungary's Fourth Amendment is not used as evidence here, since it was not an application of his theory. Would be overturned by: a mechanism in the theory, not in officials' character, that can block a governing majority's "determination".
- `de-benoist-european-new-right` — Does the right to difference ("ethnopluralism") hold together? → **On balance, it does not.** It assigns the right to peoples, not persons, so the individual who marries out, assimilates or leaves has no standing in it. The requirement of generality ("I may claim my difference only if I defend yours") is consistent and separates the doctrine from a ranking of races. The draft's appeal to ancient DNA is deleted. De Benoist's peoples are "ethnocultural", he disavows biological prejudice, and Bronze-Age admixture says nothing about whether cultural peoples have lasted. Grade: **on balance**. Would be overturned by: an account of the people as a cultural unit, specifiable without ancestry, that gives its members standing to leave.
- `hungarys-fundamental-law` **(forecast check)** — The liberal forecast that Fidesz's constitutional order could not be voted out → **failed** on 12 April 2026 (figures above). Grade: **strong**. Would be overturned by: the new majority being prevented from governing by cardinal acts and appointed bodies. That would revive a narrower form of the charge, entrenchment of policy rather than of the party.

## Course verdict (`the-argument-assembled`)

### The thesis as now written (last paragraph of `intro` in course.yaml)

> Its answer is that it cannot [survive on liberal principles alone]. Liberal freedom is real and worth keeping, but it does not found itself: it lives on a nation, an elite held under law and an inherited culture that it did not create and cannot replace. ### The module verdicts behind each clause

**1. "A nation cannot survive on liberal principles alone."** Supported **on balance**.
- Module 16: the premise holds on balance, and Habermas grants its motivational half.
- Module 2: the commonwealth was held by a public worship, and both sides of 384 acted on that (**strong**).
- Module 4: consent did not found the governments Hume surveys (**strong**).
- Module 15: membership was decided in 2015 outside the people's institutions, and the correction came only after electoral pressure (**strong** on procedure).
- Forecast checks: integration "by itself" failed for the non-EU second generation, and the expected secularization did not come (**strong**). Liberal institutions did not reproduce a shared culture automatically.
- Firmness is limited because the falsifier, trust and transfers where shared identity weakens, is not yet bound, and the Third Republic case is open.

**2. "Liberal freedom is real and worth keeping."** Supported **strong**.
- Module 5: the wide franchise did not end liberty or stability in Britain.
- Module 7: parliaments held where they were defended.
- Module 9: the civil core of rights became justiciable.
- Module 11: the republican peace holds.
- Module 9: Condorcet's material forecasts held.
- Module 16: a constitutional order was voted out in 2026.

**3. "It lives on a nation, an elite held under law and an inherited culture that it did not create and cannot replace."**
- *A nation:* **on balance**. Module 6: membership is received rather than chosen (**strong**). Module 15: Miller over Carens (**on balance**). Module 16: Hazony (b), there is no neutral civic state (**strong**).
- *An elite held under law:* **strong**. Module 7: a minority always rules; regimes differ in juridical defence and in whether the many can remove the minority (Mosca, Michels; **strong**). Weimar shows what happens when the law does not hold the elite.
- *An inherited culture that it did not create:* **on balance**, from modules 2 and 16 and the forecast checks.
- *"Cannot replace"* goes beyond the evidence. The Third Republic case says a free state can build a nation; Habermas says the state can justify itself from its own resources. Neither is settled (**open**).

**4. "The radical right's alternatives failed by their own tests."**
- *The decider:* **on balance**. Module 7: the commissarial remedy as practised had no criterion that kept it commissarial. Module 8: the doctrine's "who decided is law" cancelled its own 1932 criterion against a legal revolution. Schmitt's diagnosis held, and that half goes to him.
- *The racial nation:* **established**. Modules 6 and 13: Gobineau and Chamberlain/Grant fail on their own tests, and ancient DNA refutes pure races.
- *The conservative revolution:* **strong** for Moeller; **on balance** for the module.
- *The constitution written by one majority:* **not supported as "failed by its own test".** Module 16 finds the Fourth Amendment (a majority overriding its court) **strong**, and finds whether the text produced cohesion, its own test, **open**. The liberal charge that it could not be voted out failed in 2026.

### Does the thesis survive?

Not intact. Clauses 1, 2 and 4 survive, with clause 1 at on balance. Two phrases go beyond the module verdicts: "cannot replace" in clause 3, and "the constitution written by one majority … failed by [its] own tests" in clause 4. Proposed wording, for the human to decide (course.yaml is not edited here):

> Its answer, on balance, is that it cannot. Liberal freedom is real and worth keeping, but it does not found itself: it lives on a nation, an elite held under law, an inherited culture that it did not create and cannot produce on demand, and an economy of property and markets that every planned alternative failed to match. The radical right's alternatives (the decider, the racial nation, the conservative revolution) failed by their own tests; the constitution written by one majority has not shown the cohesion it was written to secure.

Adopted in `intro` (2026-11-01), with one clause added by the author after round 1: **"an economy of property and markets that every planned alternative failed to match"** — grounded in `burnham-managers-and-the-suicide-of-the-west` (market economies outperform planned ones: **established**, necessary not sufficient) and `marx-manifesto` (Marx's distinctive forecasts failed: **strong**). The course's position is set out as a derivation in `the-doctrine` (plan: `DOCTRINE-PLAN.md`).

### The strongest objections to the thesis, and the answers

- **Rawls's line, as the course stages it.** A just basic structure generates its own support. Citizens raised under just institutions acquire a sense of justice that stabilises them, so liberal principles found their own allegiance. *Answer:* the course has not tested this; the reciprocity and stability route in module 10 is **open**. Two things tell against it inside the course. Rawls's own theory assumes a closed society entered by birth and death, and places self-determination at the level of peoples (§58). Carens's brief concedes the first, and the Miller verdict uses the second. So the theory presupposes the bounded "we" it does not derive. Second, the integration and secularization checks show that liberal institutions did not by themselves produce shared allegiance in the non-EU second generation. Grade of the answer: **on balance**. What would settle it: evidence that support for just institutions forms at equal rates in groups with and without a prior shared identity.
- **Carens's line.** The bounded "we" cannot be justified from liberal premises, and birthright membership is feudal privilege, so the thesis rests on privilege. *Answer:* `miller-strangers-in-our-midst` (**on balance**). Moral equality grounds a right to some adequate place, not to this one. Carens's own empirical premise that few would move fails in its inference (**strong**), and his public-order brake and nonideal clause license control. What remains standing is the duty owed to people above Miller's floor but far below the options in rich states. That stays **open**, and the thesis should not claim to have closed it.
- **MacIntyre's line.** Modern moral language is the fragment of a lost tradition. Modern conservatism is "a mirror image of the liberalism" it attacks, so a nation and its inherited culture cannot supply what the lost tradition supplied. *Answer:* none from the register. The course leaves MacIntyre's decisive question open: can a tradition defeat its rivals without neutral standards (`macintyre-after-virtue`, no verdict)? The thesis is narrower than what MacIntyre denies. It claims a loyalty that bears burdens for strangers, not a shared account of the good. If MacIntyre is right that the loyalty cannot outlast the account, the thesis's supplement is weaker than it says. Grade: **open**.
- **Habermas's reply to Böckenförde** (staged by the audit, `texts/audit-right-shading/habermas-ratzinger-2004-exchange-excerpt.txt`). The constitutional state can give itself a secular justification. Its dependence on solidarity is motivational, and a "double learning process" between faith and secular reason can sustain it. *Answer:* Habermas grants the half of the thesis that matters for clause 1, the motivational dependence. He contests the justificatory half, which the thesis does not need. The thesis's "cannot replace" is what his reply most threatens, and the register does not support that phrase (above). Grade: **on balance** for clause 1 against this objection; **open** for "cannot replace".

## The doctrine (`the-doctrine`)

The lesson composes the verdicts above into 17 postulates and 9 theses (grades composed, no new evidence). Its own Verdicts weigh hard cases and critics against those grades; none adds a new finding, and none is counted in the Balance table below.

### Hard cases
- Hard case 1 — May a free state screen applicants by religion as such, rather than by declared hostility to its constitutional order? → **Open.** For: second-generation Muslims keep their parents' religiosity, and integration by itself failed for the non-EU second generation (both **strong**, module 15). Neither finding separates religion from origin, education, sex and declared ideology. Screening by declared hostility follows from T1 and T3, **on balance**. Decided by: no series separating religion from those four in predicting integration. Would be overturned by: integration outcomes by cohort, employment by sex, second-generation attachment and age-and-sex-adjusted suspect rates with religion separated from them.
- Hard case 2 — How many is too many? → No Verdict (prose). The criterion follows from T3 and P8 (a cohort stops converging on the population in employment by sex, second-generation outcomes and trust); the number cannot be derived until a series relating cohort size and composition to integration is gathered.
- Hard case 3 — Can a free state produce over generations the solidarity it cannot produce on demand? → **Open.** Decided by: the cases for and against are partial; the strongest case for (the Third Republic) rests on a summary of one book [secondary], and Hungary's cohesion is unmeasured. Would be overturned by: a checked account of a nation built under free institutions within a generation or two, or a cohesion series moving with a deliberate schooling programme and not with the economic cycle.

### Critics
- Rawls (hits P8, T2) → his objection does not overturn P8; his stability route is untested. Grade: **on balance** (the stability route **open**). Decided by: his theory presupposes a bounded people (§58); integration did not by itself produce allegiance. Would be overturned by: support for just institutions forming at equal rates in groups with and without a prior shared identity.
- Carens (hits T3) → does not overturn T3; the duty to those above the floor of rescue stays **open**. Grade: **on balance**. Decided by: a right to some adequate place, not this one; the movers' inference failed (**strong**). Would be overturned by: an argument that equal worth grounds a right to enter this state, or evidence that under free access realised migration runs far below stated desire.
- MacIntyre (hits P9, T6, and through them P8) → whether a burden-bearing loyalty can outlast the lost tradition is **open**. Decided by: no evidence measures it. Would be overturned by: a cohesion series in societies that lost a shared religion, tracked against willingness to bear burdens for strangers.
- Habermas (hits P8) → leaves P8 standing; bears on hard case 3. Grade: **on balance**. Decided by: P8 claims only the motivational dependence, which he grants. Would be overturned by: the trust-and-transfers series P8 names, showing no fall where shared identity weakens.
- The post-liberals (hit P16 and T1) → the remedy fails, **on balance**: it offers no check that could replace the removable rulers of T1. Decided by: Vermeule's safeguard lies in officials' good faith (**on balance**); the majority that wrote its presuppositions into Hungary's constitution overrode its court (Fourth Amendment, **strong**). Cohesion under that constitution is unmeasured (**open**) and is not used as evidence; the causal claim (liberalism, not wealth or technology, dissolved the bonds) is **open**. Would be overturned by: a mechanism in the theory, not in officials' character, that can block a governing majority's determination.
- The racial right (hits T4, P9) → the racial nation fails as a ground of membership; T4 does not rest on the genetic share being zero. Grade: **established**. Decided by: the racial accounts fail on their own tests (modules 6, 13); within-group heritability cannot apportion a between-group gap. Would be overturned by: an independent measure of ancestry that predicts the rise and fall of civilizations better than institutional or ecological causes.

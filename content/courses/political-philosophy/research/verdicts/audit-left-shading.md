# Audit of the verdict register for leftward shading

Scope: every verdict in `01-04.md`, `05-08.md`, `09-12.md` and `13-16.md`. Each verdict was checked on four counts. (1) Is the grade earned by the evidence the verdict cites? (2) Is strong counter-evidence for the right-leaning reading left out? (3) Does the wording lean? (4) Was the right-leaning author's strongest form tested?

A verdict is flagged only on evidence, argument or framing grounds. Several flags below move a verdict toward the right-leaning reading, and one or two move a verdict that already favoured the right. In every case the fix is to bring the verdict back to what the evidence carries.

New sources read for this audit are saved under `research/texts/verdict-audit/`, each with a `.meta.yaml` sidecar. All of them are abstracts or publisher summaries. Anything taken from search summaries or press coverage, and not from the primary document, is marked `[secondary]`. Anything not checked at all is marked `[UNVERIFIED]`.

Two patterns run through the flags:

- **The register is firmer than its own briefs.** In six places the brief says "mixed", "disputed", "both columns true" or "leave the weighing to the reader", and the register turns that into a grade against the right: Huntington, Burnham, Voegelin, British franchise, 1933 and Hazony.
- **The weak form gets tested.** Maistre was tested on the metric system rather than on constitutions. Hazony was tested on a definition of empire he does not use. De Benoist's cultural peoples were tested with ancient DNA. The ancient-inheritance thesis was tested on Europe's output in 1000.

### 1. `09-12.md` — Module 11, `testing-the-clash` (module verdict)

**Current verdict:** the clash hypothesis fails as a prediction about the frequency of conflict and about alignment. It survives only as a narrow claim about Islam–West and the first phase of kin-country rallying. Grade: **strong**.

**Problems:**
- **The register goes beyond its own brief.** The brief's closing ledger (`testing-the-clash.md`, link 8, l. ~331–345) says the theory "was checked and gave a mixed answer, with one corollary its author stated as a test contradicted by the record". The register turns "mixed" into "strong".
- **Most of the frequency evidence comes from before 1993.**
  - Russett, Oneal & Cox use MID dyad-years c. 1950–1992 (brief l. 123). The brief itself lists "the period" as Huntington's objection.
  - "No post-1989 shift" rests on a few post-1989 years in the data.
- **The objection is answered with a factual error.** The register says Huntington's escalation claim is one "he did not specify". He did. The 1993 article (`texts/huntington/clash-1993-foreign-affairs.txt` l. 195) says intra-civilizational conflicts "are likely to be less intense and less likely to expand than conflicts between civilizations".
- **Counter-evidence is left out.** Tusicisny (2004) is on the brief's own optional list, and it partly supports exactly the claim the register says nobody measured. On UCDP conflict-years, 1946–2001:
  - each of three post-war periods has more intercivilizational conflict-years than the one before;
  - after the Cold War, conflicts within civilizations became less likely to escalate to war, while the intensity of conflicts between civilizations stayed at Cold War levels;
  - "the majority of intercivilizational conflict-years during the post-Cold War period have involved Islamic groups";
  - Sinic–West conflict stayed marginal.

  Toft (2007) finds Islam involved in a disproportionate share of civil wars, 1940–2000 ("Islam's bloody borders").
- **The record after 2001 is missing.** Afghanistan, Iraq, the Islamic State wars, the Sahel and jihadist terrorism in Europe are Huntington's best case. Neither the brief nor the register mentions them. This is the omission the course author named.
- **Ukraine is cited one-sidedly.** The corollary "violence between Russians and Ukrainians should be low" is refuted. But in the same article (l. 173) Huntington draws the civilizational line that "cuts through … Ukraine separating the more Catholic western Ukraine from Orthodox eastern Ukraine". The 2014 annexation and the Donbas war took place on the eastern side of that line, and Ukraine has since moved toward the West. The 1996 book reportedly forecasts that Ukraine could "split along its fault line", with the eastern part drawn to Russia [secondary; wording UNVERIFIED — check *The Clash of Civilizations and the Remaking of World Order* (1996), ch. 7]. That is a partial hit within the same case.

**Evidence:**
- Tusicisny, *JPR* 41:4 (2004), abstract — `texts/verdict-audit/tusicisny-2004-abstract.txt`
- Toft, *International Security* 31:4 (2007), summary — `texts/verdict-audit/toft-2007-summary.txt`
- Huntington 1993, ll. 173 and 195

**Proposed verdict:** the theory gets a mixed answer, as the brief has it. Grade: **on balance**, by part:
- **Frequency** (across-line conflict more common than within-line): **fails, on balance.** The dyadic tests mostly predate 1993.
- **Islam at the centre** of post-Cold-War cross-line conflict: **holds, strong** (Tusicisny, Toft). The brief should bind a post-2001 UCDP count [UNVERIFIED until bound].
- **Escalation** (cross-line conflict more intense): **partly supported, open.** Tusicisny supports it; the dyadic tests did not measure it.
- **Restraint between Russia and Ukraine:** **refuted, strong.**
- **Bosnia as a test of alignment:** fits the first year, fails after 1994, **on balance.**

The module answer becomes: civilizations are real units of identity. The clash describes the Islam-centred conflicts of the period well and the rest of world politics badly.

### 2. `09-12.md` — `burnham-managers-and-the-suicide-of-the-west` (the 1964 thesis)

**Current verdict:** "Liberalism is the ideology of Western suicide" **fails on the test Burnham himself set**. Grade: **on balance**.

**Problems:**
- **The register overrides its brief.** The brief (l. 316–331) gives both readings and calls the evidence "disputed". Its exercise (l. 477–479) says the events of 1989–91 "do not settle it without a further premise".
- **The test equivocates on "liberalism".** Burnham's word means a specific ideology: his 19 propositions, guilt, anti-anticommunism, and accommodation in place of rollback. It does not mean liberal democracy. "Liberal states led the coalition that won" uses the second sense to score a test written in the first.
- **The test's logic is not checked.** The 1980s reversal came under governments that had rejected détente liberalism in Burnham's sense. Reagan gave Burnham the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 1983 [secondary]. That is consistent with Burnham's conditional, not a refutation of it.
- **An irrelevant point is used against him.** That the former imperial states grew richer is beside a thesis defined by territory ruled, and the brief records that Burnham chose that measure on purpose.

**Evidence:** Burnham, *Suicide of the West* (1964), `texts/burnham/suicide-of-the-west.txt` l. 2573: "if the contraction … should cease and be reversed, then the ideology of liberalism, deprived of its primary function, will fade away".

**Proposed verdict:** the 1964 thesis is **not settled by 1989–91**. Grade: **open**.
- Contraction measured by territory ruled was real to 1975, and it reversed in Europe in 1989–91.
- Whether liberalism in Burnham's sense faded with it depends on a measure the course does not have.
- What would settle it: a series on elite opinion for Burnham's propositions, 1964–2000. For example, positions on rollback against containment, or on decolonisation and guilt. The test is then whether those positions weakened before or after the reversal.

The 1941 half ("established") is fair.

### 3. `09-12.md` — `voegelin-gnosticism`

**Current verdict:** modern mass movements as one Gnostic phenomenon descending from Joachim **fails as a genealogy**. It holds only as the observation that these movements borrowed eschatological symbols. Grade: **on balance**. It is decided by (a) the vagueness test and (b) "the author's own 1974 recantation".

**Problems:**
- **(a) The vagueness test is treated as unanswered.** The brief supplies the reply (link 8, l. 43 and exercise 7, l. 93). The typology does discriminate: it has three faculties and three variants, and England and America are expressly excluded as early waves. The register states the charge and leaves the reply out.
- **(b) The 1974 sentence is misused.** It comes from the Introduction to *The Ecumenic Age* (quoted via Lilla). There Voegelin abandons the unilinear plan of *Order and History*, the "one line" of history leading into the present. He does not withdraw the Gnosticism diagnosis. The brief itself asks the reader which of the two it recants (l. 43). The register treats the open question as answered against him.
- **The structural thesis is reduced to "borrowed symbols".** Its strongest form is that the totalitarian movements share the structure of an immanentized eschaton: a final state of history within time, reached by human action and known by a saving knowledge. That has independent support in Cohn, *The Pursuit of the Millennium* (1957), and in Löwith, *Meaning in History* (1949). Neither is in the dossier.

**Proposed verdict:**
- (i) **Genealogy** (a textual and causal line from Joachim to the modern movements): **not shown.** Grade: **on balance.**
- (ii) **Structural diagnosis** (the major totalitarian movements share an immanentized eschatology, and the typology excludes the Anglo-American constitutional tradition): **holds.** Grade: **on balance.**

Drop the 1974 sentence as decisive evidence. Report it as a revision of Voegelin's plan of history whose bearing on the diagnosis is disputed.

### 4. `05-08.md` — Module 8, `the-right-in-1933` (module verdict)

**Current verdict:** each doctrine gave reasons both to join and to refuse. Those who refused did so on grounds outside the doctrine, and Schmitt and Heidegger had no norm by which to refuse. Grade: **strong**. It would be overturned by "a passage in Schmitt's or Heidegger's pre-1933 work stating a criterion by which a decision … could be judged illegitimate, and which they could have applied in 1933".

**Problems:**
- **The overturning passage exists, and the register cites it.** *Legalität und Legitimität* (July 1932) argues that a constitution may deny the "equal chance" to parties that would use legality to abolish it. That is a stated criterion of illegitimacy, applicable in 1933. The register's own sentence concedes it ("a year after the same theory had argued for excluding the NSDAP"). So does the brief: "the doctrine did not choose the side; what moved him was the step from 'someone must decide' to 'the one who has decided is the law'" (`the-right-in-1933.md` link 2, l. 51).
- **The failure lies in a different step.** The doctrine had a criterion. What it also had was the clause *auctoritas non veritas facit legem*: once a new authority has decided, its decision is law. That clause cancels the criterion. It is a narrower finding than "no norm".
- **Selection: a counter-case is missing.** The roll-call leaves out Edgar Julius Jung. He was the Conservative Revolution's main theorist of the "new Reich" against mass rule. He wrote Papen's Marburg speech of June 1934 and was killed in the purge [secondary; not in the course]. His refusal came from his doctrine: the elite against mass rule. A verdict drawn from a sample that leaves out the clearest counter-case is too firm.
- **The liberal comparator is set aside.** The objection notes that the liberal parties and the Centre also voted for the Enabling Act. The register says that failure is "argued in `article-48-weimar`". But that lesson does not ask whether liberal doctrine gave its holders a ground to refuse. One standard requires the same question to be put to both.

**Proposed verdict:** the doctrines gave reasons on both sides.
- Schmitt's contained a criterion against a legal revolution (1932). It also contained the step that made whoever succeeded the source of law (1933–34). In him the second step won.
- Heidegger's historicity gave no criterion.
- Spengler refused on grounds his morphology does not supply.
- Jung and Evola refused on grounds their doctrines did supply.
- The finding is narrower than "no norm": a doctrine that makes the successful decision the source of law has no ground to refuse a revolution once it has succeeded.

Grade: **on balance.** The brief should add Jung and should ask the same question of the liberal parties' vote of 23 March 1933.

### 5. `05-08.md` — Module 7, `article-48-weimar` (module verdict)

**Current verdict:** the theory's prescription failed on its own terms. Grade: **strong**. That it helped produce the collapse is graded **on balance**.

**Problems:**
- **A hit for Schmitt is scored as a failure.** "In 1933 the 'equal chance' the theory meant to deny the regime's enemies was used by them through Article 48 and an Article 76 two-thirds vote." That is the 1932 warning coming true. The brief says so: "the equal-chance doctrine said a constitution may deny legality to its enemies; the Enabling Act was the enemies using legality against the constitution" (l. 450–453). It belongs in the diagnosis column, not the prescription column.
- **The remedy Schmitt urged was never applied.** In winter 1932–33 the plan from Schleicher's circle, which Schmitt advised, was an emergency regime that would keep the NSDAP out (brief l. 167: "to Schmitt's dismay, Hitler…"). Hindenburg declined it and appointed Hitler by the parliamentary route. Papen's decree regime, and the Prussian coup the court split on, are fair evidence against the commissarial model. "The remedy failed" should be limited to them.
- **The "helped produce it" half ignores its own overturning evidence.** After July 1932 the NSDAP and the KPD held 319 of 608 seats [secondary: standard election figures], a negative majority. No government could command a parliamentary majority without one of them. That is the overturning evidence the register asks for, for the period after July 1932. For 1930–32 a majority without both was still possible in arithmetic.

**Proposed verdict:**
- **The diagnosis held:** the Reichstag could not form a will, and the equal chance was used to abolish the constitution, as Schmitt warned in 1932. Grade: **strong.**
- **The commissarial remedy, as practised 1930–32, had no criterion that kept it commissarial.** Grade: **on balance.** The remedy Schmitt urged at the end was never tried.
- **Whether the theory helped produce the collapse:** **on balance for 1930–32; open after July 1932**, when no majority without the NSDAP or the KPD existed.

### 6. `13-16.md` — Module 14, `auditing-the-case-for-colonialism`, the title and ledger halves

**Title. Current verdict:** "Conquest of a people that already owned its land and governed itself had no title under any standard the course holds." Grade: **strong**.

**Problem:** the course holds Vitoria, and Vitoria's legitimate titles include defence of the innocent against tyranny and human sacrifice "even if the victims consent" (`vitoria-right-to-the-americas.md` l. 251). They also include enforcing travel, trade and preaching by war, and in his scheme a just war could end with the removal of rulers. The register's own lesson verdict on Vitoria grades those titles "a licence". So a standard the course holds did give a title in some circumstances. Aztec human sacrifice is the case it was written for.

**Proposed title verdict:**
- The titles from discovery, papal donation, unbelief and natural slavery **fail. Grade: strong.**
- On Vitoria's premises there was a **limited title to intervene**: defence of the innocent, and the rights of travel and trade. Whether a lasting conquest could rest on it is **on balance no**, because the conqueror judged its own case, as the Vitoria verdict already says.

**Ledger. Current verdict:** the Case's general thesis of net benefit is "not established", and uniform harm is not established either. Grade: **on balance**. The strongest objection ("the burden lies on whoever claims that alien rule was beneficial") is accepted: "The objection is right about the burden".

**Problems:**
- **Burden.** Under one standard, the burden lies on whoever asserts a net effect in either direction. Conceding a one-sided burden is a tilt.
- **Omitted evidence that meets the register's own overturning condition** ("a design that compares colonised with uncolonised places under a credible counterfactual"):
  - Feyrer & Sacerdote (2009) use islands, with colonial duration instrumented by wind patterns. Years as a European colony are "strongly positively related" to GDP per head today and negatively related to infant mortality. Years after 1700 count for more.
  - Easterly & Levine (2016) find "a strong and uniformly positive relationship between colonial European settlement and development". The relationship holds even at low settler shares, against the extractive-institutions literature.
- **Heldring & Robinson are reported only through the Case.** Their own abstract says colonialism "probably had a uniformly negative effect on development in Africa", while "in the world context, colonialism had very heterogeneous effects". The register cites their African result and drops their world-scale qualification.

**Evidence:** `texts/verdict-audit/nber-w12546-abstract.txt`, `nber-w18162-abstract.txt`, `nber-w18566-abstract.txt`.

**Proposed ledger verdict:** the effect **depends on the kind of colony**.
- Where Europeans settled, and on the islands, colonial tenure goes with higher income today: **on balance**, from an instrumented design on islands and a cross-country association for settlement.
- In sub-Saharan Africa the best assessment is negative: **on balance** (Heldring & Robinson).
- A general verdict of net benefit or net harm **is not established in either direction (open)**. The burden lies on both claims equally.
- Islands are not the African mainland, so Feyrer & Sacerdote do not decide the Case's African claims. They do show the counterfactual design exists.

### 7. `13-16.md` — `albions-seed-and-who-are-we`, (b) Huntington on Hispanic assimilation

**Current verdict:** **failed on language and identity** (high intermarriage, attachment rising). Grade: **strong**. Education is **on balance** supported.

**Problem:** one of the two sources the verdict cites contradicts its identity half. Telles & Ortiz (2008) followed Mexican Americans over 35 years and four or five generations. They found:
- English proficiency is near-universal by the second generation;
- "the majority of fourth generation Mexican Americans continue to live in Hispanic neighborhoods, marry other Hispanics, and think of themselves as Mexican";
- economic progress halts at the second generation;
- educational attainment "declines for the third and fourth generations".

So "high intermarriage" and "identity: failed" cannot both rest on this study. Citrin et al. (2007) measure attachment to the United States, which rises with generation. That bears on Huntington's claim of a threat to American identity, not on whether a Hispanic ethnic identity persists.

**Evidence:** `texts/verdict-audit/telles-ortiz-2008-publisher-summary.txt` (publisher summary [secondary to the book]).

**Proposed verdict:**
- **Language:** the forecast failed. Grade: **strong.**
- **Attachment to the US:** rises with generation, against the "threat" claim. Grade: **on balance.**
- **Persistent ethnic identity, endogamy and residential concentration** into the fourth generation: Huntington partly borne out. Grade: **on balance** (Telles & Ortiz). Ethnic attrition, meaning descendants who stop identifying as Hispanic, may bias that sample toward people who kept the identity, so the grade stays on balance.
- **Education stalls:** borne out. Grade: **on balance.**

### 13. `09-12.md` — Module 12, `the-borrowing-ledger` (module verdict)

## The 2015 verdict (`13-16.md`, Module 15) — framing issues only

This verdict is being rewritten elsewhere, so only framing is noted here:

1. **"Capacity, not principle"** removes the decision itself from the record. The suspension of Dublin returns for Syrians (BAMF, 25 August 2015) and the decision of 4–5 September 2015 not to close the border were taken by the executive, without a Bundestag vote [secondary]. Whether a people's consent was asked is the module's own question. The verdict should state the decision as a question of principle, then the capacity failure.
2. **The order of the integration figures leads with the favourable ones.**
   - The headline 64% and the 86% male figure come first. Women at 31% against 72% comes last.
   - The 86% after eight or more years compares earlier and smaller arrival cohorts, which have been through selective attrition, with the male average. It is not a like-for-like comparison.
   - All the employment figures should come first, in one block, with women's figure beside men's.
3. **The security record is missing.** Attacks by people who arrived in or after 2015 include:
   - Cologne, New Year's Eve 2015/16;
   - Würzburg and Ansbach, July 2016;
   - Berlin Breitscheidplatz, December 2016, by an asylum seeker who arrived in 2015;
   - Solingen, August 2024;
   - Aschaffenburg and Munich, early 2025.

   [secondary; bind each to a court or BKA record.] Like the crime ratios, these belong in the verdict, stated as dated facts.
4. **"Murray's paralysis prediction failed"** dates the correction to instruments of 2016, 2024 and 2026, ten years on. "His 'never asked' was answered at the ballot box" turns a later electoral reaction into consent after the fact. The register's own strongest objection grants that the reversal followed electoral pressure. The fair wording: the liberal order corrected itself late, under electoral pressure, through its own instruments. Murray's claim that it could not act fails. His claim that the public was not asked holds.
5. **"Failed on employment"** should be "partly failed". Employment at 64% against 77%, and women at 31%, is a gap that has narrowed, not a refuted forecast.

# Verdict register: liberal and left forecasts against the record

This file is a supplement to `01-04.md` … `13-16.md`. The human's review found that the register tests right-wing forecasts against the record far more often than liberal and left ones, so the right loses more often by selection. This file runs the same test on the other side. Each entry gives:
- the forecast, dated and quoted (or stated precisely), with who made it;
- what happened, with figures and sources;
- the verdict and its grade (established / strong / on balance / open);
- what would overturn the verdict;
- the lesson it belongs in.

Entries are grouped by host lesson, in course order. Each host was confirmed against its brief, and the step in the brief that stages the forecast is cited.

All documents read for this file are saved under `research/texts/liberal-forecasts/<subdir>/`, each with a `.meta.yaml`. Anything read only as an abstract, a press report or a secondary account is marked so. Figures already bound in `13-16.md` (IAB 10/2024, BKA 2024, the 2025 election) are cited from there.

The standard is the one applied to Spengler and Powell:
- A forecast with a date or a measurable direction is judged on the record.
- A forecast protected by an open horizon ("in the long run", "one day") is called untestable in that part. A forecast cannot pass that way, just as Spengler's morphology could not.
- A forecast that held is recorded as held.

## Module 9 — `voegelin-gnosticism`

The brief stages Condorcet's progress of the human mind as the course's example of progressivism, the teleological variant of immanentization (explanation link 4; set piece "taxonomy worked on one case"; exercise 1, figure 2). Its question is "what exactly is wrong with believing history has a direction and an end?" Voegelin's answer, that history has no eidos and so "progress as a law" treats a symbol of faith as a proposition, is an argument about the form of the claim. It needs no record and stands as argued. What the record *can* test is Condorcet's concrete forecasts, and the lesson should test them. Otherwise the reader learns that the progressivist error is a category mistake without learning that most of what the progressivist expected happened.

- `voegelin-gnosticism`: **Condorcet's forecasts of progress** (*Esquisse*, written 1793–94, publ. 1795; Tenth Epoch, Eng. tr. London: J. Johnson 1795, pp. 316–372). The three hopes: "the destruction of inequality between different nations; the progress of equality in one and the same nation; and lastly, the real improvement of man" (p. 317). The particular forecasts:
  - colonial "monopolies, our treachery, our sanguinary contempt for men of a different complexion" ended, and the slave trade with them (pp. 321–322);
  - instruction made equal, which "corrects the natural inequality of the faculties" (pp. 330–331);
  - "the total annihilation of the prejudices which have established between the sexes an inequality of rights" (p. 355);
  - war ranked "in the number of those daring atrocities" (pp. 356–358);
  - "the mean duration of human life will for ever increase", though "man will not become immortal" (pp. 367–369).

  **Record:**
  - World life expectancy rose from 28.5 years (1800) to 73.2 (2023). Source: OWID, from Riley 2005 and UN WPP 2024.
  - World literacy rose from 12% (1820) to 87.7% (2024). Source: OWID; the definitions change over time.
  - The Atlantic slave trade and legal slavery were abolished, and the colonial empires were dissolved by the 1960s–70s. These are standard facts; the brief should bind a standard history.
  - Equality of the sexes in rights is now law across the West.
  - Between-country inequality moved against him for 160 years and then turned. As a share of global inequality (Theil), it was 11% in 1820, 57% in 1980 and 32% in 2020. Within-country inequality "now [is] even greater than … between countries" (World Inequality Report 2022, Fig. 6, p. 13).
  - War: the 20th century's two world wars came first. After 1950, deaths in state-based conflicts per 100,000 fell from 22.2 (1950) to 0.19 (2005), then rose to 3.56 (2022) and stood at 1.87 in 2025 (OWID/UCDP; the three columns summed).

  **Verdict:**
  - (a) The material forecasts **held**: longer life, universal instruction, abolition of slavery, end of colonial rule, legal equality of the sexes. Grade: **strong**. They were directions, not dates, but they were checkable directions and could have failed.
  - (b) Equality between nations **failed for a century and a half**, and has moved his way only since 1980. Grade: **on balance** (failed over most of the horizon; the current trend is his).
  - (c) The decline of war **failed through 1945**, and has held in rate since 1950, with a reversal since 2005. Grade: **open**.
  - (d) "Progress" as a necessary law: **not testable**. Voegelin's objection to it stands as argument. Condorcet's own "indefinite" perfectibility is the open-horizon clause the register refuses to credit.

  **Would be overturned by:** for (a), a sustained reversal (a fall in world life expectancy or literacy over decades, not a pandemic year); for (c), a return to pre-1950 rates of conflict death.

  **Note for the human:** this verdict goes *for* a progressive forecast. It is earned: the series are long and the direction is unambiguous.

## Module 11 — `huntington-clash-of-civilizations`

The brief stages "the end of history (one liberal world)" as the first rival Huntington rejects (explanation link 1, l. 214; References for the essay). Huntington's 1993 text names it (`texts/huntington/clash-1993-foreign-affairs.txt` l. 146). The register currently lists this lesson under "no verdict", because the clash itself is tested in `testing-the-clash`. The rival, though, is a liberal forecast with its own record, and the lesson that stages it is where it is judged. That makes this one lesson verdict here, not in the closing lesson.

`deneen-post-liberalism` also names "Whig history and Fukuyama" (ch. 1, l. ~276). It can Ref this verdict rather than repeat it.

- `huntington-clash-of-civilizations`: **Fukuyama's end of history.** Francis Fukuyama, "The End of History?", *The National Interest* 16 (Summer 1989), p. 4:

  > "What we may be witnessing is … the end of history as such: that is, the end point of mankind's ideological evolution and the universalization of Western liberal democracy as the final form of human government."

  Hedged in the next sentence: the victory "has occurred primarily in the realm of ideas or consciousness and is as yet incomplete in the real or material world. But there are powerful reasons for believing that it is the ideal that will govern the material world in the long run."

  Particular claims:
  - **China** "can no longer act as a beacon for illiberal forces around the world". Students returning from abroad will not be "content for China to be the only country in Asia unaffected by the larger democratizing trend" (pp. 10–11).
  - **Islam**: "the doctrine has little appeal for non-Muslims, and it is hard to believe that the movement will take on any universal significance" (p. 14).
  - **Nationalism**: it is not "an irreconcilable contradiction in the heart of liberalism" (pp. 14–15).

  **Record:**
  - *1989–2005: the trend bore him out.* Free countries numbered 88 in 2025, "more than double" the number in 1973. In 2005, 46% of countries were Free and 24% Not Free.
  - *Since then: twenty consecutive years of net decline* (Freedom House, *Freedom in the World 2026*, pp. 3, 5).
    - Not Free countries rose from 45 to 59; Not Free went from 24% to about 30% of countries.
    - In 2025, 54 countries declined and 35 improved.
    - 21% of the world's population lives in Free countries and 40% in Not Free.
  - V-Dem (*Democracy Report 2026*, p. 4):
    - 74% of the world's population lives in autocracies and 7% in liberal democracies.
    - "Democracy is back to 1978 levels for the average global citizen."
    - There are 92 autocracies against 87 democracies.
    - 44 countries are autocratizing, holding 41% of the world's population.
  - China remains a one-party state.
  - Political Islam produced the main armed ideological challenge to the liberal states after 1989: 9/11 and the jihadist wars. The register already counts that in its reconsideration of Huntington's grade.
  - Fukuyama himself, 2017: "Twenty five years ago, I didn't have a sense or a theory about how democracies can go backward … And I think they clearly can." (*Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists*, 13 Feb 2017, quoting a *Washington Post* interview [secondary].)

  **Verdict:**
  - (a) The **forecast of spreading liberal democracy failed** from 2006, after holding for the sixteen years before. Grade: **strong**. Both indices agree, and the run is twenty years long.
  - (b) The **China forecast failed**. Grade: **strong**. No liberalization followed; Fukuyama himself put the decision with the returning elite.
  - (c) **Islam would not acquire universal significance**: **split**. As a creed with appeal to non-Muslims, political Islam did not become a universal rival, and this narrow claim **holds** (on balance). As a force that kept history moving, with wars and terror waged in its name, the sentence **failed** (strong).
  - (d) The core claim, that no rival *ideal* with a universal claim has displaced liberal democracy "in the long run": **not testable as stated**. The "long run" clause protects it exactly as Spengler's morphology was protected, and the register credited Spengler with nothing for that clause. The register should say this the same way here.

  **Would be overturned by:** for (a), a reversal of the indices back to their 2005 level; for (b), a competitive election in China; for (d), a dated, checkable restatement of it.

## Module 11 — `testing-the-clash` (with a Ref to `hazony-virtue-of-nationalism`)

`testing-the-clash` stages Russett, Oneal & Cox (2000), whose title sets the clash against "Realism and Liberalism Déjà Vu", with joint democracy and trade as controls (brief, Sources 1). `hazony-virtue-of-nationalism` stages Kant's *Perpetual Peace* (set piece 6) as the anti-nationalist paradigm. The liberal forecast underneath both is Kant's.

- `testing-the-clash`: **Kant's republican peace.** Kant, *Perpetual Peace* (1795), First Definitive Article (Campbell Smith tr. 1903): where "the consent of the subjects is required to determine whether there shall be war", they will "weigh the matter well, before undertaking such a bad business". A federation of free states "is to extend gradually over all states and so lead to perpetual peace … the federation would extend further and further" (Second Definitive Article). Its policy form is the Schuman Declaration, 9 May 1950: pooling coal and steel "will make it plain that any war between France and Germany becomes not merely unthinkable, but materially impossible".

  **Record:**
  - Russett, Oneal & Cox (*JPR* 37:5, 2000; abstract read), on militarized interstate disputes 1950–92: "liberal influences of joint democracy and interdependence" together with realist ones "provide a much better account of interstate conflict" than civilizations do. "Among Huntington's eight civilizations, interstate conflict was significantly less likely only within the West."
  - No war between France and Germany, or between any two members of the European Communities/Union, since 1950.
  - The Union grew from six members to twenty-eight, and one (the UK) left by treaty.
  - The 2022 war in Europe was begun by a state that is not a democracy (V-Dem, FH above).

  **Verdict:** Kant's forecast that states governed by citizens' consent would not make war on one another **holds**. So does the Schuman forecast. Grade: **strong**.

  **Limits:**
  - Republics have gone to war freely against non-republics, so the mechanism Kant gave (citizens weigh the costs) is not shown to be the cause.
  - Whether democracy, trade or alliance carries the effect is disputed in the literature. Rosato 2003 and Gartzke 2007 are the standard critiques [not read; References only].
  - "Extend gradually over all states" fails with (a) in the Fukuyama verdict above.

  **Would be overturned by:** a war between two established democracies, or between two EU member states.

## Module 15 — `carens-open-borders`

The brief stages Carens's conclusion (Sources, pp. 270–271, l. 983: "most people do not love to move"). `the-2015-crisis-read-three-ways` applies it ("flows track how bad life is"). The register now lists this lesson under "no verdict", as one side of the dispute Miller decides. Carens's empirical premise is a separate, checkable claim, and this is the lesson that states it.

- `carens-open-borders`: **the premise that open borders would bring few movers.** Carens, "Aliens and Citizens: The Case for Open Borders", *Review of Politics* 49 (1987), p. 270:

  > "Open borders would threaten the distinctive character of different political communities only because we assume that so many people would move if they could. If the migrants were few, it would not matter … And, as Walzer observes, most human beings do not love to move … They seek to move only when life is very difficult where they are."

  **Record:**
  - Gallup World Poll, 2023: "16% of adults worldwide -- which projects to over 900 million people -- said they would like to leave their own country permanently, if they could". This was up from 12–15% in 2011–18. The US is the top desired destination, at 18% of would-be migrants (about 170 million); Canada is second at 9% (Ray & Pugliese, Gallup, 30 Oct 2024).
  - 2025: 15% of adults, about 900 million. The US is named by 15% (about 134 million); Germany, the UK, France and others by roughly 3–5% each (Gallup, 23 Apr 2026).

  **Verdict:**
  - The premise **holds as stated**: about 84% of adults do not wish to move.
  - The inference Carens draws from it, that the migrants would be "few", **fails**. Even at 3% naming it, the would-be migrants to Germany number about 27 million, a third of its population. The US figure exceeds half its population.
  - Grade: **strong**. A stated wish is not a move, which is why this is not "established". Where moves were cheap and legal, though, the realised flows ran *above* official forecasts, not below: the EU's 2004 enlargement, forecast at 13,000 a year for the UK, judged in `murray-strange-death-of-europe`.

  **Would be overturned by:** evidence that, under free access, realised migration runs an order of magnitude below stated desire. Carens's nonideal clause ("size of demand" may justify restriction) concedes the point in advance.

## Module 15 — `murray-strange-death-of-europe`

The brief stages three liberal forecasts as the "excuses" and the integration record:
- the economics excuse, with the UCL study as the worked example, and the ageing excuse ("the pyramid") (explanation link 3; set pieces 4–5);
- integration "takes a generation; wait" and Spencer's "We just believed migrants would integrate" (link 5; Murray ch. 3 l. 1799–1801), with Merkel at Potsdam (ch. 6 l. 2773 ff.);
- the demographers' objection that the Muslim share projections assume no secularization, which Murray concedes "may be the case or may not" (link 6; ch. 16 l. 7837–7840; exercise 5 asks for "secularisation rates among Muslim Europeans").

All three are judged here. The 2015 claims go to the next lesson.

- `murray-strange-death-of-europe` (1): **immigration as a net fiscal gain that helps carry an ageing population.** Dated statements:
  - The European Commission, *A European Agenda on Migration*, COM(2015) 240, 13 May 2015, §III.4, p. 14: "without migration the EU's working age population will decline by 17.5 million in the next decade. Migration will increasingly be an important way to enhance the sustainability of our welfare system".
  - The UCL working paper's headline (Dustmann & Frattini 2013), as the brief stages it: EEA migrants paid "34 per cent more in taxes than they received".
  - The Home Office RDS paper of 2001 (Glover et al.), which the brief tells the writer to read. Its own fiscal sentence is not yet bound [brief to add].

  **Record, split by origin and motive wherever the sources split it:**
  - *Netherlands* (van de Beek, Roodenburg, Hartog & Kreffer, *Borderless Welfare State*, Eng. ed. 2023; lifetime, 2016 euros, second generation included):
    - total net cost 1995–2019 €400bn, about €17bn a year (p. 17);
    - per immigrant, by motive: labour +€125,000, study −€75,000, family −€275,000, asylum −€475,000. "Only labour immigration generates a positive average net contribution" (p. 18);
    - by origin: Western +€25,000, non-Western −€275,000 (p. 18);
    - a positive contribution "requires … at least a bachelor's degree" (p. 19);
    - "None of the 22 second-generation groups … made a positive net contribution" (p. 98).
  - *Denmark* (Finansministeriet, *Indvandreres nettobidrag … i 2019*, rev. Sept 2023, Table 1.1, p. 5; DKK bn, immigrants and descendants):
    - Western immigrants and descendants: +5 (2014) to +11 (2019);
    - non-Western: −39 to −27, of which the MENAPT countries (Middle East, North Africa, Pakistan, Turkey) −29 to −24;
    - per person in 2019: MENAPT descendants −109,000 kr (Table 2.1, p. 8).
  - *United Kingdom* (MAC, *EEA migration in the UK: Final report*, Sept 2018, ch. 4, summarising Oxford Economics):
    - 2016/17, per adult against the average resident: EEA +£2,300, non-EEA −£840, UK-born −£70 (p. 73);
    - lifetime: EEA about +£78,000 and non-EEA about +£28,000 per head (p. 76). The UK result differs from the Dutch and Danish ones in sign for non-Europeans.
  - *OECD* (*International Migration Outlook 2021*, ch. 4):
    - immigrants' total net contribution is "between ‑1% and +1% of GDP for most countries";
    - with pure public goods apportioned per head, it is negative in 18 of 25 countries, against 20 of 25 for the native-born, in deficit years (Table 4.1);
    - counting immigrants' native-born children lowers the figure by about 0.5 pp of GDP;
    - EU-born immigrants fare better than non-EU-born, and labour-migration countries better than humanitarian ones.
  - *Ageing* (UN Population Division, *Replacement Migration*, 2000, executive summary):
    - holding the support ratio constant 2000–2050 would need 674 million migrants into the EU (13.5 million a year) and 181.5 million into Germany;
    - this is "out of reach" and "totally unrealistic";
    - raising the working-age limit to about 75 would do it instead.

  **Verdict:**
  - (a) **Immigration as such is a net fiscal gain: fails** as a general claim. In the two lifetime and origin-split studies of high-welfare states, the result is negative overall and strongly negative for asylum and family migration and for non-Western origins. It persists into the second generation (NL). Grade: **strong** for the Dutch and Danish type of welfare state. For the OECD as a whole, the honest statement is "small either way", and that is not the gain claimed.
  - (b) **Selected labour migration, and migration from rich and EU countries, is a net gain: holds.** Grade: **strong**. All four sources agree.
  - (c) **Immigration can carry the pension burden: fails.** Grade: **established**. The UN's own arithmetic requires numbers it calls unrealistic, and immigrants age (Murray's pyramid, conceded by the source).

  **Would be overturned by:** lifetime studies in the same countries that include dynamic effects (productivity, innovation, prices) and reverse the sign for asylum and family cohorts; for (c), a demonstration that a feasible inflow holds the support ratio.

  **Where it belongs:** link 3 and set piece 4. The worked example should end on the split, not on either headline.

- `murray-strange-death-of-europe` (2): **"they will go home" and "integration will happen by itself".** Dated statements:
  - The German–Turkish recruitment agreement, 30 Oct 1961, clause 9: "Die Aufenthaltserlaubnis wird über eine Gesamtaufenthaltsdauer von zwei Jahren hinaus nicht erteilt" (rotation; bpb reproduction).
  - The federal cabinet decision of 3 Feb 1982 (BT-Drs. 9/1629, 5 May 1982): limit further immigration, "die Rückkehrbereitschaft zu stärken", integrate those long resident.
  - Sarah Spencer's retrospective on 1997–2005 British policy: "There was no policy for integration. We just believed migrants would integrate" (Murray l. 1799–1801).

  The admissions:
  - Merkel, Potsdam, 16 Oct 2010: "We kidded ourselves a while, we said: 'They won't stay, sometime they will be gone', but this isn't reality … [the multicultural approach] has failed, utterly failed" (BBC tr.; Spiegel: „absolut gescheitert"; press reports, no official transcript).
  - Cameron, Munich, 5 Feb 2011: "Under the doctrine of state multiculturalism, we have encouraged different cultures to live separate lives" (gov.uk).
  - Sarkozy, TF1, 10 Feb 2011: « Oui, c'est un échec » (wire report).

  **Record:**
  - Of about 870,000 Turkish recruits before the 1973 stop, about half a million returned (bpb background note [secondary]). The rest stayed and brought families.
  - Second generation (OECD/EU, *Indicators of Immigrant Integration 2023: Settling In*; native-born with foreign-born parents against native-born with native-born parents):
    - employment at ages 15–34, not in education: 68% against about three-quarters (p. 194). In the EU it is "particularly low" for non-EU parentage, while EU parentage matches native parentage;
    - EU, ages 25–34: highly educated 32% against 40%, low-educated 21% against 14% (p. 186);
    - lacking basic reading skills: 29% against 18%;
    - perceived discrimination rose 5 points from 2010–14 to 2016–20, "entirely driven by" the non-EU-parentage group (p. 208);
    - the NEET gap is narrowing (−3 points 2012–20), and the report heads the trend "catching up".
  - Whether multicultural *policy* caused the shortfall is disputed (all abstracts only):
    - Koopmans (*JEMS* 2010) finds multicultural policy combined with a generous welfare state associated with low employment, high segregation and high conviction rates;
    - Wright & Bloemraad (2012) find "multiculturalism in no case hinders" political engagement;
    - Heath & Demireva find no slower intergenerational change among recognised groups.

  **Verdict:**
  - (a) **The guest workers would return: failed.** Grade: **established**. The policy's own authors said so in 1982 and in 2010.
  - (b) **Integration would follow by itself, without a policy: failed** for second-generation Europeans of non-EU parentage on education, employment and felt membership. It **held** for those of EU parentage, and the gap is narrowing. Grade: **strong**.
  - (c) **The admissions' causal claim**, that multiculturalism as a policy produced the failure: **open**. Koopmans and his critics measure different outcomes, and the course has only abstracts. It would be settled by a cross-national panel relating policy indices to second-generation employment and education with welfare generosity controlled.

  **Would be overturned by:** for (b), third-generation data at parity on employment and education.

  **Sources the brief must add:** a segregation series (e.g. dissimilarity indices for a German, Dutch or British city); none was fetched here.

- `murray-strange-death-of-europe` (3): **Muslim-origin Europeans will secularize as Christian-origin Europeans did.** This is the assimilationist expectation, and the brief states it at full strength as the demographers' objection ("switching may run both ways"). Murray leaves it open: projections "assume that Christians will continue to become non-religious whereas Muslims will not, which may be the case or may not" (l. 7837–7840). Pew's projections build in a 10% defection rate for rising Muslim cohorts (*Europe's Growing Muslim Population*, 29 Nov 2017, fn. 5, p. 34). The scenarios give 7.4% / 11.2% / 14% Muslim in 2050, from 4.9% in 2016 (p. 5).

  **Record:**
  - France (INSEE 2023, Drouhot, Simon & Tiberj, TeO2 2019–20):
    - 91% of those raised Muslim keep the religion, against 67% of those raised Catholic;
    - the predicted probability that religion is "very important" is 70% for children of immigrants from strongly religious Muslim homes, against 21% from strongly religious Catholic homes;
    - 58% of the population without migrant ancestry has no religion.
  - France, earlier survey (Drouhot 2021, *AJS*; full chapter read in the 2018 dissertation): Muslims show a "religiosity differential resisting intergenerational assimilation", and nativity "is unrelated to Muslims' difference from French natives".
  - Fifteen countries (Güveli & Platt 2023, ESS 2002–18): second-generation Catholics and Protestants converge to natives, while Muslims "have levels of religiosity that are comparable to their first-generation counterparts".
  - Germany (Diehl & Koenig 2009, Turkish migrants; abstract only): "Weder nimmt die Religiosität zwischen erster und zweiter Generation ab".
  - Netherlands: some decline in identity and practice (Maliepaard, Lubbers & Gijsberts 2010), but a "revival of religious attendance among the second generation" from 1998 to 2006 (Maliepaard, Gijsberts & Lubbers 2012). Both abstract only.

  **Verdict:** the expectation **fails**. Across the second generation, Muslim religiosity has not declined at the rate Christian-origin religiosity did, and in several countries it has not declined at all. Murray's open question is answered on the side he leaned to. Grade: **strong**. Several countries, several designs, one direction; only the Dutch data show some decline.

  **Limits:**
  - Pew's 10% defection assumption is close to the French retention figure (91%), so Pew's projections are not biased by this verdict.
  - The verdict says nothing about the third generation.

  **Would be overturned by:** third-generation data showing convergence to native levels.

  **Where it belongs:** link 6 and exercise 5.

## Module 15 — `the-2015-crisis-read-three-ways`

The brief stages the week of 25–31 August and "Wir schaffen das" (link 2; set piece 2) and asks what each theory predicted. The official and semi-official forecasts of autumn 2015 are predictions too, and the register's module verdict did not judge them as such. This entry does, and per conceded cause (2) it leads with the figures that went worst.

- `the-2015-crisis-read-three-ways`: **"Wir schaffen das" and the autumn-2015 forecasts.**

  **Forecasts:**
  - Merkel, summer press conference, 31 Aug 2015: "Deutschland ist ein starkes Land … Wir haben so vieles geschafft – wir schaffen das! Wir schaffen das, und dort, wo uns etwas im Wege steht, muss es überwunden werden" (bundesregierung.de transcript, Wayback copy).
  - Dieter Zetsche (Daimler), 14 Sep 2015: "im besten Fall kann es auch eine Grundlage für das nächste deutsche Wirtschaftswunder werden – so wie die Millionen von Gastarbeitern" (VerkehrsRundschau, 15 Sep 2015 [press report]).
  - Against both: Andrea Nahles, labour minister, Bundestag, 10 Sep 2015 (Plenarprotokoll 18/121, p. 11753 C–D): "Der syrische Arzt ist nicht der Normalfall … Nicht einmal jeder Zehnte kann direkt in Arbeit oder Ausbildung kommen."

  **Who arrived:**
  - First-time applicants in 2015 (441,899): 69.2% male, 71.1% under 30, 31.1% under 18; men aged 18–29 were 31.4% of all applicants (BAMF, *Das Bundesamt in Zahlen 2015*, p. 21).
  - 2013–16 arrivals (IAB-Kurzbericht 24/2016): 13% held a university degree and 6% a vocational qualification, against 21% and 59% of the German population; 9% had no schooling; 9% of 2015 arrivals were employed at interview.

  **Record nine years on** (IAB-Kurzbericht 17/2025, Brücker, Jaschke & Kosyakova, "10 Jahre Fluchtmigration 2015 – Haben wir es geschafft?"):
  - *Women: 35% employed, against 69% of all women in Germany.*
  - Men: 76%, against 72% of all men.
  - Overall: 64% in social-insurance employment against 70% of the population; about 70% counting self-employment.
  - Full-time median pay is 70% of the all-worker median, just above the low-wage line (66%).
  - 34% of working-age refugees live in benefit households.

  **Security** (13-16.md, BKA 2024): asylum-status immigrants are 8.8% of all suspects against about 3.7% of the population, and 12.2% in offences against life.

  **Consent and retraction:**
  - The decision of 4–5 September was taken by the executive, without a vote of the Bundestag or of the Union.
  - Merkel, 19 Sep 2016: "Wenn ich könnte, würde ich die Zeit um viele, viele Jahre zurückspulen …". She said "Wir schaffen das" had become "beinahe (zu) einer Leerformel" (dpa report [press]).

  **Verdict:**
  - (a) Zetsche's **"next Wirtschaftswunder" failed**. Grade: **strong**. The cohort has not reached the population's employment rate after nine years, earns 70% of the median, and its women are at half the female rate.
  - (b) Nahles's sober forecast **held**. Grade: **strong**. It was the official forecast that proved right, and it came from the governing coalition, not from its critics.
  - (c) "**Wir schaffen das**", read as a forecast that Germany would absorb the inflow as it had absorbed earlier ones: **fails on balance**.
    - The state did not break down.
    - Male labour-market integration reached the male average, and the IAB itself treats that as the main success.
    - Female integration, wages, benefit dependence, a two-to-one overrepresentation in serious crime, and the absence of prior consent were not "managed".
    - Its author withdrew the phrase within thirteen months. She did not withdraw the decision.
    - Grade: **on balance**. The record splits by sex and by outcome, and the IAB states that "unklare Erfolgskriterien" make an unambiguous answer impossible. The split is reported, not resolved in the phrase's favour.

  **Would be overturned by:**
  - for (c), female employment converging on the female average;
  - crime overrepresentation disappearing once age, sex and location are adjusted (the ifo/BKA dispute in 13-16.md);
  - for (a), a fiscal study of the 2015 cohort showing a lifetime net gain.

  **Note for the human:** this entry should be read together with the module 15 verdict in 13-16.md, whose framing ("capacity, not principle") the human found incomplete. The consent point above belongs in that verdict too.

## Staged forecasts not given a verdict here

- **Progressive realization of the 1948 social rights** (`udhr-read-against-the-right`). ## Closing note: how many held, failed, open

Counting sub-verdicts separately:

- **Held (7):** Condorcet's material forecasts (life, literacy, slavery, colonies, sexes; one item); Fukuyama's narrow claim that political Islam would win no universal appeal (on balance); the Kantian republican peace; the Schuman forecast; Carens's premise as stated; the fiscal gain from selected labour and EU/rich-country migration; Nahles's 2015 forecast. To these add the liberal assimilation forecast against Huntington, already in 13-16.md.
- **Failed (10):** Fukuyama's spreading democracy since 2006; Fukuyama on China; Fukuyama on Islam as a force in history; immigration as a general fiscal gain; immigration carrying pensions; the guest workers' return; integration by itself; Muslim secularization across generations; Zetsche's Wirtschaftswunder; "Wir schaffen das" (on balance). Also Carens's inference from his premise (strong), making **11**.
- **Open or untestable (5):** Condorcet's equality between nations (on balance against him over the horizon, his way since 1980); Condorcet on war; progress as a law (untestable); Fukuyama's core "in the long run" (untestable); the multiculturalism-policy causal claim. Mill's China is open and not judged.

**Balance:** the liberal forecasts that held are the long-run, broad ones: longer lives, literacy, abolition, peace among democracies. The ones that failed are the specific claims about immigration, integration and religion, and the post-1989 spread of democracy. Those are the forecasts on which policy in Europe was actually made.

### A2. Module 12, "Ancient inheritance" (l.335)

Old:
```
- *Ancient inheritance.* An ancient lead in wealth or technique fails: Song China was ahead, and Europe borrowed heavily (paper and numerals secure transfers, gunpowder probable). **Strong**. What Europe did with its borrowings (the mechanised paper mill, corned powder, print that no one could suppress) is where the difference lies.
```
New:
```
- *Ancient inheritance.* A continuous Western lead from the Greeks, "almost always ahead by a large margin", fails. Writing, cities, law codes and mathematics came first in Mesopotamia and Egypt: proto-cuneiform at Uruk c. 3350–3200 BC; Hammurabi r. c. 1792–1750 BC; Old Babylonian mathematics c. 1800–1600 BC [secondary, uncontested dates]. The Greek alphabet is the Phoenician one adapted. Rome was of one order with Han China: the Han census of 2 AD gives 57.7 or 59.6 million (Scheidel 2012), against 44–70 million Romans (Scheidel & Friesen 2009); Roman output per head was $610–700 against Maddison's conjectural $450 for China, a Roman edge inside the uncertainty (**on balance**). Around 1000 the Islamic world and Song China were ahead (Maddison, conjectural: Iraq and Iran $650, China $466, western Europe $427; Pamuk & Shatzmiller 2014 [abstract]; Broadberry, Guan & Li), and Europe borrowed heavily from them (paper and numerals secure transfers, gunpowder probable). **Strong**.
- *Why Europe trailed around 1000: a shock, not a deficiency.* The western Roman economy collapsed. Italy fell from $809 per head in year 1 to $450 in 1000, and western Europe from $576 to $427 (Maddison, conjectural). European lead emissions in Greenland ice peaked under the Empire and were "low for >500 years" from the Antonine plague (McConnell et al., *PNAS* 2018, abstract). Ward-Perkins (2005) and Wickham (2005) describe the material collapse [both read through reviews]. The recovery was fast: western Europe went from $427 in 1000 to $771 in 1500, Italy from $450 to $1,100. **On balance**: the per-head series are conjectural, the books were read through reviews, and the lead proxy falls from the second century.
- *What is true in the claim of an old lead.* In political thought and philosophy the lead is Greek (`greek-miracle`, on balance). In science it opens c. 1400 (strong). In wealth it opens c. 1300 in Italy and becomes decisive in the north-west in the early modern period (on balance). The margin has widened since. What Europe did with its borrowings (the mechanised paper mill, corned powder, print that no one could suppress) is where the difference lies.
- *Objection (author's review):* "The West was almost always ahead by a large margin — Greeks, Romans, a hit from Rome's fall, a fast recovery and a widening margin." The shock and the recovery are conceded and enter the verdict. "Almost always ahead by a large margin" fails on the pre-Greek Near East, Rome and Han, and the Abbasid world and the Song.
```

### A3. Module 12, "Cause" (l.336)

Old:
```
- *Cause.* Fragmentation (Landes), kinship (Henrich), a religious ethic (Weber) and an inheritance of institutions from the high Middle Ages (the Church's kinship rules, corporations, universities, chartered towns) are each partly supported, and none is decisive. **Open**. What would settle it: within-Europe designs that separate the timing of kin-dissolution, state competition and literacy, and show which predicts where innovation was kept.
```
New:
```
- *Cause.* There are five candidates. Fragmentation (Landes), kinship (Henrich), a religious ethic (Weber) and an inheritance of institutions from the high Middle Ages (the Church's kinship rules, corporations, universities, chartered towns) are each partly supported, and none is decisive. The fifth is genetic selection (Clark, *A Farewell to Alms* 2007; Cochran & Harpending 2009): the rich out-bred the poor in England 1200–1800 and spread bourgeois traits. Its parts:
  - (i) The rich had more surviving children: **strong**. But the advantage was not English. It is found in southern Germany, Austria, France, Sweden and Switzerland (Allen, *JEL* 2008), so it cannot explain why England came first.
  - (ii) Status persists at a rate that fits an additive genetic model: a correlation falling by a factor of only 0.79 a generation, 422,374 English people, 1600–2022 (Clark, *PNAS* 2023). The pattern is **strong**; the genetic mechanism is **not shown**. Clark: "there is no proof here that additive genetic transmission causes social status". Non-genetic transmission, wealth above all, may mimic the pattern (Benning et al. 2023–25, abstracts; Breen 2015). His surname data give the same persistence in China, Japan and Korea (Clark 2014).
  - (iii) Ancient DNA shows selection on alleles associated with test scores and schooling in West Eurasia, but "largely driven by selection before ~2000 BP, after which χ tends toward zero" (Akbari et al., *Nature* 2026).
  - (iv) East Asian school-test scores are at or above Europe's (PISA 2022 [secondary]), and China and Japan fell far below their IQ-predicted income in 1820 and 1900 (`geography-or-institutions`, strong). China did not industrialise first.
  - (v) Regimes and growth change within one population faster than selection can act (module 13, R1).
  The genetic contribution to the West's rise is **open**. Its England-specific form, that selection unique to England explains why England came first, **fails, on balance**. The whole cause is **open**. What would settle it: for the four cultural and institutional candidates, within-Europe designs that separate the timing of kin-dissolution, state competition and literacy, and show which predicts where innovation was kept. For the genetic candidate, polygenic scores from dated remains or documented lineages in England, the continent and China, 1000–1800, rising first in England; and within-family genomic designs on Clark's lineages that separate genes from inherited wealth.
```

### A7. Module 13, after "Institutions" (l.367)

Old:
```
- *Institutions.* Only institutions have a clean natural experiment behind them (the Koreas, Nogales). It shows institutions can produce gaps as large as any between nations. It does not apportion the world's gaps.
```
New:
```
- *Institutions.* Only institutions have a clean natural experiment behind them (the Koreas, Nogales). It shows institutions can produce gaps as large as any between nations. It does not apportion the world's gaps.
- *Regimes (R1).* Regimes and freedom are not explained mainly by genes. **Strong**. In each of these cases the same ancestry lived under opposite regimes within a lifetime:
  - The Koreas: homogeneous before 1945 (Why Nations Fail l.683, l.769).
  - Taiwan: martial law 1949–87; first direct presidential election 1996; Free, 94/100 (FH 2025). Its population is "nearly 95 percent" Han (ROC portal). In the Taiwan Biobank, 79.9% have southern Han ancestry and 14.5% fall in a group "similar to other southern Han Chinese" (Chen et al. 2016). About 1.2 million arrived in 1949; a 2004 survey gives 8% mainlanders. The "13%" figure is not bound.
  - Germany: Weimar, 1933 and the two states of 1949. East and West were "ex ante uniform", with no income difference in 1928–36 (Alesina & Giuliano 2015). By 1950 the East was at about half the West's output per head, and it never closed the gap (Conference Board via OWID).
  - Spain and Portugal: dictatorships to the mid-1970s, constitutions of 1978 and 1976.
  Would be overturned by: genetic divergence on relevant traits within those periods.
- *Culture (R2).* Culture persists across generations and is not put on at will. **Strong**. The evidence: World Values Survey cultural zones (Inglehart & Baker 2000, abstract); origin-country trust into the fourth generation of American descendants (Algan & Cahuc 2010); values change "rarely within two generations" (Alesina & Giuliano 2015); second-generation Muslim religiosity (INSEE 2023; Güveli & Platt 2023). Regimes also shape culture: East Germans remain more pro-government after reunification.
- *Objections (author's review)*, each stated at full strength in the brief and answered:
  - (a) Peoples live as they want; Muslims and the Chinese do not want freedom; culture is made by genes. Gene–culture coevolution is a real field (Richerson, Boyd & Henrich 2010; lactase). The value differences are conceded as cultural: sharia 84% in South Asia and 74% in MENA (Pew 2013); "Eros far more than Demos" (Norris & Inglehart). "Do not want freedom" fails on the democracy surveys: the difference is "extremely modest"; at least 73% in eight Arab countries call democracy best (Arab Barometer 2025). "Genetic" fails on Taiwan.
  - (b) The best Germans died in the war. Over 5.3 million German military dead [secondary for Overmans]. But Weimar fell before the war; there was East–West parity in 1928–36; no source shows unequal losses between East and West [not bound]; the gap opened under occupation.
  - (c) The GDR was a foreign imposition. The imposition is conceded. But the population was German: 18.4 million in 1950, 16.4 million in 1989 (Statistisches Jahrbuch 1990). The Soviet forces were a garrison: 337,800 soldiers and 208,400 dependants and civilian staff (BT-Drs. 12/1064). About 3 million fled in 1949–61 (Bundesarchiv), and the regime fell in 1989. The objection concedes R1.
  - (d) The Taiwanese are not the same Chinese population. Answered by the population facts above, and by the same population living on both sides of 1987.
```

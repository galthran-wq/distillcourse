# Verdict round 2 — agent C (module 14, plus slavery as a "positive good")

Scope: the author's review items 1–4 for module 14 (`empire-and-guilt`).
Briefs edited directly: `colonial-balance-sheet.md`,
`auditing-the-case-for-colonialism.md`, `vitoria-right-to-the-americas.md`,
`locke-and-mill-on-empire.md` (host chosen for item 3). `inherited-guilt-and-reparations.md`
unchanged (item 4: none of items 1–3 bears on its four verdicts).

Why `locke-and-mill-on-empire` hosts item 3: the "positive good" defence is the
nineteenth-century tutelary argument ("for their benefit") with the time limit
removed, written in the same decades as *On Liberty* (Calhoun 1837, Fitzhugh
1854/1857). Mill's own clause — despotism legitimate "provided the end be their
improvement, and the means justified by actually effecting that end" — supplies
the empirical test the record is set against. `carlyle-heroes` was the other
candidate (Fitzhugh "plants himself" on Carlyle), but it is a lesson about
hero-worship with its own verdict; it is reached by a backward `<Ref>` only.

New:
```
- `vitoria-right-to-the-americas` — Spain's claimed titles → **every unjust title fails** on Vitoria's premises. The Indians had cities, laws and marriages, so they owned. The just titles he leaves (travel, trade, preaching, defence of the innocent), enforceable by war, are **a licence rather than a restraint**, because the party that judged a refusal went to war over it. Grade: **strong** on the first, **on balance** on the second. Would be overturned by: a mechanism in his scheme letting a neutral party judge a refusal. The author's-review objection, "they were stronger and more capable, and that is all the right you need" (the Athenians at Melos, Thrasymachus, Callicles in the *Gorgias* 483d–484c), is stated at full strength and answered. As a description it is upheld: see `thucydides-power-and-justice`. As a theory of right it collapses right into success. It gives every colony that won its independence (the United States, Haiti, Spanish America, Algeria), and anyone who crosses an undefended border, the same title, and leaves no ground to say anything ought to be otherwise. Socrates' reply (488d) turns it on Callicles: the many are stronger. Vitoria had already excluded it: "enlargement of empire cannot be a cause of just war". It is one more title that fails. Grade: **strong**, within the first part.
```

### 3. `locke-and-mill-on-empire` — slavery as a "positive good"

Old (line 409, exact):
```
- `locke-and-mill-on-empire` — Locke's "waste" and Mill's tutelage: lapses from their principles or consequences of them? → **Consequences.** Both follow from conditions written into the principles: Locke's labour criterion applied to land, and Mill's exclusion of "backward states of society" in *On Liberty* itself. ```
```

New:
```
- `locke-and-mill-on-empire` — Locke's "waste" and Mill's tutelage: lapses from their principles or consequences of them? → **Consequences.** Both follow from conditions written into the principles: Locke's labour criterion applied to land, and Mill's exclusion of "backward states of society" in *On Liberty* itself. Grade: **strong**.
  - Second verdict (author's review): slavery as a "positive good" (Calhoun, Senate, 1837; Fitzhugh, *Sociology for the South* 1854 and *Cannibals All!* 1857). Its three claims: the master's interest protects the slave, as an owner would not wreck his car or lame his horse; the abuse is propaganda; to be ruled by a superior culture benefits the slave. → **Fails** against the record. Grade: **strong**.
  - Interest. The owner's interest protects the asset, not the person, and only where a replacement costs more than the loss. Fitzhugh concedes the condition: in Cuba, "the most efficient cause of cruelty and neglect, is the African slave trade, which makes it cheaper to buy than to rear slaves". The record follows it. In the British Caribbean few colonies ever reached natural increase. After the trade ended in 1807 the slave population fell from 775,000 to 665,000 by 1834; Jamaica's fell from 347,000 in 1810 to 311,070. In 1810 fewer than 800,000 slaves lived there, against some 1.7 million Africans imported. In the United States, where import was dear after 1808, the black population of 1807 was already over three times the number of Africans imported (Higman 1986, with Engerman's comment). The interest also never forbade what raised the asset's value: the whip that got more work, and the sale that parted a family.
  - Abuse. "Propaganda" fails on the defence's own best modern evidence. On Bennet Barrow's plantation Fogel and Engerman counted 0.7 whippings per hand a year. Their critics counted 1.19, and a whipping witnessed by the men about every six days [secondary via Vedder 2024]. Of 12.5 million Africans shipped, 10.7 million landed: about one in seven died at sea, though the shippers' interest was to land them alive (TAST [secondary]).
  - Benefit. The benefit claim fails on revealed behaviour: the Fugitive Slave Act of 1850, and Saint-Domingue 1791 / Haiti 1804. It also fails Mill's test, because a wardship made hereditary and permanent effects no improvement.
  - True part, stated as such: where buying was dear, slave populations grew (the United States after 1808).
  - Would be overturned by: natural increase where buying was cheaper than rearing, or enslaved people choosing to stay where flight or self-purchase was open.
```

The pricer built here is the module's theory made executable: backward
induction prices the claim, the delta process replicates it tick by tick with
zero error, and the nodes where intrinsic beats continuation trace out the
exercise boundary.

Everything the lab builds was proved in the two modules before it. The
recombining lattice, backward induction and the wealth equation are
<Ref lesson="multiperiod-binomial" />'s, along with the replication theorem
the lab measures rather than cites; the risk-neutral probability the pass
weights every up-child with is <Ref lesson="martingales-risk-neutral-pricing" />'s
martingale condition; the maximum at every node, the exercise region it
produces and the convex-payoff theorem that empties that region for a call
are <Ref lesson="american-derivatives" />'s. The 80-stock tree of
<Ref lesson="crr-arbitrage-trade" /> and Shreve's doubling-or-halving market
return as test vectors — the numbers the module computed by hand are what the
code has to reproduce first.

What the lab contributes is the module's claims as measurements. The
replication theorem says the hedge's wealth equals the claim's value at every
node of every path; the lab walks all $2^n$ paths of three trees and prints
the largest mismatch, below $10^{-10}$. The exercise region of the
three-period put is a handful of nodes; on a fifty-step calibrated tree it is
a region of the plane, and the lab extracts its edge as one number per date,
the object <Ref lesson="first-passage-perpetual-put" /> found in closed form
for the put with no expiry. The call's region is empty, and the written
checkpoint asks which hypotheses of the theorem made it so. The closing task
runs the pricer out to thousands of steps: the price oscillates in a sawtooth
whose amplitude shrinks like $1/n$, and the target is the continuous limit
<Ref lesson="random-walk-to-brownian-motion" /> identifies next, to within
four thousandths of a cent — closer than the plain lattice reaches at any
step count a notebook holds in memory, so the last step is an idea about the
sawtooth rather than a bigger tree.

The lab runs as a Jupyter notebook — open it in Colab with the button above,
or in any Jupyter you like. Pair the notebook with the code from this page;
each checkpoint submits your function's outputs for verification and the
checklist above tracks what stands. The seven exact checkpoints are the
required set — the lattice, the risk-neutral probability, the European pass,
the delta and money-market lattices, the hedge walker, the American pass and
the boundary extractor; the written answer and the open convergence task are
optional, and partial completion is a normal way to finish a lab. Expect
about four hours of real work. The lattice, the martingale condition and the
two backward passes are printed as formulas above their blanks — the work
there is turning a recurrence into a vectorized pass over a ragged lattice;
the delta is described rather than displayed, and the hedge walker has only
the wealth equation in prose. The open task is scaffold-free, and its
threshold is set where a bigger tree alone does not reach. If a check goes
red, the verdict often names the exact bug; read it before rereading your
code.

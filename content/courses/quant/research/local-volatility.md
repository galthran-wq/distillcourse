## Key points
1. The Nikkei paradox as the opening flaw (flaw-first per playbook): the term structure alone already contradicts constant σ — before any smile; the resolution demands σ depend on time at least; the smile demands state too. So try the LARGEST model that keeps everything the course has built (one factor, complete markets, delta hedging intact): $dS = rS\,dt + \sigma(S,t)S\,dW$.
2. What the surface determines: Breeden–Litzenberger gives every marginal density (Ref — "the same object, differentiated once more in $T$" thread redeemed). But marginals do not pin a process — the companion paper's two-line counterexample REPRODUCED in full (OU vs time-scaled BM: same Gaussian marginals, different processes). The martingale (driftless-discounted) restriction is what buys uniqueness — stated as Dupire's theorem, proof structure shown, analytic details fenced.
3. The derivation: the forward (Fokker–Planck) equation for the density (STATED, with its meaning — probability conservation; proof fenced to Shreve Ex 6.9); integrate it twice against the payoff to get the Dupire equation $C_T = \tfrac12\sigma^2(K,T)K^2C_{KK} - rKC_K$; solve for $\sigma^2(K,T)$. Each integration-by-parts step shown; the boundary terms' vanishing argued.
4. Sanity checks: flat surface ⟹ $\sigma_{loc} \equiv \sigma$ (compute it); the admissibility conditions are exactly the no-static-arbitrage tests (calendar $C_T > 0$, butterfly $C_{KK} > 0$) — the radicand's positivity IS arbitrage-freeness; skew ⟹ local vol higher below spot (the qualitative shape the previous lesson's forward exercise sketched — redeemed).
5. The limits, stated as precisely as the powers: (a) a surface generated by a binary jump at a known date is NOT fittable by any diffusion (the companion's boundary case — prices jump discontinuously in maturity); (b) fitting prices ≠ fitting dynamics: local vol's PREDICTED smile evolution (the smile moves with spot in the wrong direction) named as its documented empirical failure — stated as a fact with Gatheral Ch.8 as the pointer, not developed; (c) the 5-cent bid stands un-absorbed (Ref volatility-smile). Local vol = the strongest defense of the diffusion, and the precise measurement of where diffusions end.
6. The conditional-expectation reading (Gatheral 1.12): local variance is the risk-neutral expectation of instantaneous variance given $S_T = K$ — the bridge under which stochastic-vol models and local vol agree on vanillas; one paragraph, fenced, as the course's honest gesture at the theory beyond.

## Pitfalls
- The 1994 article is summary-based: every claim sourced to the VERIFIED 1993 companion or Gatheral; nothing attributed to the 1994 text beyond its confirmed opening; References carry the access note. This is the course's citation-honesty on display.
- The Fokker–Planck equation is the lesson's load-bearing fence — state it with meaning and cite; do NOT fake its derivation; Shreve assigns it as Ex 6.9, say so.
- Local vol ≠ implied vol: $\sigma_{loc}(K,T)$ is a model coefficient at a POINT of state space; $\sigma_{imp}(K,T)$ is a coordinate of one PRICE; the rough rule (implied ≈ average of local along the path; skew slope doubling) stated as heuristic only.
- Keep the dynamics-failure claim honest: local vol reprices today's vanillas exactly; its forward smiles flatten — a documented deficiency, one sentence, pointer to Gatheral Ch.8; no hedging-performance study here.

## EXERCISE PLAN
1. Warm-up: verify the flat case — plug $C^{BS}(K,T;\sigma)$ into Dupire's formula symbolically (or numerically at a grid) and recover $\sigma_{loc} = \sigma$ everywhere.
2. Derivation skipped: carry out the two integrations by parts from the forward equation to the Dupire equation with the boundary terms written and killed explicitly (the step the lesson compresses to one line each).
3. Boundary probe: a calendar-arbitrage surface ($C_T < 0$ at one node): show the radicand goes negative — the formula fails EXACTLY where module-1-style arbitrage exists; construct the offending calendar trade.
4. Claim to refute: reproduce the companion's counterexample fully — "two diffusions with identical marginals at every t must be the same process": compute both processes' marginal laws (means/variances), confirm identity, exhibit differing transition densities, and name the added restriction (driftless-discounted/martingale) under which uniqueness holds.
5. Research-shaped: on the September 2005 SPX two-expiry data (transcribed), compute $\sigma_{loc}$ at three interior grid points by finite differences; compare with the implied vols at the same moneyness (local skew ≈ 2× implied skew — the rough rule checked).
6. Mixed/spaced (E3): show a tree/PDE consequence — modify the module-2/3 binomial pricer conceptually to node-dependent $u, d$ matching a given σ(S,t); state which module-2 theorems survive untouched (replication per node) and which formulas change ($\tilde p$ per node).
7. FORWARD (E7): with σ(S,t) in hand, list what a desk still cannot do: predict how TOMORROW'S surface moves when spot moves.

# Lab: the stochastic calculus test bench — research brief

Lesson: `quadratic-variation-lab` (module: continuous-limit, type: lab, planned). NEW — added after structure synthesis at the human's request: module 4 is the hardest mathematics in the course and was the only post-M2 module where the learner built nothing.
Goal: Build a test bench that puts the module's limit theorems on trial numerically — paths, a quadratic-variation meter, stochastic-integral quadratures, an Euler–Maruyama solver — and measure what the theorems claim: QV pins to $T$, the $-\tfrac12$ correction appears in a computed integral, strong order is $1/2$.

## ARC
- Inherits: the scaled walk carrying QV $= t$ at every $n$ (random-walk-to-brownian-motion), the QV theorem and the $dW\,dW = dt$ dissection (brownian-motion), the Itô integral's left-endpoint construction and isometry (ito-integral), the Itô–Doeblin formula and GBM's exact solution (ito-formula).
- Contributes: the module's claims as measurements rather than assertions — the same move estimation-lab makes for the Cramér–Rao floor in classical-ml. The learner watches sampled QV converge to $T$ while a smooth path's dies, computes $\int W\,dW$ and finds $(W^2(T)-T)/2$ appear out of arithmetic, and measures the strong order $1/2$ that returns one module later as the $1/\sqrt n$ hedging-error law.
- Hands off: the coupled-refinement discipline (same Brownian increments across every grid) and the seeded-study habit to discrete-hedging-lab (which builds its own exact log-Euler GBM engine — see DEPARTURES) and monte-carlo-lab; the QV-based $\hat\sigma$ of the metric checkpoint to module 8's realized-volatility estimators (realized-implied-smile).
- Spine step: "Refining the tree does not smooth the randomness away" (step 4, closing item).

## LAB PLAN

Artifact: one notebook building a stochastic-calculus test bench — `walk(n, T)` (the scaled random walk / discrete Brownian path), `sampled_qv(path, stride)`, `ito_sum(integrand, path, point)` (left / midpoint / right evaluation), `em_solve(mu, sigma, x0, dW)` — and the three studies the bench exists for: the QV table across meshes and processes, the $\int W\,dW$ evaluation-point comparison, and the strong-convergence log-log fit. It is Higham's 10 MATLAB programs rebuilt in NumPy with quadratic variation added, and it ends by using QV for something: estimating $\sigma$ from discretely observed paths.

CANON GROUNDING: Higham (2001), "An Algorithmic Introduction to Numerical Simulation of Stochastic Differential Equations", SIAM Review 43(3) — cached complete in research/texts/higham-2001-sde-simulation.pdf — an article written FOR "undergraduate and beginning postgraduate students" that consists of 10 programs the reader reproduces: `bpath1–3` (discrete Brownian paths — checkpoint 1), `stint` (Itô vs Stratonovich evaluation of $\int W\,dW$ — checkpoint 4), `em`/`emstrong` (Euler–Maruyama on GBM and its measured strong order $1/2$, Higham's own headline experiment — checkpoint 6), `chain.m` §8 ("we quote and numerically confirm the stochastic chain rule", max discrepancy 0.0151 — checkpoint 5). The QV half is the computational form of Shreve II §3.4 (Theorem 3.4.3's $L^2$ argument, the Remark 3.4.4–3.4.5 dissection); Hull §21.6's path-simulation recipe is the same genre in spreadsheet form. 18.S096's biweekly problem sets confirm the genre at university level (the set PDFs themselves were not readable — sources.yaml).
DEPARTURES, state them in the lab: (a) Higham works in MATLAB one listing at a time; the bench is vectorized NumPy and the studies run thousands of paths with seeds, reporting distributions (the classical-ml validation habit); (b) Higham has no quadratic-variation section — QV is Shreve's, and it leads here because it is the module's spine; (c) Milstein, weak convergence and linear stability (Higham §§6–7, `milstrong`, `emweak`, `stab`) are deliberately OUT — the course never needs order beyond $1/2$, and stability belongs to a numerics course; say so rather than silently truncating the source.

Demarcation lines, binding: the stop-loss hedge belongs to stop-loss-paradox (the lesson) and its measured plateau to discrete-hedging-lab's `stop-loss-benchmark` — this lab never hedges anything. This lab owns the PROCESS and the CALCULUS; module 5's lab owns the hedge built on them. The brownian-motion lesson's exercise 6 (a quick QV-mesh script plus the t(3) walk) is this lab's teaser: the lesson keeps the pencil-side question (what breaks in the variance argument), the lab is where the harness, the grading and the composition live.

Datasets / inputs: none shipped — every path is simulated in-notebook under fixed seeds. Fixed test vectors: stored walk/BM path values at seed and $n$ for the exact checks; Higham's `chain.m` setup ($dX = (\alpha - X)\,dt + \beta\sqrt{X}\,dW$, $\alpha=2$, $\beta=1$, $X_0=1$, $V=\sqrt{X}$, $\delta t = 1/200$) as one named reproduction target. Private: the build's high-precision references (QV tables, integral values, the strong-error curve) and, for the metric checkpoint, ~200 discretely observed GBM paths with per-path $\sigma$ drawn from a seeded range and held out.

Checkpoints (plan; lab-author may split or deepen):
1. `scaled-walk` (exact, warm-up): the scaled symmetric random walk on $[0, T]$ — $\pm\sqrt{\Delta t}$ increments, Higham's `bpath` in NumPy; checked on stored values at fixed seed and on increment moments.
2. `sampled-qv` (exact, warm-up): sampled quadratic variation of ANY discretely observed path at a given stride; checked on a smooth function (vanishes with the mesh) and a stored Brownian path (pins to $T$).
3. `qv-study` (exact, the first composition): the QV table across meshes for four processes — $W$ (→ $T$), $f(t) = \sin t$ (→ 0), $W + ct$ (→ $T$: drift invisible, cross terms die at $\Delta t^{3/2}$), $\log S$ for GBM (→ $\sigma^2 T$ — the realized-variance identity, named). Consumes 1–2.
4. `ito-vs-stratonovich` (exact): Higham's `stint` — the left-endpoint sum of $\int_0^T W\,dW$ against the exact $(W^2(T) - T)/2$, the midpoint sum against $W^2(T)/2$; same path, two answers, the gap equal to half the sampled QV. Consumes 1–2.
5. `ito-formula-check` (exact): Higham's `chain.m` generalized — verify Itô–Doeblin numerically: $f(T, W(T)) - f(0, 0)$ against the summed $f_t\,dt + f_x\,dW + \tfrac12 f_{xx}\,dt$ terms along the path, for one $f(t,x)$ with a genuine $f_t$ term, with the max discrepancy shrinking as the mesh refines (Higham's own 0.0151-style check, now a convergence claim). Consumes 1, 4.
6. `euler-maruyama` (exact, the composition): `em_solve` for GBM against the exact solution driven by the SAME increments, then Higham's `emstrong` study — endpoint error across $\Delta t \in \{2^{-4}\Delta, \dots, \Delta\}$ with every resolution consuming one shared fine Brownian path; fitted log-log slope required in $[0.4, 0.6]$. Consumes 1.
7. `why-left-endpoint` (review, required: false): the learner states why finance forces the left endpoint — the integrand is a POSITION, adapted, settled before the increment it multiplies — and what the midpoint evaluation would let a trader do (peek half a step ahead; checkpoint 4's gap is the cash value of that peek). Rubric grades the naming of non-anticipation, not the prose.
8. `qv-sigma` (metric, required: false, LAST): estimate $\sigma$ for each of the private discretely observed paths; score $= -\mathrm{RMSE}$ against the held-out truths, threshold calibrated by the build against the plain $\sqrt{[\log S, \log S](T)/T}$ estimator. The QV route wins over fitting a distribution to returns at this observation budget; the hint after the attempt names module 8's realized-volatility estimators as this checkpoint grown up.

Known-wrong variants worth declaring: increments scaled by $\Delta t$ instead of $\sqrt{\Delta t}$ (QV collapses toward 0 with the mesh instead of pinning — the single most diagnostic slip); sampled QV divided by the number of increments (the variance-estimate confusion: the number looks stable and is wrong by a factor of $n$); the right-endpoint sum offered for checkpoint 4 (converges to $(W^2(T) + T)/2$ — the correction's sign flips, and the hint must name the evaluation point, not the arithmetic); `emstrong` run with independent Brownian paths per $\Delta t$ (the order-$1/2$ slope drowns in Monte-Carlo noise — the coupled-path discipline IS this checkpoint's lesson); Euler applied to $\log S$ instead of $S$ (that scheme is exact, the measured error is $\approx 0$ and the study degenerates — declare it and require EM on the price SDE, with the exact scheme as the reference it is compared against).

NOT blanked (ships complete): plotting helpers (path plots, the QV table renderer, log-log axes), the seed/grid fixtures and stored test vectors, the slope-fit utility, the `chain.m` parameter block, and the private-path loader for the metric checkpoint. The learner writes the walk, the meter, the sums, the solver and the three studies — the mathematics, not the chrome.

# Research brief: affluent-society (The Affluent Society and Its Boundaries)

status: planned
module: postwar-america
type: core
goal: Analyze postwar prosperity and suburbanization, and the exclusions that defined both.

THESIS ROLE. The thesis's prosperity peak measured (GDP doubling,
homeownership 40% to 60%, the manufactured middle class) with its policy
inputs named — and the exclusions documented in the same sections, because
the same instruments (FHA terms, GI Bill administration) distributed the
boom along the color line the redlining chapter mapped. Brown v. Board opens
the enforcement arc: the Court reversing its own 1896 ratification is the
self-correction machinery restarting, with the Cold War's documented pressure
(the Justice Department's amicus brief) as a causal input the thesis's
world-power thread supplies.

## ARC (L10)

- **Inherits:** cold-war has established containment as the organizing frame
  of American foreign policy and closed the domestic loyalty-state chapter
  of anticommunism with the Senate's censure of McCarthy as the enforced
  boundary.

- **Contributes:** This lesson carries the spine's affluence half: the
  manufactured middle class and suburbanization measured against the

  founding's enforcement arc with Brown v. Board.

- **Hands off:** civil-rights-movement takes up the enforcement Brown v.
  Board opened, tracing the movement from Montgomery through Selma to the
  Civil Rights and Voting Rights Acts.

## Key points the lesson must cover

1. **The boom, quantified.** GDP roughly doubling 1945-1960; median family
   income up sharply; homeownership from about 40% to about 60%; the baby boom;
   car ownership and television saturation. Causes: pent-up wartime savings,
   the GI Bill, federal mortgage insurance, highway construction, defense
   spending, and an international position with no industrial competitors.
2. **The suburb as a policy product.** FHA and VA mortgage terms making buying
   cheaper than renting; Levittown's production methods; the interstate program
   (1956) subsidizing dispersal; and the explicit racial exclusions — restrictive
   covenants (unenforceable after *Shelley v. Kraemer*, 1948, but persisting in
   practice), FHA underwriting standards, and builder policy. Suburbia was built
   white by design; connect back to redlining.
3. **The urban other half.** Disinvestment, urban renewal clearing largely Black
   neighborhoods ("Negro removal"), highway routing through them, and the
   resulting concentration of poverty. Use the Renewing Inequality numbers.
4. **Conformity and its critics, in proportion.** Organization man, suburban
   domesticity, and the mass-culture critique — alongside the fact that roughly a
   fifth to a quarter of Americans remained poor, largely invisible until
   Harrington's *The Other America* (1962).
5. ***Brown v. Board* (1954).** Four consolidated cases; NAACP litigation
   strategy over two decades building precedent; Warren's unanimous opinion
   holding separate educational facilities inherently unequal; *Brown II*'s "all
   deliberate speed" and the massive-resistance response — the Southern Manifesto
   (1956), school closures, and Little Rock (1957) requiring the 101st Airborne
   under EO 10730. Ten years after Brown, a tiny share of Southern Black students
   attended integrated schools; give the number.
6. **The Cold War mechanism.** Segregation as a foreign-policy liability; the
   Justice Department's amicus brief in *Brown* said so explicitly. This is a
   causal claim with documentary support, not a rhetorical flourish.

## Set pieces and worked examples to recreate

- **The Brown opinion's first page** read as an artifact: four cases consolidated,
  the Chief Justice's authorship, the unanimity.
- **Plessy → Brown, 58 years apart** — reuse the two-quote pairing set up in
  jim-crow ("separate but equal" against "inherently unequal") as spaced review.
- **A mortgage comparison, computed**: monthly cost of buying a Levittown house
  on VA terms vs. renting a city apartment in 1950 — then the same computation
  for a Black veteran denied the mortgage. The arithmetic is the wealth-gap
  mechanism in one worked example; the parallel exercise projects both households
  forward twenty years.
- **Massive resistance measured**: Southern Black students in integrated schools,
  1954-1964, as a near-flat line.

## Pitfalls / subtleties to flag

- The "affluent society" was real and unevenly distributed; keep both facts in
  the same section rather than in separate ones.
- *Brown* did not desegregate schools; enforcement came with the 1964 Civil
  Rights Act's funding leverage and later court orders. Say so, or the next
  lesson's stakes vanish.
- Restrictive covenants became judicially unenforceable in 1948 but remained in
  deeds and in practice; do not treat the ruling as the end of the practice.
- Suburbanization was not simply "white flight" — it was federally financed
  construction of new housing that Black buyers were largely barred from.
- Avoid the sitcom image of the 1950s; cite labor-force participation, poverty,
  and household data instead.

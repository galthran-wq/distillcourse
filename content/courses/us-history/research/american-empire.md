# Research brief: american-empire (American Empire, 1898)

status: planned
module: industry-and-empire
type: core
goal: Analyze why the United States took an overseas empire in 1898 and the arguments made against it.

## Sources to consult

- **MIT 21H.102 Lecture 8 "Becoming a World Power"** (published notes) — the
  overdetermined-causes framing: the panics of 1873 and 1893 driving a search for
  markets, resource competition, Manifest Destiny and missionary ideology, and
  Roosevelt's "strenuous life" masculinity — all converging on 1898, against an
  organized Anti-Imperialist League and Bryan.
- **American Yawp Ch.19 "American Empire"** — the Spanish-American War, the
  Philippine-American War, and the imperialism debates.
- **Give Me Liberty Ch.17** — empire under the freedom-boundaries lens: the
  constitutional question of governing people without citizenship.
- **Milestone Documents** #49 De Lôme Letter, #50 Joint Resolution Annexing
  Hawaii, #51 Platt Amendment, #52 Roosevelt Corollary.

## Key points the lesson must cover

1. **The preconditions.** Industrial overcapacity and the export search after
   1893; a modern steel navy built from the 1880s under Mahan's sea-power
   doctrine; missionary networks; and a closed continental frontier (Turner,
   1893) redirecting expansionist energy outward.
2. **Hawaii.** The 1893 planter-led overthrow of Queen Liliʻuokalani with US
   Marines present, the failed 1893 annexation, and annexation by joint
   resolution in 1898 during the war — with the Kūʻē petitions signed by a
   majority of Native Hawaiians opposing it. Annexation happened over documented
   mass objection; say so.
3. **Cuba and the war.** The Cuban independence war and Spanish reconcentration
   camps; the yellow press; the De Lôme letter and the *Maine* explosion (cause
   still disputed; the 1898 board blamed a mine, later studies suggest internal
   combustion); the Teller Amendment disclaiming annexation of Cuba; ten weeks of
   fighting; the Treaty of Paris transferring Puerto Rico, Guam, and the
   Philippines for $20 million.
4. **The Philippine-American War (1899-1902 and after).** The war of conquest
   that annexation required: Filipino forces who had fought Spain now fighting
   the US; roughly 4,200 US military deaths, some 20,000 Filipino combatant
   deaths, and civilian deaths in the hundreds of thousands from war, famine, and
   disease. The scale contrast with the ten-week Spanish war is the point.
5. **The anti-imperialist argument.** The Anti-Imperialist League (Twain,
   Carnegie, Addams, Gompers among its supporters): empire contradicts
   self-government, corrupts the republic, and imports racial subordination —
   though some anti-imperialists opposed annexation precisely because they did not
   want Filipinos as citizens. Present both strands honestly.
6. **The constitutional settlement.** The Insular Cases (1901 onward) holding
   that the Constitution does not fully follow the flag — territories "belonging
   to but not part of" the United States. Puerto Rico's status today is the
   living residue.
7. **Cuba after the war.** The Platt Amendment (1903) making Cuban independence
   conditional on US intervention rights and Guantánamo; the Roosevelt Corollary
   (1905) generalizing intervention across the Caribbean.

## Set pieces and worked examples to recreate

- **The causal-arrows diagram** (MIT Lecture 8): panics → markets; resource
  competition; missionary/exceptionalist ideology; martial masculinity — four
  arrows converging on 1898. Recreate as the lesson's framework figure.
- **"Remember the Maine" as manufactured consent**: circulation figures, the
  headlines, the De Lôme letter's publication, and what the physical evidence
  did and did not support. Worked example: separate what was known from what was
  asserted in February 1898; parallel exercise on a Philippine-war press claim.
- **Rough Riders on San Juan Hill** as the image the war produced, and TR's
  career trajectory from it.
- **The Kūʻē petitions** — mass documented opposition, as a counter-source to
  the annexation narrative.
- **The two wars compared**: ten weeks and ~2,000 US deaths (mostly disease) vs.
  a three-year counterinsurgency — the tally is the argument.

## Pitfalls / subtleties to flag

- 1898 was not the beginning of American expansion; it was continental expansion
  continued by sea. Say this explicitly to connect with conquering-the-west.
- The *Maine*'s cause remains genuinely unresolved; do not assert sabotage or
  accident as settled.
- Yellow journalism did not cause the war by itself; McKinley's decision followed
  business, strategic, and political pressures. Avoid the single-cause story.
- Casualty figures for the Philippine war vary widely and the civilian estimates
  are contested; give ranges with sources.
- Anti-imperialism was not uniformly anti-racist — flag this rather than letting
  the League stand as the lesson's moral hero.

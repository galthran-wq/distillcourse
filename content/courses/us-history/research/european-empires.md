# Research brief: european-empires (Spanish, French, and Dutch North America and the Black Legend)

status: planned
module: contact-and-colonies
type: core
goal: Compare the Spanish, French, and Dutch colonization models and explain what each left in North America.

Second lesson of the course (pedagogy C3): still an easy opener, polished, ending
in a felt win — the reader finishes able to name the three colonization models,
place them on a map, and read one piece of period propaganda (the Black Legend)
as a source rather than as a fact.

THESIS ROLE. The course argues that the English colonies' population and
institutions were unusual. That claim needs a comparison class, and this lesson
supplies it: three other European empires, each with a different model, none of
which produced a settler society of self-governing, literate families. The
lesson also inherits lesson 1's precondition (the emptied land) and shows the
first empires operating on it.

## Sources to consult

- **American Yawp Ch.1-2** — Spanish colonization (encomienda, missions, the
  Pueblo Revolt), New France's fur-trade alliances, and New Netherland. The
  spine for all three models.
- **Give Me Liberty Ch.1-2** — the comparative-empires chapters; supplies the
  numbers on Spanish, French, and Dutch colonial populations against the
  English.
- **Johnson, *A History of the American People*, Part One** ("Europe and the
  Transatlantic Adventure") — the Iberian Atlantic system (Madeira sugar, São
  Tomé, Tordesillas, 'No Peace Beyond the Line') read directly from the
  verified front-matter excerpt; the "first boy and girl born on Madeira were
  christened Adam and Eve" set piece.
- **Las Casas, *A Short Account of the Destruction of the Indies* (1552)** —
  the Black Legend's source text; free full text widely available. Quote a
  short passage, then trace its Protestant printing history (De Bry
  engravings, English and Dutch editions).
- **MIT 21H.101 Week 2** — the Indians'-America-first ordering carried over
  from lesson 1.

## Key points the lesson must cover

1. **Three models, stated as systems.** New Spain: tribute, silver, missions,
   and a legally stratified society atop a large surviving Indigenous
   population — a colonization of *rule*. New France: a thin population
   (under 70,000 French by 1750 against over 1 million British colonists)
   embedded in Native alliance networks through the fur trade — a
   colonization of *exchange*. New Netherland: a company entrepôt at
   Manhattan run for trade, polyglot from the start — a colonization of
   *commerce*. State each model's labor system, church, and relation to
   Native nations in one comparable frame.
2. **The Spanish borderlands are part of US history.** St. Augustine (1565,
   the oldest European city in the later US), New Mexico (1598), Texas and
   California missions later. The Pueblo Revolt of 1680 — the most successful
   Indigenous rising against a European empire in North America, expelling
   Spain from New Mexico for twelve years — and the horses it dispersed
   (cross-reference indigenous-america's Plains-horse thread).
3. **The Black Legend, read as a source.** Las Casas was a Spanish bishop
   writing to reform his own empire; Protestant printers turned his indictment
   into anti-Spanish propaganda that justified English and Dutch colonization
   as rescue. Apply the lesson-1 method (records / argues / omits): the
   atrocities were real; the framing that Spain was *uniquely* cruel was the
   propaganda. English colonization was sold partly as the humane
   alternative — a claim the rest of the module lets the reader test.
4. **Population arithmetic decides the continent.** By 1750: New Spain's
   northern borderlands thinly held; New France ~70,000; the British colonies
   over 1.1 million and doubling every 25 years (Franklin's own 1751
   estimate). The English model — families on farms — out-reproduced the
   rival models; that demographic fact, not battlefield superiority, is why
   North America ended up English-speaking.
5. **What each empire left.** Horses and cattle (Spain), Catholic missions
   and place names (Spain and France), alliance diplomacy with Native
   nations (France), and — through New Netherland — a commercial, polyglot,
   tolerant port the English inherited intact in 1664 (forward pointer to
   middle-colonies).

## Set pieces and worked examples to recreate

- **The three-empires comparison table** as the lesson's spine figure:
  population, labor system, church, Native relations, export — one row each.
- **Las Casas worked reading**: quote one passage, show a De Bry engraving's
  amplification, and separate what the text records from what the printing
  argued. Parallel exercise: the reader applies the same reading to an English
  promotional tract (e.g., a Virginia Company pamphlet's claims).
- **Franklin's 1751 doubling estimate** ("Observations Concerning the Increase
  of Mankind") as a period source that states the demographic argument in the
  era's own words — and previews the settler-selection measurements of the
  next lesson.
- **The Pueblo Revolt narrated tightly**: Popé's coordination by knotted cords,
  the 1680 expulsion, the 1692 return — Native agency as fact, not theme.

## Pitfalls / subtleties to flag

- Do not run the Black Legend in reverse: debunking the "uniquely cruel"
  frame must not become a defense of the encomienda. State the documented
  mortality and coercion, then the propaganda history, as separate findings.
- "Spain enslaved, France traded, England settled" is a first-order model;
  note the exceptions in one line each (Spanish settler societies in Mexico,
  French plantation slavery in Louisiana and the Caribbean, English
  extermination wars in the next lessons).
- The Dutch entry must not read as trivia: New Netherland's 40-year run set
  Manhattan's commercial and pluralist character — the payoff lands in
  middle-colonies, so plant the pointer.
- Populations are estimates; give ranges and sources as lesson 1 modeled.
- Johnson is an opinionated narrative source: use his set pieces as color and
  his Part One structure as a map, but rest claims on the Yawp/Give Me
  Liberty numbers.

# Research brief: indigenous-america (Indigenous America and the Columbian Exchange)

status: drafted — this brief describes the lesson AS DRAFTED (lesson.mdx is the
authority); it exists so later revisions and QA have the plan on record.
module: contact-and-colonies
type: core
goal: Explain how European contact transformed the Americas demographically and ecologically.

Lesson 1 of the course (pedagogy C3): the most polished, ending in a felt win —
the reader finishes able to read a 1502 map as evidence and to state the
demographic argument with numbers.

THESIS ROLE. For the course's standing question — how a strip of colonies
became a continental power — this lesson supplies the precondition, and the
drafted lesson states it in its own words: "the land was takeable because it
was emptying." The cheap land of every later chapter (headrights, the
frontier, the Homestead Act) is the post-contact demographic collapse,
inherited. The lesson also installs the course's standing source-reading
method (records / argues / omits).

## The drafted lesson's five-step structure (as shipped)

1. **The ground floor: 1491.** Cahokia (10,000-20,000, contemporaneous
   London's band; Monks Mound's 700,000 m³) and Tenochtitlán (~200,000) as
   the baseline; the engineering arithmetic behind a fold (Aside), with the
   Chaco timber numbers. KeyFact: no city north of Mexico matched Cahokia's
   peak until after the American Revolution.
2. **The world that sailed: Europe in 1492.** Granada, the Columbus contract,
   the Portuguese Atlantic system (Madeira sugar, Elmina, the caravel and
   volta do mar); news at print speed (Columbus's letter vs the Norse sagas);
   the disjoint-toolkits inventory — ships as the single line that set the
   direction of contact. Remark keeps the geographic explanation
   (Crosby/Diamond) in its weak form.
3. **The Columbian Exchange.** The 1493 cargo; Crosby's term kept precise
   (biological and agricultural, not cultural); eastbound flow raising Old
   World carrying capacity (Nunn-Qian potato estimate), westbound flow
   dominated by pathogens plus livestock; the Plains-horse
   forward-link to conquering-the-west.
4. **The collapse, counted.** Cook-Borah's tribute-register series (25.2M →
   2.65M, 1519-1568) as a FigureSequence with a reader Task computing the
   interval declines; the contested starting height (Sanders, Rosenblat) vs
   the uncontested shape; Koch et al. 2019's hemispheric pooling (60.5M,
   ~90% decline) and the ice-core CO₂ corroboration (Aside). Remark:
   "disease is not an alibi" — encomienda, war, and forced resettlement
   raised mortality and blocked recovery.
5. **Conquest followed collapse.** Cortés (~500 men) and Pizarro (168) as
   the small third input behind epidemic mortality and Indigenous alliance;
   the Andean pre-conquest epidemic and succession war; the myth corrected
   by force ratios.
6. **Reading a source: the Cantino planisphere (1502).** The artifact
   introduced, then the standing method box (records / argues / omits)
   applied: African coast recorded, Tordesillas line argued, American
   interior omitted. Closing Aside on where the documents are (no American
   documentary series before 1776; archaeology, tribute registers, charts,
   conqueror narratives — each with its interest).

## Pitfalls the draft observes (keep in any revision)

- The collapse is not presented as a natural accident absolving conquest;
  the mechanism Remark carries the labor-draft and resettlement evidence.
- Population estimates given as ranges with methods attributed; the
  percentage stated as better constrained than the absolute count.
- No "empty/pristine Americas," no flattening of nations into one category;
  specific peoples named where evidence supports the name.
- The Columbian Exchange kept biological, not metaphorical.
- No civilizational-superiority claims; toolkits presented as disjoint, not
  ranked.

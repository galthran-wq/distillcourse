# Research brief: new-deal (The New Deal)

status: planned
module: depression-and-war
type: core
goal: Analyze what the New Deal built, whom it excluded, and why it did not end the Depression.

## Sources to consult

- **Kennedy, *Freedom from Fear*** — **Ch.5** "The Hundred Days", **Ch.7**
  "Chasing the Phantom of Recovery", **Ch.8** "The Rumble of Discontent" (Long,
  Coughlin, Townsend), **Ch.9** "A Season for Reform" (Social Security, Wagner
  Act, WPA), **Ch.10** "Strike!", **Ch.11** "The Ordeal of Franklin Roosevelt"
  (court-packing and the 1937-38 recession), **Ch.12** "What the New Deal Did".
- **American Yawp Ch.23** — including the "Equal Rights and the New Deal"
  section documenting institutionalized racial discrimination in New Deal
  programs.
- **Give Me Liberty Ch.21 "The New Deal"** — the redefinition of freedom to
  include economic security, and Hoover's rival "liberty" claim.
- **Milestone Documents** #61 TVA Act, #62 NIRA, #63 National Labor Relations
  Act, #64 Social Security Act, #65 FDR's Second New Deal radio address.

## Key points the lesson must cover

1. **The Hundred Days were improvisation, not blueprint.** Bank holiday and the
   Emergency Banking Act; leaving the gold standard; the CCC, AAA, TVA, FERA, and
   NIRA. Kennedy's reading: "try everything", ideologically incoherent, aimed
   first at restoring confidence and stopping the deflation.
2. **What the First New Deal attempted, and where it failed.** NRA codes
   suspending antitrust in exchange for wage and hour floors — unworkable and
   struck down in *Schechter* (1935); AAA paying for acreage reduction, which
   pushed Southern tenants and sharecroppers off the land and was struck down in
   1936.
3. **Pressure from outside made the Second New Deal.** Huey Long's Share Our
   Wealth, Father Coughlin's radio ministry reaching tens of millions weekly, and
   Dr. Townsend's old-age pension movement. Kennedy's argument is that the 1935
   legislative wave answers this pressure, not just the Depression.
4. **The durable institutions.** Social Security (old-age insurance,
   unemployment insurance, aid to dependent children and the blind and disabled),
   funded by dedicated payroll and excise taxes rather than general revenue —
   a deliberate design choice to make the program politically untouchable
   ("those taxes were never a problem of economics... with those taxes in there,
   no damn politician can ever scrap my social security program"). Also the
   Wagner Act, FDIC, SEC, the minimum wage and hours law (1938), and the WPA.
5. **Labor's surge.** The Wagner Act plus the CIO's industrial-union strategy;
   the Flint sit-down strike (1936-37) and the recognition of the UAW; union
   membership roughly tripling by 1940.
6. **Exclusions by design.** Agricultural and domestic workers — about two-thirds
   of employed Black Americans — were written out of Social Security and the
   Wagner Act; local administration of relief and housing entrenched
   segregation; the FHA and HOLC wrote race into mortgage credit (extra lesson:
   redlining). These are not oversights: they were the price of Southern
   Democratic votes.
7. **Overreach and limits.** The 1937 court-packing plan after a landslide,
   the "Roosevelt recession" when spending was cut back, the conservative
   coalition consolidating from 1938 — and the fact that unemployment stayed in
   the mid-teens until war mobilization. The New Deal changed what government is
   for; it did not end the Depression.

## Set pieces and worked examples to recreate

- **The Social Security signing photograph**, with Frances Perkins the only woman
  present, as the institutional moment; then the payroll-tax design argument.
- **The relief-politics data** that Kennedy's critics highlight: uneven relief
  spending by state, and roughly 75% of relief recipients voting for FDR in 1936.
  Use it as an honest complication about how spending programs and coalitions
  interact — noting it comes via a critical review of Kennedy, not from Kennedy's
  own argument.
- **Court-packing as a political-capital case study**: a landslide in November
  1936, a plan announced in February 1937, and a stalled second term. Worked
  example: list what the plan sought, what it cost, and what it nonetheless
  achieved (the Court's "switch in time").
- **Coverage arithmetic**: compute the share of Black workers covered by the
  original Social Security Act given the agricultural and domestic exclusions.
  This is the lesson's sharpest quantitative exercise.
- **Long's Share Our Wealth** as a costed proposal and an electoral threat.

## Pitfalls / subtleties to flag

- Do not credit the New Deal with ending the Depression: state the unemployment
  series and the military-Keynesian conclusion plainly (Kennedy Ch.12, Ch.18).
- Equally, do not conclude it failed: the institutional legacy is the point, and
  the counterfactual is a deflationary spiral with no floor.
- The New Deal was not one program: First and Second have different logics and
  different constituencies.
- FDR's popularity is not evidence of policy effectiveness; the two claims need
  separate evidence.
- The exclusions must be stated in the same breath as the achievements, not
  appended as a caveat.

# Research brief: new-england-covenant (Plymouth and Puritan New England: The Seedbed of Self-Government)

status: planned
module: contact-and-colonies
type: core
goal: Explain how Plymouth and Massachusetts Bay built self-governing institutions — compact, congregation, town meeting — and the literate, family-based population that ran them.

THESIS ROLE. This is the lesson where the course's first two causal claims are
established with measurements: (1) the settler population was unusually
selected — family migration, dissenting motivation, near-universal male
literacy engineered by statute, print density; (2) self-government compounded
from the bottom up — Mayflower Compact → covenant congregation → covenanted
town and town meeting → General Court — the institutional ladder the founding
module later shows reaching the Constitution. The Wampanoag alliance of 1621
and Thanksgiving belong here as documented history, not ceremony: a treaty
that held some 54 years, with its artifact.

## Sources to consult

- **American Yawp Ch.2-3** — Plymouth, Massachusetts Bay, Puritan theology and
  society, the New England town. Spine narrative.
- **Mayflower Compact (1620)** — full text (one paragraph; Avalon Project and
  Yawp reader). Quote it whole and read it as text: who signs, what is
  created, by what authority.
- **Winthrop, "A Model of Christian Charity" (1630)** — the "Citty upon a
  Hill" sermon; quote the famous sentence in period spelling and read the
  covenant logic around it (the "eyes of all people" clause is a conditional
  warning, not a boast — say so).
- **Bradford, *Of Plymouth Plantation*** — the primary narrative for the 1621
  treaty and the harvest feast; Winslow's *Mourt's Relation* passage is the
  only contemporary account of the feast itself. Both free full text.
- **Massachusetts School Laws of 1642 and 1647** ("Old Deluder Satan Act") —
  operative clauses quotable; the statutory engineering of literacy.
- **Johnson, *A History of the American People*, Part One** — the Winthrop
  section (read directly in the verified excerpt), including the diary
  providence-readings; the "first nation whose most distant origins are fully
  recorded" claim as a quotable framing.
- **MIT 21H.101 Weeks 1-3** — the Dane-family device (used in
  colonial-societies; cross-reference, don't duplicate).
- Literacy and wage measurements: Lockridge's New England literacy estimates
  (~60% male signature literacy c.1660 rising toward ~90% by 1790) and the
  colonial real-wage literature (colonial free workers' living standards
  above English by the 18th century). Verify specific figures before quoting;
  attribute the method (signature counts on wills and deeds).

## Key points the lesson must cover

1. **The Compact, read as text.** 41 signatures, one paragraph, written
   because the ship had landed outside the Virginia patent and the "strangers"
   aboard argued no government would bind them. Consent creating authority —
   not a theory imported later, but a practical fix dated November 11, 1620.
   Its afterlife as template, not as constitution: it creates a "civil body
   politic" empowered to make "just and equal laws."
2. **The Wampanoag alliance of 1621, both-sided.** Massasoit's position:
   epidemics of 1616-19 had devastated the coastal Wampanoag while sparing
   the rival Narragansett; a Plymouth alliance was rational power politics.
   Tisquantum (Squanto) — kidnapped, sold in Spain, returned via England to
   find Patuxet dead — as translator with his own agenda. The treaty's terms
   (mutual defense, mutual justice) and its duration: it held until the
   1670s, some 54 years. The autumn 1621 harvest feast — ~50 colonists,
   ~90 of Massasoit's men, three days, documented in one Winslow paragraph —
   is the alliance's artifact; the national holiday came in 1863
   (forward-link to union-victory's era). State facts, no ceremony.
3. **Covenant as an institutional technology.** The congregation formed by
   explicit mutual covenant; the town formed the same way — land granted to
   a congregation, self-governed by town meeting, taxing for church and
   school. Self-government was not a later achievement; it was the unit of
   settlement, reproduced at every scale. The General Court (assembly) atop
   it, with the franchise tied to church membership — state the boundary
   plainly (this was a covenant community, not a democracy; dissenters were
   banished — Williams 1636, Hutchinson 1638, the Quaker hangings 1659-61).
4. **The demography, measured.** Family migration (the Great Migration's
   ~21,000, sex ratio ~3:2), longevity (a man of 20 reaching ~70), six to
   eight surviving children, population doubling each generation — the
   figures colonial-societies then deploys comparatively. Establish them
   here from the county studies.
5. **Literacy and print, engineered.** The 1642 law requiring households to
   teach children to read; the 1647 law requiring towns of 50 families to
   hire a schoolmaster and towns of 100 to keep a grammar school; Harvard
   (1636, six years after landing); the Cambridge press (1638, first in
   English America); the Bay Psalm Book (1640, first book printed). By the
   Revolution, New England male literacy approached universality — the
   highest in the world — and the colonies sustained more newspapers per
   capita than England. This is the settler-selection measurement the thesis
   runs on; state the signature-count method and its limits.

## Set pieces and worked examples to recreate

- **The Compact worked reading**: the full text with the operative phrases
  marked (covenant and combine / civil body politic / just and equal laws),
  then the records/argues/omits method applied. Parallel exercise: the reader
  applies the same reading to a town covenant (Dedham's 1636 covenant is
  printed and quotable).
- **The treaty's terms tabulated**: the six clauses of the 1621 agreement as
  Bradford records them, read for reciprocity (mutual defense, mutual
  disarmament at meetings, mutual justice) — an actual diplomatic instrument,
  not a feast story.
- **The literacy ladder computed**: from the 1647 law's thresholds, compute
  what share of Massachusetts towns were legally required to run schools by
  1700; then the signature-literacy series as a small chart.
- **Winthrop's sentence in context**: "wee shall be as a Citty upon a Hill,
  the eyes of all people are uppon us" — quote with the next sentence (the
  curse if the covenant fails) so the conditional reads as written.
- **The 54-year arithmetic**: 1621 to Massasoit's death (1661) to the 1675
  breakdown — the alliance outlasted every signatory; the war that ended it
  is the next lesson's subject (explicit forward pointer).

## Pitfalls / subtleties to flag

- No Thanksgiving pageantry and no debunking pageantry either: one documented
  feast, one documented treaty, one 1863 holiday — three separate facts with
  three dates.
- The Compact is not a constitution and did not found American democracy by
  itself; it is the first rung of a ladder, and the lesson's claim is about
  the ladder.
- Puritan self-government excluded: non-members from the franchise,
  dissenters by banishment, and (in the next lesson's frame) Native people
  from the covenant entirely. State the boundaries in the same sections as
  the institutions — the thesis is selection and compounding, not virtue.
- Squanto's biography is documented and astonishing; tell it in three
  sentences without novelistic embroidery.
- Literacy estimates are signature-based proxies with known biases (they
  undercount readers who could not write, especially women); attribute the
  method.
- Johnson's providence-reading quotes (smallpox "cleared our title") are
  Winthrop's own words — usable as primary evidence of the settlers'
  self-understanding, with the source named.

## Canon additions (curated expansion, July 2026)

- **Lutz (ed.), *Colonial Origins of the American Constitution*** (FREE
  Liberty Fund PDF; full text cached `texts/lutz-colonial-origins.pdf`/`.txt`)
  — upgrades this brief's primary-source base from two documents to the whole
  documentary ladder: Mayflower Compact from the 1669 Morton reprint, the
  earliest surviving text (doc 3, with the gem that contemporaries called it
  "The Plymouth Combination" — "Mayflower Compact" dates from 1793); Plymouth
  Oath of Allegiance 1625, a colony founded by oath alone (doc 4); Salem
  Covenant 1629, "probably the briefest covenant in American history" (doc 5);
  Oath of a Freeman (docs 9/15) for citizenship-by-oath; Pilgrim Code of Law
  1636 explicitly citing "the combination made at Cape Cod" as its own legal
  basis (doc 20) — one foundation document citing an earlier one, the
  compounding claim in a single quotation; Massachusetts Body of Liberties
  1641 with the headnote's 7-of-26 Bill of Rights lineage claim (doc 22).
  Use for the Compact worked reading's parallel exercise and set-piece quotes.
- **Lutz, *The Origins of American Constitutionalism*** (full OCR cached
  `texts/lutz-origins.txt`; verify quotes vs print) — the analytic frame for
  key point 3: church covenant (Charlestown-Boston 1630) as the template;
  the foundation-elements ladder (covenant → +institutions = constitution);
  federalism-from-covenant (Fundamental Orders uniting three towns, 1639).
  Quotable: "it makes more sense to call Locke an American than to call
  America Lockean" (Locke was 9 when the Piscataqua Combination was written).
- **Fischer, *Albion's Seed*, Ch. 1** (summary-based; cached
  `texts/albionseed-*`; contested synthesis — attribute, don't assert) — the
  East Anglia→Massachusetts migration as a selected population in Fischer's
  telling: ~21,000 of ~80,000 emigrating Puritans, gentry/artisan/professional
  skew, "ordered liberty" as the regional folkway. Color gems (1648
  stubborn-son law never applied to a child; 94%/98% marriage rates;
  literacy roughly double the English average) support key points 4-5 but
  each needs a page-verified citation before use.
- **Tocqueville forward-link** — Vol. 1 Ch. V: "In New England townships were
  completely and definitively constituted as early as 1650." The
  tocqueville-democracy lesson (module 3) picks up this exact sentence; the
  ladder figure drawn here is the one his 1831 observations re-certify.

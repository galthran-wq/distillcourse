# Research brief: rise-of-the-right (The Rise of the Right)

status: planned
module: postwar-america
type: core
goal: Analyze the conservative movement's road to power — markets, law and order, and the culture wars.

## Sources to consult

- **American Yawp Ch.29 "The Triumph of the Right" (2nd ed. "The Rise of the
  Right")** — the Reagan revolution, culture wars, the AIDS crisis, and the New
  Right.
- **Give Me Liberty Ch.26 "The Conservative Turn, 1969-1988"** — plus the Voices
  of Freedom pairing of Reagan's inaugural with Schlafly's anti-ERA argument:
  two definitions of freedom, one economic and one traditionalist.
- **MIT 21H.102 Session 23 "Conservatives in Power and the Long Life of 'Law and
  Order'"** — the rise of mass incarceration as a bipartisan construction — and
  **Session 24 "The Rediscovery of the Market and the Rise of a Digital Age"** —
  neoliberalism, tech-industry labor, and Barlow's cyberlibertarian manifesto.

## Key points the lesson must cover

1. **The movement built institutions before it won elections.** Goldwater's 1964
   defeat as an organizational beginning; think tanks (Heritage 1973, Cato,
   AEI's expansion), the Powell Memorandum (1971), business political
   mobilization and the growth of corporate PACs, direct-mail fundraising, and
   the legal movement that becomes the Federalist Society (1982). Reagan's 1980
   victory is the output of two decades of institution-building.
2. **Three revolts, one coalition.** Against taxes and regulation (Proposition 13,
   the supply-side argument); against crime and disorder (law and order,
   busing, the "silent majority"); against the cultural 1960s (the religious
   right, abortion, the ERA, school prayer). The coalition's internal tensions —
   libertarian versus traditionalist — are as important as its unity.
3. **Reaganomics, measured.** The 1981 tax cut lowering the top marginal rate
   from 70% to 50% and later to 28%; deregulation; the Volcker disinflation
   (with the 1981-82 recession as its cost, unemployment near 11%); air traffic
   controllers fired in 1981 and what it signaled to unions; defense buildup and
   tripling federal debt. Give the outcome series: inflation defeated, growth
   resumed, inequality rising, union density falling.
4. **Law and order and mass incarceration.** The bipartisan construction from the
   1968 Omnibus Crime Control Act through the 1984 and 1986 drug laws (including
   the 100:1 crack-powder sentencing disparity) to the 1994 crime bill; the state
   and federal prison population rising from about 200,000 in 1972 to over 1.5
   million by 2000, with severe racial disparity. Present it as policy with
   measurable outputs, and name both parties' roles.
5. **Culture wars.** The ERA's defeat; *Roe*'s politicization and the pro-life
   movement's growth; the Moral Majority (1979) and the Christian Coalition;
   school prayer and textbook fights; and the AIDS crisis — over 20,000 US deaths
   before Reagan spoke the word publicly in 1985, ACT UP's founding in 1987, and
   the FDA and NIH policy changes that activism forced. The AIDS section is where
   the lesson's freedom lens bites hardest.
6. **The Cold War's end.** The renewed arms race and the "evil empire" rhetoric;
   INF (1987); Gorbachev's reforms as the primary cause; the debate over how much
   US pressure contributed. Present the historiographical dispute rather than
   resolving it.

## Set pieces and worked examples to recreate

- **Reagan's inaugural against Schlafly's anti-ERA argument** (Voices of Freedom
  pairing): two claims about what freedom is — government off your back, and
  traditional roles protected. Worked example: extract each argument's subject
  ("free from what, for whom") and show why they coexist in one coalition.
  Parallel exercise: do the same for a Moral Majority statement and a
  supply-side manifesto.
- **The tax-rate ladder computed**: effective tax change for households at
  several income levels under the 1981 and 1986 acts — who gained how much.
- **The incarceration series** with policy dates marked, presented as a
  cause-and-effect argument the reader can test against the crime-rate series.
- **AIDS timeline against federal response**: deaths per year against
  appropriations and public statements — the gap is the argument.

## Pitfalls / subtleties to flag

- Do not treat 1980 as a sudden turn: the deregulatory shift began under Carter
  (airlines, trucking) and the fiscal turn began with the 1978 tax revolt.
- Supply-side claims about revenue are contested and were not borne out in the
  1981-83 receipts; state what happened and note the deficit response of 1982-84
  tax increases.
- Mass incarceration is not a Republican project alone; the 1994 crime bill and
  Democratic support are part of the record.
- Distinguish the Cold War's end causes: internal Soviet economic and political
  failure is the primary driver in most current scholarship; US pressure is
  debated as a contributing factor.
- Avoid presenting the culture wars as symmetrical grievance; describe the
  specific policy demands each side made.

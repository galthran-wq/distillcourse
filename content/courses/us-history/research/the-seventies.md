# Research brief: the-seventies (The Unraveling: 1968-1980)

status: planned
module: postwar-america
type: core
goal: Explain the 1970s crisis of confidence — Vietnam's end, Watergate, stagflation, and the new identity politics.

THESIS ROLE. The constitutional machine's modern stress test, run in public:
Watergate worked through courts, press, and congressional oversight to remove
a president without a shot — the accountability chain worked as a case study
in the institutions the course has tracked since 1787, with the honest
caveats (contingent disclosure, Church Committee revelations) stated. The
economic break (stagflation, the end of Bretton Woods) closes the postwar
boom the thesis measured and sets up the next lesson's turn; the trust-in-
government series is the decade's thesis-relevant measurement.

## Key points the lesson must cover

1. **Vietnam ends badly.** Vietnamization, the secret bombing and invasion of
   Cambodia, Kent State (1970), the Pentagon Papers (1971) and *New York Times
   Co. v. United States*, the 1973 Paris accords, the 1975 fall of Saigon, and
   the War Powers Resolution (1973) as Congress's attempt to reclaim the power it
   delegated in 1964. ~58,000 US dead; Vietnamese deaths in the millions.
2. **Watergate as a constitutional stress test.** The break-in, the cover-up, the
   Senate hearings and the taping system, *United States v. Nixon* (1974)
   rejecting an absolute executive privilege claim, impeachment articles, and
   resignation. What worked: courts, press, and congressional oversight. What it
   cost: a durable collapse in trust in government — plot the survey series.
3. **The economy stops working the old way.** The end of Bretton Woods (1971),
   the 1973 OPEC embargo and the 1979 shock, deindustrialization in steel and
   auto regions, and stagflation — simultaneous high inflation and high
   unemployment, which the prevailing Keynesian framework did not predict.
   Real wages stop growing; the postwar economic consensus breaks.
4. **The New York fiscal crisis (1975)** and the emergence of austerity politics
   in cities — a compact case study of the decade's fiscal turn.
5. **Movements mature and diversify.** Second-wave feminism (Title IX 1972, the
   ERA's passage by Congress and failed ratification, *Roe* 1973), the Chicano
   movement and the UFW, the American Indian Movement and Alcatraz, gay
   liberation after Stonewall, and the disability rights movement (Section 504,
   the 1977 sit-ins). The manifesto genre is the connective tissue.
6. **Backlash becomes a governing identity.** Nixon's "silent majority" framing,
   the busing conflicts in Boston and elsewhere, the tax revolt (California
   Proposition 13, 1978), the emergence of the religious right, and Carter's 1979
   "crisis of confidence" speech — which named the mood and was punished for it.
   Sets up rise-of-the-right directly.

## Set pieces and worked examples to recreate

- **The trust-in-government series** (from about 75% in 1964 to about 25% by
  1980) plotted against Vietnam, Watergate, and the oil shocks — one chart that
  is the lesson's thesis.
- **Watergate as an accountability chain, worked**: what each institution did and
  what would have happened if any one link had failed. Worked example; the
  parallel exercise asks the reader to apply the same test to another
  constitutional crisis in the course (1798, 1876, or 1937).
- **A stagflation reading**: the misery index (inflation plus unemployment)
  1965-1982, with the two oil shocks marked — and a one-paragraph explanation of
  why supply shocks break the inflation-unemployment tradeoff.
- **The ERA's ratification map** and the counter-campaign (Schlafly's STOP ERA),
  which the Voices of Freedom pairing sets against Reagan's inaugural in the next
  lesson.

## Pitfalls / subtleties to flag

- The 1970s were not simply a decade of decline: environmental law (EPA, Clean
  Air and Water Acts), OSHA, Title IX, and major civil-rights enforcement all
  date from Nixon's presidency. State the paradox.
- Stagflation had multiple causes (supply shocks, accommodative monetary policy,
  the end of Bretton Woods, productivity slowdown); do not assign it to one.
- Watergate is not simply "the system worked" — the disclosure depended on
  contingencies, and much of the abuse (COINTELPRO, surveillance) was revealed
  separately by the Church Committee.
- Busing conflicts were about school assignment plans, not integration in the
  abstract; describe the actual policies and the actual grievances.
- The religious right's mobilization is often dated to *Roe*; the documented
  early trigger includes federal action against segregated Christian academies.
  Note the historiography.

# Research brief: the-twenties (The New Era: The Twenties)

status: planned
module: reform-war-normalcy
type: core
goal: Analyze the consumer economy, cultural modernism, and reactionary politics of the 1920s.

THESIS ROLE. Mass production arrives (thesis element 4's technology thread at
full scale — the assembly line, electrification, the price of a Model T
falling through the decade) and with it the first mass consumer economy in
history, measured in durables per household. The lesson keeps the course's
double ledger: the measured prosperity, its uneven distribution (farm incomes,
the quota laws closing the immigration channel the thesis counted), and the
structural fragilities Kennedy's Ch.1 catalogs as the setup for 1929.

## Sources to consult

- **American Yawp Ch.22 "The New Era" (2nd ed. "The Twenties")** — consumer
  culture, the Harlem Renaissance, the Klan revival, normalcy.
- **Give Me Liberty Ch.20 "From Business Culture to Great Depression: The
  Twenties, 1920-1932"** — business culture as an ideology of freedom, and the
  contest over what freedom meant in a consumer economy.
- **MIT 21H.102 Session 10 "Culture and Conflict in the 1920s"** — consumer
  culture, immigration restriction, and the New Woman (reading list only; no
  published lecture notes).
- **Kennedy, *Freedom from Fear*, Ch.1** — "The American People on the Eve of the
  Great Depression": 1920s prosperity and its hidden fragilities (farm
  depression, thin credit, weak banking structure). This chapter is the bridge
  into the next module and should close this lesson.

## Key points the lesson must cover

1. **Mass production met mass credit.** Assembly-line output, the Model T's price
   falling through the decade, electrification of urban homes, and installment
   buying — by the late 1920s a large share of durable goods was bought on
   credit. Consumption became a form of participation and a form of debt.
2. **A national culture.** Radio networks, Hollywood, chain stores, national
   advertising, and spectator sport produced, for the first time, a
   simultaneously shared commercial culture — and the advertising industry's
   explicit theory that desires could be manufactured.
3. **The Harlem Renaissance and the New Negro.** Hughes, Hurston, Locke's
   anthology, Bessie Smith and Armstrong; Garvey's UNIA as a mass movement; the
   Great Migration's cultural payoff in Northern cities.
4. **Modern women.** Suffrage won, the "New Woman", falling birth rates,
   expanding clerical employment, and the gap between the flapper image and
   most women's actual work and wages.
5. **The reaction, in equal weight.** The second Ku Klux Klan — reborn in 1915,
   peaking in the mid-1920s with millions of members, national in scope
   (Indiana, Oregon) and targeting Catholics, Jews, and immigrants as well as
   Black Americans; Prohibition and its enforcement failure; the Scopes trial
   (1925); Sacco and Vanzetti (executed 1927); and the National Origins Acts of
   1921 and 1924 setting quotas by 1890 census shares and barring most Asian
   immigration outright — closing the door studied in immigration-and-the-city.
6. **Prosperity's uneven distribution and hidden fragilities.** Agriculture
   depressed all decade; coal and textiles in trouble; income concentration
   rising; thousands of small unit banks with no deposit insurance; brokers'
   loans and margin buying. Kennedy's Ch.1 list is the setup for 1929.

## Set pieces and worked examples to recreate

- **The 1924 quota arithmetic, computed.** The Act set each nationality's quota
  at 2% of its US population recorded in the **1890** census — a base year chosen
  precisely because it predates the southern and eastern European wave. Worked
  example: compute the Italian quota under the 1890 base and under a 1910 base
  and compare. This single computation exposes the law's design intent better
  than any characterization; the parallel exercise repeats it for another
  nationality.
- **Installment credit as a mechanism**: the household balance sheet before and
  after buying a car on time, and what happens to that household if income drops
  10% — the setup for the Depression's demand collapse.
- **Klan membership and geography**: the 1920s revival mapped, to break the
  "Southern relic" assumption.
- **Farm vs. urban income divergence** across the decade.

## Pitfalls / subtleties to flag

- The "Roaring Twenties" describes an urban, white, middle-class experience; farm
  households and most Black and immigrant workers did not share it. Do not let
  the decade's imagery stand as its economics.
- The second Klan is a different organization from the Reconstruction Klan and
  from the 1950s-60s Klan — different membership, geography, and targets.
- Prohibition did reduce alcohol consumption measurably; its failure was
  enforcement, corruption, and political durability, not total ineffectiveness.
- Do not present 1929 as visible in advance; Kennedy's point is that the
  fragilities were structural and largely unremarked.
- The Harlem Renaissance was not a departure from politics: it was in
  conversation with the NAACP, Garveyism, and the migration.

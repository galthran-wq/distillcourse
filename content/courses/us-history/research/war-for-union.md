# Research brief: war-for-union (The War for Union, 1861-1863)

status: planned
module: civil-war-and-reconstruction
type: core
goal: Explain how both sides mobilized for war and why the short-war illusion died at Bull Run, Shiloh, and Antietam.

THESIS ROLE. The system's supreme test opens: the war is told in the
Lincoln–Douglass register the course states in its intro — the Union cause as
the constitutional order defending itself against a counterrevolution made on
behalf of slavery (the seceders' own declarations, from sectional-crisis),
and the cost counted without flinching. The material-imbalance section is
also the thesis's industrial argument mid-stride: the mobilization capacity
measured here is what the enabling-conditions chapters built.

## Key points the lesson must cover

1. **Sumter and the second secession wave.** Lincoln's First Inaugural offered
   no interference with slavery where it existed but refused disunion; the fort's
   resupply forced the choice; Anderson surrendered after 34 hours; Lincoln's
   call for 75,000 volunteers pushed Virginia, Arkansas, Tennessee, and North
   Carolina out. The war's first act converted a seven-state crisis into an
   eleven-state war.
2. **The material imbalance, quantified.** Population (~22 million vs. ~9
   million, of whom 3.5 million enslaved), industrial output, railroad mileage,
   and financial capacity. Then state immediately why the imbalance was not
   decisive on its own: the Confederacy needed only to survive, the Union had to
   conquer and hold a country the size of western Europe.
3. **Two strategies.** Union: blockade, control of the Mississippi, and pressure
   on Richmond (the "Anaconda" concept). Confederate: defensive-offensive,
   European recognition through cotton diplomacy, and endurance to exhaust
   Northern will.
4. **The casualty escalation.** First Bull Run (July 1861) ends the ninety-day
   assumption. Shiloh (April 1862): about 23,000 casualties in two days —
   exceeding all previous American wars combined. Antietam (September 1862):
   the bloodiest single day, 20,000+ casualties. Present this as a ladder; the
   escalation is what radicalized the war's conduct and aims.
5. **Mobilizing societies, not just armies.** Conscription North and South
   (with substitution and commutation), war finance (greenbacks, bonds, income
   tax; Confederate inflation), and the home-front consequences — culminating in
   the New York draft riots of July 1863, the deadliest urban insurrection in
   US history, whose targets were Black New Yorkers.
6. **Photography changed the audience.** Gardner's Antietam photographs, shown
   in Brady's New York gallery as "The Dead of Antietam", brought unburied
   corpses to a civilian public for the first time.

## Set pieces and worked examples to recreate

- **The Fort Sumter telegram**: Anderson's message on pasted tape strips, April
  18, 1861 — the war's first official document, read as an artifact.
- **The First Inaugural manuscript page** with Lincoln's handwritten revision
  strip pasted over a printed galley — revision made physically visible. Pair
  with the fact that Seward drafted the closing paragraph Lincoln then rewrote
  into "the better angels of our nature": a worked example of how a famous
  sentence was produced.
- **The casualty ladder** as a computation: casualties per day of battle across
  Bull Run, Shiloh, Antietam, and (forward) Gettysburg, with the prior
  US-war total as a reference line.
- **Harper's Weekly on the draft riots** (Aug 1, 1863): five panels including a
  lynching, read as how the illustrated press reported a domestic crisis.
- **Gardner's Antietam dead** as the moment photography entered war reporting.

## Pitfalls / subtleties to flag

- Casualty ≠ death: the standard figures combine killed, wounded, captured, and
  missing. Define the term once and use it consistently.
- The revised total war-dead estimate (~750,000, Hacker) supersedes the older
  620,000 figure and belongs in union-victory; if it appears here, attribute it.
- Do not present Union victory as inevitable from the resource tables — the
  material argument has to be paired with the political-will argument (see
  union-victory).
- Border states (Missouri, Kentucky, Maryland, Delaware) were slave states inside
  the Union; that fact constrains everything about emancipation policy in the
  next lesson.
- Keep battle narration disciplined: names, dates, and consequences, not
  tactical detail.
